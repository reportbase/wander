The room

A reader stands at home, the room's corner. Its addresses are given there, before anything arrives: the inscribed sweep, every direction from one wall to the other, gives where things are: directions, no amounts. A thing's shape about its own centre is a radius per direction, an amount, had only by ranging (a writing) or from further standpoints: its shape, not its size. Holding a thing and the wall at once gives its size in the room's units. To put a thing at a place in metres the reader must become a writer and move.
