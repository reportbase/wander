The angle and the radius

One shape, two places to stand. From a point of the outline, home, the addresses are given: each direction, a ratio, and no amount; what arrives paints it and carries no distance. From the centre the directions are given too, and the radius along each, how far the outline lies, is an amount, had only by ranging (a writing) or from more standpoints. The inscribed sweep gives the angle, the radius from the centre an amount. Neither gives the unit: scale the world and neither the addresses nor the shape moves. Only the writer, holding all three, draws the thing at its size.

The world, as only the writer sees it: the outline, the centre C with its fixed directions (gold) and home A with its rays (teal).

The addresses from home, the inscribed sweep: directions, laid by v/h on the turn, (2/π)·arctan(v/h), the corner at ½. No lengths anywhere.

From the centre: an amount along each given direction (ranged, a writing, or from more standpoints). The axis has no unit: the common factor is not read, so it holds the shape in amounts (each over their geometric mean), not the size.

The writer: the outline at its true size in the reader's own H, only with all three. The unit is one amount carried in from outside, the radius along one direction in H, as one known move gives it.

A uniform sweep from each pivot (log of the count across). Inscribed: the median settles at the corner, 1; the mean (dashed) keeps climbing. Central: the mean settles, since amounts add.

Polar coordinates about a pivot split a point into an angle and a radius; the two sweeps take one each. The address is the ratio itself: the projection through the standpoint has done the dividing. The radius from a centre is an amount, written or had from more standpoints, in a unit no reading fixes.
