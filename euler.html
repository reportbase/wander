Euler's plane

Write a point as z = d·e^(iθ): its direction θ (where it is) times its size d. Take the logarithm and the two come apart onto two axes: ln(z/H) = ln(d/H) + iθ, H the thumb. One Cartesian grid, the writer's, is drawn three ways.

Cartesian: straight lines, every cell alike. Euler's plane: rays become horizontal lines, circles vertical ones. Turning the world slides everything up or down; scaling the world, or moving the thumb, slides it sideways and changes nothing else. The shells at one, two, four, eight thumbs are vertical lines ln 2 apart: no nesting, an even grid. The two corners are mirror lines: θ = 45° (a reading and its reciprocal) and one thumb out (a distance d and H²/d). The Cartesian grid lines become curves running off without end both ways.

The thumb's lay: the distance axis squeezed by (2/π)·atan(d/H), which is (2/π)·atan of e^x: Mercator's own function, so the distance laid by the thumb is a turn too. The near field in proportion, the far bunched on the horizon's edge, never reached. As a quarter disc it is the hemisphere's slice.

The writer's sphere: put the plane on a sphere so that one thumb is its equator (stereographically). The eye is the south pole and the horizon the north. The three charts are its maps: Euler's plane is its Mercator chart, the lay square its plain latitude and longitude, the quarter disc a map out from the eye. One thumb in or out is the reflection in the equator. Turning the world turns the sphere about its axis; moving the thumb is a boost along it, every latitude sliding toward a pole. The two commute, which is why both are slides on Euler's plane. The sphere is the writer's drawing; the reader has the lay.

The heat depends on the chart. Counted per unit of each chart's own area, the read is flat on the lay, sech(ln(d/H)) on Euler's plane, endless at the eye on the Cartesian grid.

A line, a spiral: on Euler's plane every straight line is the same curve moved, and a logarithmic spiral is a straight line. Each is drawn with its mirror in one thumb: a line's is a circle through the eye.

n reads: (1 + iθ/n)ⁿ, θ turned in n reads composed. The polygon closes on e^(iθ), the writer's limit; its angle falls short by about θ³/(3n²), and its length grows by about θ²/(2n), a length no read sees.
