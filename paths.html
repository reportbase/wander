One sweep, three paths

From all of h to all of v, along a circle, a straight line or a square's two edges, each swept evenly along its own length. The reading v/h is the direction alone, the same on every path. What differs is how much of the sweep is done when a direction is reached. Treating the two parties alike fixes home, the corner and the horizon, and nothing between. Only the circle is even in the turn, and that is where 2/π comes from.

Three paths, one direction

Home is the corner of the axes; the directions from it are the addresses. The chosen path is cut into 24 equal lengths, and each cut's direction is drawn: even on the circle; on the line, sparse near the corner and crowded at the ends; on the square, the other way round. The ray at θ meets all three at one reading.

How much of the sweep is done

b against θ. Every curve passes through home (0°, 0), the corner (45°, ½) and the horizon (90°, 1), and each is the same turned half round the corner, b(θ) + b(90° − θ) = 1. Only the circle's is straight. Dashed: the circle weighted by sin² 2θ, fair too, but flat at both ends.

The turn and its shadow, on the circle

Equal steps of turn cast unequal pieces of shadow on the line across the view, and the pieces cover it exactly once. A writer, holding the whole turn, measures the central sweep as the turn over its shadow and the inscribed sweep as the shadow over the turn: π/2 and 2/π at the horizon, both the writer's numbers. In parallax the same ratio is the relative distance error over the relative parallax error, γ/sin γ, with γ the pair's facing, twice its sweep angle: π/2 on the Thales circle (parallax from the corner).

No mean, unless the weight leaves the horizon

The mean, taking the readings up to S, against S. On all three paths it climbs by the same amount every tenfold, forever: no mean. The weight sin² 2θ keeps its median at the corner but vanishes at the horizon, and its mean settles at 4/π. Every median is at the corner, s = 1.

Geometry only, in unit relations: no breadth and no distance enters.
