Fingers browsing fingers browsing a gravity array. Below the ground, masses (rods seen end on). Each pulls on the ground with its own Cauchy: one fixed shape, as wide as the mass is deep, times its amount. Gravity's fingers. Added up they make the array: a place on the ground, and the pull there.

Above the ground, a reader at height a turns evenly. Its fingers land on the ground by its own Cauchy, as wide as it is high, and each is painted by the array's value where it lands. The bottom bar is those fingers, one column an equal turn.

The mean over the reader's fingers equals the masses read from a + d: the heights add (gold line on the dashed one). Raise the reader and it is the same as sinking the masses. Mass ×10 repaints every finger and moves none; the depth and the mass are never told apart this way.

A row. Masses one spacing apart at one depth, without end. Read from height a the heights add, u = (a + d) spacings, and the row's ripple is 2/sinh(2πu): at one spacing a 0.0075 ripple, at two 1.4·10⁻⁵. Uneven masses are told apart only as e^(−πu). Raise the reader past about one spacing and it holds the total mass per spacing and almost nothing of how that mass is shared out; pulling the masses back out of the reading (downward continuation) fails at u ≈ ln(1/noise)/π.

The two kinds of fingers are different plays on one stage: the reader's a law over directions, gravity's a weight times a mass, summed. A pull is not a read.

Both are one heat map, derived two ways. The reader's is built about its look from a point picked; each mass's is built from the mass itself, with no point picked and so no facing. That is not the dual hemispherical vantage, which is a synthesis and stood at by no one. And the reader takes in the whole ground at once, every mass landing in its one heat as paint.
