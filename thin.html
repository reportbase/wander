The same signal, a smaller share

A ball sends the same light whether you are near it or far. What changes is how much of it you catch: the light thins as it spreads over a bigger sphere, the inverse square. Your grain decides how the loss shows. Near, the ball covers many grains, each as bright as ever, and distance costs grains. Far, it is under one grain, and distance costs light. The switch is your resolution limit: the ball one grain across. The law itself has no scale.

What reaches you: 15 by 15 of your grains, each ε = 0.02 across, round the ball. A grain's shade is the light it catches. The ring is the ball's true outline.

Grains lit and how bright

Gold: grains lit (never fewer than one). Teal: the brightest grain. White, dotted: the light caught, their product, 1/d² all the way. The dashed line is the resolution limit, where the ball is one grain across. Log scales.

Where the rest goes

The ball sends to every direction alike. At distance d its light is spread over a sphere of 4πd² standpoints (each an aperture of 1). Each catches 1/(4πd²) of it; all of them together catch it all, at every d. One reader holds one share: the cost of being situated.
