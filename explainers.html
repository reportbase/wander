Explainers

Scroll, and the picture follows. An explainer takes one idea from the essays and shows it a step at a time: the text down one side, a scene beside it whose clock is set by how far you have read, each step a stretch of the scene that can also be played on its own. The scenes are made in the leaf editor of the draw project and played by its TVF player, so the explainers open there.

What an array is

Ten slots are a write's: a count held, each slot a share of the way, every share moving when the array grows. A read from two slots out from the foot turns in equal steps, landing thick near the foot and thin far along. Where the slots end the paint stops, at 0.874 of the turn, 0.937 at twenty; with no length to find, a horizon. The slots can be laid on the quarter turn, on the blueprint, their boundaries crossing a post one thumb away at the half-Cauchy's quantiles, but that needs N. A program recreates the read by a boundary, K = 32 slots of its own, a world of 100 stopping its paint at slot 29. And a[i] read twice: the ray from home at θ and the radius from the centre at 2θ, meeting on the circle.

  2. The reader's sweep
  3. The horizon
  4. Laid on the quarter turn, by a writer

Why the plane is special

A standpoint above a line: equal turns land as the Cauchy, and walkers released there land by the same law; a new zero and a new thumb carry the standpoint and the law together. Inside a circle, off its centre, an even turn lands unevenly, and twice that landing less the uniform is the circle's harmonic measure, its mean the standpoint. On the sphere the same three rules give three curves, peaks 6, 7 and 9 times the uniform, where the plane gave one. Two books given the same two quarter turns in the two orders end 120° apart. An arrow carried round the octant's loop comes home turned 90°, the loop's solid angle. And what survives: above a flat wall the turn and the walker still agree, inside a ball they part, and the wedge's corners and thirds hold in any number of dimensions.

  2. Four parts, one law
  3. On the sphere, three

explains essay 18, §9 · 5 steps

Three loops

A breath is a read and a write taken and undone. On Euler's plane a point goes up by a turn, across by a scaling of the thumb, down and back, and comes home; another goes across first and meets it at the far corner at the same moment. In space a turn and a scaling by one factor commute too, so a book turned and scaled and both undone comes home, and with the scaling left undone it comes home facing as it began, only larger. Two reads about two axes, 40° each, do not close: the book comes home turned by 26.87°, and the same again every time round, its arrow's tip stepping round one ring. A read with a stretch by c = 2 comes home squeezed, a little more each breath, ζ = 0.4507 (every ellipse from its matrix). And at c = 1 the squeeze is gone: a breath is steady exactly when its write is in one length.

  1. A breath on one plane
  2. In space, too
  3. Two reads
  4. A read and an unequal write
  5. Steady at one length

The Cauchy map

A photograph's dark corners, cos⁴α, and the cos³α under them that is geometry. Rays turned by equal steps crowding on a flat screen, the half-Cauchy drawn from its formula: the heat is given, the paint is met. Evenly spaced tiles, flat on the screen, crowding per unit of angle: the Cauchy is the turn per unit of the blueprint's grid. Five seeded walkers released at the standpoint, and twenty thousand landings under (1/π)·d/(d² + y²): harmonic measure, Laplace's steady state. A pattern seen from d and from 2d, each wave dimmed by e^(−d|k|), none lost. And the second map, in distance, laid by the thumb, the product hottest on the look one thumb out.
