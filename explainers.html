Explainers

Scroll, and the picture follows. An explainer takes one idea from the essays and shows it a step at a time: the text down one side, a scene beside it whose clock is set by how far you have read, each step a stretch of the scene that can also be played on its own. The scenes are made in the leaf editor of the draw project and played by its TVF player, so the explainers open there.

explains essay 1 · 5 steps

Reading and writing

The test that sorts what a reader holds. A room read from one corner, directions only, a room half the size on the very same rays; the world scaled by 1.5 and no ray moving; one step along the wall, the second rays crossing at every thing, the room 3.57 steps wide; the step counted as six strides of 0.7 m, writing the room 15.0 m; scaled again, the steps unmoved and only the metres moving.

  1. From one place, directions only
  2. Scale the world
  3. One step
  4. Count the stride
  5. Scale the world again

One length turned

An arm raised through a quarter turn traces a circle: one length, turning. Its two shares and the corner where they are equal; a ray from each end of a chord meeting on the circle (Thales) and the radius from the middle at twice the angle; the arc unrolled beside the arm, π/2 × 2/π = 1.

  1. An arm held out
  2. Two shares, and the corner
  3. Two rays, meeting on the circle
  4. One point, read twice
  5. π/2 and 2/π

explains essay 3 · 6 steps

Addresses given, payloads met

Why the Cauchy belongs to the fingers and nothing the window does can move it. A hand at home with twelve fingers laid by an even turn, painted by a stained-glass window; the window moved and changed while no finger moves; the fingers carried on to a line, where they land as the Cauchy with half below the corner; the far ones running off with no mean; amounts arriving and changing while no landing moves.

  1. A hand at home
  2. A window paints them
  3. Another window, the same fingers
  4. Laid on a line: the Cauchy
  5. A median, and no mean
  6. Change what arrives, and no address moves

Near and far

Sixty-four fence posts a step apart, first as the number line has them, then as the reader lays them: in proportion up to the corner at H, and each doubling past it given the same room; the whole far field squeezed between H and 2H on the in-place lay; then H made sixteen, every post sliding along the same lay.

  1. Fence posts on a plain
  2. Inside the corner: proportion
  3. Past the corner: every doubling the same room
  4. Reality in 2H
  5. H is a zoom

explains essay 6 · 5 steps

Many standpoints

One eye sees a target as a ray, things half and twice as far on the same ray; a second eye a baseline away, and the rays cross at the target. The target walked through the circle on the baseline, its error patch round at the corner and stretched elsewhere, until past the far edge it opens back into a ray; then one counted baseline writes the distance, in centimetres or AU, the picture unchanged.

  1. One eye: a direction is a line
  2. Two eyes: two lines cross
  3. Parallax has a corner
  4. Past the far edge, a ray
  5. One length writes them all

explains essay 10 · 6 steps

The Tangent Bridge

The theorem behind the curve format: a curve read at eight leaves, one kernel turned to each, their sum through every leaf exactly, a close look at the gap between two leaves, then sixteen and thirty-two leaves closing on the curve. Every curve is computed from the paper's formulas.

  1. A curve is a function on the circle
  2. Read it at the leaves
  3. One kernel, turned to every leaf
  4. Their sum passes through every leaf
  5. Between the leaves: close, and bounded
  6. Double the leaves, and the curve closes in
