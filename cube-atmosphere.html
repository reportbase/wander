The payload cube and the hemisphere

Left, the payload: a cube of dots one unit apart, without end in every direction, uniform. The eye stands at one dot and looks one way. Right, the Cauchy hemisphere in front of the look: 16 rings of 32 dots, the rings at equal turns off the look. Every dot of the cube in front points to the one hemisphere dot whose turns it falls in (joined and counted: the dots within the window's reach of the eye, inside the dashed circles, since the endless cube is the same every way; the grey ones past it are the window's corners). The cells shrink toward the middle, so most of the cube piles up on the sides and the fewest reach the middle: the rim ring takes about twenty times the centre's. Look along a row of the cube and its rows show in the counts.

Point at a dot (in either) for its line and its numbers. Drag to turn the view. "Equal areas" lays the rings at equal shares of the directions instead: then every hemisphere dot receives the same count. "Laid by the thumb" keeps each dot's distance too: one thumb out half way to the rim, two, four, eight thumbs closer and closer under it, the rim the horizon, never reached.
