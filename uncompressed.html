The Cauchy, uncompressed

Below, the read as a hemisphere: points laid evenly, the same share of directions for every point and the same step of the thumb's lay of distance, g = (2/π)·atan(d/H), from the eye (0) through one thumb (half way) to the horizon (the rim, never reached). Nothing random: a regular grid. Its colour is the read's heat, cos³α off the look times sech(ln(d/H)), hottest one thumb straight ahead.

Above, the same points uncompressed: each slides out along its own direction to d = H·tan(πg/2). Half of them are within one thumb, at any H. Past it, each ratio of distance holds ½·sech(ln(d/H)) of them, the same at d and at H²/d: the shells from one to two thumbs, two to four, four to eight hold less and less (0.205, 0.139, 0.077), and the far field thins as 1/d⁴ toward a horizon the cloud never reaches. Near the eye the points crowd as 1/d².

Move H, the thumb: the cloud grows or shrinks and nothing in the hemisphere moves. "In place" draws both from one eye and slides each point along its ray: the near field barely moves, one thumb stays where it is, the far field streams out. The flat screen at one thumb shows where the directions cross it: cos³α of them per unit of screen.
