The Cauchy, uncompressed

Uncompressed is the writer's three-dimensional Cartesian space: points one unit apart on straight grid lines, going on without end. The eye stands in the middle of a cell and looks up; the read sees the half in front.

Compressed is the Cauchy hemisphere. Every point keeps its direction from the eye and is laid along it by the thumb, g = (2/π)·atan(d/H): one thumb out lands half way to the rim, two, four and eight thumbs closer and closer under it, and the horizon is the rim, never reached. The straight grid lines go through the same map. A line through the eye stays straight; every other line bends, crowding toward the rim, and its far end lands on the rim at one point. All of the endless lattice past the window lands in the thin shell just under the rim.

"In place" puts both at one eye and slides every point and line along its own ray between the two: from the hemisphere (compressed) to the Cartesian grid (uncompressed). Inside one thumb little moves; one thumb stays where it is; the far field streams out. Move H, the thumb, and the hemisphere's picture changes while the grid does not. Each point's colour is the read's heat, cos³α off the look times sech(ln(d/H)), hottest one thumb straight ahead.
