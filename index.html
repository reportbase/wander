Wander

A world full of solids, above and below as well as all around: pawns, rooks, knights, gems, vases, crystals and more, the shapes of the rung editor, each a radius r(θ, h) about its own axis, and each with its own way of carrying on: some stay still, some spin, tumble, breathe or turn from one shape into another. Where they go is gravity's: each has a mass, its size cubed. Most gather in systems, a star with planets going round it, some planets with moons, some stars with a belt, a few pairs of stars going round each other; others travel between, pulled by every star they pass, and a few push themselves toward you or away. There is no shared now: each system keeps its own time and steps at its own rate, and at each step sends a signal. Signals travel at 20 units a second, and you see only what has reached you: each body as it was when its signal left. What you see is read through your range of addresses: each address holds only an address back to the system that arrived there.

Drag with one finger to look, sideways to turn and up or down to tilt; drag with two fingers up to fly forward and down to fly back. Tap a body to go round it, always looking at it: one finger then speeds the orbit or turns it and rises or sinks, two fingers bring you in or out. The readout shows the pairs arriving from the point you tapped, its g, its arrivals against its own ticks (below 1 it is coming closer, above 1 going away), and a trail of its last six seconds of addresses; tap the readout, or press T, to change the trail. While you travel, the readout also gives how far A is, and how big it is across, in your own lengths (from how fast its direction turns against your own travel), and says when it is coming straight at you and how soon it reaches you (from how fast its g grows). Tapping the readout cycles the trail: as it arrived, your turning taken out, your turning and travel taken out, which leaves the body's own path. A long press (or R) hides or shows the readout. With a body chosen as B and one going round it as A, tap A again to hold still: the readout times A's loop about B from where A sits from B as you see them, and with two bodies' loops about the same B it checks Kepler's law, the ratio of loop sizes cubed against the ratio of periods squared. If you have measured how far B is (by making it your A and travelling), the readout also weighs B: the loop's reach in your own lengths, cubed, over its time squared, times 4π², is how hard B pulls (GM). Then it rebuilds A's circle about B in your own lengths and draws where A will be over the next half loop (blue dots), checks each guess three seconds later against what arrives, and gives the speed of A's signals in your own lengths a second. Meanwhile it watches every body near B's direction on the same sheet: those whose track goes round B are listed with their loop times and reaches, each weighing B again, and ringed in blue; a body that only seems to go round B (going round something else behind or in front) weighs B differently and is ringed in grey. While A is chosen, two panels show its shape: on the left what its address points back to, the system's own shape, back and all; on the right the shape rebuilt from arrivals alone, each point of A's surface (which the range names by A's own θ and h) placed from the directions it has arrived along as you go round, with A's own turning found and taken out. It is had up to a scale, and only where you have looked. Tap that line of the readout (or press N) to turn names off: then the reader keeps its own tracks of every arrival, unnamed, each with its own label (#12), and A, B and B's family are those tracks; the shape is then rebuilt from the addresses reached from A's middle through neighbours whose surface names run on, and the whole shape the address points to is not shown, since there is no name to follow. Going round uses only what arrives: A kept at home on your addresses, its span held at the size you zoom to, a step sideways at the rate you choose. Which body is in front is decided by your own distance to each: with names, the one at which the body's own shape, as its address gives it, spans what its arrivals span; without, from parallax. With names on, a dotted ring shows A followed with no name: each arrival taken unnamed, and the one that fits where A's arrivals have been heading, and how large A spans, taken as A; the readout scores it against the name. Some cells hold a cluster: bodies moving inside a bound they cannot see, colliding and bouncing. With A and B chosen, the readout says whether they merely overlap on your addresses or are in contact (from their distances through the address, from their ticks once you have learned the signals' speed by flying straight at or away from a star, and from parallax while you travel), when they will touch, and after a touch, how their masses compare from their changes of speed. Tap a second body and the first stays chosen as B (dashed ring); while you travel, the readout says how many times as far B is as A, from how fast their directions turn against your own travel, with no distance or unit. Tap empty space to fly free again. Pinch, the mouse wheel or the trackpad zoom: going round a body they bring you in or take you out, flying free they carry you forward or back along where you look; scrolling sideways turns you. Keys: arrows look, W and S fly, A and D step sideways, Escape leaves an orbit.

arrivals against ticks (DOP)

Every system ticks on its own time and sends a signal at each tick; signals travel at 20 units a second through the world. The readout's arrivals against ticks is the time between two signals' arrivals over the time between their tags: 1 when the distance holds, below 1 closing, above 1 opening. It is a reading with its corner at 1: approach on the front side, recession on the back. A reading fair to the two facings (R162) would carry approach at a speed onto recession at the same speed by the flip, s ↦ 1/s. Here a lone body, or the reader, moves straight along the line between them at β = v/20 = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4, away and toward; the signals are Wander's own (tickSystem), received by Wander's own rule (the latest signal that has travelled as far as the body then was from the reader now), and the ratio is the readout's own. Prediction (written before the run): the world's medium is at rest, so the classical forms hold: the source receding reads 1 + β and approaching 1 − β; the reader receding reads 1/(1 − β) and approaching 1/(1 + β). Approach and recession are not each other's flip: their product misses 1 by about β². Which one moves matters. The relativistic factor √((1 + β)/(1 − β)) keeps the flip exactly, does not care which one moves, and is the geometric mean of the source-moving and reader-moving readings. Kill : a measured reading off its classical form by more than 0.5%, or approach and recession reciprocal in Wander's world. Teal : the reading is fair to the facings (product within 0.1% of 1); gold within 1%; red beyond. A correspondence, not a claim about light.

a ball against its distance (BAL)

Wander's g for a body is the share of the reader's addresses it spans. Here a ball of radius a sits straight ahead at distance d, and the share is counted address by address, the addresses laid as Wander lays them, evenly in turn across and up over the front half; the reading is the ball's own size against its distance, s = a/d, as for SPN's glowing disc and dipole. Prediction (written before the run): far off the share goes as s²/π, the inverse square; it fills exactly every address at s = 1, where the reader touches the ball, and stays full past it (from inside, every address meets the ball); so the ball's corner is contact, and its near law cannot be its far law read through the flip, since there is no far side to flip onto. Where the middle falls, half the addresses filled, is not predicted: it is what the lab reads. Kill : the far law off s²/π by more than 1% at s ≤ 0.05, or the share short of 1 at contact. Teal : the row's share within 1% of its predicted form; gold within 5%; red beyond (between the two laws no form is predicted, and the rows there are left uncoloured). Second run (3 Oct, a reading; plans/bal-olb-readings.md): the first run is the geometry, size and distance given (layer 1, no reader). Now you walk toward a ball whose size and distance you are not given, holding at stations of your own travel, 0 to 90, then 94, 95, 96; at each, your filled addresses give sin α by your own lay, and 1/sin α = (d₀ − x)/a is a line in your travel. Prediction (written before): the radius 5 and the start 100 within 1%; contact foreseen from the far stations within 1% of 95; the share short of 1 at 94, full at 95 and 96. Kill : any not so. Run : not killed : 5.0000, 100.000, contact 95.000. The 1% line assumed the grain would cost about 1%; it costs nearly nothing, because you invert your count through your own lay, the lay the addresses were filled in. The grain would show if your model of your lay differed from your lay, which is not tested.

Rømer's lag (RMR)

From SPN §9.7 and §13: the signals' speed, which the view from nowhere puts in first, comes out last for a reader, as Rømer found it in 1676 from the eclipses of Io. Here the clock is a beacon that turns at a steady rate in a system of its own (tickSystem keeps its turn exactly: y₀ + ω·t), and each full turn, carried in its signals, is an event, as a pulsar's pulses are; in pulsar timing this term is still called the Rømer delay. The reader, on the line through the beacon, times the turns by their arrivals while it holds still, which gives the period; then it flies 200 of its own lengths straight away (or toward), holds still again, counts the turns that went by, and finds them late (or early) against the period. Its own flight over the lag is the signals' speed in its own lengths a second. Each signal is received by Wander's rule, taken at the first moment it holds; a turn is placed between two signals. First run (a moon's eclipses behind a planet under Wander's gravity, ticked at h = 0.1): killed , 4.8% off, by the lab's clock: the stepped orbit precesses, its eclipses wandering ±0.012 s and its distance at eclipse ±0.19, so a period learned from a few eclipses and carried over fifty missed by a third of a second. Io keeps far better time than that; the beacon does too. The prediction and kill below are unchanged. Rows: the reader flies, or the beacon's system travels the same 200 instead, at β = 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2. Prediction (written before the run): the lag is the change of distance over the signals' speed and nothing else, 200/20 = 10 seconds whichever one moves, whatever the speed, so every row reads 20 to the lab's grain; while the distance changes, the eclipses come apart by DOP's factors (1/(1 − β) for the reader flying away, 1 + β for the system going away), and the lag is that stretch added up. Kill : any row's speed off 20 by more than 0.5%, or the lag changing with β by more than 0.5%. Teal : within 0.1%; gold within 0.5%; red beyond. A correspondence, not a claim about light.

distance from your own travel (PLX)

From SPN Prop 9.6 and §9.7: the readout's distance is its own step , run here without drawing on a body's arrivals as Wander delivers them (each signal received by Wander's rule and glided between the last two, as the view draws it). Over the last 0.6 s it takes how far the body's middle has turned against how far you have travelled across that line: distance = (your travel across) / (its turn). Its whole turn is used, so a body's own motion across the line counts as parallax. The classical way out, the astrometric solution, is a two-part reading : fit the body's place and its own steady motion together against your own known path, over 10 s. The rows: a body 30 of your lengths off; you fly at 4 a second straight across, at 45° or at 15° to the line; the body still, or moving 2 a second across the same way as you, the other way, along the line, or up (across both); you going round a body that moves; and, for the two-part reading only, you flying back and forth across instead of straight, and a body going round a star. Prediction (written before the run): the step reads the distance times |your own motion across| / |your motion across less the body's|, the body's motion taken as it arrives (its own times (1 + your speed toward it / 20) / (1 + its speed away / 20), the light-time): exact for a still body; twice as far when it moves across half your speed the same way, two thirds when it moves the other way, 1/√1.25 ≈ 0.894 when it moves up; going round a moving body you share its motion and read it long or short around the loop by that same ratio (the readout's 14.5 against 7.1 is one of these). The two-part reading recovers the distance whenever your own travel turns (back and forth, or round a loop), still or steadily moving body alike, and gives the body's own motion as well; flying straight it cannot: a near body moving with you and a far one moving more are the same arrivals, so the reading is undetermined, which is why the Earth's yearly swing is needed for stellar distances. For a body going round a star (its own motion turning within the 10 s) no size is predicted: the lab reads it. Kill : a step row off its predicted ratio by more than 2%; a two-part row with a steady body and your travel turning off the distance by more than 1%; or a straight flight that does separate the two. Teal : within 0.5%; gold within the kill; red beyond; rows with nothing predicted are left uncoloured. First run (3 Oct): killed on two counts, recorded here and left as run. The arrived track is not steady in your own time, since its signals' delay changes as the body crosses, and only motion across bends along the same line as your back-and-forth. Refitted with each arrival moved back by its distance over the signals' speed (the speed RMR reads), the across rows fall to 0.16% and 1.0%, and the own motions come right (along 2.02 for 1.82, which is 2/(1 + 0.1), DOP's factor). keeping tracks apart (TRK)

TMP left Gibbs open: from the same arrivals, a reader that keeps the balls' tracks apart reads a mixing rise of ln 2 per ball, one that only counts reads none; TMP took keeping tracks apart as done. Here it is earned or not. TMP's world two (300 balls, speed 4); the reader gets, frame by frame, every ball's arrival with no name: a direction and a tag. The tags are one system's clock, so they name no ball. two still readers in company (CMP)

The first lab that joins two readers' records. Readers A and B, about 8 apart, never move. B's directions come in its own axes, turned by a rotation A does not know, and B's clock runs at its own rate from its own zero. Each reader can send ticks and answer the other's. the corner in physics (COR)

Does a physical reading change where the reader's own quantity meets the world's, at their ratio s = 1, and is the far side the near side read at 1/s? Six cases, each predicted before the run, two of them controls that should say no. (1) EPC , a body's loop seen from the reader's own loop, as planets from a planet (Copernicus): the swing of the direction about its mean turning, sin = s inside, 1/s outside. (2) ESC swept across escape: the binding share 1 − s, then the share of the launch kept far away, 1 − 1/s. (3) CRY across sampling's fold: predicted to fold, 2 − s, not to invert. pairs, read on two clocks (PRS)

Quantum, a first lay of the land. Wander 's world is classical, so here the world holds a state and draws each outcome by the Born rule: built in. A source between readers A and B sends pairs; each reader chooses its own setting at random moments of its own clock and keeps only its own clicks. B's clock runs at its own rate from its own zero. Two sources: one that draws the joint outcome when read (on demand), one whose every value is fixed beforehand (held in advance). From nowhere : Bell (1964); Clauser, Horne, Shimony and Holt (1969); Weihs et al. (1998), who joined independent clocks' records afterwards. Prediction (written before the run): alone, an even share at every setting, whatever the other does; in company, the records joined by the rhythm of the clicks alone (no shared now), |S| = 2√2 on demand and 2 held in advance; turning both readers' angles together moves nothing.

one quantum at a time (ONE)

Single quanta through two openings onto a row of detectors, one click at a time (Taylor 1909; Tonomura 1989); a second reader at the openings may mark which way, plainly or in the erasing combination (Scully and Drühl 1982). Built in: the world's draw. Prediction (written before the run): fringes of visibility 1 build up in clicks, their spacing giving the wavelength once the openings' spacing and distance are given; a mark takes them from the screen reader alone, either way it is read; sorted in company by the erasing marker's records they come back as fringes and anti-fringes, half a spacing apart; a phase between the openings shifts them; an overall phase moves no click.

two quanta at a splitter (TWO)

Two quanta meet at a half-silvered splitter; readers C and D at its two outputs keep their own clicks on their own clocks, joined by rhythm as in PRS (Hong, Ou and Mandel 1987).
