Wander

A world full of solids, above and below as well as all around: pawns, rooks, knights, gems, vases, crystals and more, the shapes of the rung editor, each a radius r(θ, h) about its own axis, and each with its own way of carrying on: some stay still, some spin, tumble, breathe or turn from one shape into another. Where they go is gravity's: each has a mass, its size cubed. Most gather in systems, a star with planets going round it, some planets with moons, some stars with a belt, a few pairs of stars going round each other; others travel between, pulled by every star they pass, and a few push themselves toward you or away. There is no shared now: each system keeps its own time and steps at its own rate, and at each step sends a signal. Signals travel at 20 units a second, and you see only what has reached you: each body as it was when its signal left. What you see is read through your range of addresses: each address holds only an address back to the system that arrived there.

Drag with one finger to look, sideways to turn and up or down to tilt; drag with two fingers up to fly forward and down to fly back. Tap a body to go round it, always looking at it: one finger then speeds the orbit or turns it and rises or sinks, two fingers bring you in or out. The readout shows the pairs arriving from the point you tapped, its g, its arrivals against its own ticks (below 1 it is coming closer, above 1 going away), and a trail of its last six seconds of addresses; tap the readout, or press T, to change the trail. While you travel, the readout also gives how far A is, and how big it is across, in your own lengths (from how fast its direction turns against your own travel), and says when it is coming straight at you and how soon it reaches you (from how fast its g grows). Tapping the readout cycles the trail: as it arrived, your turning taken out, your turning and travel taken out, which leaves the body's own path. A long press (or R) hides or shows the readout. With a body chosen as B and one going round it as A, tap A again to hold still: the readout times A's loop about B from where A sits from B as you see them, and with two bodies' loops about the same B it checks Kepler's law, the ratio of loop sizes cubed against the ratio of periods squared. If you have measured how far B is (by making it your A and travelling), the readout also weighs B: the loop's reach in your own lengths, cubed, over its time squared, times 4π², is how hard B pulls (GM). Then it rebuilds A's circle about B in your own lengths and draws where A will be over the next half loop (blue dots), checks each guess three seconds later against what arrives, and gives the speed of A's signals in your own lengths a second. Meanwhile it watches every body near B's direction on the same sheet: those whose track goes round B are listed with their loop times and reaches, each weighing B again, and ringed in blue; a body that only seems to go round B (going round something else behind or in front) weighs B differently and is ringed in grey. While A is chosen, two panels show its shape: on the left what its address points back to, the system's own shape, back and all; on the right the shape rebuilt from arrivals alone, each point of A's surface (which the range names by A's own θ and h) placed from the directions it has arrived along as you go round, with A's own turning found and taken out. It is had up to a scale, and only where you have looked. Tap that line of the readout (or press N) to turn names off: then the reader keeps its own tracks of every arrival, unnamed, each with its own label (#12), and A, B and B's family are those tracks; the shape is then rebuilt from the addresses reached from A's middle through neighbours whose surface names run on, and the whole shape the address points to is not shown, since there is no name to follow. Going round uses only what arrives: A kept at home on your addresses, its span held at the size you zoom to, a step sideways at the rate you choose. Which body is in front is decided by your own distance to each: with names, the one at which the body's own shape, as its address gives it, spans what its arrivals span; without, from parallax. With names on, a dotted ring shows A followed with no name: each arrival taken unnamed, and the one that fits where A's arrivals have been heading, and how large A spans, taken as A; the readout scores it against the name. Some cells hold a cluster: bodies moving inside a bound they cannot see, colliding and bouncing. With A and B chosen, the readout says whether they merely overlap on your addresses or are in contact (from their distances through the address, from their ticks once you have learned the signals' speed by flying straight at or away from a star, and from parallax while you travel), when they will touch, and after a touch, how their masses compare from their changes of speed. Tap a second body and the first stays chosen as B (dashed ring); while you travel, the readout says how many times as far B is as A, from how fast their directions turn against your own travel, with no distance or unit. Tap empty space to fly free again. Pinch, the mouse wheel or the trackpad zoom: going round a body they bring you in or take you out, flying free they carry you forward or back along where you look; scrolling sideways turns you. Keys: arrows look, W and S fly, A and D step sideways, Escape leaves an orbit.
