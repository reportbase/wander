The conics' exchange

An ellipse of eccentricity e and a hyperbola of eccentricity 1/e, about one focus. Each orbit's anomaly is taken evenly and lands on a direction from the focus. Both landings use the same factor k, so the two orbits are one map read two ways. The parabola, where e = 1/e = 1, sits between them, and there k is infinite.

ellipse, e = tanh η; hyperbola, e′ = coth η. One focus at the cross, one nearest point at distance 1. Dots: the anomaly taken evenly (E on the ellipse, F on the hyperbola), with the ray from the focus to each.

The landing as one line, t = k·w. Across: w = tan(E/2) for the ellipse, w = tanh(F/2) for the hyperbola. Up: the direction's half-slope t = tan(ν/2). The ellipse uses the whole line; the hyperbola only |w| < 1, so its directions stop at |t| = k, its asymptotes (gold).

From ORB runs 6–8 (exact at 50 digits). Each part is classical: the eccentric and hyperbolic anomalies, Barker's equation for the parabola. That the ellipse of e and the hyperbola of 1/e share one k exactly, with the parabola as the fixed point between them, is how those runs sort them. ν, E and F are the writer's angles; the half-slope t is the page's own number.
