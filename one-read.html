One turn, one read

One direction from home, given before anything arrives, meets one point, and that one read breaks down two ways. Counted as a direction from where you stand, it is the address, the inscribed sweep; counted from the centre of the circle on the chord, it is the point where the paint lands, the central sweep. Both end at the same point: the address points at the payload. Over the whole quarter turn the two sweeps, as a writer measures them, take the line as π/2 and 2/π (numbers with π in them, the writer's); their product is the 1 that only the writer holds. In complex form the point is the direction squared, ½e^(2iθ) from the centre: where the address runs off to ∞ at the horizon, the radius has made its half turn to home, e^(iπ) = −1.

The chord from home A to B, and the circle drawn on it. Gold: the address, A's ray at θ. Teal: where the paint lands, the radius from the centre at 2θ. Violet: the arc from B already turned through, over its chord.

Over the quarter turn, as a writer measures it: the central sweep's arc over chord (teal, rising to π/2), the inscribed sweep's chord over arc (gold, falling to 2/π), and their product (white), 1 throughout.

The same read on the Cayley disc, w = (s − i)/(s + i) = −e^(2iθ) for the reading s = tan θ: the standpoint i goes to the centre, zero to −1, the corner to −i, the horizon to +1. The Thales circle's landmarks, turned a half turn.
