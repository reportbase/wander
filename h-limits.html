What is H?

H is not a size anyone measured. It is the one value the reader picks to start the system. Every direction is read the same whatever H is; H only decides where amounts land. Slide it from nothing to everything and watch a world of amounts, from a thousandth to a thousand, cross the quarter turn. Only an H among the things read makes a corner. At H of 0 and H of ∞ the reader's lay becomes the two rulers that favour nothing: the logarithm and the number line.

H = 1

The world on the quarter turn

Each amount x sits at the direction arctan(x/H), from home (all of h) to the horizon (all of v). The corner is the amount equal to H. Make H large and everything crowds into home; make it small and everything runs out to the horizon. Tap two dots to compare them below.

Three rulers

The same 31 amounts on three rulers, each stretched to the world's ends. Top, the number line; bottom, the logarithm (or the reciprocal, for the in-place lay); middle, the reader's lay at this H. With H past the top of the world the middle ruler is the number line; below its bottom, the logarithm. Only an H inside gives a corner (gold).

Held at a finite count

How many of the reader's addresses the world occupies, for every H. At both ends it is lost whole, three ways: into home's first address, past the held levels (the reader's lay keeps the world's width in levels and only moves it out), or into the horizon's last address (the in-place lay squeezes it).

The best H for two

The angle between two amounts on the quarter turn, for every H. It is widest at their geometric mean, the corner of the pair, where it is arcsin((r − 1)/(r + 1)), and falls to nothing at both ends. In levels they are ln r apart at every H: H decides how much of the turn they take, never their pattern.

What a larger H gives and takes

One object, the amount you tapped last and a stretch past it, and the detail the reader gives it (log₂ of the addresses it spans) for every H. Inside the corner, more H means more proportional room for the same count: one level fewer per doubling. Past the corner the two lays part: the reader's lay gives the object what its distance allows whatever H is, and reaches it only while it is within the held levels; the in-place lay gives it more as H grows, most at the corner of the object's own ends, √(x(x + Δ)).

Geometry and its placing only: the amounts are given from nowhere, as physics would assign them; the reader's directions never change with H. Choose unlimited addresses and the value of H stops mattering: a unit is needed because a reader's addresses are finite, and choosing its value is where the scale of the world first reaches the reader. The same corner, the geometric middle of what is read, is the best H for a pair here, the best half-baseline for parallax over a range, and the best estimate of a size within bounds.
