One blueprint, three vantages

The same property held three ways. The writer has the blueprint: every length in metres, the whole of it. The reader is a circle round one point: nothing on the lot sends, so it receives the light the lot scatters, each direction painted by the first thing on its line, and no distance. The address reader stands at a corner and receives directions alone, each [h : v]: where things are, never how far. Scale the world and only the writer's numbers change. Write: one counted stride turns the address reader's two looks into the writer's metres.

The blueprint, in metres

Every length an amount, stated whole: both sides of every wall, the lot's full extent. Its ruler is the number line, the same at every length, no corner. The metres need a unit measured beforehand: the scale bar. Its circle (faint blue) is round the whole lot, looking in, its radius an amount. Tap anywhere to stand the reader there; the address reader is at the gold corner.

the reader

A circle, painted by what arrives

Passive: 128 directions round one point, each dot the colour of what its line meets first. Nothing on the lot sends, so no distance arrives and every dot sits on the circle. Send a pulse and a range comes back along each direction, drawn over their geometric mean: the shape, but in the unit the pulse carried in (its speed and a clock), so ranging is writing. Scale the world and nothing here moves.

Directions alone, from home

At home, a corner, the quarter turn from one wall (h) to the other (v), 256 directions, each painted by the first thing met: the fan, then the same laid straight, g = (2/π)·arctan(v/h). No distance arrives; the turn's middle, v = h, has half the addresses either side, whatever is there.

The writer is the vantage that writes: it needs the whole and a unit. Its circle is round the whole, looking in; the reader's is round one point, looking out, painted by what arrives, holding no distance.
