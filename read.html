What a read is

Nothing in the world is drawn here. What is drawn is the read itself: the geometry the crossed thumbs give, wherever they point.

  1. The crosshair. Three thumbs, each one thumb long, square to each other, with the look down their diagonal. Where they cross, every reading is one: the corner. It is the middle of the screen and the top of the hill.
  2. The addresses. The directions, taken evenly, land on the screen square to the look as the 3D Cauchy, thick at the crosshair and thinning outward without end. The reader's K addresses are equal shares of them: rings, each holding the same share, cut by rays. On the left in thumbs, as they fall on the screen: the cells grow, one thumb off, one in 2, one in 4, one in 8, toward a horizon no ring reaches. Switch the view to "by count" and every cell gets the same room: the same rings now bunch toward the rim.
  3. The thumbs and the wedge. The three thumbs point out at 120° from each other, the wedge the triangle between them, a quarter of what is in front. Each pair's corner is a line from its home, on the triangle's edge, through the crosshair to the third thumb, its horizon.
  4. Where the reader stands. Bottom left, from the side: the reader is a point behind the screen, the look running from it through the crosshair. Every mark on the screen is a direction from that point, landing tan α off the crosshair; the thumb leaves at 54.74° and lands at √2, its home at 35.26° the other side; the horizon, 90° off the look, runs parallel to the screen and never lands.
  5. The hill. On the right the same law as a height. Its contours are the rings, each enclosing the same share; its tails go on and never reach zero. Drag it to turn it.
  6. A read is every sweep at once. "Read again" draws the sweeps out from the crosshair to the horizon. The geometry is given, not computed; it is the same whatever is there.
  7. Nothing changes. Turn the look and the thumbs turn with it: the same addresses. Scale the world and no reading moves: there is no V in a read, only the thumb's own shares. What would change is the paint, and that is physics, left out.

After Reads and Writes §6 ("The crosshairs") and §7 ("One thumb, two thumbs"; "the reader has a tunnel").
