What a read is

Nothing in the world is drawn here. What is drawn is the read itself: the geometry the crossed thumbs give, wherever they point.

  1. The crosshair. Three thumbs, each one thumb long, square to each other, with the look down their diagonal. Where they cross, every reading is one: the corner. It is the middle of the screen and the top of the hill.
  2. The quarter turn. A read is a quarter turn: every pair of thumbs from its home (reading 0) to its horizon (reading ∞). Down the diagonal that is the wedge, the triangle whose corners are the thumbs and whose edges are where a reading is 0 or ∞. Outside it are other facings; the full round of rings about the crosshair is what the head turned all the way round would give, the writer's join ("turned all the way round" in the menu shows it).
  3. The addresses. The directions, taken evenly, land on the screen square to the look as the 3D Cauchy, thick at the crosshair and thinning outward to the wedge's edges. The reader's K addresses are equal shares of them: rings, each holding the same share, cut by rays. On the left in thumbs, as they fall on the screen: the cells grow, one thumb off, one in 2, one in 4, one in 8, toward a horizon no ring reaches. Switch the view to "by count" and every cell gets the same room: the same rings now bunch toward the rim.
  4. The thumbs and the wedge. The three thumbs point out at 120° from each other, the wedge the triangle between them, a quarter of what is in front. Each pair's corner is a line from its home, on the triangle's edge, through the crosshair to the third thumb, its horizon.
  5. Where the reader stands. Bottom left, from the side: the reader is a point behind the screen, the look running from it through the crosshair. Every mark on the screen is a direction from that point, landing tan α off the crosshair; the thumb leaves at 54.74° and lands at √2, its home at 35.26° the other side; the horizon, 90° off the look, runs parallel to the screen and never lands. Shaded, the near half of the read, the Cauchy's body: a dome one thumb about the reader over the wedge, here an arc from the home's ray to the thumb's, closed, holding half of every direction's addresses. Dashed, the far half, the long tail: the arc at 2, 4, 8 thumbs, opening out and never closed.
  6. Drag the thumb. Bottom right, a row of things at their distances. Above, the writer's line in units, H the gold mark, the reader's distance addresses H·tan(kπ/2K) as ticks: drag along it to make H larger or smaller. Below, the reader's lay: the same addresses, each the same room, half before the corner and half after. Drag H and the ticks below never move; the things slide along them, more of them into the near field (teal) as H grows, out past the corner (violet) as it shrinks. Nothing on the screen or the hill moves either: the Cauchy is the same at every H. H decides only where the things land.
  7. The shape swept whole. The even read comes from the three thumbs turned together, one rigid frame, every turn weighed alike. "Swept" in the menu shows the other way: each thumb swept through its own angle, evenly, one after the other (pan, then tilt; or tilt, then pan). Every pose is still a turn of the frame, but the poses are weighed otherwise: the directions pile toward one thumb, where the other sweep shrinks to a point, the hill is no longer round and no longer peaks at the crosshair, and one pair of thumbs leaves its corner (0.343 below it, not a half). The pile goes to a different thumb when the order is changed. Swept together through the quarter turn the wedge fills evenly by area, every pair at its corner, with no order, since no pair is taken past its horizon.
  8. The hill. On the right the same law as a height. Its contours are the rings, each enclosing the same share; its tails go on and never reach zero. Drag it to turn it.
  9. A read is every sweep at once. "Read again" draws the sweeps out from the crosshair to the horizon. The geometry is given, not computed; it is the same whatever is there.
  10. Nothing changes. Turn the look and the thumbs turn with it: the same addresses. Scale the world and no reading moves: there is no V in a read, only the thumb's own shares. What would change is the paint, and that is physics, left out.
