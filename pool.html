Pool from inside

Each ball reads every other from where it is: the other's direction, and how much of its view the other fills. Two balls touch when their two readings add to one, h + v = 1, the straight line of the facing; two equal balls touch at the corner, each filling 60° of the other's view. The table below is played from inside, beside a referee that plays it from nowhere.

The table played from inside. Tap a ball to stand on it: lines to the others, coloured by how many levels each is from touching it (gold: touching).

The chosen ball's pairs as points (h, v): h how much of each other's view it fills, v how much of its view the other fills. Each pair runs out along its own ray, set by the two sizes; contact is the line h + v = 1. Equal balls touch at the corner.

What the chosen ball sees: round its whole turn, each other ball as the arc it fills. A touching ball of equal size fills 60°.

Keep the case: h and v are readings, ratios of a size to a distance, from 0 to 1; contact needs no unit. Speeds and impulses are amounts, so each ball works in its own size, and the masses come from the sizes by one material (the bridge). A straight cushion fills half the view at any distance, so it has no reading r/d; meeting it is meeting your own mirror image, and contact is again at the corner, with you as both parties. A pocket takes a ball when it fills the ball's whole view. Still played from nowhere, and named so: one clock, and no delay. Plan and runs: plans/pol-plan.md.
