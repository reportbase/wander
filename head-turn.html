Turning the head

Turn about the point you see from and every direction turns alike: nothing shifts against anything else, so nothing gets a distance. A head is not that: the eye sits in front of the neck, so turning carries it along a chord, and that chord is a baseline. The parallax is the eye's step, not the turn. Set the offset to zero and watch everything stop being placed.

The head from above, close up: the axis (white), the eye before (gold) and after (teal) the turn, the chord between them (violet) and the eye's sideways step.

What the eye reads: how far each post's bearing moved between the two looks, against the farthest point (or the fixated post), teal one way and gold the other. With r = 0 nothing moves.

The world on the reader's lay (in proportion to 1 m, then 1 + ln d): posts true (white), placed by the two looks (teal ring), or left as a direction (red ray) when their parallax is under ε. The dashed circle is the reach.
