Only a name

Many laws carry π or e. In some of them it does work no other number could; in others it only names a share or a slope, and the same relation can be written exactly with fractions and square roots and no π or e at all. This page shows the difference on one law, then sorts all 189 laws two lists hold: the 82 of Reads and Writes that carry π or e, and 107 standard formulas listed by a fresh agent.

1. One turn, written two ways

A direction of slope s. Classically one takes its angle θ (so θ = arctan s, and π comes with the angle) and writes the turn by twice that angle with cos and sin. Written in the half-slope s itself, the same turn is a pair of fractions. Pick s; the left side is the float way, the right the exact one. n turns compose by multiplying fractions, and the exact side never needs an angle.

in the angle (uses π)

in the slope (fractions only)

Each turn is the same law, written in two charts: cos 2θ = (1 − s²)/(1 + s²), sin 2θ = 2s/(1 + s²), e^(2iθ) = (1 + is)/(1 − is). NTN's checker counts a law as notation when such a restatement agrees to 10⁻³⁰ at three or more points and carries no π, e, trigonometric, hyperbolic or logarithmic function.

2. Where π is content

A limit is different. Leibniz's series and Wallis's product are sums and products of fractions, and every partial result is a fraction; the law is that they tend to π/4 and π/2. Take away π and nothing is left to state. Every limit on both lists, 74 of them, kept its π or e.

Leibniz: 4·(1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − …)

Wallis: 2·Π 4n²/(4n² − 1)

A count of reads closes in the same way. One read of slope 1/m composed with itself is (m + i)ⁿ in whole numbers; the first n past the horizon, N, makes 2N/m a fraction above π by under 2/m + 2/m² (0.86 at m = 2, 7·10⁻⁶ at m = 100,000), and only slope 1, the corner, lands on the horizon exactly, in two steps. Along the scale, (1 + 1/m)ᵐ sits below e by about e/(2m). Every step is a fraction; the limit is neither (BLD; essay 25).

4. What sorts them

The two lists differ overall: 30 of 82 against 18 of 107. Crossed by both blind sortings, the limits are content on both lists, and the laws among directions that take no limit are notation at nearly the same rate on both. The paper holds more of those, which is most of why its count is higher. The laws with an amount and no limit do not agree between the lists, and the paper has only seven of them.
