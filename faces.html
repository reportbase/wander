Two faces on H

Two points, H and V. One thumb on H turns one quarter turn, from lying along the line toward V to standing up. Facing V, it reads near: s = v/h, V in terms of H. Facing away from V, it reads far: 1/s = h/v, H in terms of V. The two readings multiply to 1 at every point of the turn, and 1 is one thumb, with no facing. At the corner they meet: s = 1/s = 1.

t = 0.000

facing V · the near reading s = v/h

facing away from V · the far reading 1/s = h/v

The thumb's shares here are h = 1 − t and v = t, so every reading is a fraction and no angle is used. The heat 1/(1 + s²) below the reading and the heat above it are equal at the corner; the heat above s is the heat below 1/s. The total heat tends to Ω (classically π/2), approached by a count, never reached. From essay 26, A face, §7 (SPN R1042, R1043).
