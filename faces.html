Two faces on H

Two points, H and V. The Ω sweep is every way H can stand relative to V. Both faces stand on H, and each turns one quarter turn, never a half turn. The inner sweep starts facing V, finishes facing down and compresses; the outer sweep starts facing away from V, finishes facing up and expands. Three routes between H and V: the long route, Ω, the short route, 1/Ω, and the line itself. The two facings multiply to 1, and 1 is one thumb.

Ω is classically π/2. The slope s is counted from each face's start and runs from 0 to the horizon for both. The inner sweep compresses to 1/Ω, the outer sweep expands to Ω, and the two facings multiply to 1 at every point of the turn: one thumb. From essay 26, A face, §7 (SPN R1017, R1018, R1020, R1022).
