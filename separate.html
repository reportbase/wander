Two lines apart

Two lines overlap. Each is a standpoint, where it sits and how wide it is, and an amount, how much of the paint it holds. How well the split between them can be had depends on how far apart the two standpoints are, in the half-plane's own distance d, and on how the noise is laid. Counted noise leaves it to d alone; noise laid evenly on channels does not.

The two lines, their sum, and a sample spectrum in the chosen noise (seeded).

The half-plane: the two standpoints, i (gold), and the circle of points at distance d from the first.

The bound on the split against the width ratio, over every ratio a pair at this d can have (e^−d to e^d): counted (teal) flat; even in frequency (red) bent; even in the turn about i (violet) nearly flat, since these pairs sit near i. The marker is the pair shown.

From SEP runs 2 and 4 (exact at 30 digits there; here in doubles). A Möbius map carries each line onto another as a density, so the information keeps its value under the map exactly when the noise's weight is carried the same way: the paint's own count is; no fixed laying of channels on the line is, since the maps keep no measure on it. Laid evenly by the turn from i, channels keep only the turns about i. On a measured counted doublet (PbSO₄'s Cu Kα, SEP run 6) the split came back from d ≈ 1.4 on and failed below it; the scatter there was the lines' shape, not the counts.
