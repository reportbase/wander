Two points, turned

An extent is two points. By itself it has no geometry. Turn one point about the other and the geometry is all there: the one that stays is the pivot, which points; the one that goes round is the trace, which walks. The reading is the slope of the pointing, s = v/h. Move the slider evenly and the trace walks evenly: that is the sweep, and everything below comes from it.

the picked point keeps its distance facings drawn

The pivot (white) points at the trace (gold). The trace's track is drawn only as far as it has walked; no circle is drawn first.

The trace's walk for each step of pointing: the heat on the slopes. Teal what the trace has walked so far; the corner s = 1 splits the walk in half.

The exchange: s facing V, 1/s facing away. The walk below the corner and above it.

Turns composed: s ⊕ a = (s + a)/(1 − sa). Does the weight of the walk survive? Only in the round measure.

    All of this is a consequence of two points turned, one at Ω (the trace, its whole walk) and one at 1/Ω (the pivot); Ω × 1/Ω = 1 is arithmetic, where each sits is the reading. The premise is that the picked point stays the same while it turns and the turn comes back: that is what picks the round measure (try the others: the corner and the missing mean survive, the Cauchy and composition do not). What the turn gives is the stage; the paint is the world's. The formulas are classical (d(arctan s)/ds = 1/(1 + s²), tangent addition); the reading is the paper's.
