Theorem 6 at a glance

The separation of address and payload. The Cauchy is the addresses, the uniform turn's own, not what arrives on them. The addresses are given, complete, before anything arrives; whatever arrives along each, near or far, changes none of them. What survives a change of payload is order, so the median; what does not is the mean. And the addresses themselves have no mean at all.

(a) Nothing that arrives moves an address

From home: the addresses (gold rays, given by the uniform turn) and what arrives along each (teal, at its reach ℓ, drawn by level). The reading is (ℓ·v)/(ℓ·h) = v/h.

(b), (c) Order survives, the mean does not

(d), (e) The addresses have a median and no mean

Against the number of directions N (10 to 10⁶, by decade): the median of the readings v/h (gold) and their mean (teal), with the corner, 1, dashed.
