One's own height

Stand on level ground. The horizon is at your eye's height, so it cuts every thing standing on the ground at the same height: the part of it below the horizon is exactly one eye height tall. Read the foot and the top, and the thing's height over yours is a ratio of two readings. Nothing in that needs a metre. Carry in your own height and every thing is written in metres at once: the survey's base, carried in the body.

What the eye sees: the horizon (gold, dashed) at eye level, the ground's distance lines below it. Each thing's part below the horizon (teal) is one eye height; the part above (gold) is the rest. Tap a thing to choose it.

The chosen thing from the side: the eye at its height, the horizon line out to the thing, the foot F below and the top E above.

The readings laid on the reader's lay (in proportion to 1, then 1 + ln): heights in eye heights, the corner at 1, one eye height tall; distances in eye heights, the foot's corner at d = H, 45° down. Rings where the referee puts them.

After Sedgwick's horizon-ratio relation (Sedgwick 2021, i-Perception 12(3), restating his 1973 and 1986 work); Observation from Somewhere §9, "The ground: one's own height as the base". Related: turning the head (the eye's step as a base), the writer and two readers, the pinhole. All the demos.
