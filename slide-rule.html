The reader is a slide rule

Every address is already a ratio: a direction from home reads v/h, and nothing is divided to get it. Lay the addresses on the levels (log₂ of v/h) and every ratio between two things becomes the gap between their places. N things hold N(N − 1)/2 ratios, but the reader keeps only N places; the ratios are read off as distances, the way a slide rule multiplies by laying lengths end to end. A tower of links telescopes for the same reason: every middle place drops out. The reader places; it does no ratio arithmetic.

The things, and their places

From home, the quarter turn from h to v, the corner (v = h) dashed. Each thing sits on its own ray at the distance a writer would give it, drawn on the reader's lay (in proportion to the corner circle, then 1 + ln). Scaling the world slides every thing along its ray and moves no reading.

The straight rule: ratios are gaps

Top, the stock: each thing at its level, log₂(v/h). Below, the slide, a second scale of levels whose 1 is the corner. Drag the slide, or tap a thing to put the slide's 1 under it: every other thing's ratio to it is read straight off the slide beneath it, all at once. Nothing is divided; the readout checks each against division.

A tower: links laid end to end

Each link is one thing in units of the next, a gap on the levels. Laid end to end from the last thing, the chain lands on the first: the middle places cancel, and the chain is the gap between its ends (teal). Closed into a loop it lands where it began. A misread link, or an error on every link, leaves a remainder: along an open chain nothing shows it; round a loop it shows, but not which link it is in.

The circular rule: directions are turns

Tap a thing to make its direction the new zero (the head turned to it). Every other direction is then read off as a turn from it, on the same dial. The writer's formula for the new reading is tangent subtraction, (s − t)/(1 + st); the turn gives it without the formula.

Two rules, one sweep: the straight rule lays the readings on the levels, where ratios of readings are gaps; the circular rule lays them on the turn, where directions from a new zero are differences of angle. At the corner the two agree. What neither gives without a step is a ratio of distances or of true sizes: that needs a second standpoint, and each such step adds one floor to the tower, after which its ratios are places again.
