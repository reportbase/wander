What can be known from where one stands

Sixteen essays, read in order. Each opens on something familiar, shows the geometry in full, says which older work saw which piece, and ends with what it changes. They are drawn from Reads and Writes, the source of truth; the earlier Observation from Somewhere, archived, holds the proofs for reference. The known physics they touch is sorted in Reads and Writes in Physics, and fourteen of them close with a paragraph on it. Each title opens its essay; the links under it are the pages that show it. Every essay also has a scrolling explainer.

  • Reading and writing. A reading is a ratio from a standpoint, free, and unchanged when the world is scaled. A writing states amounts, and costs an amount carried in.
  • The Cauchy. The heat of a read's directions, hottest along the look and cooling toward the horizon, the circle traced by one length turned: its median the corner, its far end the horizon. Where it meets computing, a direction is an address, with the heat under it.
  • Two tests. Scale the world: is it a reading or a writing? Change what arrives: is it a direction or a payload, heat or paint?

essay 21 · opens on a door opened square

Omega: the sweep's total, given its own name

essay 22 · opens on a map pinched on a phone

God mode and a vantage: what a walk on whole numbers showed

Classical geometry is god mode: no standpoint, every point at once, totals and joins, and so tied to the whole and its count. One page walks a field on whole numbers alone: thumbs from rational turns, no trigonometry, every reading a fraction. The frame is a tower whose middle cancels; the eye's place is a running total that has to be written over. Things are kept by their size on the screen, as classical level of detail keeps them. Given each scale its Cauchy share, scales without end cost a fixed total, and the view a millionth deep is the same view. Going half way to a thing leaves every slope the same fraction; only the place pays, a binary digit a halving. A read says only that there is something to read, and holds K of it. Set beside a representation thing for thing, along walks and out to 10¹⁰⁰ units, it holds the same things and draws the same picture, storing none; a stored representation's cells grow four times a doubling of its side, and with D things to a cell it holds D times as many, while a read held to K by its own count holds about 2,100 whatever D. Drawn a classical way, the same walk takes 2,400 sines and cosines and gives the same picture. Things that move are read from T alone, each on its own loop, against every mover stored and stepped: the same places, out to T = 10³⁰. Balls that collide are read the same way while their masses are equal; once they are not, the state to keep spreads, so a law in T, not interaction, is where a read from T stops. A city in the sky shows both kinds at once: cars on their own loops placed from T alone, cars in traffic whose state has to be held. What a single vantage is for: what is held is the whole front hemisphere, the flat screen about 28% of it; the dual hemispherical vantage stays for what acts unseen, for more than one standpoint and for editing; at galaxy size no representation can be stored, and a read, a seeded rule and writes for what has changed share the work. No trigonometry and no π in the loop buys exactness and reproducibility, at a cost in speed and digits; and a bouncing ball's ticks, under one pull, write its size.

essay 23 · opens on a reading lamp over a page

One share, and the shape of a law

A flat, evenly bright disc lights a point on its axis by exactly its share of the front half of directions, 1/(1 + s²) with s its distance over its radius. Close up that share is the flat near field, far off the inverse square, and the two are complements: the lamp at d and at a²/d add to the whole, half each at d = a. Scaled with its distance the disc moves nothing; 1/d² appears only when its size is held. Two discs read each other: the ratio of the reads gives their sizes, the product their distance. Not only a round source pairs so: any flat source whose edge, read from the foot, has its log-radii spread symmetrically does, the corner their geometric mean; with the foot outside the source, near and far mirror instead, brightest at the corner. The same share runs through an RC filter's crossover and, read through the level of a ratio, the best points of an elastic collision, a matched load and light crossing between two equal indices, 4k/(1 + k)². A pendulum's and an orbit's ticks read no size until the pull is carried in. Behind them all is the tower: middle denominators cancel, and a law of that shape is what a chain leaves once its middle has come down; Euler's −1 is such a cancellation. Sorting the paper's 125 laws by that shape killed the prediction: a quarter, not three quarters, and a change of chart a common third shape. Of the 82 laws that carry π or e, 30 lose it when restated in slopes and shares, the loops that come back turned among them; what keeps it is a limit, a chart or a total stated for itself, and a whole turn counted as a period. On a textbook's list it is 18 of 107: most standard formulas carry an amount, and the laws that relate directions without a limit lose it about three times in five on either list.

essay 25 · opens on a ratchet wrench counting clicks to a half turn

Counted toward: building π and e from reads, and where they meet

essay 26 · opens on a stick run along a picket fence

A face: Ω, and flowing over the discrete

A child runs a stick along a fence: the pickets are separate, the run is continuous, and the way the child faces decides the order the pickets come in. A count has no face: 514 strides say nothing of which came first, and add the same in any order. The circle drawn whole has no face either, since it holds every direction at once. A read has one: a standpoint turned one way, the cut that gives the directions a beginning at home and an end at the horizon. That is how something continuous passes over something discrete, as the read's own count of places is laid in order from home to the horizon. Ω, the classical π/2, is the measure of one face, its two halves equal at the corner; the familiar constants are a count of faces times it, the count discrete and each face continuous. A face's end has an exact mark: reads that compose to the horizon have landing points multiplying to −1, in fractions. Order, the median and the horizon need a face; counts and sums do not. A write is that discreteness, with scale and size and no face; a read is continuity, with neither, and a face. Facing V gives 1/Ω, facing away gives Ω, and both at once leave the non-facing value of one thumb.

essay 27 · opens on a "more like this" button

One standpoint or none: how alike, in AI and in a read

Press "more like this" and a machine compares points held in one frame, each coordinate its own amount, by the cosine of the angle between them: from no standpoint, over both hemispheres, the dual hemispherical vantage. A read asks from one standpoint: the query is the look, every other item read in its terms, every reading over the same denominator, with a front and a horizon. That shared denominator holds the readings together: their logs correlate at ½, and one in n + 1 has every reading inside its corner, where orthogonal axes give one in 2ⁿ. In front of the look the two rank exactly alike, so they part only in the standpoint, the behind and the trigonometry. essay 28 · opens on the theorem list at the back of a geometry book

Two statements of one theorem: classical results from no standpoint and from one

A geometry book states its theorems from no standpoint: the figure drawn whole, every side in view, lengths, angles and π. The same facts can be stated from one standpoint, every direction a slope in terms of one thumb, nothing completed. Some theorems come through whole, with an exact form in slopes and no π: Pythagoras as 1 + s² = (c/a)², adding angles as (s + a)/(1 − sa), Euler's identity as a reading and its exchange landing on points that multiply to −1, Thales as two readings s and 1/s crossing square, parallax as B/d = 1/s₁ + 1/s₂. Some come through only by renaming a constant, like the Cauchy's total. Some do not come through at all, because they state a completion: the circle's area, the sum of reciprocal squares, the Gaussian, e itself. The line falls where earlier tests found it, between relations among directions and completions held whole.

essay 30 · opens on two people dancing side to side in a corridor

Reading each other: where the back-and-forth closes, and where it does not

Two things read each other, each in its own thumb, and each writes its next step from that read. Stepping a share of the gap in levels, the endless exchange is one expression: it ends at the pair's corner, the geometric mean of their two corners, where the product of the readings is 1 and neither has its own corner. Then the doubts. The rule was chosen, and another rule ends elsewhere. Real strides settle only while they are short against the distance where the pair meets; longer ones swing for ever, like the corridor dance. Taking turns gives a two-cycle, and reading one round late halves the room for a write. With the Cauchy heat on each read the pair never settles, wandering exactly as wide as one read. Many things fix their shape and not their place. Where there is no law, there is no collapse. Among many, the heat on each read grows along a chain, its gaps several reads wide. An iteration, not a result.

essay 31 · opens on a car's speedometer and odometer

Calculus from one standpoint: where it needs N, and where it does not

The dashboard carries both halves of calculus, a rate and a total, both taught through a limit. Does calculus need N, a count held from no standpoint? A slope of one length on another is a read, unmoved by scaling the world; every higher derivative and every rate against the clock moves, a write. For functions built from fractions the derivative needs no limit at all: a number with ε² = 0 carries it exactly, and the chain rule is the tower, its middle cancelling. The integrand summed strip by strip misses at every N by Euler and Maclaurin's 1/N; the antiderivative's differences telescope exactly at every partition, and N drops out. N comes back only where the antiderivative leaves the fractions, in ln, the arctangent and π. essay 33 · opens on a plumb line

One stage, two plays: a look and a pull

A plumb line hangs along the one line left when every piece of matter in every direction has pulled on it. An eye at the same place faces one way and keeps its directions apart. Eight exact runs put the two side by side. They stand on one stage: the weight a look lays over directions is the weight a mass lays over a plane, half of it within √3 heights of the foot. A pull differs in two ways only. It carries an amount, and it joins a facing to its opposite by subtracting where a look's two halves add. "No facing" turns out to be all facings at once, each keeping its direction. Inside a shell every facing equals its opposite, so the nothing there is a balance. A flat sheet leans by exactly what a facing takes away, which is why its pull lays out as a look does. Two masses meet at a corner of their own, and light bent past a mass keeps the Cauchy along a line through it. Exploration: no ruling is changed, the pull is never called a read, and none of it is new physics.
