# What can be known from where one stands

A series of twenty-two essays, read in order, drafted from 28 October 2026. Each opens on something familiar, shows the geometry in
full, says which older work saw which piece, and ends with what it changes. *Reads and Writes* (`../readers-and-writers.md`)
is the source of truth; where an essay and it differ, the essay is the one to fix. Its physics, known physics sorted by
the two tests, is gathered in *Reads and Writes in Physics* (`../reads-and-writes-physics.md`), and fourteen of the essays (all but 8, 10,
12 and 16) end their body with a paragraph, "In physics", on the known physics that carries their geometry. *Observation from Somewhere* and its companions
are archived (`../archive/ofs/`), kept unchanged for reference: their proofs stand, and where their wording differs from the
later rulings, the later rulings stand.

Two ideas run through all of them:
- **Reading and writing.** Two properties sort everything (since 12 November, R896; a reading, the series' own):
  continuous or discrete, and moved or unmoved when the world is scaled, the thumb and stride with it and the units left
  outside. A read is continuous and unmoved: a ratio from a standpoint, a slope, free. A write is discrete and moved: a
  bounded thing with a size, a stride, a mark, a metre bar, or an amount put down in a unit carried in; it is over once
  made and is used only by being read. A count ("514 strides") is discrete and unmoved, a description laid over writes,
  and a length as an exact number in a unit is continuous and moved, the blueprint; both are tools of description. Among
  acts the two properties are one, so reading is the verb for the continuous and writing the verb for the discrete; π and
  e are how both hemispheres at once, completeness and no vantage are put into numbers. An
  *extent*, a distance in thumbs or steps (V\*/H, a gap across Euler's plane), is not a third kind: held by two looks
  together it is a read, a ratio of their slopes, and counted out it is a count. (*Reads and Writes* was rewritten on
  10 November, R788, on read, extent and write; essays that still make an extent a third kind, or a kept count a write,
  are to be read, and fixed, by this.)
- **The Cauchy.** The heat of a read's directions, a continuous weight hottest along the look and cooling toward the
  horizon, what the thumbs, pointed at the mountain, give, the circle's geometry
  with no circle held and no second breadth: its median the corner, its far end the horizon, no mean, since directions have
  none and no V was ever in them. Where the essays meet computing, a direction is an address, the heat under it (R707).

Two tests sort any quantity: **scale the world** (reading or writing?) and **change what arrives** (direction or payload, heat or paint?).
Each is an invariance. By the first, reading and writing are the scale-invariant and the scale-variant, the old
distinction of dimensional analysis; the names say more, since they name the act that turns one into the other (an
amount carried in, as no function of invariants can vary) and the vantage that holds each. Together they say why the two
acts never race: a read adds nothing, and a write to the payload moves no direction. The heat is given; the paint is met.

Where the essays say **the blueprint** they mean classical geometry's view from no standpoint, both hemispheres at once.
It has no vantage, so it is neither a read nor a write and not what they add up to; it is set beside them for comparison.
