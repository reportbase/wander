# What can be known from where one stands

A series of fourteen essays, read in order, drafted 28 October 2026. Each opens on something familiar, shows the geometry in
full, says which older work saw which piece, and ends with what it changes. *Observation from Somewhere* (`../observation-from-somewhere.md`)
holds the proofs and stays the source of truth; where an essay and it differ, the essay is the one to fix.

Two ideas run through all of them:
- **Reading and writing.** A reading is a ratio from a standpoint, free and unchanged when the world is scaled. A writing
  states amounts, and costs an amount carried in.
- **The Cauchy.** The law of a reader's directions, the circle traced by one length turned, with no second breadth: its
  median the corner, its far end the horizon, its missing mean the trace of the V it lacks.

Two tests sort any quantity: **scale the world** (reading or writing?) and **change what arrives** (address or payload?).
Each is an invariance. By the first, reading and writing are the scale-invariant and the scale-variant, the old
distinction of dimensional analysis; the names say more, since they name the act that turns one into the other (an
amount carried in, as no function of invariants can vary) and the vantage that holds each.
