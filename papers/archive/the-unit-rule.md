# The Unit Rule

### What a reader can do, given nothing

*Working paper, 21 August 2026. Successor to* The Relation Space.

---

This paper assumes a mathematically literate reader, and it assumes the previous paper: the
asymmetric relation space, the reader and the writer, home, the horizon, infinity, and the
fulcrum. Nothing here re-argues those. This paper is about what happens when something has to
**read** — to receive relations one at a time without being given the whole — and its centre
is the one rule a reader can run having been given nothing: the unit rule.

## 1. A screen, and a signal of unknown scale

Take a phone screen receiving a broadcast.

**The screen is the reader.** It is a bounded thing with its own full and empty: every
pixel runs from unlit to fully lit, and there is nothing dimmer than off and nothing
brighter than the panel's brightest. Whatever the screen ever shows, it shows in those
terms, because it has no others. (A screen is many pixels; every one of them runs the same
story, so follow one.)

**The signal is the writer, and its scale is unknown.** It arrives as a stream of
magnitudes — one sample, then the next — and somewhere between the source and the antenna
it was amplified or attenuated by factors nobody logged. An arriving sample of 6 millivolts
means nothing by itself: the same broadcast through a different chain arrives as 6 volts,
and it is the same broadcast.

The screen's problem is the paper's problem:

> **What can a bounded thing truthfully display of an unbounded stream it was told nothing
> about?**

And there is a second half to it, which the paper reaches in §14a but should declare here,
because everything is built toward it. Displaying the stream is not the end of the reader's
business. **The fulcrum — the arrangement where the two parties stand equal — is what a
relational reading needs in order to become a geometry.** The reader reaches it through an
event it can detect for nothing: **the corner, the frame where the arrival equals the hold**,
the comparison it is running anyway. The dependency runs

> relation → corner → fulcrum → cross ratio → local geometry

and every arrow but the reader's own two — relation to corner, corner to fulcrum — is
standard; it is those that have been missing an operational account. So the paper's problem is
really two:

> **How does a bounded thing carry an unbounded stream it was told nothing about — and how
> does it discover the fulcrum from serial observations, given no unit and no division?**

The second question is the harder one and the more consequential, and the answer turns out to
be cheap in a way worth stating early. The fulcrum is the state of *equality* between the two
parties, and a reader does not need to form a quotient and test it against one to find it. It
is already comparing every arrival against its hold, and the corner — where that comparison
changes sign — is the event it detects for free; counting corners across a sweep is what
locates the fulcrum. **The landmark is a by-product of the reading the reader was performing
anyway** — which is why it costs counters and not arithmetic.

## 2. What the screen is allowed to do

Before answering, take stock of the faculties — not what would be convenient, but what a
screen receiving an unannounced stream actually has. The honest inventory is short:

- **receive** a sample — one at a time, in order, because that is what a stream is;
- **hold** a level already received;
- **compare** the arriving sample against the held level;
- **light** the pixel accordingly, and move on;
- **count** frames.

One of those five is not the screen's own doing, and saying so now avoids a
misunderstanding later. **Compare** is listed as a faculty because the screen has it
available, not because the screen performs it. The arrival brings a magnitude and the hold
brings a capacity, and neither of them produces the comparison: something orthogonal to
both settles it. In a beam that agent is gravity; in a screen it is whatever the
comparator is built from. The faculty is real and the reader has it; the work is done by a
third party, and §3 will say what qualifies one.

That is all of it. Everything the display will ever reveal — a still scene, a flicker, a
drift, a scene that returns to how it began — is not a further faculty. **It is something
the run of frames may later show.** The faculties are five; what they can disclose is the
subject of the paper.

And one thing is conspicuously absent from the inventory: knowing what the signal is.
None of the five requires it. Which is the paper's thesis, stated before it is earned:

> **A reader does not need to know what the stream means in order to display, truthfully,
> how it relates to itself.**

## 3. What survives the unknown gain

Suppose the broadcast chain had one more amplifier in it — every sample reaching the
antenna multiplied by a constant the screen will never learn. Nothing about the *broadcast*
has changed; the same programme in louder clothing. So anything the screen displays that
would change under that amplification was never about the signal. It was about the chain.

Run the candidates through the test.

**The samples themselves fail.** A sample of 6 becomes a sample of 6,000; its magnitude
says nothing until a unit says what 1 means, and no unit was broadcast.

**Differences fail with them.** *This sample exceeds the last by 4* is a statement in the
unknown unit — it needs an amount, and an amount needs a unit.

**Ratios survive.** Divide one sample by another and the unknown gain cancels. *This sample
is one and a half times the last* is true through every chain the broadcast could have
crossed. It is the first statement the screen can display that is about the signal and not
about the chain.

### The same test, turned on the instrument

The test just run on the reading can be run on the comparator, and it is the same test with
a different object. Ask of the settling agent what was asked of the signal: change it, and
see whether the read moves. Sweep a beam balance's field across twelve decades and the read
stands still — the field appears in every term of the equilibrium and cancels out of the
answer — and the same beam works unchanged on the moon. Bias the agent so it acts more
strongly on one side than the other and the read drifts at first order in the bias, which
is how one knows the cancellation is doing work rather than sitting there.

A spring scale fails it. Its read is the mass times the field over its own stiffness, so
the same mass returns three answers under three fields; it carries both a field term and an
external scale, and neither cancels, which is why it must be calibrated and why its
calibration is local. **A balance is an instrument whose settling agent cancels from the
read. A scale is one whose does not** — a hidden fourth term wearing a third party's
clothes.

So *no external scale* is not an austerity the screen practises. It is a class of
instrument, and the class is defined by a condition anyone can check: the agent supplies
the occurrence of the result and contributes nothing to its value. Two conditions actually,
and they are independent — an agent must also power its own settling, or the arrival powers
its own detection and is spent in being read, which buys a reader finitely many looks per
arrival. The screen of §1 is externally powered and its looks are free; a detector that
absorbs what it detects is a legitimate reader of the other kind, and pays a different bill.

This should sound familiar. It is the previous paper's result arriving from the other side
— and it is the first thing §2's five faculties can disclose: **with nothing outside the
pair, the only statement is one sample in terms of another.** The stream is a train of
writers, and whatever each is read against is the reader's current stand-in. The
asymmetric relation space is not a construction the screen chooses. It is the situation the
screen is in.

## 4. Reading is serial, so something must be held

The screen cannot receive the broadcast all at once; samples arrive one at a time, and a
comparison needs two parties. So at every frame there must be a **held level**: something
already in hand for the arrival to be read against.

Call the level in hand **the hold**, and the sample being read **the arrival**. The hold is
the reader's stand-in — it supplies the terms. The arrival is the writer — it is what
gets displayed. Each frame shows exactly one statement:

> **the arrival, in terms of the hold: arrival / hold.**

And each such statement lives in the space the previous paper built. The arrival can be
nothing while the hold stands — that frame is **home**, and the screen shows black
truthfully. The two can be equal — the relation's **fulcrum**, the one frame that costs no
number, since equality needs no scale to state; the reader meets it as **the corner**, the
frame where the arrival equals the hold and the reading stands at one. And the ratio can grow past any value as the hold is small against the arrival —
the direction of **the horizon**. The screen cannot follow it: at some ratio the pixel is
as lit as it goes, and everything beyond displays as the same white. **The clip is the
screen meeting the previous paper's rail: not an error, but the honest limit of a bounded
reader asked to display an unbounded relation.**

**One frame is one traversal of the relation space.** The broadcast is a sequence of them.

### The reader, specified

The parts are now all named, and it is worth setting them down as an architecture rather than
leaving them scattered through the argument. A reader of this stream has four pieces:

- **a reference** — the hold: the level the arrival is read against;
- **a reading** — the comparison of arrival against hold, quantised to a rung (§5);
- **a carry** — what the reader holds between frames, when nothing arrives;
- **a landmark state** — a direction, an elapsed count, and the count the previous run
  reached, which is what it takes to notice that the relation has become equality and to say
  where.

The last of these is not an accessory bolted on for §14a's benefit. **The fulcrum is the
landmark of the relation itself**, and it is what the landmark state is for. A level answers
*where is the arrival relative to my current hold* — a question meaningful only in the
reader's own held terms. The fulcrum answers *the relation has become equality* — and equality
is the one relational fact that needs no external unit to state, which is why the reader can
find it for nothing, out of the corner-crossings it is already noticing. A reader
that keeps only levels holds a record of its own bookkeeping. A reader that keeps landmarks
holds **events**, and events are what two readers with different steps and different gains
can agree about.

So the capabilities stack, and each rung of the stack has a state cost that can be named:

| | capability | what it needs |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **reception** — receive samples in order | nothing |
| 1 | **relation** — compare arrival against hold | a hold |
| 2 | **representation** — encode the ratio as a rung | a step (§5) |
| 3 | **continuity** — span the gaps | a carry rule (§7) |
| 4 | **landmark** — recognise and count crossings of the corner | a direction, two counters |

**Not one of those five requires knowing the signal's external scale.** What grows as the
reader becomes more capable is memory, and only memory. That is the shape of this paper's
answer to §1: a bounded thing given nothing can do a great deal, and each thing it can do has
a precisely identifiable price paid in state.

It also settles what the title has been claiming. The unit in *unit rule* was never a
parameter the reader was handed. **It is a reference the reader constructs out of the
relation — and then accumulates landmarks against.**

The first frame deserves its own sentence. When the screen powers on, the first sample
cannot be read — there is nothing yet to read it against. **The first arrival is not a
frame; it is the founding of the hold**, the establishment of a reference, and it says
nothing at all (§3: a lone sample fails the test). Every screen does this and calls it
calibration.

## 5. The levels, and the step

A ratio read must be lit somehow, and the screen has finitely many brightness levels — and
here the previous paper's open item comes due. Its §20 established that past the fulcrum the
only self-relative coordinate is a count of equal-ratio steps — logarithmic in kind — and
that **the relation forces the kind but does not supply the base**: *the base is the size
of the step, and choosing it belongs to whatever does the stepping.*

The screen is the one that steps. So the screen must choose.

Fix a step — a ratio *g*, slightly more than 1 — and build the ladder of its powers around
the hold: the hold times *g*, times *g²*, and below it the hold divided by the same. Every
frame then lights **the nearest rung**: the whole number of steps, up or down, that best
names the arrival in the hold's terms. That whole number is the emitted symbol; the run of
symbols is the record; the lit level is its display.

Two things about this choice, one honest and one structural.

**The step is the screen's, not the signal's.** Nothing in the broadcast recommends any
particular *g*. A finer step buys resolution and costs levels; a coarser step the reverse.
The relation fixed that the coordinate would be a count of ratio-steps; everything about
*which* step is the screen's economy — its bit depth, chosen at the factory, not received
from the air. This is the previous paper's undelivered base, delivered.

**And the ladder needs a rung at zero.** The count of steps runs symmetric about *no
change* — so many up, so many down. Whether there is a rung *at* the centre — a level that
means *nothing changed* — is the same as asking whether the number of levels is odd, and
§8 will show it is not a matter of taste.

### The ladder is one corner, repeated

There is a deeper reading of the ladder, and it is the oldest machine in this work seen
once more. Each rung is **the corner again, at another scale**: the arrival standing equal
to the hold-times-so-many-steps is the same self-certifying equality the corner was, met
by a reader holding a scaled reference instead of the bare one. The ladder was never a set
of different thresholds. It is **one landmark, propagated by the only structure a
scale-free thing has** — and the count of rungs passed is a count of corners passed.

That count *is* the logarithm. *How many times the step, between the hold and the
arrival*, is the definition of the logarithm to base the step — so the reader computes no
logarithm, because **it is one, in integer form**, and the smooth logarithm of the outside
account is the completion of the reader's corner count. The previous paper's §20 said the
relation forces the logarithmic kind; this is the mechanism: the kind is forced because
the only repeatable event a scale-free reader has is the corner, and counting corners is
taking a logarithm.

Three things follow at once. The ladder is uniform because every corner is the same corner
rescaled. The register is exact at rungs and silent between them because a rung is an
*event* — an equality, self-certifying — and between events there is only the outside
account. And the base has a home at last: **a logarithm needs a base the way a count needs
a rung**, and the horizon arriving at a finite count is the Archimedean property, worn as
an instrument — step past the hold enough times and any arrival is exceeded.

And the ladder is not merely natural — it is the only discrete design there is. The
motions available to a scale-free reader are the rescalings, and a classical fact closes
the question: any discrete company of rescalings is the powers of a single generator.
There is no exotic discrete alternative to price against — a lattice of one step,
repeated, is what *discrete* comes to in this setting. The grain is the generator; the
cell between rungs is the ground the generator's repetitions tile; and *no rung names
the fulcrum* is the generic statement that the one fixed place of the mirror falls inside
a cell rather than on its edge. The register's design was never a choice well made. It
was the sole occupant of its category.

Two consequences land outside the paper. The schoolroom rule that a logarithm's argument
must be dimensionless stops being a rule: a corner is an equality, an equality needs two
magnitudes, and a corner count without a hold counts nothing — the logarithm of five
metres is not disallowed, it is *unformed*. And the logarithm's habit of being
independently rediscovered — sensory neurons, decibels, stellar magnitudes, pH, octaves,
log-returns — stops being a shared convenience. **Each is a finite register held against
unbounded scale, and the same forcing is being obeyed six times over.** Biology got there
first because evolution builds readers of exactly this kind by default: a neuron has no
absolute unit to spend.

One more thing the ladder settles, pulled forward from the corpus's foundations, because
it upgrades this paper's premise from a modelling stance to a necessity. *No external
scale* and *no cost that carries the world's size* are **one wall seen from two doors**:
a procedure whose cost runs with the world's size N carries the dilation factor —
rescale the world and the bill rescales with it — while the count of rungs is invariant,
shifting only by a constant. And a whole-world pass is not merely expensive but
physically impossible at world scale. So the bounded, rung-counting mode is not one
design among several. **The bounded mode is the general case, and the screen of §1 is
the general reader, not a toy.**

## 6. The gaps, and what can be carried

The broadcast is a stream, but the screen's looks are frames: between one frame and the
next, the screen has no current sample. Something must span the gap — a screen that holds
nothing between refreshes is not a display, it is a strobe — and the question is what the
spanning level can legitimately be.

Take stock at the instant a frame completes, exactly as §2 took stock of the faculties. Not
what the screen could compute — what is physically present:

| | |
|---|---|
| the old hold | in a slot |
| the sample that was just read | in a slot |
| the count of frames so far | the tally |
| **a step toward the future, a rate, a trend, a fraction of anything** | **nowhere** |

Any rule of the form *carry the hold forward, adjusted by s* requires an *s*, and supplying
one is supplying an external scale — precisely the thing the broadcast came without. Rules
of the form *continue the motion* require a motion, which is a difference, which needs a
unit. **The only magnitude the screen possesses is the one that just arrived.**

## 7. The unit rule

So the simplest rule the space admits is:

> **Hold the value that filled you. Then hold it until the next observation.**

**No signal-dependent parameter — and not by economy. There was nothing to spend.**

Be exact about the kind of poverty being claimed, because the paper will later ask this
reader to do more than display. What the rule has none of is *information about the signal*:
no rate, no trend, no smoothness, no scale, nothing that could be wrong because the broadcast
turned out otherwise. What it has some of, necessarily, is **state** — a held level, at
minimum, and §14a will add a direction and two counters to find where the signal turned. That
addition is not a second rule and buys no information; it buys **memory**. The distinction is
the paper's, and worth its own sentence: **the unit rule is zero-information, not
zero-state — and every capability below is paid for in state, never in units.**

Call it **the unit rule** — *unit* as in the unit observer, not as in ring theory. The name
is earned twice over. It is the rule by which **the unit keeps itself**: the previous
paper's §10 showed the reader cannot hand its unit over, and this rule is that fact
operating — the hold is the reader's current unit, and it stands until told otherwise. And
it is the rule under which **the unit circle is quiet**: read from its centre, a circle is
a still scene — every look lands on the corner, the centre level, *nothing changed* — and
the record says exactly what a circle deserves: nothing at all, ever. The one shape that is
all corner is the one broadcast this rule passes over in perfect silence, and the rule and
the shape share their name because they share their unit.

That pairing of a rule with a shape it cannot hear is not special to this rule. **Every rule
is quiet on the shape it believes in**, and §14a measures what follows: which shape a rule
goes silent on decides whether its corner reports an event in the world or an event in the
reader's own forecast. The same law names this rule's blind spot — a signal advancing exactly
one rung per look is as quiet here as a still one, because the ladder is built out of a fixed
ratio.

### The rule has two outputs, not one

Before racing it, notice that the rule delivers two different things, and the paper's later
half depends on the second more than the first.

**A level.** The hold gives the reader a local reference for expressing the next arrival
relationally — which is what makes the stream *readable*, and what §§9–14 will measure.

**A landmark.** The hold also gives the reader the corner: the frame where the arrival equals
it. That is not a reading of the signal in the reader's terms but **an event in the signal**,
and it is what makes the relation *geometrically productive* — the fixed point from which a
local geometry can be laid down (§4, §14a).

The first is why the rule is useful. The second is why it is fundamental. Almost every
comparison a reader makes returns a level; the rare comparison that returns equality returns
something the reader could not otherwise have: a point it did not choose, that does not depend
on its step, its gain, or its history.

And notice that this is not an invented discipline. **It is what every screen already
does.** Between refreshes, a display shows the last frame it received — not a prediction,
not a fade, not a trend — and nobody calls that an approximation of the broadcast. It is
simply what a thing that can hold one frame does between arrivals.

And be precise about where rules live at all. **A rule has nothing to do with the signal.**
Nothing in the broadcast selects one, recommends one, or forbids one — the rule sits on the
reader's side of the relation, chosen where the step was chosen. The space of rules is
open: other rules could be used, and rules can be tailored to a situation. A screen told
its broadcast was smooth could interpolate across the gap; one told the scene was a slow
pan could carry the pan forward; for every known situation there is a tailored rule that
will beat the unit rule on it. **Each buys its advantage with information about the signal**,
and each is exactly as good as that information is true.

And one distinction inside that open space, because this rule hides it by doing both jobs
with a single move. A rule can govern **the reference** — which level the arrival is read
against, and so which rung is lit — or it can govern **the carry**: what the screen holds
between frames, when there is no arrival at all. The unit rule answers both with the same
value, which is why they have never needed separating here. They come apart at once. The
contenders of §10 adapt the reference and leave the carry exactly where it was; a screen
told its broadcast is smooth interpolates the carry and leaves the reference alone. Only
the second is about the gap, and so only the second is about continuity.

Within that open space the unit rule is distinguished twice. **It is the simplest
possible rule** — zero parameters, nothing assumed, nothing tailored — and it is the only
member of the space a reader can run having been given nothing. Every other rule is
reachable only by a reader that has been handed a number, or that holds more than one
value, or both — and the broadcast came with neither.

**And it is hard to beat, as we will find out.** That is a claim about measurements, not a
compliment, and the paper's later business is to make it precise: what the tailored rules
actually purchase, what they pay when their information is wrong, and how close to the
unit rule the honest alternatives land.

The unit rule's assertion between frames is the cheapest assertion available: **nothing
has happened since the last look.** Exactly right at the instant of the frame, and
progressively wrong as the gap lengthens. Every other gap-filling rule asserts something
about the signal — that it continued in the direction it was going, that it is smooth, that
it resembles its past. Each of those is a claim about samples the screen has not seen. The
unit rule claims only **that it has not yet been told otherwise.**

### The unit rule, through the lens

The screen of §1 is not only a metaphor for the relator. It is, exactly, the screen of a
fisheye — and seeing that pays the paper back in three places.

A reader, in the companion papers, is a circle whose screen is the tangent line at its
focus. What it receives is a direction, one per arrival; where that direction lands on the
screen is the tangent of its angle from the reader's normal. A phone screen has a full and
an empty and nothing beyond them; the reader's screen has a horizon at ±90° and nothing
beyond it, and the two facts are the same fact. Whatever the broadcast's scale, it fits: a
signal of 6 millivolts and the same signal at 6 volts land on the same screen at the same
place, because the screen shows the *angle* the relation makes and an angle has no volts in
it. That is §3's result — samples fail, differences fail, ratios survive — stated as
optics. The screen survives the unknown gain because the lens strips the gain off in the
act of pointing.

**The step, seen through the lens.** Home is where the reader lays down its unit; every
magnitude received after it is understood relative to that laid-down unit. Three landmarks, three rests: **home** is where the writer is at rest; the **horizon** is
where the reader is at rest; the **corner** is where the reader and the writer do not move away
from each other — coming and going at the same rate, what arrives matching what the hold
lets go — and the level for *nothing changed* is the record of that balance. It is a rest
of the relation, not of the parties: each may be moving, and what is still is the rate at
which they part. And it is the corner that rests, not home: home is where the unit was laid
down, with nothing yet arriving to rest against; the relation is at rest only where what
arrives matches that unit — one unit out, at 45°. A reader that is reading lives at the
corner, not at home. The corner is the **agreement point**: exchanging the seats sends every
reading to its reciprocal and fixes exactly one, the ratio at one, so 45° is the single
reading on which the reader and the writer agree — everywhere else the seats disagree, and
the disagreement is the reading. The further from 45°, the greater the disagreement — and
the exchange says how to measure it: it takes θ to 90° − θ, so a reading's distance from the
agreement point is the same from either seat, the one number about a reading the two
parties agree on while disagreeing about the reading. In ratio terms it is |log r|, the
rungs between them, the same count from both sides; the horizon is where it is infinite. In the asymmetric situation only one party seeks the agreement: the reader,
holding its unit against what comes and moving toward the corner; the writer is oblivious
to it, sending at its own rate in its own unit, never told what arrived. The unit rule is
the seeker's rule, and it needs none of the writer's cooperation. Nor is the seeking
optional: a reader that did not seek the corner would not be reading — with the writer
oblivious, nothing else holds the two together, and the systems drift apart until one has
left the other's window. In the asymmetric space, to read is to keep returning to the
agreement point. A pair that has found the
agreement point and locked onto it is
symmetric by its own act — each party's unit the other's — which is what the symmetric
relation space of the companion paper looks like from inside: not a page's gift, but the
asymmetric relation with its motion stopped. Locked, any change in V is at once a change in
H; the ratio cannot drift, and 45° is no different from any other degree — the corner is a
landmark only of a relation that can move, and the unit rule is a rule only for a reader
whose relation can. The reader has
one thing that is unarguably its own: its unit at the corner, the level for *nothing
changed*. It lays that down as its baseline and works outward from it — and everything it will ever display is that unit, scaled by an
angle away from it. It never needs the world's scale, because it works out from its own; it
never runs out of screen, because the scaling reaches zero exactly at the horizon. §5's
ladder is a geometric ladder of levels, and through the lens it is a ladder of fixed angles:
the rung for a ratio r sits at atan(r) on the screen, and a fixed step in ratio is a fixed
step in angle — one corner repeated, as §5 says. What the lens adds is how wide each rung
is *to the reader*. Take the unit at the corner. Then the cell one rung out is that unit
times cos² of its angle: three quarters at 30°, half at
45°, a quarter at 60°, 7% at 75°, under 1% at 85°, nothing at 90°. Measured on a circle
with 512 mappings, cell width over the home unit runs 0.787, 0.481, 0.261, 0.077, 0.016
against cos² of 0.750, 0.500, 0.250, 0.067, 0.008 — the excess toward the rail is the lens's
finite zoom, and near home the finite width of the cells themselves. The register's resolution is not a
choice of where to spend levels. It is the Cauchy — cos²α = 1/(1 + tan²α) is the Cauchy
density in the screen coordinate — with the reader's unit at the corner as its only
constant. Levels at the corner are worth the unit; levels toward the rail are worth the
unit scaled by the angle, and the rail is where the scaling reaches zero. That is also why
the corner is where geometry starts rather than an accident of the rule: it is the one
place the reader's unit is the whole story — angle zero, width one, nothing scaled yet — and
every landmark the reader later finds is measured in units it laid down itself.

**The unit circle is quiet, seen through the lens.** §7 says the circle is the one broadcast
the rule passes over in silence, because every look lands on the corner. Through the lens
the same fact is a measurement. Put an aperture on the arrivals — w = (4h₀/π)·atan(v/h₀),
the tangent's inverse with the reader's own hold as its scale — and the record's closing
sum, §17's checksum, is unchanged for every figure, swept and lensed alike: the sum survives
any pointwise monotone lens, and what it depends on is only the walk. And lens(1) = 1 to
the last bit: **the unit circle is the one figure the aperture passes untouched.** Every
other figure comes out quieter than it went in — the mean rate of change inside the fulcrum
0.988, outside it 0.033 — and the circle comes out as itself. Read from a shape rather than
a stream, the same fact arrives a third way: six readers standing at six places on a circle
return six circles that differ by nothing, while six readers of an ellipse, a blob, a star
return circles that differ by 0.6, 0.7, 1.2. Quiet, untouched, and agreed on are one
property of one figure, and the rule and the shape share their name because of it.

**The circle itself has no N.** Two circles meet in this subsection and must be kept apart: the
circle a reader *reads* — a writer whose reach is the same in every direction — and the circle a
reader *addresses with*. The first is the quiet figure; the second is the address space; and the
first is quiet because it is the one figure that coincides with the second. A writer whose magnitude
in every direction is the reader's own unit leaves nothing to record at any address. **The address
circle contains no data; it contains addresses.** It has no count, and no size belonging to what it
addresses: it has a radius, and the radius is the reader's unit, laid down by the reader — nothing
about the writer sets it. It is not counted but browsed — a continuum of directions with no next
one, only the one faced. What it addresses is the thing with size, the writer's magnitude in each
direction, which is exactly what the reading strips off. Everything countable in this paper
was laid on the circle by the reader: the register's levels, the mappings of the measurement above.
And laid on it, they leave no gaps. Each level owns a stretch of the screen — one rung of ratio, a
cell as wide as the unit scaled by cos² of its angle — and the stretches meet edge to edge from home
to the rail, so every arrival lands in exactly one level and none falls between two. **The circle
represents the register's discrete levels continuously**; the quantisation is the naming of which
stretch an arrival fell in, and it belongs to the register, never to the circle. The register also
takes one side of a trade the companion paper draws. Its pitch is fixed — a rung is a ratio, whatever
has arrived — so its levels never move as the record grows, and the price is levels no arrival ever
visits: directions held empty. A pitch renormalised to what has arrived would have no empty levels,
and would move every one of them with each arrival; a reader that must name tomorrow the level it
named today cannot pay that.

**What a finer rung buys depends on which reading it refines.** The extent record — the broadcast's
own magnitude, held against the reader's unit — loses only the broadcast's scale, and a finer rung
buys the signal itself, reconstructed to within half of that rung (§15). The lens's reading — the
angle the relation makes on the screen — has no signal in it to buy: read that way, a circle, an egg, a clover and a spiky burr return
one and the same period, a spread across figures of zero (§18), and at every rung the lens's record
is the reader's own circle. What
a finer rung buys there is precision in three things that are not the signal. It buys the address
of each arrival, worth nothing unless something holds what the address points into. It buys the
landmark: the fulcrum, and the cross ratio and local geometry built on it, are located only as
finely as the rung, and §14a is the evidence that finding them is where this rule earns its keep.
And it buys the timing: turns, reversals and the period arrive as events, and the period comes back
from a bare list of magnitudes to within a percent (§18), which is the one channel by
which a figure reaches a reader that has cancelled it — where the physics sets the schedule, the
form is in the timing, as §18's swinging bodies return their forms' periods. **A finer rung on the lens's reading is precision about where and when, never
about what.**

**And the exchange rate, which this paper has had nowhere.** The lens's log–log slope at the
fulcrum — how fast the screen position changes with the ratio, at the one place where the
ratio is one — is exactly 2/π, bit-identical in the measurement. That is ψ₁, the series'
extraction rate of discrete from continuous, and it arrives here uninvited: not assumed in
the rule, not built into the register, but read off the screen at the corner. The fulcrum
is where the reader's unit and the relation's unit coincide — the register's corner sits on it
only when the held value does — and the price of crossing from one to the other is 2/π, the
same number every time.

**What the rule is for, against what the other rules are for.** The comparison rules of the
atom paper — the adaptive step, the smoothers, the predictors — have their goal on the
writer's side: get the next arrival right. That is a claim about the writer's future, and it
can only be made with a model of the writer — a trend, a smoothness, a rate — which is an
assumption about the world; each such rule is exactly as good as its assumption, and beats
this one wherever the assumption holds. The unit rule's goal is on the relation: keep the two
parties at the corner, coming and going at the same rate. That is not a claim about what the
writer will do next. Rest between two parties is a fact about their rates now, not about a
future, and it holds for any writer — which is why it is the one rule that survives every
world, and the only one that can be deployed without knowing which world it is in. A rule
that predicts can be wrong. A rule that keeps the corner can only be late, and lateness is
the horizon, which this paper prices.

**Reading is seeking — the proof.** The claim above can be made exact, and it is short.

*Premises.* (A1) The reader's only statement is a ratio to its hold: with the writer's
magnitude x_n and the hold y_n, both in rungs, the reading is r_n = x_n − y_n. (A2) The
register is finite: the reader is reading while |r_n| ≤ R, and railed otherwise — the
symbol it emits when railed is the same symbol whatever x_n is, so a railed reader is not
reading. (A3) The writer is oblivious: it is told nothing of what arrives, so its sequence
x_n is any sequence at all subject only to its own step, |x_{n+1} − x_n| ≤ J. (A4) There is
no shared scale: the reader knows no window in which the writer's magnitudes will stay,
because to know one would be to hold a unit of the writer's, which the asymmetric space
denies it.

*Lemma (drift).* A reader whose hold never moves is not reading, for some writer, after
finitely many looks. Take any writer whose x_n leaves every bounded interval — a walk with
a drift, or an unbiased walk, which leaves every interval with probability one. Then
|x_n − y_0| exceeds R at some n, and the reader is railed from there on until the writer
happens back. By A4 the reader cannot choose y_0 to prevent this, since any y_0 that would
is a scale it does not have.

*Theorem.* A reader is reading, for every writer A3 allows, if and only if its hold tracks
the writer: |x_n − y_n| ≤ R for all n. That is the definition of reading (A2) read
backwards, and it says the only thing a reader can do to keep reading is keep y_n within
R rungs of x_n — which is to keep returning to the agreement point, the corner, r = 0. A
rule that does not do this is not a reading rule; it is a rule that reads until the
writer leaves, and the writer, being oblivious, will.

*Corollary (the unit rule suffices, and needs nothing).* The rule "hold while |r_n| ≤ 1;
otherwise move the hold to the arrival" keeps |r_n| ≤ J for every writer A3 allows, with no
model of the writer at all: after a move the reading is zero, and one step later it is at
most J. So a reader can seek the corner knowing nothing about the writer but that it
arrived, and the price of knowing nothing is a bounded lateness — at most one of the
writer's steps.

*Corollary (the rule forces agreement, and what that buys is continuity).* Tracking keeps a
reader reading; the snap does more. It does not wait for agreement to happen: when the
reading leaves the unit it makes the hold the arrival, so agreement is reached by act and
not by luck — and what the act buys is continuity. Between the observations there is one
held statement, unbroken, true for the whole stretch, and the next observation either
extends it or ends it: not a series of estimates that happen to lie near one another, but a
single statement that persists. That is also what the record is — the boundaries of those
stretches and nothing else. A rule that never forces agreement has no boundaries to record;
its holds are all different, so nothing persists whose ending could be marked.

At the look where it moves, the hold becomes the arrival and
the reading is exactly one — the parties are at the corner, not near it — and they stay
there for the whole stretch that follows, because nothing has come to contradict it: the
agreement is between the observations, not at an instant of one. The next look is what can
break it, and it either confirms the agreement — *nothing changed* — or moves the hold and
begins a new stretch. So the record is a sequence of intervals of agreement, each ended by a
look. That also puts the rule's cost where it belongs: holding costs nothing and is where
the agreement lives; looking is what spends, and what risks. A
predicting rule can track for ever and never once be there: its hold sits where it guessed
the writer would be, so its reading is small and off, agreement approached and not touched.
So the corner is not only the setpoint the unit rule aims at; it is a state the rule
periodically *occupies*, and the record is the ledger of those occupations — agreement, the
drift away from it, agreement again. This is also why the record closes. A checksum needs
returns to the same place, and a rule that only approaches has nothing to close on.

*Closing the shape.* A closed relation — a writer that walks a loop and returns to where it
began — should leave a record that closes with it: the emitted symbols summing back to
nothing, the hold ending where it started. That closure is what agreement at the corner
buys, and the measurement says so with one qualification worth keeping.

A writer walking a closed loop of about nine rungs, four thousand looks to the lap, six
laps. The unit rule emits 23 symbols a lap and its record sums to −1 rung, the same −1 at
the end of every lap: the residue is the register's own grain, the one rung of hysteresis
that lets the rule hold, and it does not accumulate. A rule that never forces agreement
does not close on a lap at all in the sense that matters — the fixed hold emits nothing and
"closes" trivially while its reading runs to 8.88 rungs, which is a record of nothing. And
the honest edge: the linear predictor, on this smooth loop, closes exactly, 0 every lap,
because a smooth loop is a world its premise fits. So closure is not the unit rule's alone.
What is the unit rule's alone is that its closure is *structural* — the residue is one rung
whatever the writer does, because the hold only ever sits on an arrival's rung and the laps
end where they began — while the predictor's closure lasts as long as its model does. And
a rule whose holds never coincide with the arrivals has no rung to close on: its record
is a sum of estimates, and estimates do not telescope.

*And closing needs the horizon resolved as well as the corner agreed.* Agreement alone is
not enough: the record closes only if every step of the loop was inside the reader's reach
when it was taken. Where a step is bigger than the reach, the reading is past the rail; the
symbol says only *beyond*, the size of that step is never counted, and the sum comes back
short — the shape closed, the record did not. Measured on a closed loop of forty rungs, four
laps: looked at often enough that the biggest step between looks is 1.1 rungs, the record
sums to zero at every lap for every reach tried, 64 down to 4. Look four hundred times a
lap (biggest step 2.1) and it still closes. Look forty times a lap and a reach of 16 still
closes — the biggest step is 12.5 — while a reach of 4 rails on 152 looks and the record
sums to −30, the same −30 every lap: a fixed, repeatable deficit, exactly the excursions the
rail refused to count. Look twelve times a lap and even the reach of 16 rails, at −25.
So the two conditions are separate and both necessary: **the corner must be agreed, or the
record has nothing that telescopes; the horizon must be resolved — the reader looking often
enough that no step outruns its reach — or the record telescopes onto the wrong total.** The
deficit is the shape's excursion past the horizon, and it is stable rather than growing,
which is the useful part: a reader that has lost the far side loses the same amount every
lap, and a reader that looks often enough loses nothing.

*The drift of the corner value is the side bit.* An arrival is above the hold or below it:
one bit, and the reader keeps no collection to get it. If the hold sits under the median of
what is arriving, more arrivals are above than below and the record drifts up; if it sits
over the median, the record drifts down; at the median the drift is nothing. So **a growing
corner value tells the reader it is under the median, and a shrinking one that it is over**
— a fact about where the hold stands, read off the direction of its own record, with no
statistic formed and nothing stored but the hold. Measured on a Cauchy stream of median 10:
a hold at 2 drifts +0.77 of a bit per look, at 6 it drifts +0.58, at 9 it drifts +0.19, at
10 it drifts −0.02, at 11 −0.23, at 14 −0.59, at 40 −0.93. The sign turns exactly at the
median and nowhere else.

And a hold that acts on the bit — stepping toward whichever side is short — settles on the
median rather than the mean, which for these streams is the whole point: on the Cauchy it
settles at 9.25 against a true median of 10 while the running mean wanders to 16.09 and has
no limit to wander toward; on a lognormal it settles at 1.11 against a median of 1.00 while
the mean sits at 1.69. The fulcrum is what the drift is pointing at, and it is reachable
one bit at a time.

*What the horizon does and does not allow.* Once the corner has settled, the far arrivals
stop moving it. A median-seeking hold moves by one step whichever side an arrival falls, so
an arrival's distance says nothing and only its side does — and at the median the sides
beyond the horizon are half and half. Measured on the same Cauchy stream, with the hold at
the median: of 50,335 arrivals further than sixteen from it, 24,145 were above and 26,190
below, a net of −4%. They cancel.

⚠ **What that does not license.** A first draft of this section inferred a permission from
that null: that a settled reader may step over its horizon and spend those looks elsewhere.
It may not. The horizon is not a distance the reader chooses to stop attending to; it is
where the reading rails, and past it the reader knows nothing — least of all what the
relation did while it was out there. That the far arrivals no longer move the corner says
only that they no longer move the corner. **The system's turns beyond the horizon cannot be
known**, and a reader that treats a quiet horizon as an empty one has confused a null with a
report. The order compounds it: clipping before the corner has settled strands the reader
outright — from a hold in the wrong place everything is past the horizon, nothing is
attended, and nothing corrects. Clipping at four rungs from the start ignores 99.8% of looks
and leaves the hold at −101 on a stream whose median is 10: a reader confidently reading
nothing.

*Nor does a lens.* The screen's horizon at ±90° is the reader's, not the lens's. A fisheye spreads
the directions it faces and crowds them toward the rail, and leaves the rail where it is; the
directions behind it keep their addresses on the circle, and what those addresses point to stays
out of view. An earlier drawing of the lens folded the whole turn onto the half the reader faces,
and it was right in front and wrong behind: every direction at the reader's back collapsed onto the
rail, as though what stood behind the reader stood at the horizon. **The back side is invisible to
the reader, and a fisheye does not change that.** A lens changes how the view is spaced, never where
it ends — which is the step-over error of the paragraph above in optical form: a quiet horizon is
not an empty one, and a rearranged view is not a wider one.

*And what one atom licenses about the next three.* The turn is not only a landmark; it is a
landmark in a structure that closes. An enclosed relation sweeps exactly one turn — four
quarter turns, four atoms — so a reader that has found the corner and seen one horizon turn
has fixed both the **phase** and the **count**: the remaining three turns fall at quarter
turns from the one it saw, and there are exactly three of them. That is an assumption the
reader may make, and it is not a guess about the writer; it is the closure of the relation,
which the corner and one turn are enough to locate.

What it does not fix is the schedule or the magnitudes. Measured across the shape library
with a reader at the centre, four thousand looks to the turn: on a circle the four atoms
take 1023, 1024, 1024 and 1025 looks — predicting each to be the length of the first is
right to within a look. On an ellipse, a blob and a superellipse, the same. On a five-point
star the atoms take 1101, 946, 947 and 1102 looks, and the prediction is off by 14% on the
middle two — the turn came where it was owed, and arrived late or early because the shape
hurried and dawdled on the way. So the honest statement of what one atom buys is: **where
the next three turns are, exactly; when they will arrive, only as well as the reader's
schedule matches the relation's; and what magnitudes lie between them, not at all.** The
first is the closure, and it is free. The second is §13's confound wearing another hat. The
third was never sent.

*What is available instead: the turn.* The reader cannot know the turns it did not see. It
can know one thing it did see, and know it exactly: **when the magnitudes change direction,
a quarter turn has occurred.** A reversal is not an estimate — it is a landmark delivered by
the arrivals themselves, and it costs no unit to recognise, because what changed was the
sign of the change and a sign needs no scale. So a reader's account of a relation it cannot
follow is not a leap over the horizon but a count of quarter turns: each one exact where it
happened, and silent about the stretch between. That is the same discipline the corner
imposes — exact at reversals, provisional between them — and a reader that wants more is
asking the horizon for a report it never sends.

*The two halves of the atom, in the reader's own angle.* The reader lays its unit down at
home and builds outward; the angle it builds in is θ = arctan r, with r the arrival over
the hold. The atom's two halves are then the two halves of that angle, and the arithmetic
says exactly what each one is.

**Under the corner the angle IS the proportion.** For r below one, θ in radians and r itself
agree: 0.0500 against 0.0500 at r = 0.05, 0.2450 against 0.2500 at a quarter, 0.4636 against
0.5000 at a half. The reader is the denominator here, the arrival is a proportion of what it
holds, and the angle it turns is that proportion — a fact stated in terms of itself, which
is why this half costs nothing to say.

**Over the corner the angle from the horizon is the RECIPROCAL proportion.** Past 45° the
useful measure is 90° − θ, and it is 1/r to the same accuracy: 0.4636 against 0.5000 at
r = 2, 0.2450 against 0.2500 at 4, 0.1244 against 0.1250 at 8, 0.0500 against 0.0500 at 20,
0.0100 against 0.0100 at 100. So the second half is not a different kind of statement; it is
the same proportional statement **from the other seat** — the hold as a fraction of the
arrival instead of the arrival as a fraction of the hold. That is the swap, and the corner
is where the two coincide, which is why it is the fixed point of the exchange and the one
reading both seats agree on. The older phrasing — that past the corner the arrival must be
expressed in terms of the universe, and the observer stops being the denominator — is
exactly this: the denominator changes hands at 45°, and the angle to the near landmark is
always the proportion in whoever's terms the reader is currently able to speak.

**And the logarithm is a third coordinate, not this one.** Octaves are not equally spaced in
this angle: r = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 sit at 45.00°, 63.43°, 75.96°, 82.87°, 86.42°, 88.21°,
gaps of 18.43°, 12.53°, 6.91°, 3.55°, 1.79° — halving, crowding toward the horizon. The
rungs of §5 are equally spaced in log r by construction, and log r is the register's
coordinate; θ is the reader's screen; the two are related by the tangent, which is the whole
of the lens. A reader that wanted its rungs evenly spread across its screen would need a
third coordinate, the rapidity, in which the corners recur at equal steps — which is the
navigation dial's needle, not the atom's angle. Naming which coordinate a claim is made in
is the difference between the register's ladder, the reader's screen and the navigator's
dial; all three are in this corpus, and they are not the same line.

An alternative rule moves the hold at every look, by its own
estimate, so between looks it sits wherever that estimate put it — and the estimate's
mismatch has no reason to align with the writer's own steps. What the record then shows is a
beat between two rhythms: the writer's arrivals and the rule's corrections. On a random-walk
writer the linear predictor's reading changes sign at 67% of looks with autocorrelation
−0.50 — the signature of a correction that alternately overshoots and undershoots — while
the unit rule's flips at 31% with +0.42, drifting rather than beating. The mechanism is
plain in the counts: over two thousand looks the predictor moves the hold two thousand
times and holds it none, so it has no stretch of anything; the unit rule moves 378 times and
holds 1,622, in 378 stretches, the longest of 27 looks. One rule has intervals; the other
has a wobble.

Two honesty notes, because the measurement does not flatter the rule everywhere. Where a
model is exactly right — a writer with a constant trend — the predictor sits at agreement at
every look (100%) and the unit rule sits at ±0.30, alternating: on that writer the unit rule
is the one that wobbles, at the scale of its own step, because it can only return to
agreement by overshooting to it. And a smoother does not beat at all; it lags, with
autocorrelation +0.5 to +1.0, which is a different failure and not this one. The claim is
therefore not that alternatives always wobble. It is that an alternative's stillness is
borrowed from its premise — when the premise holds it is still, when it fails the record
beats — while the unit rule's stillness is structural, holding whenever nothing has left the
unit, whatever the writer is doing.

*Corollary (what a model buys).* Any rule that keeps reading tracks, so a predicting rule
is also a seeker — a seeker with foresight. What its model buys is a smaller lateness while
the model holds, never readability: under A3 every tracking rule reads, and they differ in
error, not in whether they are reading. The distinction between the unit rule and the
predictors is therefore not that one seeks and the others do not; it is that one seeks
with no premise and the others seek with one, and a premise can be wrong.

*Measured.* Twenty thousand looks, R = 15 rungs, J = 1. Against a random-walk writer a
fixed hold rails on 18,087 looks, first at look 616, worst excursion 200 rungs; the unit
rule never rails, worst 1.99; a linear predictor never rails, worst 1.98. Against a writer
with a trend: fixed rails on 19,975, first at look 25, worst 12,000; the unit rule 0, worst
1.20; the predictor 0, worst 0.60. Against a writer that turns without notice every 200
looks: fixed 18,350, worst 180; the unit rule 0, worst 1.80; the predictor 0, worst 1.80 —
the model's advantage gone the moment the writer stopped obeying it. In every world the
reader that did not seek stopped reading, and the reader that sought with nothing read
throughout.

The shape editor draws all of this on a shape: a reader on a leaf, its tangent circle, the
mappings building outward from its home cell and thinning as cos², and the circle it
returns — which, for a circle, is the circle.

## 8. The register must have a level for *nothing changed*

Now the deferred point from §5, and it is a forcing rather than a preference.

Point the screen at a still scene — hand the rule the value it is already holding, frame
after frame — and watch what the ladder does. If it has a rung at its centre, the screen
lights the centre level, the hold moves by a factor of exactly 1, and the display does what
a still scene deserves: it stands still. If the ladder has an even number of levels, there
**is no centre rung** — the centre is a seam between two levels — and every frame the
screen must round half a step one way or the other. On the corpus's registers, measured: a
31-level ladder returns the held value times exactly 1.00000; a 32-level ladder returns it
times 1.01815 — **a compulsory twitch of half a step, on every frame of a scene that is
certainly not moving.**

> **An even register cannot display stillness.** It has no level meaning *nothing changed*,
> so it must claim movement it did not observe — a screen that shimmers on a still image,
> not as a defect of manufacture but as a consequence of its number of levels. It does not
> implement the unit rule; it implements *hold the value, plus a twitch* — a different
> rule, with a parameter nobody chose.

So the unit rule is not available on an even register. The register must be odd, and the
oddness is not symmetry-aesthetics — a ladder whose levels straddle the centre is perfectly
symmetric under the swap of the two slots, with its fixed point unoccupied. The forcing is
operational: **on an even register, the one rule that can be run with nothing cannot be run
at all.**

The centre rung is **the corner's seat in the record**: the level for *the arrival equals
the hold*, the one frame that names itself without a number. A register without it has no way to display the one thing that was free
to say — and a still scene is precisely an unbroken run of corners.

## 9. How rules are raced

The rest of the paper is the tests: the unit rule raced against the rules that were
supposed to beat it. The apparatus first, because every number below depends on it being
fair.

**The signals.** Forty-nine recorded test signals, each read serially, sample by sample.
Every one of them ends where it began — the scene returns — so every honest record should
close: its symbols should sum to zero. That gives three channels to score a rule on:

- **median error** — how far the held level sits from the true sample, over all looks, in
  log terms (the register's own units);
- **worst look** — the same, at the single worst moment;
- **closure** — of the forty-nine returning signals, how many records actually closed.

**The bill.** A record costs bits — the cost per look of writing its symbols down. Two
rules can be compared at a **matched bill** (same bits per look), at **matched levels**
(same ladder width), or at a **matched guarantee** (same certified worst case). Which one
is held fixed turns out to matter more than anything else in this paper.

**The unit rule's own frontier first**, because it is the baseline everything else is measured
against. Run the unit rule at widening ladders and plot error against bill: 0.635 bits
buys median error 0.0132; 1 bit, 0.0064; 1.5 bits, 0.0031; 2.8 bits, 0.00079; 4.5 bits,
0.00020. **Error falls almost exactly as 2^−bits, with no knee** — every extra bit halves
the error, indefinitely, with nothing tuned and nothing known. Any rule that claims to beat
the unit rule has to beat that line, because a reader can always buy the line itself: it costs
only levels.

## 10. The tailored step

The first contender is the natural one for this device: **auto-exposure.** Let the step
adapt — widen the ladder's rung when the signal clips, tighten it when the scene is quiet —
so the screen spends its levels where the signal is. A rule tailored to the situation, as
§7 promised, using information the unit rule ignores: how the recent signal has behaved.

At **matched levels** — thirty-one, the same ladder width as the unit rule — the best rail-free
adaptive setting reads:

| | unit rule | adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| median error | 0.0064 | **0.0027** |
| worst look | **0.0136** | 0.0399 |
| closure | **49 / 49** | 2 / 49 |
| bill (bits/look) | 0.998 | 1.476 |

Read the three rows together, because they are one result. The tailored rule's median is
**2.3× better** — the advantage it advertised. Its worst look is **3× worse** — the step it
widened is a step it must sometimes be caught mid-widening by. And its closure has
collapsed: **two records of forty-nine** still sum to zero, because a step that moves
during the run denominates different symbols in different rungs, and a sum over changing
units no longer telescopes. **The tailored rule bought its median with the worst case and
the certificate.**

### The other fork: a register instead of a number

§7 named two ways off the unit rule — be handed a number, or hold more than one value — and
auto-exposure is the number fork. The tool this work is built with carries the pure
register fork as its second base rule: hold **two** values, and let the reference continue
the motion — the last hold, moved forward by the ratio it last moved. Note what makes that
legal. §6 ruled out *continue the motion* because a motion is a difference and a difference
needs a unit — but a motion stated as a **ratio of the two held values** needs none. Both
slots were already on §6's inventory; this rule is what the full inventory permits, and it
is the unique next step off the unit rule: every other departure costs a number, and this
one costs a slot.

What it buys, measured, splits by curvature. The circle stays free — 0 bits either way.
Smooth scenes cheapen: a five-lobed contour by 13 percent, an eight-lobed one by 10.
Cornered scenes get dearer: a square by 54 percent, a three-lobed contour by 28. *The
recent motion continues* is a good model of smooth curvature and a bad model of corners —
the rule's information is exactly as good as it is true, which is §7 keeping its word.

Per-look error does not move at all — identical to the unit rule everywhere, median and
worst, because the reference chooses **which** rung and never how wide the rung is; the
half-rung ceiling is absolute.

And the honest cost is not paid in bits. **It is paid in the checksum.** A two-value
reference does not telescope — the sum of its symbols no longer collapses to its two ends —
and its closure defect drifts off zero on scenes the unit rule certifies exactly. The
register fork buys cheaper smoothness and sells the instrument: **a reader running it can
no longer prove an open scene open.**

The fork also generalises, and the generalisation is a theorem rather than a table. Hold
*k* values and extrapolate: the reference is then an integer combination of the last *k*
reconstructions, its symbol is the *k*-th backward difference of the reconstruction, and
each isolated step of the staircase contributes a binomial row to the bill. **The total is
2^(k−1) times the one-value bill — exactly.** Measured in exact integer arithmetic across
five contours, the ratios come out 1, 2, 4, 8 on every one, and all four readers build
**the identical reconstruction** — the projection of §15, holding across the whole family.
So the k-tap extrapolators are not a ladder of increasingly sophisticated readers. **Each
additional slot doubles the bill and buys nothing at all** — a geometric series of
increasingly expensive ways to say one thing. (The conjecture this was run to test said
the premium would be *k*. It is 2^(k−1) — the binomial row was never going to let a linear
guess survive. And a floating-point version of the run nearly refuted the projection
identity through pure cancellation; exact integer offsets restored it under both rounding
conventions, which is the lattice discipline earning its keep.) Refutable by: a
lattice-preserving reference whose bill is not 2^(k−1) times the invariant for some
integer *k*, or a k-tap whose reconstruction differs from the one-value reader's in exact
arithmetic.

### Levels again

And the purchase was unnecessary. A **unit-rule** register at sixty-three levels — no
adaptation, no information, just more rungs — reads median 0.0031 at 1.479 bits: the same
bill as the adaptive rule, nearly the same median, worst look **six times better** than the
adaptive's, and closure intact at 49 of 49. What sophistication bought, **levels buy
cheaper.**

## 11. The exchange rate, quoted four times

Chase that comparison through every way of making it fair, and a pattern appears that is
the honest headline of this paper. The adaptive rule's advantage over the unit rule, quoted
with progressively stricter fairness:

| what is held fixed | adaptive advantage |
|---|---|
| nothing — unit rule mis-tuned, adaptive clipping freely | ≈ 5× |
| matched guarantee, quoted in fidelity | 2.34× |
| matched bill, adaptive tuned for fidelity | 1.15× |
| matched bill, adaptive tuned for rate | 1.18× |
| released from its guarantee entirely | 2.52×, uncertified |

> **The advantage halves every time the comparison is made fairer.** Five-fold when the
> unit rule is handicapped; barely a fifth above parity when both pay the same bill and keep
> their certificates; large again only when the tailored rule is excused from certifying
> anything.

That last row deserves its own sentence: 2.52× is real, and it is **for sale only in the
currency of the guarantee.** A reader that needs its worst case certified cannot spend it.

## 12. Foresight, priced

The strongest contender is the unbeatable-sounding one: **an oracle.** Hand a rule the
scene's entire brightness schedule in advance — perfect information, the thing §7 said
every tailored rule is an approximation of — and let it pick its step at every look.

The oracle reads median 0.00023 against the unit rule's 0.0064 at the same width: **28× better,
rail-free, closing 49 of 49.** For one turn of the arc, that number looked like the end of
the unit rule.

Then the ablations, and they dismantle it term by term. Feed the oracle **another scene's
schedule**: 21×. **Shuffle its own schedule** so the foresight is destroyed but the
distribution kept: 24×. Replace the schedule with **a constant step at its finest rung** —
no knowledge of any kind, just a fine ladder willing to clip: **22.7×.** So about **four
fifths of the oracle's advantage is not foresight at all.** It is a finer rung, available
to anyone willing to rail.

And the residue is purchasable. A unit-rule register at 1,921 levels — knowing nothing —
**matches the oracle's median** (0.000228 against 0.000208), **beats its worst case 28×**,
closes 49 of 49, at the same bill (4.46 bits against the oracle's 4.33). The oracle's
entire performance, bought with levels.

What foresight actually costs is shown by breaking it slightly. Take a schedule learned
perfectly on one pass and replay it **offset by a single sample**: it clips 536 times with
a worst look of 0.408 — some two thousand times the oracle's — while the **median does not
move.** The failure is silent in the channel a reader watches. Only the clip counter sees
it. **Information about the signal is exactly as good as it is true (§7), and when it goes
false, it goes false in the channel nobody is looking at.**

## 13. The certificate

One more thing separates the unit rule from its contenders, and it is not a performance number.

For the adaptive family there exists a worst-case bound in closed form — a formula the
register can evaluate in constant time from quantities it already holds, so the instrument
**carries its own certificate** rather than depending on a fact someone else established.
The bound was validated the hard way: exact on adversarial signals, falsified once on the
test library, repaired by restoring a dropped term, then held at zero violations across
thousands of configurations, densities and widths, with both of its branches attained in
the regimes they were built for.

Two facts from that formula matter here. **The unit rule is its degenerate case** — fix
the step and the whole expression collapses to half a rung, which is the unit rule's entire
error story told in one term. And the adaptation's growth parameter is **not a tuning
knob but the term that decides whether a bound exists at all**: at growth exactly 1 the
formula diverges, and the measured register follows it — fifty-nine consecutive clips and
an error twelve times the bounded setting's. A tailored rule does not merely risk more; at
the wrong parameter it is **uncertifiable**, and no channel it reports routinely will say
so.

## 14. Hard to beat, stated precisely

§7 promised the claim would be made precise, and the tests above are its content.

**At a matched bill, with certificates kept, the honest advantage of sophistication over
the unit rule is about 1.2×.** Every larger number in this paper decomposed under audit
into one of three things: **levels in disguise** (four fifths of the oracle's 28×), **a
handicapped baseline** (the 5×), or **a surrendered certificate** (the 2.52×). And levels
are purchasable by the unit rule itself, which converts every extra bit into a halving of
error, on a frontier with no knee, knowing nothing.

None of this says the unit rule is best. §7's tailored rules genuinely win in the
situations they were told about, and the oracle's residual fifth is real. What the tests
say is narrower and harder:

> **Beating the unit rule costs information about the signal, and the price of that
> information being wrong is paid in the channels a reader does not routinely watch — the
> worst look, the clip count, the certificate. The unit rule is what remains when nothing
> has been paid, and the distance from there to the frontier is smaller than every
> advertised number in this paper.**

## 14a. Finding a landmark, where the advantage is not 1.2×

The races above all asked one question — how closely does the held run track the signal? —
and answered it in error per look. There is a second question a reader can ask of a
broadcast, and the rules separate on it far more sharply. It is the question §4's landmark
state was specified for and §1 named as the paper's second half, and this section is where
that state is raced, priced and built. What follows is not a further experiment appended to
the races; it is what the races were clearing the ground for.

**The question is: where in the stream did something happen?** Not what the level was, but
where the signal turned. This paper's whole vocabulary is built for it. §4 named the frame
where the arrival equals the hold — **the corner**, the one statement that costs no number,
because noticing *no change* requires no scale. Track the side the ratio falls on and the
record carries a sign: rungs above the corner while the signal climbs, rungs below it while
it falls. **A turn in the signal is the sign of the record changing.** The screen does not
need to know the scale, the rate, or where the sweep began; it needs to notice that the
arrival stopped exceeding the hold and started falling short of it.

That noticing costs almost nothing to build: a held value, a bit for the sign of the last
comparison, a counter, and the count that the previous run reached. Four registers, one
comparison per arrival, no arrivals kept.

### Why the landmark matters more than the level

A landmark is worth more than a level because it is **an event in the world rather than a
reading of it**. A level is only meaningful in the reader's own held terms; a turn is a fact
about the signal, and two readers with different steps, different gains and different
histories will agree that it happened. Everything a reader can build without a unit is built
on such agreements. A count of arrivals between two turns is a duration in the signal's own
terms. The turn's position within that count locates the fulcrum — the arrival where the
relation stands at one — and that is the reference the reader was never handed. **A reader
that can find its landmarks can found its own unit; a reader that can only track levels
cannot.**

So the interesting race is not *whose held run is closest* but *whose held run turns where
the signal turned*.

### The race, run in the gaps

The contenders are §7's, and this is where its distinction between the reference and the
carry does its work. Between arrivals the unit rule holds the value it was filled with. A
rule told the signal is smooth carries the last increment forward. A rule told the signal is
noisy carries a decayed blend. With an arrival every frame the three barely differ — the
carry never operates, and a first version of this test measured nothing because of it. The
rules come apart only across a gap, so the gaps must be real: frames dropped, and frames
missing in bursts, which is what a stalled or lossy channel actually delivers.

Same four registers, same landmark logic, same signal; only the carry differs. The measured
error in the recovered fulcrum:

| frames dropped | unit rule | carry the trend | decayed blend |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | 1.61% | 1.61% | 2.44% |
| one in five | 2.08% | 2.01% | 2.82% |
| half | **3.01%** | 60.58% | 4.31% |
| four in five | **6.55%** | 27.28% | 8.91% |

And in bursts, which is the harder and more realistic loss:

| burst length | unit rule | carry the trend | decayed blend |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 frames | **1.72%** | 23.41% | 2.56% |
| 80 frames | **1.67%** | 22.61% | 2.35% |
| 200 frames | **1.66%** | 11.24% | 2.51% |

**The unit rule is flat across burst length.** A stall of two hundred frames costs it what a
stall of twenty costs it, which is nothing measurable. The trend rule is seven to fourteen
times worse at every length.

### When the world will not supply a circle

The machine above assumes the broadcast is an extent going round — a rod, a swept bar,
something whose magnitude rises and falls because a single thing is turning. That assumption
should be tested against the world rather than granted, and when it is, it fails almost
everywhere.

Six real signals were put through the gates: four handheld video clips, a percussive chirp, a
ride cymbal, a fire alarm and a voice. **All six were refused, and every refusal had a cause
that can be named.** The video never returns — two of the clips wander without leaving their
own neighbourhood, held flat by automatic exposure, which is a chain destroying the reading on
purpose, and is the audio-and-optics twin of the AGC this paper's races would also refuse. The
chirp returns only after it stops sweeping. The cymbal, alarm and voice all return strongly —
and are refused anyway, because each is many circles at once: a ride's partials sit at 2611,
4700, 522 and 1567 Hz together, and a voice's fundamental carries formants at nearly equal
strength. **Recurrence is ordinary in the world; a single circle is close to an artificial
object.** The rod of §14a is one because it would be built to be.

That is a real limit on the extent reading, and it has an escape that costs one subtraction.
If the reader forms not the magnitude but **the magnitude divided by its own increment**, the
size of the thing cancels — for anything locally sinusoidal the increment is the quadrature
partner, so the quotient is the tangent of the phase and the figure has gone. The reader is
then holding a ratio it manufactured from a list it was given, using the hold it already
maintains.

Measured, this recovers a fulcrum and an exact projective frame from every stream tried,
including all four video clips that the extent reading refused: cross ratios exact to between
one part in 10¹⁰ and 10¹⁵. But be exact about what the quotient cancels, because it is less
than it appears: writing the list as f·sin, the two-component ratio is f·sin over f·cos and the
figure cancels outright, while the single-list quotient is f·sin over (f′·sin + f·cos) and the
figure remains. **Dividing a list by its own increment cancels the gain, not the shape.**
Measured on four figures swept identically, two components return the same number for every one
— a spread of 0.0000 percent — and one list returns four different numbers, a spread of 87.6
percent. The escape works where the list is close to a single mode and degrades in proportion
to how far it is not. One point of mechanism changes with the reading, and it is worth
recording because it moves the count: in the extent reading the fulcrum is the **median** of a
leg, while in the ratio reading it is the **upper quartile** — a turn advancing evenly puts
the tangent below one exactly three-quarters of the time. Home is the median there, and the
fulcrum is at the three-quarter count.

**What this does not establish**, and the distinction matters more than the result: the frame
so obtained is a frame over the *arrivals*, not a certificate about a world. White noise
yields a fulcrum and an exact cross ratio as cleanly as a tone does. The fulcrum recovered from
a single list is invariant when the whole list is rescaled — correct, since with one channel
there is no second scale to find — but its value tracks how fast the list moves against
itself, so it may yet prove to be a rate measure in a geometry's clothing. Whether it is
useful is an open question this paper does not close.

### What the other rules' corners mark

The corner is not the unit rule's private possession. Every rule has one, because every rule
holds something, and every hold admits the frame where the arrival matches it exactly. What
differs is **what that frame is about**, and it is set entirely by what the hold was.

Under the unit rule the hold is the last arrival, so the corner says *the signal did not
change*. Under a rule carrying the trend the hold is the last arrival plus its increment, so
the corner says *my forecast was right*. Under a decayed blend it says *the arrival met the
blend*. The seat is the same in all three; the tenant is not. **Only the first is a statement
about the signal. The other two are statements about the reader.**

That difference is visible in where the corners land. Taking the corner properly — the centre
rung of the register, an arrival within half a step of the hold — and reading a rotating rod
at a realistic rate, the shares of corners falling near a turn against those falling on a
flank come out at 31.5% against 13.4% for the unit rule, and 21.2% against 41.5% for the
trend rule. **The unit rule's corners cluster at the turns; the trend rule's cluster on the
flanks** — the stretches where the signal runs locally straight and a forecast comes true —
and it falls silent at exactly the frames a landmark hunter needs. Its corner is not a weaker
landmark but an inverted one: a mark on the absence of events. The decayed blend fires
rarely and cannot separate a turn from a stall.

So there is a general shape to this, and §7's remark that the rule and the shape share their
name turns out to be the special case of it:

> **Every rule is quiet on the shape it believes in, and its corner is the landmark of that
> shape.** The unit rule believes in stillness, so its corner reports the world holding
> still. The trend rule believes in constant motion, so its corner reports its own forecast
> coming true. Stillness is a property of the signal; a forecast's accuracy is a property of
> the reader — which is the whole reason only one of these corners points outward.

And the same law names the unit rule's own blind spot, which honesty requires stating in the
same breath. **A signal advancing exactly one rung per look is as quiet to the unit rule as a
still one** — its record is a run of identical symbols either way. The unit rule is not quiet
only on stillness; it is quiet on anything moving at the pace of its own ladder. Measured, a
geometric ramp is 100% corners under every rule tested, the unit rule included. Each rule is
blind to the shape it is built out of, and the unit rule's ladder is built out of a fixed
ratio.

### The machine, and what it costs

The section has claimed the landmark is cheap to find. Here is the machine that finds it,
built and measured, because a claim of cheapness that never counts the parts is not a claim.

The signal is a rod turning steadily in a plane, its projected extent read once per frame —
the broadcast rises from nothing, reaches its full length, falls back to nothing, and repeats.
(A rod is one convenient way to arrange this; what the reading needs, as the passage above
records, is any single centre of gravity in steady periodic motion, whatever shape surrounds
it.)
Under the unit rule the arrivals reverse direction at every one of those events, so the record
supplies its own punctuation. A leg is the stretch between two of the *low* reversals, and the
fulcrum sits one quarter of the way along it.

Four registers and a comparison do the whole job: the held value, the direction bit, a counter
running from the last reversal, and the count that the previous leg reached.

And note what the machine never does. It does not form the ratio and test whether it equals
one. **Equality is not computed here; it is crossed.** The reader is already comparing each
arrival against its hold, and the corner is where the outcome of that comparison changes
sign — so the turns fall out of the reading the reader was performing anyway, which is why
the whole apparatus costs counters rather than arithmetic. Locating the fulcrum from them
would seem to want a division — the fulcrum is a quarter of the way along the leg, and
dividing is a luxury a minimal reader should not be assumed to have.

**It does not need one. A counter that ticks on every fourth arrival holds the count quartered,
for nothing.** Two toggling bits do what a divider would, which is what a ripple counter has
always been: bit *k* of one is the count over 2ᵏ, available as a wire rather than an operation.
So the reader's whole per-arrival cost is one comparison, one increment, and one two-bit
toggle. No division, no remainder, no arithmetic on the values at all beyond the comparison
that was already there.

**And one rule about which landmark to count from, which cost three failed designs to learn.**
The reversals come in two kinds, and they are not equally good. A reversal at the top happens
where the signal is flat, so a threshold-based detector notices it late; a reversal at the
bottom happens where the signal is steepest, so the same detector notices it promptly.
Measured on a clean signal with the events at known positions: **the top is detected 65
arrivals late, the bottom 11** — a sixfold difference, and the reason is nothing but the slope
at each. So counts are anchored on the low reversals only. The top may be used to establish
what kind of sweep was made; it must never be used to start a count.

Measured, on a clean signal, the machine returns the fulcrum to **1.9%** — and with its known
detection lag removed, to **0.2%**. That decomposition is the useful part: the counting, the
quartering and the landmark logic contribute nothing to the error. **All of it is the lateness
of the threshold**, the price of declaring a reversal only once the reading has moved clear of
it. On noisier streams the figure is 1.9% at four parts in a thousand of noise and 3.1% at one
percent, with the scheme collapsing at two percent, where the threshold grows until it swallows
the landmark it was meant to find.

The reader can even estimate its own lateness — it is roughly the threshold divided by the
slope just after the reversal, and it holds both quantities already. Enabled, this improves a
steady stream (1.9% to 1.4%, and 3.1% to 1.6%) and **makes every unsteady one worse**, because
the correction inherits the noise of the thing it corrects. It is reported here as a fixed
option rather than a default, since the honest version of a self-correction is one that says
when not to use it.

None of these numbers is impressive against a reader that keeps its arrivals and sorts them,
which locates the same fulcrum to a twentieth of a percent. That is the trade this paper has
made everywhere: **the four-register machine is not more accurate, it is more possible** — it
runs on an unbounded stream, forever, on a stream that never ends and a memory that never
grows, and it reports the fulcrum once per leg rather than once per record. What it loses in
precision it takes back in the only currency the reader of §1 actually has.

### Why the tailored rule fails here, and fails structurally

The reason is not economy, and it is not that the trend rule was given bad information. It
is that **a carry which extrapolates is most wrong exactly at a turn**, because a turn is the
one place where *it continued as it was* is false by construction. A gap that straddles the
top is a gap in which the trend rule confidently manufactures a continuation the signal did
not make — inventing a landmark that is not there, and misplacing the one that is. Its
information is not merely unhelpful at the event; it is false at the event, and nowhere else
so badly.

The unit rule's carry says only **that it has not been told otherwise**. Across a gap that
assertion grows wrong in *magnitude* — the held level ages — but it is never wrong about
*direction*, because it asserts no direction. And direction is the whole of what a landmark
needs. The one rule that claims nothing about the signal is the one rule that cannot be
wrong about the signal's turns.

> **At tracking a level, the honest advantage of sophistication is about 1.2× (§14). At
> finding a landmark, the unit rule wins by seven to fourteen times, and wins structurally:
> the information that buys a tailored rule its advantage between events is precisely the
> information that is false at them.**

This also sharpens §7's remark that the rule and the shape share their name. The unit circle
is quiet under this rule because every look lands on the corner. A signal that turns is a
signal that *visits* the corner, and the rule that goes silent on a circle is the rule that
hears the visit most clearly — the same stillness, read once as silence and once as a
landmark.

## 15. What the record reconstructs, and why it does not drift

The record is a run of symbols; the display is a run of held levels. What is the held run,
as a reconstruction of the broadcast?

A value carried forward and repeatedly corrected sounds like a thing that drifts — every
rounding an error, every error compounding, the display slowly walking away from the
signal. **It does not, and the reason is structural.** The held level is the reconstruction
rather than the truth, so each look's rounding is handed the *accumulated* discrepancy and
rounds it away with everything else. Measured across fifty-six recorded scenes: the error
in the first tenth of a run against the last tenth differs by a factor of 1.03 — and 1.1
even with five percent noise added to every sample. The end of a long run is as good as its
beginning.

The deeper form is better. **Every level the unit rule ever holds is a rung of the ladder
anchored at the founding sample** — the hold moves only by whole steps, so it can never
leave the lattice it started on. And rounding from any rung lands on the rung nearest the
arrival, wherever the hold was standing. **So the reconstruction is the nearest-rung
projection of the signal, with no dependence on the path at all** — verified in exact
integer arithmetic, every sample of every scene. The recursion *looks* like a loop with
memory, compounding as loops do. It is not: it is a projection wearing a loop's clothes.

One distinction keeps this honest. The unit rule's assertion is progressively wrong
**within a gap** — the longer since the last sample, the staler the held level. It is not
progressively wrong **across a run**: the error at the end is the error at the beginning.
Staleness between looks, and no decay across them.

## 16. Continuity, from the inside

The unit rule fills every gap, so at no point does the screen hold nothing — and the viewer
experiences that as continuity: an unbroken picture, from a broadcast that was only ever
samples.

It is worth stopping on what kind of achievement that is. Continuous *reception* is not
difficult; it is impossible — a reader takes one sample at a time or it is not reading. And
yet continuity is recovered, by the crudest available means, and it improves without limit:
the faster the frames, the closer the held picture lies to the signal it was cut from.
**What was proved impossible is not thereby restored. It is approached** — the same
standing the relation space has to its own far end, appearing one level up. **The
impossible thing is not the continuity. It is the occupying.**

The continuity is in the record, not in the reception. The screen never held the levels
between its frames; it asserts them — asserts, per §7, only that it has not yet been told
otherwise, which is the one assertion that costs nothing.

And then the reading turns over, and this is the finding rather than the recovery:

> **It is not that holding the last frame is why the picture is continuous. The signal does
> not need continuity; it is simply a signal. What needs continuity is the reader.** A
> thing that holds one value can go on holding something only if the next reference is the
> last arrival — and that condition is what continuity *is*, from the inside.
>
> **Continuity is not a property the world has and hands to the reader. It is the shape of
> the only door a one-value reader can come through.**

One scoping, so the finding is not read wider than it is. What is forced is **that there be
a rule.** A serial reader holds something between arrivals, and whatever settles what it
holds is a rule whether or not anybody has named it — even the strobe's *hold nothing* is
one, and it is the member of the space that yields no continuity at all. What is not forced
is **which rule.** The unit rule is the simplest member of that space, and the only member
a reader given nothing can run; §6 establishes the second and §7 the first, and neither is
a claim that it is *the* rule. Other readers, with a number or a slot or a history to
spend, come through other doors. Whether different situations call for different rules, or
whether a long-lived reader ends up running something it learned, is a question this paper
does not open and does not need to.

So the finding generalises past its own instance. **The continuity is the carry rule,
whatever the carry rule is** — and this one is what a reader is holding before it has paid
for anything else.

## 17. The checksums of the returning scene

§9 scored closure without unpacking it. Now the unpacking, because the returning scene is
where the record is interrogated hardest.

Suppose the broadcast ends as it began — the scene returns. An honest record should be able
to say so, and it has **two independent things to check**:

**The reading returns.** Sum the symbols. Every departure the record wrote on the way out
must be answered somewhere by a return; the sum telescopes; if the run closed, the total is
exactly zero — not nearly zero, zero, because a telescoping sum keeps only its two ends and
here they coincide on the lattice. This check is **order-blind**: it would pass with the
symbols in any order, because a sum forgets sequence.

**The landmarks return.** Separately, read the record's *shape*: the sequence of its
crossings — corners passed, approaches toward home, clips at the rail. A returning scene
must bring the landmark sequence back around to the standing it began from. This check is
**order-bearing**: it is exactly the sequence the sum forgot.

Neither implies the other, and that is what makes them worth holding together — a record
can pass one and fail the other, and each failure means something different.

And their strength must be stated honestly, because it is asymmetric and the step prices
it. A scene that ends **almost** where it began — within half a rung of its start — rounds
to *returned*, and the record closes over a world that did not: the coarser the step, the
larger the class of near-returns the reader cannot tell from returns. The finer the step,
the more firmly a false closure is refused. So:

> **An open record proves the scene did not return. A closed record does not prove it did —
> and how much it can refute is priced by the step.** The checksum is a refutation
> instrument, and refutation is what there is.

## 18. What the screen was able to discover

§1 asked what a bounded thing could truthfully display of an unbounded stream it was told
nothing about, and the paper can now answer in one paragraph.

It can display **which statements are the signal's rather than the chain's** (§3). It can
give every frame **an address in the relation space** — a share or a tally, home, the
fulcrum, the clip (§4). It can choose a step and keep **a record whose cost buys error at a
fixed exchange rate, with no knee** (§5, §9). It can span its own gaps with the one rule
that assumes nothing and **reconstruct the signal as a projection that does not drift**
(§7, §15). It can experience **continuity** — because continuity was never the signal's to
give, only the reader's way of coming through (§16). It can find **where the signal turned**,
and so found a reference it was never handed, on four registers, a comparison and two toggling
bits — no division anywhere (§14a). And it can interrogate a returning
scene with **two independent checksums** whose refusals are proofs and whose passes are
priced (§17).

All of it from five faculties, four registers, and a rule with nothing in it — raced against
every contender that was supposed to beat it, and beaten only by readers that paid in
levels, in information, or in certificates (§10–§14). And on the one question where the
contenders were best placed to win — locating an event across a gap they were built to span —
beaten by none of them, by a factor of seven to fourteen (§14a).

And the five capabilities of §4 are that answer's spine, because each one names its own
price. Reception costs nothing; relation costs a hold; representation costs a step;
continuity costs a carry rule; landmarks cost a direction and two counters. **What rises
across that list is memory. What never appears on it is a unit** — no rung of the stack
buys its capability with access to the signal's external scale, and the reference the reader
ends up using is one it built out of the relation and then kept.

Late in this work the reader was turned on a harder case than the rod: an ordinary list of
magnitudes, of the kind most real streams are, with no circle in it at all. **The magnitudes
are what make a signal, and the magnitudes are what hide its closure** — how large things are
swamps whether anything goes round. But the rule's own comparison is a way through. Divide the
arrival by its own increment, which is a hold and a subtraction and nothing more, and the size
cancels; what is left is the turning.

It comes back exactly. For a single tone the fulcrum and the length of a cycle stand in a fixed
ratio — the samples in one cycle are the fulcrum times 2.6026 — and measured from 55 Hz to
3 kHz the period returns within a hundredth to a hundred and one hundredths of a percent.
Rescale the whole list by any factor and the answer does not shift at all, because a common
factor cancels in a ratio of a value to its own increment. **A held value, a subtraction, and
a count give the periodicity of a stream with no unit anywhere in the derivation.**

And it tells the reader something it must know before anything else: whether its own sampling
is fine enough to hold four turns apart. That comes out at 50 samples per quarter turn for a
tone, 9 for a voice, 4 for a cymbal, and 0.9 for noise — which is not a stream that fails a
test but one whose turns are finer than its looks. A fire alarm returns more strongly than any
natural signal measured here, at 0.98, and its landmarks are still beneath its sampling.
Closure and resolution are two requirements, and this reading prices the second for the cost of
one register.

If one sentence has to carry the paper, it is not that the unit rule is hard to beat. It is
this, from §14a:

> **A reader that can find its landmarks can found its own unit; a reader that can only track
> levels cannot.**

The races matter, but they measure a convenience. That sentence names a capability. The rule's
worth is not that holding the last value happens to predict well — it is that holding the last
value is what lets a bounded reader pass from a received relation, to a recognised equality, to
a local geometric reference, without ever being handed a scale and without performing a
division.

There is also a reading of the fulcrum that this paper has not needed but should record, because
it gives the reader's landmark a physical referent rather than only a relational one. Let the
arrivals be the elements of a body — one look per element, sampled evenly by material, each
reported as one component over the other from wherever the reader stands. Then the arrivals'
**location is the direction to the body's centre of gravity** (measured: a disk to 0.019
percent, a bar to 0.028, two separated lumps with no mass at all at their common centre to
0.036), and their **spread is the body's angular size**, times a constant set by how the mass
is arranged — 0.808 for a disk, 1.418 for a ring, 0.040 for a thin bar, each stable across a
factor of five in distance.

Let that body move — its centre of gravity travelling a repeating path while the reader stands
still — and the reader holds a stream it can read as it reads any other. Measured: four bodies
of quite different form, a disk, a hollow ring, a thin bar and a lump-sided disk, set on the
same orbit, return recovered periods agreeing with one another to better than two percent. **To
this reader they are one object.** And the motion itself comes through — orbits of 80, 140 and
200 looks return within half a percent to two and a half. So what a reader of this kind
actually watches is **a centre of gravity in motion**: not a shape, and not a signal in the
abstract, but where a thing is and how it is moving. Form reaches the reader only by governing
that motion, which is why a rod and a disk hung from the same pivot are distinguishable at all.

Two consequences for this paper. The first is that the reader's insistence on the median over
the mean stops being a robustness preference: **the mean is wrong here not because it is
fragile but because it estimates nothing** — the arrivals have no mean, and the median is the
estimator that answers to the geometry. The second is that the fulcrum acquires a referent. A
landmark found by counting, with no unit anywhere, turns out to name where a thing is and how
big it looks from where the reader is standing.

A last word on what such a list *is*, since calling it impoverished gets the matter backwards.
**A list of magnitudes is the figure, held whole** — the parallel reading, every value of the
shape available at once, in which figures are plainly distinguishable from one another.
Nothing is missing from it. What it cannot do is state a rate without a clock, because a
collection held all at once has no order to be traversed. The conversion is not a repair; it
is a purchase, and the parallel form is what is spent.

One further thing was learned about that periodicity, and it changes how the reader's poverty
should be described. It has been natural to say that a divided reading *loses* the shape of
what it watches — that the size cancels and the figure with it. True, but the sign is wrong.
**If the shape survived into the reading, the rate could not be recovered at all**: shape and
schedule would be one degree of freedom, and a single stream would yield neither. Measured,
with every figure swept at an identical rate and read both ways, the divided reading returns
the same period for a circle, an egg, a clover and a spiky burr — a spread across figures of
zero — while the reading that keeps the shape returns a different period for each, spread over
seventy percentage points. **The cancellation is not the price of the rate. It is what makes
the rate a fact.**

Nor is the shape lost to the reader for good, only lost to its *arrivals*. A body that moves
under its own physics has already written its form into the schedule, because a swing's period
is set by its moment of inertia about the pivot. Five bodies of one size, given nothing but a
magnitude per look, return their periods within two percent, and the ratios of those periods —
which need no unit and no clock — land within a few percent of what the mechanics requires.
The reader is blind to form and sensitive to dynamics: it cannot tell a cog from a circle when
a hand turns both, and can tell a rod from a disk when gravity does the turning.

Taken together these say what the reader has been watching, and it is not what the device of
§1 suggests. Not a signal's shape, and not an outline: **a centre of gravity, and the way it
moves.** Four bodies whose centres orbit identically — a disk, a hollow ring, a thin bar, a
disk with a lump on one side — hand the reader periods agreeing with one another to within two
percent, each within three and a half percent of the truth. The form is carried by the body
and left at the door; the motion is what arrives. And a form that governs a motion arrives with
it, which is why a rod and a disk swinging under gravity are distinguishable when a cog and a
circle turned by hand are not.

This also says what such a reader should be pointed at, which matters more than any refinement
of the rule. Not a chosen shape — the shape is the one thing that provably does not matter.
**One object in steady periodic motion, against a still background.** A pendulum, a metronome,
a wheel. Nothing about the form wrapped around that centre needs to be known or even seen.

What remains open should be stated as openly as the results. The frame the reader builds this
way is exact over any arrivals whatever — noise yields a fulcrum and a perfect cross ratio as
readily as a tone does — so its exactness is no evidence that it refers to anything. **What
knowing a stream's periodicity, and the local geometry its fulcrum carries, is finally good for
is not established by this paper.** The mechanism is closed; the use is not. Three tests would
decide it, and none has been run: whether the fulcrum tracks a change in the world or only in
the sampling, whether two readers at different rates agree once each speaks in rungs from its
own fulcrum, and whether the frame earns anything in company where a unit is available anyway.

> **The unit rule is what a reader is before it has been told anything. The rest of this
> program is what it can become — and every becoming is priced in state, not in units.**
