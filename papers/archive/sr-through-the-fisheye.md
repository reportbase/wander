## 0. Where to stand

Start where the series starts. A reader holds one term and another arrives; the reading is the arriving term in units of the held one, and there is no third thing in the room. Three arrangements are landmarks: home, where the reading is zero; the fulcrum, where the two terms stand equal and the reading is one — the one arrangement that costs no number; and the horizon, everything arriving and nothing held, a direction the reader is always going in. Below the fulcrum the arriving term is a share of what the reader holds; above it the reader's capacity is a share of what arrives, and the coordinate turns into a count of doublings. The unit changes hands at the fulcrum, once.

SR is this reader with its slots filled: the held term is the observer's own time, the arriving term is space picked up by moving. The reading is the tilt, read three ways — as the taken-on sin θ = β, as the kept cos θ = 1/γ, as the slope tan θ = γβ, the observer's own speed. The horizon is light. That much is sr.md §1.

The fisheye adds one instrument and one separation. The instrument is a lens: it takes an angle and returns a position, p = s·tan(θ − f), and it has no opinion about what is at the position. The separation is between two conversions that are easily confused — the *layout*, index to angle, fixed by the data alone; and the *lens*, angle to position, fixed by the observer alone. The horizon belongs to the second. Everything below is what those two things say when the data is the sky and the observer is moving.

## 1. The sky is the layout; the boost is the lens

An isotropic sky lays its sources evenly round the sphere. That is the first conversion — a statement about the data, made before any observer exists: this many things, spread evenly, no radius, no focus. A boost is the second conversion. It moves every direction on the sphere by tan(θ′/2) = tan(θ/2)/k, k = √((1+β)/(1−β)), and touches no source. Two observers in relative motion look at one sky through two lenses; neither ever touched the data, they only addressed it.

This is the fisheye's governing claim — *a lens is orthogonal to the data it observes* — and it is the relativity principle said from the instrument's side. The world fixes the layout and never touches the lens; the observer fixes the lens and never touches the layout. The invariance of c is the same sentence about the horizon: the horizon belongs to the lens, so every observer carries it at the same address on its own rim, whatever the data and whatever its speed. sr.md §7.1 put this as "the wall is in the chart before it is in the energy." The fisheye says which chart: the second conversion, and only it.

## 2. The sky is a Cauchy, and the boost is its scale (C1)

Read the isotropic sky stereographically — each direction's tangent of its half-angle from some axis — and the readings are distributed as the standard Cauchy: median 0, quartiles ±1, the tail at the antipode. A boost along that axis multiplies every reading by 1/k, so the boosted sky is a Cauchy of scale 1/k. Measured on a uniform sky of 200,001 directions: at rest the quartiles are ±0.99999; at half c, k = 1.732, they are ±0.57735 against 1/k = 0.57735; at nine tenths, k = 4.359, they are ±0.22941 against 0.22942. The median does not move.

Two things follow that the Cauchy paper already proved for readers in general. First, the scale of a Cauchy is the *account's*, not the relation's — the paper's ownership rule by counting — and so the speed is not a property of the sky; it is a property of the account through which the sky is read. Second, "among relations met evenly in the turn there is one Cauchy," which here reads: there is one sky, and the boosts are its accounts. The rapidity is minus the log of the Cauchy's scale, and rapidities add because scales multiply.

## 3. Which way the lens faces (C2) — a correction to §9.2.1

The forward hemisphere of the rest sky is squeezed into 60° at half c and 25.8° at nine tenths — the headlight effect — while the aft hemisphere opens out to fill the rest.

So the moving observer's *focus is aft*. The k-ladder makes the rungs concrete. ## 11. Is it just the fisheye?

The question the nine findings raise is whether the identification can be pushed all the way — whether special relativity *is* the fisheye, discovered in one domain and named after it. The answer divides cleanly, and the dividing line is the fisheye's own governing claim.

**Where it is literally the fisheye.** The Lorentz group is the Möbius group of the celestial sphere. That is a theorem, not a reading: every boost, rotation and combination of them acts on the sky as a conformal map, and every conformal map of the sky is exactly one Lorentz transformation. The fisheye's readers — zoom, which is the radius; pan, which is the focus; roll, which is the rim's turn — are the Möbius maps of a circle, and on the sphere they are the whole Lorentz group, three-transitive. So the *group* of special relativity is the group of fisheye readers of the sky, with nothing missing and nothing extra. Aberration is the reader's law (§1). The Doppler factor is the radius. Velocity addition is radii multiplying. Rapidity is the log of the radius. The Wigner rotation is the rim's turn when a pan is made between levels (§6). Time dilation and length contraction are functions of the radius, γ = (k + 1/k)/2. Every one of those is the lens and only the lens, and every one was checked above to floating precision. In this exact sense the kinematics of special relativity is not *like* a fisheye. It is one.

**Where it is not.** A lens is orthogonal to the data it observes: it takes an angle and returns a position, and it has no opinion about what is there. Three things in special relativity are opinions about what is there, and the fisheye cannot produce any of them.

*That the zoom is the observer's velocity.* The fisheye does not say what sets a reader's radius; a browser sets it by hand. Special relativity's content is that the radius is set by relative motion — that the atom's second slot is filled by the same register one act ago, the H-setting, the sign bit of sr.md §0 and §6. The same lens on the same circle with the other sign is a rotation, and nobody calls rotations special relativity. The world chose the setting, and the fisheye did not.

*Where the horizon sits in the world's units.* The fisheye's horizon is at ninety degrees from the focus — dimensionless, free, the same for every reader. Special relativity says that in metres per second the horizon is at 299,792,458. No lens supplies that number. It was read off the sky, and it could have been anything.

*The magnitudes.* The fisheye's whole theorem is that it discards magnitudes and keeps addresses. Half of special relativity is about magnitudes: mass, energy, momentum, the invariant E² − p² = m², the interval between two events rather than the angle between two directions. That half is the array behind the lens — the thing the fisheye points at and does not carry (§10). The dynamics of special relativity is not fisheye. It is precisely what the fisheye cancels.

So the defensible form of the thesis is this. **The kinematics of special relativity is a fisheye; what physics discovered was which lens the world wears, where its horizon is, and what stands behind it.** The Two Settings had this in relation-space terms — the world supplied two items, which setting and where the bracket sits — and the fisheye sharpens the first of them: the zoom, not the pan.

**And a correction to the history in the question.** Physics did not find special relativity as a lens. Lorentz and Einstein found the algebra — the transformation under which Maxwell's equations keep their form, and then the construction from clocks and light signals. That the sky is a fisheye and a boost is a zoom was recognised in 1959, by Penrose and by Terrell independently, fifty-four years after the theory, and it is still taught as a curiosity of relativistic optics rather than as what the theory is. The honest sentence is therefore not that they discovered the fisheye and called it special relativity. It is that they discovered the fisheye's algebra by other means, named it after the domain, and recognised the lens half a century late. Browsing the Fisheye §12 records the same pattern for the Cauchy: one map, found repeatedly, named each time after whatever was at hand, the lens never named as the thing the names had in common. Special relativity is that table's largest entry, and the one with the most at stake, because here what stands behind the lens is the physical world.

## 12. Did physics miss that it is an address? (C9, C10)

The Cauchy paper's central sentence is that the reading is an address, not a magnitude — it says where something sits, not how much of it there is — and that two centuries of calling the Cauchy a pathology come from filing an address where averages are the point. The natural question is whether physics made the same filing error about the same function in special relativity. The answer is in three parts, and they come out differently.

**For directions, no — and not because it was noticed.** The sky was always kept as angles. Nobody computes the mean stereographic coordinate of an isotropic sky and is puzzled that it does not exist, because nobody treats a direction as a quantity to average. §2's fact — that the stereographic reading of the sky *is* the standard Cauchy — is a theorem and not folklore, and aberration is taught as "angles change," full stop. Directions were never mistaken for magnitudes because they were never mistaken for numbers. Right by default, not by understanding.

**For speed, yes, fully, and it cost a century of puzzles.** β is where an address lands on the reader's rim — tanh of the rung count — and physics filed it as a magnitude: a speed, a quantity that ought to add. Everything that made the theory feel strange is what an address looks like when it is read as an amount. Velocities "do not add": positions on a rim do not add, counts do. The "cosmic speed limit": a ceiling on a magnitude, where the lens says it is the edge of a chart — the horizon at ninety degrees, no more a maximum speed than the rim of a picture is a maximum distance. "Infinite energy to reach c": γ = cosh of the rung running off, the tangent flying away at the horizon, priced as a dynamical wall (sr.md §7.1: dynamics prices a road topology never built). "Relativistic mass": γm treated as a magnitude that grows, and abandoned by physics itself once it became clear that it was a chart quantity wearing a mass's name. Rapidity — the count — was found in 1910, five years in, and has been carried ever since as a device for composing boosts, while β stayed the "real" speed. The fisheye reads it the other way: rapidity is the count, and β is where the count lands under the lens. That is the Cauchy's filing error exactly, made about the same function one sign over — tanh for tan — and, as with the Cauchy, the arithmetic was repaired and the framing never was.

**And there is a third place, where the Cauchy sits in physics under its own name.** The Breit–Wigner line — the Lorentzian — is the energy distribution of a resonance, (Γ/2)/((E − E_R)² + (Γ/2)²), and it is treated as a distribution of a magnitude, energy, with a known nuisance: its mean is undefined, so resonances are located by the peak or the pole rather than by moments. But the scattering phase δ runs through π across the resonance, and tan δ = (Γ/2)/(E_R − E). Push a phase spread evenly over (0, π) through that relation and the energies are Cauchy with scale Γ/2: median E_R, quartiles E_R ± Γ/2 to six places, the histogram matching the Lorentzian to seven parts in a thousand on a 200,001-point sweep. The amplitude e^{iδ} sin δ lies on the Argand circle — centre i/2, radius ½, off it by 2e−16 — and equal steps in δ are equal arcs on it. So the resonance *is* a circle, which physicists draw, and the energy axis is that circle serialised: dE/dδ = (Γ/2)(1 + r²) with r = (E_R − E)/(Γ/2), the fisheye's derivative to six places. E_R is the focus, Γ/2 is the lens's scale, the phase is the layout, and the cross section sin²δ = 1/(1 + r²) is the Cauchy density in E — the arc element seen from the energy side. The energy is the address into the phase. Physics has every piece of this and states the reverse: the energy is the physical quantity and the phase is derived from it.

The Unit Circle §21 measured the address statement in the corpus's own numbers before this record was written, and it belongs here. Equal arcs of the circle carry equal mass, and a five-degree arc unfolds through the tangent to intervals of wildly different length: 0–5° to 0.0875, 40–45° to 0.1609, 70–75° to 0.9846, 84–89° to 47.78 — every one carrying mass 0.0556. *The tail is not extra mass; it is the same mass stretched.* That is what "the infinity belongs to the chart" means when it is weighed. The same section found that the Cauchy is in the *departures* — two unbiased components read against each other — and that a shape leaves no Cauchy footprint: a heavy tail is what the absence of structure looks like. For the sky that reads: isotropy *is* the Cauchy, any structure in the sky is a departure from it, and the boost is the one map that keeps it Cauchy while moving everything — which is why the boost is a lens and a galactic plane is data.

**A lead, tested and killed.** If Γ/2 is the lens's scale and the lens's scale is a reader's radius, then a resonance's lifetime would be a radius, and the fisheye's composition law — readers compose by multiplying radii — might say something about resonances that overlap or broaden one another. A lens of a lens does multiply — k₁ = 3 read at k₂ = 4 puts tan δ at exactly 12 tan δ — but that is one resonance read at a different unit, not two resonances. Broadening is a Cauchy sum, not a nested reader. What survives the kill is the address statement and nothing more: E_R is the focus, Γ/2 the scale, the energy an address into the phase; the lifetime is a scale, and a scale is not a radius until something reads through it.

So: physics never made the mistake about directions, made it fully about speed and repaired the arithmetic without the framing, and still makes it about resonances. One thing did not survive the check, and it is the one to keep. Reader-geometry called the family joining two readers, R(t) = (B∘A⁻¹)^t∘A, "the natural animation" and "unique." It is the unique one-parameter *subgroup* through them, but it is not the geodesic. With the rim's turn held at zero at both ends the family runs a hypercycle — distance from A at t = ¼, ½, ¾ is 0.4581, 0.9113, 1.3561 against the geodesic's 0.4479, 0.8959, 1.3438 — and reaches B only at the end (V2). The geodesic — constant acceleration in one direction, the physically distinguished path — requires the rim to turn along the way by an angle that equals the pan when A is the identity, 0.3500 rad here, and is a Wigner rotation in general. So the fisheye's smooth zoom-and-pan between two views is *not* the constant-acceleration path between two velocities; it is that path with the frame allowed to rotate, and the difference between them is exactly the Thomas precession, seen on a screen. The animation is still natural — it is what the group does — but "unique" was too strong: there are two natural paths, the subgroup's and the geodesic's, and they differ by the rim's turn.

## 14. The fourth star, with the real laws (F)

§9 ran the audit with an invented deflection law. It has now been run with the real ones, on real positions. Eight bright stars at J2000 (Betelgeuse, Rigel, Aldebaran, Sirius, Procyon, Capella, Pollux, Regulus), the Sun at ecliptic longitude 84.3° (mid-June, 15–17° from Aldebaran and Betelgeuse), Earth's orbital speed 29.78 km/s toward its apex (β = 9.93×10⁻⁵, k − 1 = 9.93×10⁻⁵), and the Sun's light deflection for an observer at one astronomical unit — (2GM/c²d)·cot(ψ/2), a constant of 4.07 milliarcseconds, the formula Gaia's own reduction uses.

Aberration moves every star by about twenty arcseconds: 20.43″ for Betelgeuse, 19.78″ for Sirius, 8.49″ for Regulus near the apex's pole. The Sun's deflection moves them by milliarcseconds: 27.8 mas for Betelgeuse at 16.6° elongation, 10.7 mas for Sirius at 43.5°, 6.9 mas for Regulus at 65.5°. A thousand times less. Now the audit. On three quadruples of stars, the complex cross ratio changes under aberration by 1.3×10⁻¹⁵, 5.8×10⁻¹⁶ and 5.0×10⁻¹⁶ — floating noise — and under the deflection by 6.3×10⁻⁷, 3.2×10⁻⁸ and 1.7×10⁻⁷. **The audit is blind to the lens and sees the bend**: the map that moves the sky by twenty arcseconds leaves every cross ratio fixed because it is a Möbius map, and the map that moves it by twenty milliarcseconds fires the audit because it is not. A boost and a gravitational field are told apart by the fourth star, at a level a thousand times above the displacement of any single one.

Two honesties. The aberration half is a theorem checking its own arithmetic — a Möbius map preserves cross ratios by construction — so its 10⁻¹⁵ is a check on the code, not on nature. The deflection half is the claim, and its test against nature is the 1919 arrangement: the same quadruple measured with the Sun near and far, and the cross ratio compared. At Gaia's precision — tens of microarcseconds per star — the effect above is several hundred error bars; the observatory in fact applies exactly this deflection correction to every star it measures, so the audit's raw material exists. What the fisheye adds is only the sentence that says what the correction *is*: the fourth star catching a chart that is not a lens.
