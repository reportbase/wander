**Upcoming measurements that can settle (or sharply constrain) the D6 questions**

The open issues are the mild \(\Omega_m\) tension (geometric \(1/\pi \approx 31.83\%\) vs DESI BAO \(\approx 29.8\)–\(30.5\%\)), the \(\sim 3\sigma\) preference for dynamical dark energy (\(w(z) \neq -1\)), and whether any evolution is consistent with the framework’s \(l=2\) back-reaction mechanism. Several Stage-IV probes will deliver decisive data in the next 1–3 years.

### 1. DESI full five-year results (primary near-term lever)
- The original five-year survey finished ahead of schedule in early 2026 with >47 million galaxies and quasars (far exceeding the design goal).
- First dark-energy analyses of the complete dataset are expected in **2027**.
- DESI continues observing through 2028 (extended coverage + deeper samples).
- Impact: Will roughly double the current BAO constraining power. If the present preference for \(w_0 > -1\), \(w_a < 0\) persists, it should reach or exceed \(5\sigma\) significance when combined with CMB + SNe. It will also tighten \(\Omega_m\) and the expansion history at the redshifts most relevant to \(z_{\rm eq}\) and \(z_{\rm acc}\).

### 2. Euclid
- **DR1 Foundation** (raw + calibrated images, catalogues, spectra over ~1900 deg²): **November 2026**.
- Complete DR1 (higher-level products for galaxy clustering and weak lensing): **mid-2027**.
- Later releases: DR2 (2029), DR3 (2031).
- Impact: Independent geometric (clustering) and growth (weak lensing) constraints on dark energy, largely orthogonal to DESI’s spectroscopic BAO. Will test whether the dynamical preference is probe-dependent and will provide a clean measurement of structure growth that can confront the \(l=2\) back-reaction idea.

### 3. Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope
- Launched **30 August 2026**.
- First images expected early 2027; core cosmological surveys begin shortly thereafter.
- Science: Wide-field infrared BAO, weak lensing, and especially a large sample of Type Ia supernovae to higher redshifts than current surveys.
- Impact: Extends the supernova Hubble diagram and provides an independent expansion-history probe. Particularly useful for testing whether any \(w(z)\) evolution continues or saturates at higher \(z\).

### 4. Vera C. Rubin Observatory (LSST)
- Legacy Survey of Space and Time officially began **June/July 2026**.
- 10-year survey of the southern sky; early data already flowing.
- Significant cosmology results (supernovae, weak lensing, clustering) expected from Year 1–2 data (**2027–2028** onward).
- Impact: Enormous low-to-intermediate redshift supernova samples and growth-of-structure measurements. Complements DESI (spectroscopic) and Euclid/Roman (space-based) with a deep, wide, time-domain optical survey. Especially powerful for testing late-time \(w(z)\) behaviour and growth rate \(f\sigma_8\).

### How these can settle the specific D6 questions

| Question | Most decisive upcoming data | Expected timeline | What would constitute a decisive test |
|----------|-----------------------------|-------------------|---------------------------------------|
| \(\Omega_m = 1/\pi\)? | DESI full 5-yr BAO + Euclid clustering | 2027 | Precision \(\Omega_m\) at the ~0.5–1% level; tension either disappears or becomes highly significant |
| Exact monopole \(w=-1\) vs dynamical? | DESI full + Euclid + early Roman/Rubin SNe + growth | 2027–2028 | Preference for \(w_0w_a\) either exceeds \(5\sigma\) consistently across probes or falls below \(2\sigma\) |
| \(l=2\) back-reaction as the source of any \(w(z)\) evolution? | Combined growth measurements (\(f\sigma_8\), weak lensing) from Euclid, Roman, Rubin + DESI | 2027–2029 | Whether the amplitude and redshift dependence of any detected \(w(z)\) match a structure-formation-driven quadrupole correction (still requires the explicit M2 calculation) |
| Flatness / overall consistency | All of the above jointly | 2027–2028 | No change expected; already excellent |

**Bottom line.**  
The single most important near-term dataset is the **DESI full five-year dark-energy analysis expected in 2027**. When combined with Euclid DR1 (mid-2027) and the first cosmology results from Roman and Rubin, it should either confirm dynamical dark energy at high significance or resolve the current mild tensions. The framework’s geometric \(\Omega_m\) prediction and its \(l=2\) resolution mechanism will both be quantitatively testable by the end of the decade.
