# The Cauchy

### The circle, as it arrives

*Working paper, 22 August 2026. Companion to* The Relation Space *and* The Unit Rule. *Title provisional.*

---

There is a distribution with a bad reputation: no mean, a tail so heavy the average of a
million samples is no steadier than one, and an exemption from the theorem that tames
everything else. This paper's claim is that every item on the charge sheet is one fact,
seen from inside — and that the distribution is not a pathology but a delivery: **the
Cauchy is what the circle looks like when it can only arrive one reading at a time.**

In plainer words, and as a signpost rather than a claim to be defended here: **the Gaussian
is the study of *N*, and the Cauchy is the study of *n*.** The quantities that make the
Gaussian useful — a mean, a variance — are statements about a completed collection, and each
new member revises them. The Cauchy has no such quantities, and the sample mean of any number
of draws is distributed exactly like a single draw, so that gathering more of it improves
nothing. That is usually read as pathology. Read the other way it is the honest signature of
a situation in which the collection was never available: not a description of a set that has
finished arriving, but of one arrival at a time. The rest of this paper is that sentence,
worked out.

## 1. What a reader can state

A reader with no outside unit states ratios — the arrival against the hold, one number per
look. The full standing of the two — both components, the resolved pair on its quarter
circle — is never what arrives; what arrives is the quotient. So there are two versions of
the same object in play from the start. **The circle is the drawn version: the set of all
ways the two can stand, bounded, both components in hand, no horizon anywhere on it. The
ratio is what arrives: unbounded, one horizon at each end of every passage, serial.** The
question of this paper is what happens to a resolved object when it is serialised for a
reader that cannot receive it whole.

## 2. One object, read twice

The answer is measured, not argued. Sample uniformly along the circle's arc — the resolved
object, taken evenly in its own length — and push each point through the quotient. The
result is the Cauchy, to the precision of two million samples: the share below the fulcrum
comes out 0.50004 against the Cauchy's 0.50000, and the tail beyond ten comes out 0.06359
against 0.06345. Nothing was added and nothing was modelled. **The Cauchy is the arc, read
in the ratio.**

And now the charge sheet reads differently, item by item. The circle has no horizon and no
pathology. The ratio has one horizon at each end — and has them for the same reason it is
the only thing a scale-free reader can state. So the heavy tail is not a defect of a
distribution; **it is the horizon, appearing in the one coordinate that has one.** The
missing mean, the wild averages, the exemption from the concentration everyone else enjoys
— those are the price of serialising a resolved object for a reader that cannot be handed
the shape whole. Not repairable by choosing a better distribution, because the distribution
was never the thing being paid for. The delivery was.

And one exactness about the drawn side, from the oldest paper in this line, so that
*circle* is never said loosely here. What the reading owns is an ordered run — home at
one end, the horizon at the other, nothing joining them. Closing that run into a circle
means identifying its two ends, and for this reading the identification has nothing to
stand on: the ends are different *kinds* — an arrangement against a place never
reached — and no rescaling carries one to the other, checked across eighteen decades of
scale. The identification is available only for signed readings, where the two unbounded
ends meet as one point. **For this reading the circle is virtual: a closure the page
performs, not a topology the reading has.** Even the shape this paper is about belongs,
as a closed thing, to the outside account; what the reading owns of it is the open run —
which is the drawn-against-arriving split of this section, stated at its sharpest.

## 3. The swap, and whose measure this is

The pair's equation is symmetric: exchange the two and it is unchanged. The ratio is not:
exchange them and one reading becomes a different reading, its reciprocal. And the
difference in kind matters more than the difference in value. In the pair, the symmetry is
an **identity** — nothing is performed; there is nothing a reader could do or fail to do.
In the ratio it is an **involution** — a map that must be applied, and the reader cannot
apply it, because a quotient has a numerator and a denominator and the reader occupies one
seat.

Yet the measure is invariant under exactly that exchange — and this is the fact worth
being exact about, because it is measured: the *weights* match under the swap at every
reading while the *values* never do. An invariance of the measure, sitting on a coordinate
with no symmetry of its own. The resolution: **the invariance is inherited.** The Cauchy is
the image of the symmetric object, and the symmetry survives serialisation as the only
trace a horizon-bearing coordinate can carry of a horizon-free one. The measure is
invariant under a swap the reader cannot perform.

Which settles whose measure this is, and commits to something checkable: the reading is one
seat's account, so the reader states the ratio and never its reciprocal — it never occupies
the other seat. A record containing the swap-image of a legitimate reading would refute
this section.

## 4. Where the measure comes from

Every section so far has used *the measure* without deriving it. The derivation is two
steps, and both belong to the resolved object rather than to the reader.

**On the circle, the unbiased weight is uniform — and not by modelling taste.** The pair
has no unit, no privileged point, and no orientation anyone can read. A non-uniform weight
would single out a place, and there is no place to single out: any re-facing of the pair
is a symmetry of its situation, so the weight must survive every re-facing, and only the
uniform one does. Equal cells of the arc are a theorem of the reader's poverty, not a
tuning of anyone's instrument. One care keeps the step honest: *uniform* must be uniform
in the object's own length — uniform along the arc, which is intrinsic. Stating it as
uniform in an angle would presuppose the circle the argument is standing on.

**Then serialise.** Push uniform-on-the-arc through the quotient, and the density in the
ratio can only be a constant over one-plus-the-ratio-squared: the re-facing invariance
kills every non-constant factor, and the total fixes the constant at one over π. **The
Cauchy is uniform-on-the-circle, worn in the ratio** — which is §2's measurement, now with
its reason: the pushforward matched to two million samples because there was nothing else
it could have been.

Two consequences worth keeping. **A band of ratios weighs exactly the arc it subtends** —
probabilities are lengths of the drawn object, quoted in the coordinate that arrives; the
weight below the fulcrum is one half because the fulcrum halves the arc, and §7's regime
change sits at the arc's own midpoint. And the reader's graduation is priced by the same
accounting: the step of the register and the count of cells the bounded lens holds trade
at exactly π — fulcrum-sized quanta tiling a bounded aperture — so given the cell count,
the step is derivable, at an exchange rate the reader will never emit. §9's sentence,
operating: the instrument is calibrated in a currency held entirely by the outside
account.

And the two constants close on each other exactly. The reading's constant is a
**density** — 0.6366… per unit of arc, the uniformity just derived. The page's constant
is an **extent** — the quarter's π/2. A density times its extent is the measure's total
mass, so their product is exactly one; but the identity says more than normalisation,
because of *which* two numbers it holds between. One belongs to the open space and never
closes; the other belongs to the closed space and never opens; both are irrational,
neither alone reaches the unit — and multiplied, **the transcendental cancels and the
exact unit remains.** The two spaces' signatures are reciprocal through the quarter turn,
and what their meeting produces is not another constant but the unit itself — arrived at,
not declared, which is the same standing the unit has everywhere in this series: **a
theorem, not a choice.**

The honesty note, matching §3's: what forces the uniformity is a symmetry *of the pair* —
re-facing is not a move the reader can perform, any more than the swap was. The reader
does not choose this measure and could not check it. It inherits it, the way it inherited
the swap-invariance: as the image of a symmetric object it will only ever meet one reading
at a time.

## 5. Whose measure, settled by counting

A later paper supplies a rule that sorts this situation in one stroke, and it is worth
applying here because it decides a question §3 and §4 could only answer by argument. The rule
is about quantities built from disagreements: such a quantity belongs to the pair exactly
when two counts come out zero — its **degree**, the number of factors above the line minus
the number below, and its **balance**, whether every arrangement appears as often above as
below. Degree zero cancels an account's determinant; balance cancels the weight an account
carries at each arrangement.

**A density has neither.** Restate the ratio by any account and the measure picks up the
derivative of the restatement, which is precisely a weight. Rescaling the ratio changes the
density by half again as much as itself; a general projectivity of the line changes it by
about half. A quantity that transforms with a weight is of nonzero degree, and by the rule it
is the account's. So the answer to *whose measure is this* is not that it is the reader's, and
not quite that it is the page's either: **it is the kind of object only an account can hold,
and it says so in how it transforms.**

One family of restatements is the exception, and it is exactly the one §4 identified.
Re-facing leaves the measure where it was: the density and the derivative cancel identically,
and the invariance holds to the last place the arithmetic carries. This is the symmetry the
measure was forced by, and the caution §4 attached to it now has a name — the measure is
invariant under a subgroup of the accounts, not under all of them, and the subgroup is the one
the reader cannot act in.

And the counterpart is worth stating, because it says what the pair has where the account has
a Cauchy. Take four arrivals rather than one. Their **cross ratio** — two disagreements above
and two below, every arrangement appearing once on each side — passes both counts, and is
unchanged by every restatement of the ratio: shears, projectivities and rescalings alike leave
it where it was, to thirteen places.

Three things the paper has been treating as one situation therefore come apart. **The
arrivals are the pair's. Their distribution is an account's. Their cross ratio is the pair's
again — and, by §1, out of the reader's reach.** The reader arrives in a measure it cannot
own, states a reading it fully owns, and stands beside a quantity belonging to its own
relation that it cannot form.

## 6. The missing mean is a coordinate artifact

The Cauchy's most famous lack dissolves under a change of coordinate — and the coordinate
that dissolves it is not an ingenious substitution. It is the reader's own axis.

In the ratio, the density is the Cauchy and the mean does not exist: measured over
independent runs, the average of the magnitude wanders by a factor of 2.4 and never
settles. Push the same measure onto the count of rungs — the axis the register is actually
graduated in, since the rungs sit at fixed ratios — and it becomes an even, quiet thing:
every moment finite, mean exactly zero. Measured: the mean in the rung coordinate settles
on zero to four decimal places, with variance 2.4597 against a predicted 2.4674. And zero
in that coordinate is the ratio at one — **the fulcrum.** The distribution was never missing
a centre. It was being read in a coordinate that cannot express the centre it has.

There is a deeper version of the same point. The reader did not *lose* a mean, because it
never had the concept the mean supplies: nothing in its constitution names a centre —
facing the other party is a relation, not a location, and a relation needs no located
point. The missing mean is the outside account asking the reading for something that was
never in its vocabulary.

One correction is carried in place, because it was instructive to make. An earlier draft
took the unit rule's measured rest position — mean log-reading of +0.00017 over the
corpus — as confirmation that the reader sits at the measure's mean. The measurement is
real and the inference does not follow: under the unit rule the reading is confined to the
fulcrum cell by construction, so its distribution is a rounding residual, and a symmetric
rounder returns mean zero whatever measure the world has — run with round-down it returns
half a step high, with round-up half a step low. **The zero is the quantiser's symmetry,
not the measure's mean.** What the rule genuinely supplies is better than a mean and needs
no statistics: it fires only on departure from the fulcrum cell, so the fulcrum is exactly
where the rule does nothing — a fixed point of the dynamics, which a quantiser cannot
fake. The reader is always returning to the fulcrum, not because its readings average there
but because that is where every symbol puts it.

### Why gathering more does not help, and what it does help

The front of this paper says that the sample mean of any number of arrivals is distributed
exactly like a single arrival, so that gathering more improves nothing. That is usually
where the account stops, with the law of large numbers named as something the Cauchy
escapes. It is worth going one step further, because the escape has a mechanism and the
mechanism is the subject of §6.

**What the law of large numbers actually rests on** is a bound: each new member of a
collection contributes an amount that is small compared to the collection. The sum then
grows in proportion to the count while its wobble grows only as the square root, so the
average steadies. Everything depends on no single member being able to speak for the whole.

**For these arrivals that bound does not hold, and it fails as loudly as it can.** Measured:
the largest of ten arrivals is nine tenths the size of their whole sum; of a hundred, nine
tenths; of ten thousand, nine tenths. The proportion does not shrink. The same ratio for a
well-behaved quantity falls from 0.83 at ten to 0.06 at ten thousand, which is the law of
large numbers happening. Here the biggest arrival is always the size of everything, so
each new one is a fresh opportunity for the whole sum to be about the newcomer. Averaging
cannot dilute what it cannot outnumber, and the result is exact rather than approximate:
the average of two arrivals, of ten, of a thousand, all have quartiles at ±1, the same
quartiles as one arrival.

And §11's last subsection says why the bound fails. The reader's run was never clipped,
so there is always further out to look; each new arrival is another chance to look
further, and there is no distance past which the looking stops. **A statistic that presumes
the collection has finished cannot be asked of a run that has not.**

**But the law's content is not lost — only the mean is.** Gathering more arrivals does buy
precision here, and buys it at the ordinary rate, provided one asks for something the
arrivals own. The median of the arrivals concentrates exactly as the law would lead one to
expect: its spread is 0.155 at a hundred arrivals and 0.0154 at ten thousand — a factor of
ten for a hundredfold increase, the familiar square root — and the measured spreads sit
within one percent of π/2 divided by the square root of the count. Over the same range the
sample mean's spread does not move at all: 1.47, 1.56, 1.55, 1.50.

The right statement is therefore narrower and less mysterious than the usual one. It is not
that these arrivals defeat the law of large numbers, or that more data is worthless here.
**It is that the mean is the wrong question to put to them, and the median is the right one
— and the fulcrum, which §7 shows the median to be, is precisely what more arrivals buy.**
A reader is not asking how big its arrivals are on average. It is asking where they divide,
and about that it can learn as much as it likes.

One caution, carried from §12: forming any of these statistics needs a collection, and a
reader holding one arrival at a time holds no collection. What this subsection describes is
what the arrivals contain, not an operation the reader performs.

## 7. The Cauchy is a shape, and the fulcrum is where it changes

The relation-space papers derived the fulcrum with no distribution anywhere in the
argument: it is the one division that names no outside scale, and it splits every passage
into a proportional regime before it and a counting regime after. The Cauchy changes shape
at exactly that point.

Before the fulcrum, read in the proportion, the density is smooth, flat-topped, and
tail-free — falling from its peak to exactly half the peak at the fulcrum. After the
fulcrum, read in the count of rungs, it is a clean exponential decay — a constant factor
per rung, which is what *logarithmic regime* means when a register says it. And the fulcrum
is the join: the weight stands at exactly one half there, and the density in the rung
coordinate takes its maximum there.

Measured against a Gaussian matched at the peak: inside the fulcrum the two track within 25
percent everywhere. At the fulcrum the ratio is 0.824. At twice the fulcrum it is 1.48; at
five times, ten thousand; at twenty times, a number with eighty-four digits. **Normal-like
inside, heavy outside, and the boundary is the fulcrum** — a boundary derived from what a
reader can say, with no distribution in the argument, and a distribution that changes
regime there anyway. Two constructions, one boundary.

Two further facts fix the fulcrum's position exactly, and neither was arranged.

**The fulcrum is where the arrivals divide in half.** The share of readings falling inside it
is one half, and outside it one half, exactly — the fulcrum is the Cauchy's quartile point,
and the median of the reading's size is one. Half of what arrives is a proportion of the
reader; half of it is a count of the reader's reaches. The two regimes the relation-space
papers derived are not merely divided at the fulcrum; they are divided into equal shares
there.

That is worth more than a measurement, because it is forced. The exchange of seats — the
writer read in the reader's terms becoming the reader read in the writer's — sends a
reading to its reciprocal, and the fulcrum is the one arrangement that exchange leaves where
it was. Suppose only that the readings' distribution respects that exchange, which is to say
that it does not care which party was asked. Then a reading falls inside the fulcrum exactly
when its reciprocal falls outside, so the two shares are equal, and being two they are each
one half.

Nothing in that argument mentions the Cauchy. **The fulcrum is the median of the arrivals for
any relation whose readings do not care which party was asked**, and the Cauchy is one
instance of the condition rather than the source of the result. Readings distributed as
e^X for a symmetric X — X normal, X uniform, X Laplace, X itself Cauchy — all divide at the
fulcrum into 0.4991, 0.5001, 0.4992, 0.5000 and 0.4999, measured at three hundred thousand
arrivals apiece, and the Cauchy sits among them rather than above them.

The condition is sufficient and not necessary, and the distinction should be kept: readings
uniform on an interval about the fulcrum also divide in half, without respecting the exchange
at all. What the argument establishes is that exchange-symmetry *guarantees* the split, not
that a split implies the symmetry.

**And the notorious failure lives entirely on the far side of it.** Taking the contribution
to the average size of a reading, the part inside the fulcrum comes to 0.441 and settles. The
part outside comes to 2.50 out to ten, 8.35 out to a thousand, 17.1 out to a million, 34.7
out to a million million — it grows as the logarithm of how far one is willing to look, and
never arrives anywhere. **The missing mean is not missing from the distribution. It is
missing from the region past the fulcrum**, and it goes missing logarithmically, which is the
regime that region is in. The failure is the counting regime, seen by a statistic that
assumed a proportional one.

That is the sharpest form of this paper's claim available. The fulcrum was derived from what
a reader with no unit can state, and with no distribution in the argument. It turns out to
be the median of the arrivals, the join between the two shapes, and the exact boundary of
the divergence. Three coincidences would be a great many. One boundary, described three
ways, is the alternative.

## 8. What a reader can discover about it, from inside

Suppose a reader is pointed at a source of such arrivals. Its resources are counts over
symbols and one free reference — the fulcrum, nameable without a scale. What can it learn?

Three count-signatures are available, and they fail in an informative order. The share of
arrivals beyond the fulcrum comes out one half — which separates this source from most, but
not from every heavy relative with its median at the fulcrum. Reciprocal symmetry — the
count above a value against the count below its reciprocal — matches to two parts in a
thousand, and again a near relative passes too. The third signature uses what the reader
most natively has: its own rung histogram. Exponential decay makes successive bin counts
fall by a *constant* ratio; lighter-tailed relatives make the ratio keep falling. Dividing
neighbouring counts is integer arithmetic, free. Measured, the ratio settles for the heavy
sources and keeps falling for the light ones — and settles equally flatly for a different
heavy law.

So the honest conclusion has a ceiling. **The reader can discover, from counts alone,
using no scale it does not have, that it is looking at something heavy — the regime. It
cannot discover that the thing is the Cauchy — the law.** Orders of magnitude are visible
to it and constants are not; a law is a metric fact, and *heavy* is an order fact. The
identification of the arrivals *as* Cauchy is the outside account's, on the same footing
as the value of π: one more thing the reader is right about and cannot know.

## 9. Where π lives

Since the constant keeps appearing at the reader's expense, its own address deserves a
section, and the oldest paper in this line supplies it exactly.

> **π is the cost of a symmetric round trip between two points, when both the linear and
> the radial distances travelled are included.**

Including both does not mean adding them. For a traveller whose facing stays radial they
are one motion measured two ways, and the two ways agree: the round trip is π of linear
and π of radial — **the same number twice, not two numbers to be summed.** Count only the
linear displacement and the trip costs 2. Count both and it costs π. And nothing comes to
2π except a full closure, which is four passages and not a round trip.

But every figure in that accounting is computed by someone who can see both ends — a
completed journey, traversed rather than discovered. **The reader inside never emits a
π**, because a π would require a scale it does not have. So when π appears in this paper —
the measure's normaliser, the variance π²/4 — it is the outside account's bookkeeping of
the arc's total, held by the description and never by the described. The reader is
normalised by a number it will never produce: one more fact it is right about and cannot
know, and the exact sense in which the circle's serialisation was priced from outside.

### What the page's number is made of

One refinement, recorded with its uncertainty attached. There is a long-running notational
debate over which arc-measure deserves to be the circle's fundamental constant — the
half-turn's or the full turn's — and in this paper's terms the debate is internal to the
page: both candidates are **totals**, symmetric-space possessions, sums over a completed
sweep, which is exactly why §9 could show the reader never emits either. But the corpus's
own quantities suggest the page's constant has parts, and that the parts are not all the
page's.

Write the constant as 2 divided by 0.6366… — and notice that the divisor is not a
rearrangement but a quantity with direct characterisations of its own, every one of them
reading-flavoured. It is the average chord-projection of a uniformly turned unit — a
ratio of two measures of one path. It is the per-unit-arc density of §4's forced measure
on the quarter — the drawn circle's own uniformity, quoted per unit. It is the exchange
rate between §9's two accountings of the round trip — linear alone costing 2, both
included costing the constant. And it is the crossing rate of the famous needle
experiment — the number the experiment *measures*, before the textbook rearranges it into
an estimate of the arc constant. A total needs the page. **A ratio of two measures of one
path is the kind of statement a reading can own.**

So the decomposition reads: **the page's number is assembled from parts the reading
owns — a count of 2, and an exchange rate — and only the assembly is the page's.** Across
the classical derivations where the arc constant famously appears — the needle, the
bell curve's turn through polar form, this measure's own normaliser — the projection
ratio appears upstream, as the thing actually measured or integrated, and the arc
constant arrives afterwards, as the arc-side renaming of it. That is a pattern, not a
proof; and whether the relation space genuinely explains the constant or only
re-describes it is left open here, on the record. What the pattern does license is the
weaker, well-supported form: the constant is not wrong, and it is not primitive in the
derivations that produce it. **It is late** — the finished total of an exchange the
reading conducted first. And *late* should be read at its exact size: an order of
derivation, not a rank of being. As accounts, neither constant is prior — they are the
inside and outside faces of one geometric fact, the reading's and the page's, exactly as
the two spaces stand to each other everywhere in this series. What is asymmetric is only
which face appears first in any derivation a reading can conduct — which is the
asymmetry this whole paper is about.

## 10. Why the collapse happens once

One structural note completes the picture. Read several arrivals against one hold and the
counting is always the same: d + 1 quantities in, d readings out — one dimension gone,
every time, and only one. The interval never changes — home at one end, the horizon at the
other, whether there is one rise or three; what grows is only the sphere of directions
beside it, and the directions carry no weight: measured at fixed radius, every direction
returns the identical weight. **The collapse is the division by scale, and scale is
one-dimensional, so it can be divided out exactly once.** The reader lacks exactly one
thing, and the lack costs exactly one dimension. Even the sign of a single reading is this
fact at its smallest: the sphere of directions at one rise is two points, and they are the
two ends.

## 10a. What the two parameters are

Before the figure is given up, something should be said about what the arrivals' two numbers
*are*. A Cauchy has a location and a scale, and this paper has so far treated them as
bookkeeping — the fulcrum, the spread. They are not bookkeeping. They are the two things a
standpoint can know about a body, and they are physical.

Take a body made of mass rather than a curve drawn on paper, and let the arrivals be its
elements: one look per element, sampled evenly by material, each reported as the ratio of the
two components of the vector from the standpoint to that element. This is the same reading as
before — one extent divided by the other — with the sweep replaced by the body's own substance.

**The location of the arrivals is the direction to the centre of gravity.** Measured against
the truth: a uniform disk to 0.019 percent, a bar to 0.028, a ring to 0.119. And the case that
makes the point sharpest, two separated lumps with no mass whatever at their common centre —
0.036 percent. The reading finds the centre of gravity of a body that has nothing there.

**The scale of the arrivals is the body's angular size**, times a constant set by how the mass
is arranged. As the standpoint recedes, the ratio of the arrivals' spread to the body's
apparent radius settles: 0.808 for a uniform disk, 1.418 for a ring, 0.040 for a thin bar,
each stable to the third digit across a factor of five in distance. The ring against the disk
is that constant doing exactly what it ought to — the same angle subtended, the mass pushed to
the rim.

### Why the median is not a robust substitute here

This also settles a matter §6 left as a coordinate artifact. The mean of the arrivals is
wrong in every case measured — −0.4319 where the truth is −0.4250, −0.4387 where it is
−0.4248 — and it is wrong not because it is fragile but because there is nothing for it to
estimate. **The median is not the robust stand-in for a mean that has misbehaved. It is the
estimator that corresponds to the physical quantity**, and the mean is a computation with no
referent in the geometry.

### Where it deviates, and what that measures

One body missed: a disk with a heavy lump on one side, off by 5.3 percent. The deviation is
not error but content. The location of a Cauchy is its *median*, and the median direction of
mass coincides with the mean direction — the centre of gravity proper — only when the mass is
symmetric about it. A lopsided body separates them, and the gap between the arrivals' location
and the true centroid is a measurement of that lopsidedness.

So the Cauchy is not a distribution this program keeps stumbling into. **It is what pointing
at a thing from somewhere produces**, and its two parameters are where the thing is and how
big it looks. That is why it appears wherever a reader takes a bearing, and why the paper's
later sections can treat its parameters as the reader's own coordinates without apology.

## 11. Not a circle in particular

Before the circle is handed back to nobody, one thing about it should be given up. This
paper has spoken throughout of the circle, and the projection that carries it to the line is
a real and familiar one: cast the circle from a point onto a line and its even measure lands
as the Cauchy, and the Cauchy gathered back stands as the even measure on the circle. The
heavy tail is not then a property of a distribution. It is what the far part of the circle
looks like laid out on a line, where a vanishing arc is spread across an unbounded stretch.
That spreading is done to the *arc* — to how much room each direction is given. Nothing in
it is done to the figure, and the paragraphs below are careful to keep the two apart.

But the circle is not what the readings determine, and the projection does not know it. A
reading is one extent divided by the other, and dividing cancels the size of both:
whatever the pair's extents are, the reading depends on the turn alone. Any figure that
meets each direction once, met evenly in the turn, therefore delivers exactly the same
readings.

### How the cancellation works

*Plainly first, before the argument. Imagine a complicated shape — not a smooth round one,
but something lumpy, a cog or a burr, with spikes of every length sticking out in every
direction. Set it turning in front of you. As each spike comes round to face you, you take
your two readings of it: how far it reaches up, and how far it reaches across.*

*Those are not two measurements of two things. They are one spike, measured two ways.
A long spike gives a big up and a big across; a short spike gives a small up and a small
across, in the very same proportion. And the only thing you ever do with the two numbers
is divide one by the other — which is to say, you ask about the proportion and throw the
size away. You learn which way the spike points. You never learn how long it was.*

*But the lengths of the spikes are the shape. That is what makes a burr a burr and a cog a
cog. So the one thing your reading is built never to report is the only thing that
distinguished the shape from a plain circle — and it is not that the shape gets faint, or
blurs, or gets flattened, or shows up as noise you might dig out with enough readings. It
is not in what you were sent. Every spike, long or
short, reports its direction perfectly and its length not at all. Turn a cog in front of
you and turn a circle in front of you, and if the spikes point the same ways, the readings
are the same readings. Not similar. The same.*

*And then the thing you would have to change, which the later sections take up. Suppose the
shape is not turned by someone cranking it at a steady rate, but is rolling along the
ground like a wheel. A round wheel rolls smoothly. A lumpy wheel judders — it hurries over
the long spikes and dawdles on the short ones, because the ground, not the crank, is
setting the pace. Now the spikes still report only their directions, but they no longer
arrive at a steady rate, and the rhythm of their arrival is the lumpiness. That is the
whole of the recovery in §12: stop cranking, let the shape roll, and read the judder.*

*With one catch, which is §13. A judder can also come from a shaky hand on the crank. If
you do not know how the shape was being turned, you cannot tell a bumpy wheel from an
unsteady operator.*

The sentence is short and the mechanism under it is worth setting out slowly, because
everything else in this section is a consequence of it and because the mechanism is not
the one the word *projection* suggests.

Fix a direction in the turn. The figure has some extent in that direction — call it the
figure's reach there — and the pair's two quantities are not two independent things but
**the two components of that one reach**: the described quantity is the reach times the
sine of the turn, the describing quantity is the reach times the cosine. The reading
divides one by the other. The reach stands in both, identically, and leaves:

*reading = (reach × sin) / (reach × cos) = tan of the turn.*

The reach is gone, and not approximately. It is not that the reach contributes little, or
contributes something that averages away over many arrivals. **It appears in the numerator
and the denominator as the same factor and cancels in every single reading, one at a
time.** Measured on the strongest version of the claim available — five figures whose
reaches range from a quarter to one and three quarters, read in *the same hundred thousand
directions*, compared arrival by arrival rather than in distribution — the readings agree
to seven parts in 10¹², which is the arithmetic's own noise and not a residue of shape.
There is no arrival on which a clover and a circle disagree.

That is why *projection* is the wrong picture even though the word fits the arithmetic. A
projection casts one figure onto another and what is lost is lost *in the casting* —
something was there, and the casting pressed it out. Here nothing is pressed out,
because the two quantities the reader receives were never two facts about the figure. They
were one fact, the reach, split into components by the turn — and a ratio of two components
of one thing cannot report the thing. The reader is not being shown a figure at reduced
fidelity. **It is being shown which direction, and asked nothing about how far.**

The geometric form of the same statement is worth having, since it explains why no
cleverness recovers the shape. Every point lying along one ray from the reader delivers the
identical reading, so the readings do not distinguish points; they distinguish **rays**. A
figure is a choice of one point on each ray. The readings see which rays the figure
occupies, which is all of them, and never which point was chosen — so two figures differ
exactly in the information the reading is constructed not to carry. This is the ownership
rule of §5 in its plainest instance: the reading is a ratio of two quantities of the same
degree, so it is of degree zero, so it is blind to any factor common to both, and **the
shape is precisely a common factor.**

Two consequences follow immediately, and they run in opposite directions.

The first is what the rest of this section measures, and it is worth naming precisely,
because the loose word for it invites the wrong picture. The perturbations of a figure —
its spikes, its lobes, its bites and swellings — **are not flattened. They are not in the
signal.** Flattening is something done to a thing; it implies a step at which the
perturbation was present and got pressed down, and there is no such step. The distribution
over readings is a distribution over *rays*, the figure can affect only where on each ray
it sits, and where on the ray is never asked. No arrival carries a diminished version of
the perturbation. **The perturbations were never addressed to the reader.**

The distinction is not fussiness about a verb; it fixes what recovery could possibly mean.
If the perturbations were flattened, then a finer instrument, or more arrivals, or a
shrewder statistic might un-flatten them, and the question of shape from evenly-met
arrivals would be an open engineering problem. Since they were never sent, none of those
help, and §13 can state the matter as a theorem rather than a difficulty.

The second consequence runs the other way, and rests on the same absence. This is not a
defect the reader suffers but the property that makes its readings worth anything. The same factor that cancelled the figure cancels
*anything* common to both quantities — including whatever an instrument between the reader
and the world multiplies them by. **A reading that cannot see the shape also cannot see the
account, and those are the same sentence.** The blindness of §11 and the invariance of §5
are one mechanism read twice; a reader that could recover the figure from evenly-met
arrivals would be a reader whose readings the account could disturb.

And it locates the one way back in, which §12 and §13 take up. Since the shape cannot enter
through *which* ray, it can only enter through **how often each ray is visited** — that is,
through the schedule. Met evenly in the turn, the schedule is flat and carries nothing.
Met at the figure's own pace, the schedule is the figure, and the arrivals crowd where the
figure lingers.

### The run was never clipped

One thing remains to be said about the two senses kept apart above, because the difference
between them is not a matter of care in the writing. It is the difference between a figure
that has been drawn and one that has not.

A drawn circle is closed. The page holds it whole — both ends of every diameter present at
once, the far side already there, the figure finished before anyone reads it — and **that
closure is what makes its tail finite.** Lay the circle out on a line and the far arc does
spread without bound, but a completed object is underneath doing the spreading, so the
account can always say which vanishing piece of arc the unbounded stretch of line
corresponds to. The infinity is parameterised rather than reached. A drawn figure has had
its approach to the horizon clipped, by being drawn.

The reader holds nothing of the kind. What it has is an open run — home at one end, the
horizon at the other, and nothing joining them (§2). There is no far side already present
and no finished figure underneath, so the stretch out towards the horizon is not a
parameterised piece of a completed thing. **It is a direction the reader is still going
in.** And that, rather than any casting or laying-out, is what the heavy tail is. The tail
is not a distortion introduced by putting a circle onto a line; it is what an unclosed run
looks like when it is described in the one coordinate that has a horizon.

Read that way, §6's missing mean stops being a curiosity of the distribution and becomes
the same fact stated arithmetically. A mean is a statement about a collection that has
finished arriving. The reader's run has not finished arriving and was never clipped into
something that could, so the sum out past the fulcrum does not stop being added to, and it
grows as the logarithm of how far one consents to look. The front of this paper puts the
point in terms of sampling — the study of *N* against the study of *n*. This is the same
point in terms of geometry: **nothing was resolved, so nothing terminates.**

It also says what the census of §11 is actually doing, which is more than choosing a
radius. The census closes an open run into a finished object. It supplies exactly the
clipping the reading never had — and it can always do so, and it costs nothing, because
nothing in the arrivals objects. That is why the recovery succeeds every time and informs
nobody: **it is not inferring a figure, it is performing a closure, and a circle is the
cheapest closure available.** A quotient does not remember its sections, and an open run
does not remember a closure it never underwent.

Measured, at two hundred thousand arrivals apiece, on figures chosen to be as unlike a
circle as they can be while still meeting each direction once:

| the figure sampled | quartiles | median | ninth decile |
|---|---|---|---|
| a circle | −1.002 · +0.993 | −0.003 | +3.072 |
| a clover, three lobes | −0.996 · +1.001 | +0.003 | +3.085 |
| a star, five points | −0.999 · +1.000 | −0.000 | +3.080 |
| a square | −0.995 · +1.007 | +0.004 | +3.095 |
| a figure whose radius swings by a factor of seven | −1.008 · +1.008 | −0.000 | +3.081 |
| the Cauchy itself | −1 · +1 | 0 | +3.078 |

Five figures with nothing in common but the directions they occupy, and one set of
readings. **The Cauchy fixes how the turn is met and says nothing whatever about the shape
met that way.** The circle is recovered from it only because a census is applied afterwards
— the resolved account's declaration that the two extents square to one — and that census is
a choice of section, not a discovery. A quotient does not remember its sections.

The recovery should be described exactly, because it is stranger than it first appears. It
is not that the circle *might* be recovered where a circle was sent. **It is recovered from
every one of these, always, and it is the same circle each time.** Send the square: the
readings arrive, the census is applied, and a circle comes back. Send the figure whose
radius swings by a factor of seven: a circle comes back. Nothing was violated in either
case, because the radius that distinguished those figures was never in the readings for the
recovery to contradict.

So the reconstruction is perfect and uninformative in the same breath. It returns the unit
circle from any input whatever, which is to say it returns the census and not the figure.
The account is not normalising a radius it received; the relation never sent one, and the
account is **deciding** it — settling on the cheapest answer available, one in every
direction. The unit circle is not what was there. It is what the census costs nothing to
say.

Two things should be added to the table above, both found later and both sharper than what
it shows. The first is that the agreement is not close but exact. The quartiles there differ
in the third decimal, which is the sampling and not the figures; taken as a shape rather than
as endpoints, the readings of a circle, an egg, a clover, a clover swinging elevenfold, a
jointed figure, and a figure that comes within a fiftieth of touching the reader agree **to
four decimal places, off by less than a hundredth of one per cent**. There is no residue to
look for. The figures are not nearly indistinguishable in the readings; they are absent from
them.

The second matters more, because it reaches past this paper. **Every instrument built on the
readings inherits this, and cannot be built otherwise.** The fulcrum is found from the
readings; the reference that finds it is driven by the readings; the shape statistic of §7 is
a statistic of the readings; and the screen that uses that statistic to ask whether a stream
was met evenly is asking of the readings. All of them have the figure divided out of them
before they begin. **They test how the turn was met. Not one of them tests what was met** —
and no amount of care or exposure can make them, because the quantity they are computed from
does not contain it. That is not a limitation to be worked around. It is the same fact as the
circle coming back, stated about the instruments instead of about the recovery.

### The whole cycle, run on figures nobody chose for it

The claim can be put through the entire loop rather than argued, and on figures drawn for
other purposes: a library of closed contours, none of them made to illustrate anything here.

Each figure is read the way a serial reader reads it. A direction is taken, the figure's
extent in that direction is found, the described party is divided by the describing party,
and the result is one arrival. The turn is stepped evenly and the arrivals are collected.
Then the readings are taken back up, the census is applied — the two extents square to one —
and whatever comes back is drawn beside the figure that was sent.

Seven figures, chosen only to be as unalike as the library allows:

| the figure sent | its radius swings by | its readings, quartiles | what came back |
|---|---|---|---|
| a circle | 1.00× | −0.994 · +1.006 | a circle of radius 1 |
| a squircle | 1.26× | −0.994 · +1.006 | a circle of radius 1 |
| a Reuleaux triangle | 1.37× | −0.994 · +1.006 | a circle of radius 1 |
| a leaf | 1.65× | −0.994 · +1.006 | a circle of radius 1 |
| a tree | 1.68× | −0.994 · +1.006 | a circle of radius 1 |
| a heart | 3.25× | −0.994 · +1.006 | a circle of radius 1 |
| a wedge cut from a disc | 5.45× | −0.994 · +1.006 | a circle of radius 1 |

The radii differ by more than fivefold across the set. The readings agree to three decimal
places on every one, and the quartiles stand where the Cauchy puts them. The recovery
returns a circle of radius one from all seven, to one part in 10¹⁶.

A heart is sent and a circle comes back. A wedge with a bite taken out of it is sent and the
same circle comes back. **The figures are not alike, the readings are identical, and the
recovery cannot tell them apart** — not because it is a poor recovery, but because whatever
distinguished them was cancelled by the division and was never among the arrivals to be
recovered from.

This is the paper's central claim, run rather than said. What the reader receives is the
turn. What the page returns is the turn, and a radius the page supplied itself.

The cycle above is now something to watch rather than a table to read. In the drawing tool
it runs as a page of pairs — on the left the figure that was sent, on the right what a reader
gathers back — and the column of recoveries is the thing worth seeing: eight identical
circles beside eight figures that share nothing, one of them passing within a hundredth of
the reader's own standpoint. Every recovery comes home at reach one to machine precision.
Nothing about that page is arranged; it is the same division and the same census as the table,
drawn instead of tabulated, and the effect of seeing them side by side is not the same as
reading that they agree.

This is worth stating plainly because the paper's own title invites the error. The reading
does not project a circle. It projects **whatever the pair is**, and delivers the turn; the
circle is what the page draws when it decides to close what it was given. The pathologies
catalogued above belong to the projection and to the turn. None of them belongs to a
circle, and a reader who inferred a circle from the arrivals would have inferred it from a
census that was never sent.

## 11a. Manufacturing the pair from a single list

Everything above assumes the reader is handed a *ratio* — two extents of one thing, divided.
That assumption is heavier than it looks, and it is worth confronting, because most real
streams are not ratios. They are lists of magnitudes: one number per look, brightness or
amplitude or displacement, with no partner arriving alongside.

Read as *extents*, such lists are unpromising, and measurement bears this out sharply. Six
real signals — four handheld video clips, a percussive chirp, a ride cymbal, a fire alarm and
a voice — were put to the test of whether their magnitudes behave as a single circle
traversed. Every one failed, and each failure had a physical cause worth naming. The video
never returns at all: two clips wander without leaving their own neighbourhood, held there by
the camera's automatic exposure, which is a chain deliberately destroying the reading. The
chirp returns only once it stops sweeping — its pitch slides from 4009 Hz to 2450 Hz, and the
correlation of its return climbs 0.13 → 0.89 → 0.96 as it settles, so it is periodic nowhere
and closed only at the end. The cymbal and the alarm and the voice all return emphatically —
0.86, 0.98, and 0.44 to 0.85 — and all three are refused, because a struck plate, a piezo in
a cavity and a vocal tract are each many circles at once. A ride's strongest partials sit at
2611, 4700, 522 and 1567 Hz together; a voice's fundamental at 237 Hz carries a formant
cluster near 750 Hz at nearly equal strength. **Periodicity is common in the world. A single
circle is close to an artificial object.**

The cancellation of §11 is the way out, and it inverts the problem. If the reading is a
ratio, the figure is gone by construction — so the reader does not need the world to supply a
circle, only a *partner component* to divide by. And a partner can be manufactured from the
list itself.

If the list is proportional to sin, its successive difference is proportional to cos, so

> **r = value ÷ its own increment**

is the tangent of the phase, carrying some unknown constant — and an unknown constant is
precisely what §12's recovery is built to absorb. The cost is a held value and a subtraction:
the same comparison the reader was already making, with nothing added.

**Be exact about what this cancels, because it is less than §11's cancellation and the
difference matters.** Write the list as f(θ)·sin θ. The two-component ratio is f·sin over
f·cos, and the figure cancels outright — that is §11, and it is why any figure met evenly
sends the same readings. The single-list quotient is f·sin over (f′·sin + f·cos), and the
figure is still present, twice. **Dividing a list by its own increment cancels the gain — a
constant multiplier — not the shape, which is a varying one.** Measured on four figures swept
identically, the two-component reading returns 2.411401 for every one, a spread of 0.0000
percent; the single-list quotient returns four different numbers, a spread of 87.6 percent.

So the escape is real but narrower than it first appears. It works where f is nearly constant
over a turn — where the list is close to a single mode — and degrades in proportion to how far
it is not. That is visible in the measurements above: the fire alarm and the chirp, both
near-single-mode, return rates within two percent of their spectral centroids, while the voice,
whose formants make f vary strongly, misses by a third. **A single list cannot be made
shape-free. The shape-free reading needs the second component; what one list buys is
gain-freedom, and a rate that carries the figure with it.**

Measured, this works on every stream tried, including the ones that failed as extents. The
tails come back at 3.08 to 3.13 against the Cauchy's 3.08 for the alarm, the cymbal, a pure
tone and white noise; the fulcrum follows; and the cross ratio built on it is exact to between
one part in 10¹⁰ and one part in 10¹⁵, across all seven signals — including a hundred and
forty-three frames of handheld video that had been refused three separate ways.

### The fulcrum is a quartile, not a median

One point of mechanism, because the ratio reading moves it. In the extent reading the fulcrum
is the *median* — half the arrivals fall either side of it. In the ratio reading it is not.
For a turn advancing evenly, the tangent lies below one exactly three-quarters of the time, so

> **home is the median of the arrivals; the fulcrum is their upper quartile.**

The Cauchy's own scale parameter *is* the fulcrum. This is the mechanism by which a reader
holding nothing but counts arrives at the unit point, and it is cheap in the way this program
requires: a quartile is a count, not a computation.

Tested where it can fail — with the two components arriving at an *unknown gain ratio*, which
is the only version that proves anything, since equal gains put the fulcrum at one by
construction — the quartile recovers the gain at 0.25, 1, 4 and 37 with identical error at
every one: 0.21% on a pure tone, 0.95% on a voice, 1.14% on a cymbal, 1.33% on noise, 8.25%
on the alarm. Scale-invariance exact, as the theory demands. And with the fulcrum in hand the
frame closes: on a voice at a gain of 37, told nothing, the reader returns a fulcrum of 36.65,
home at 1.13, and harmonic conjugacy against the recovered pair to two parts in 10¹³ over
four thousand arrivals.

### What this does not buy

The frame arrives free of the figure — and free of any warrant that a figure was there.
**White noise yields a fulcrum and an exact cross ratio as readily as a tone does**, because
the Cauchy tail certifies that the phase advanced evenly, and even advance has at least two
causes: a traversal at constant rate, and randomness. What separates them is not in the
arrivals' shape but in their order — the spread of successive phase increments, which is
0.000 for a pure tone against 0.74 for a cymbal, 1.02 for an alarm, 1.41 for a voice and 1.43
for noise. That test certifies a *constant-rate* traversal only; a circle traversed unevenly
is indistinguishable from noise by it, which is §13's confound arriving from a third
direction.

So the honest statement of what the manufactured pair delivers is narrow and worth keeping
narrow. **It delivers a frame — home, a fulcrum, a horizon, and an exact projective coordinate
over the arrivals — from any list of magnitudes, at the cost of one register.** It does not
deliver a claim that the frame refers to anything. Whether such a frame is useful is not
established here: the fulcrum it finds from a single list is invariant under rescaling the
list, which is right, but its value tracks the rate of the list against itself, so it may
prove to be a rate measure wearing a geometry's clothes. The two-component version, where the
fulcrum recovers a real unknown gain, has a referent; the single-list version has not been
shown to.

## 12. The disagreement, recovered from the arrivals

Everything so far has run one way: the relation is given, the disagreement is its primitive,
and the Cauchy is what a serial reader receives. The traffic can be reversed. Suppose only
the arrivals — a stream of numbers, no relation supplied, no geometry, and nothing said about
where they came from — and ask what can be got back.

**The arrivals fix a group.** The Cauchy family is closed under a particular set of
transformations and not others. Measured by a scale-free shape statistic, which for any
Cauchy whatever stands at 3.078: the arrivals themselves give 3.06; shifted, 3.06; scaled,
3.06; sent to their reciprocals, 3.08; put through two general Möbius transformations, 3.09
and 3.07. Cubed, 28.7. Bent by a quadratic, 5.10. The family survives the Möbius maps and
nothing wider.

**The group fixes a space.** Those maps do not stay inside the line. The exchange alone sends
the near end to a place the line does not contain, so a set of arrivals whose family is
closed under it is not a set of numbers on a line at all; it is a set of points on the line
with its far end restored — the projective line, the pair's own space, recovered from the
arrivals rather than assumed for them.

**And the space fixes the pairing, up to one thing.** On that space the only pairing of two
arrivals that the group respects is antisymmetric and vanishes exactly when the two coincide;
in the chart the arrivals arrived in, it is their difference. That is the disagreement.

The one thing it does not fix is the scale, and the way it fails is the whole of this
paper's thesis arriving from the other direction. A single difference is not preserved by
those maps: measured over twenty thousand trials it moves by a factor of a hundred thousand.
Four of them balanced two over two — the cross ratio — move by two parts in 10¹². **The
arrivals return the disagreement exactly as far as the disagreement belongs to the pair, and
no further.** Its sign and its vanishing come back; its magnitude does not, because the
magnitude was the account's and the arrivals never carried an account.

### The frame, from the stream

The pairing is not the only thing the arrivals give back. The three stations come with it,
and once they are up the whole apparatus of the companion papers is computable on data that
arrived with no geometry attached.

The **near station** is where the arrivals balance: their median. The **fulcrum** is the
rescaling that makes them indifferent to which party was asked — the value at which half of
them fall inside and half outside, which is to say half the distance between the quartiles.
The **far station** is where the exchange sends the near one. Three stations fix a projective
coordinate completely, so every subsequent arrival can be placed, and cross ratios, fourth
harmonics and the rest follow from arrivals alone.

Whether this survives an unknown account is the question worth asking, and it does, in a
precise and limited way. A stream was put through six accounts — a shift, a scaling, the
exchange, two general Möbius maps — the frame recovered from each distorted stream on its
own, and the same arrivals placed in the recovered coordinates. In every case the arrivals
came back with their median at 0 and their quartiles at ±1.

The coordinates themselves do not agree between accounts, and the way they disagree is
exact. Comparing the recovered placements as turns rather than as ratios, every account's
recovery differs from every other's by **one constant turn**, to within four parts in a
thousand at four hundred thousand arrivals — the same constant for every arrival in the
stream, not a distortion but a rotation.

**The frame is recoverable from the arrivals up to a re-facing, and no further.** That
residue is not an incidental limit of the method. Re-facing is the one family of
restatements this paper has already shown the measure to be invariant under, and the one
§4's honesty note says the reader cannot perform. What cannot be recovered from the arrivals
is exactly what the arrivals were never able to distinguish.

One caution carries over from §12. Recovering a frame is something done with a *stream* —
quantiles are a property of the collection — and the reader that receives the arrivals holds
one at a time and no collection. The result says what the arrivals contain, not what their
recipient can do.

So the two directions close on each other. Given the relation, the disagreement is primitive
and the Cauchy is what arrives. Given the arrivals, the disagreement and the frame are
recoverable, the magnitude and the facing are not, and what is missing in each case is
exactly what belonged to somebody else. Neither direction supplies a unit, and neither needs
one.

### What the arrivals do not give back, and what would

A natural hope follows the frame, and it should be put down carefully because it is
half right. If three stations come from the arrivals, might the figure itself?

**Not from these arrivals.** §11 settled that, and the frame does not disturb it: the
readings cancel the radius, five figures whose radii swing by fivefold deliver the same
readings, and the frame is three stations on the reading line — precisely where the radius
went missing. Recovering the frame recovers no more of the figure than the readings held,
which is none of it.

What is worth noticing is *why* the readings held none of it, because the reason is a
condition and not a law. They held none because the turn was stepped **evenly**. Nothing
requires that. A process moving along a figure at its own pace advances the turn quickly
where the figure stands far off and slowly where it stands near, and its arrivals are then
not even in the turn — and not Cauchy either. Measured on the same shape statistic that
reads 3.078 for any Cauchy: stepped evenly, a circle gives 3.08, a clover 3.07, a
five-pointed star 3.06, an egg 3.07 — all Cauchy, all indistinguishable. Walked at constant
speed, the circle still gives 3.08, but the clover gives 5.02, the star 2.98, the egg 2.98.
**The departure from the Cauchy is the figure showing through the sampling.**

And it is not merely a signature but a measurement. The rate at which such a walker advances
the turn is the figure's arc element, so the density of its arrivals returns that element
exactly: on the four figures above, to one part in 10¹⁶. What comes back, though, is
*sqrt(r² + r′²)* rather than *r* — one function of the figure, not the figure. Recovering
*r* from it is a first-order equation, and it is solved only up to a constant.

That constant is worth identifying, because it is not an anonymous integration term. All of
*r* = 1 and *r* = sin(a + c), for every c, have arc element identically one. The first is a
circle drawn about the reader; the others are circles of the same size drawn *through* the
reader. Same figure, moved. The constant left free is where the reader was standing.

### The constant is fixed by the reading itself

The family is not, however, a family of candidates the arrivals have no way to choose
between, and the reason is worth stating because it costs nothing. **A figure being read is a
figure in front of the reader.** Every arrival is an extent in some direction, and an extent
is a positive quantity; a member of the family that goes negative somewhere is a figure that
passes behind the reader on that part of the turn, and a reader reading it would have had
nothing to read. Such members are not rival answers. They are not figures the reading could
have been of.

That requirement is decisive, and decisive in the strong sense: **two smooth figures that
share an arc element and both stand in front of the reader are the same figure.** The
argument is three lines and is given below, where the recovery's foundations are set out.
Of the circles sharing an arc element of one, *r* = 1 stays in front and every
*r* = sin(a + c) does not, and there is nothing else. On figures with more structure the
same thing holds, and it was looked for before it was proved: a wide search over positive
periodic solutions, on a circle, an egg, a three-lobed clover, a lopsided figure, a
five-pointed star and a peanut, converged six hundred and fifty-eight times, and **every
convergence landed on the figure the arc element came from.** What the search did turn up
each time was the negation of the true figure — the equation being unchanged when *r*
changes sign — and, in the circular case, the whole continuum of circles through the
reader. All of them go behind.

### The recovery, run

The claim can be carried out rather than argued, and the procedure is worth setting down in
full, since each step is either something the arrivals supply or something the reader is
entitled to assume, and nothing else enters.

**What is given.** A figure, and a reader that meets it at the figure's own pace rather than
evenly in the turn. Nothing about the figure is handed to the recovery: not its size, not its
shape, not the reader's position in it, and no unit.

**Step one: the arc element, from the arrivals.** The reader's arrivals crowd where the turn
advances slowly and thin where it advances quickly, and the rate at which the turn advances is
the figure's arc element. The density of arrivals is therefore that element, up to the one
constant that any density carries — and since what follows is a ratio of the element to
itself, that constant does not survive to matter.

**Step two: the figure, from the arc element.** The element is √(r² + r′²) as a function of
the turn, and the figure is the *r* in it. Recovering *r* is a first-order equation, and it
is solved here by fitting *r* in a **Fourier series** — which is not a convenience but a
requirement. Solved instead on a grid of sample points with a two-point difference for *r*′,
the even and odd samples decouple and the problem admits sawtooth solutions that no smooth
figure corresponds to; a solver run on that formulation converges to those, to fifteen
decimal places, and reports them as answers. In a series the figure is smooth and closed
before the solving begins and the artifact cannot arise. The fitting is ordinary
Gauss–Newton, from seeds carrying no information about the figure.

**Step three: the constant, from the reading.** The equation is unchanged when *r* changes
sign, so every solution is accompanied by its own negation, and in general by more besides.
Here the reader's own situation decides: an extent is a positive quantity, so a solution
negative anywhere describes a figure standing behind the reader over part of the turn, and a
reader reading *that* would have had nothing to read there. Those solutions are discarded not
because they fit worse — they fit exactly as well — but because they are not figures the
reading could have been of.

**What came out.** On four figures from a library assembled for other purposes, chosen only
for having a radius that varies and a centre they enclose, the figure recovered from its arc
element alone matched the figure that was walked to **within 0.005% of its size** — which is
to say the two curves are drawn on top of each other and cannot be told apart. Beside each,
the recovery draws the discarded solution: the same figure inverted through the reader,
standing entirely behind.

**What it rests on.** Everything above rests on one thing being true: that an arc element
belongs to one figure and not to several. It can be argued rather than searched for, and the
argument is shorter than the search.

Suppose two figures share an arc element. Both satisfy *r*² + *r*′² = *f*² with the same
*f*, so subtracting one from the other and factoring each difference of squares gives

    (r₁ − r₂)(r₁ + r₂) + (r₁′ − r₂′)(r₁′ + r₂′) = 0

with no approximation anywhere. The equation is quadratic, so this is exact and not a
linearisation of anything.

Now take the turn at which the two figures disagree most. The difference between them is
largest there, so the rate at which that difference changes is zero, and the second term goes
with it. What is left is the difference times the sum, and it is zero. The difference is not
zero, since the two figures disagree at that turn by assumption. **So the sum is: at the turn
where they differ most, the two figures have equal and opposite extents.** One of them is
standing behind the reader.

Said as geometry it is one line. The pair (*r*, *r*′) is a point on a circle of radius *f*,
so two figures sharing an arc element are two points on one circle, and the sum and the
difference of two points on a circle are perpendicular. At the turn of greatest disagreement
the difference lies wholly along the reading; the sum must therefore lie wholly along the
rate, which is to say the two extents there cancel.

**Two figures in front of the reader that share an arc element are the same figure.** So the
positivity of the last step is not a filter applied afterwards to a list of candidates. It is
the hypothesis that makes the list one long — the reader's own position is not extra
information brought to the recovery, it is what makes the recovery a recovery at all.

The known rivals sit exactly in the one gap the argument leaves, which is worth checking
since they were the reason to doubt. The argument fails only where the two figures sum to
zero at the turn of greatest disagreement. A figure's negation fails it everywhere, the sum
being zero throughout. The circles through the reader fail it at exactly one turn, and that
turn is the turn of greatest disagreement: *r* = 1 and *r* = sin(a + c) differ most where the
sine is −1, and there the two extents cancel exactly. Not a near miss. The one excluded case,
excluded for the reason that excluded it before — the rival is passing through the reader
precisely where it would have to differ.

Two things the argument does not claim, and both are the paper's own doctrine arriving once
more. It settles the figure for a *given* arc element, and the arrivals supply the element
only up to a constant; so what comes back is the figure **up to overall size**, the arrivals
never having carried a unit. And it needs the figure smooth, since the difference of the two
must have a rate at the turn where it is largest. A figure with a **hinge** in it — a place
where the extent turns abruptly, so that it has one rate arriving and another leaving — has no
rate there at all, and the argument has nowhere to stand. (Such a place is an ordinary corner
in the geometric sense, and it is worth saying plainly that it has nothing to do with *the
fulcrum* of §7, which is the calibration where a reading and its reciprocal agree.) The hinges
are not a technicality to be tidied up later. **They are a real exception, and they can be
built.**

The equation is even in *r*, which is where the negation comes from and what positivity is
for. It is also even in *r*′: the arc element records how fast the extent is changing and not
which way. That is where the hinges come from. A figure may reverse the sign of its rate at
any turn it likes and the arc element will not record the reversal. Smoothness is what forbids
reversing except where the rate is already zero.

Reversing deliberately builds rivals. Take the unit circle, whose arc element is one at every
turn, and write the solutions as *r* = sin θ with θ advancing with the turn. Reversing the rate
sends θ to π − θ and leaves *r* where it stood, so the two pieces join at a hinge with the arc
element one on both sides. Reverse whenever θ reaches π − ε, and again at ε, and θ sawtooths
instead of running: the figure rises and falls between sin ε and one, and if the sawtooth fits
a whole number of times into the turn, it closes. Three reversals give a figure running from a
half up to one and back, three times round — three arcs of circles through the reader, joined
at three hinges. **Its arc element is one at every turn, to the last place the arithmetic
carries, and so is the unit circle's.** Four reversals, five, seven: the same construction with
a smaller dent each time, hugging the circle more closely and never coinciding with it.

A reader walking either one at its own pace receives the same arrivals, since the arrival
density *is* the arc element and the two elements agree exactly. The figures do not agree: one
is round and one is hinged, and at the turn where they differ most they differ by half the
circle's size while standing on the *same* side of the reader — which is exactly what the
argument forbids when its hypothesis holds, and permits here because the hypothesis does not.

So the ledger carries two evennesses and one condition against each. The equation cannot see
which side of the reader the figure stands on, and *being read* settles that. It cannot see
which way the extent is turning, and *being smooth* settles that. The two conditions are not of
the same kind, and the difference should be stated rather than glossed. The first is something
the reading supplies about itself: there was a reading, so the figure was in front. The second
is not. Nothing in the arrivals says the figure had no hinges in it, and a recovery that returns a
smooth figure has assumed one — an assumption belonging to the account and not to the relation,
which is the debt this paper has been itemising throughout, arriving one last time in the final
step of its own construction.

That debt is real and it is narrower than it first appears, which is worth establishing rather
than conceding. **A hinged figure is usually loud.** The recovery reports a residual, and the
residual is how it says so. Run on the hinged figures a shape library already contained —
regular polygons and stars, drawn for other purposes — the fit never reaches the equation at
all: against a residual of one part in 10¹⁶ at the true figure, the best smooth fit stands
between four parts in a hundred and three in ten, and the figure it returns departs from the
one sent by 2.4 per cent on an octagon, 21 per cent on a square, 67 per cent on a five-pointed
star and 69 per cent on a triangle — ordered, as one would expect, by how few and how sharp the
hinges are. **And it is not a limit of the fitting.** Widening the series from twenty terms to
a hundred and sixty leaves the residual where it was. There is no smooth figure with a
polygon's arc element, and the recovery is reporting exactly that.

What makes the family above different is that for it there *is* one — the circle — and the
recovery finds it. **So the two cases are distinguishable from the inside, and the ledger
should say which is which: a hinged figure whose arc element no smooth figure shares announces
itself in the residual, and a hinged figure built so that a smooth rival exists does not.** The
second had to be constructed on purpose. The reader is not blind to hinges; it is blind to
hinges that were arranged to be invisible.

The wide search, run before the argument was found, agrees with it: six hundred and fifty-eight
convergences over six figures, every one landing on the figure the arc element came from, and
the only rivals returned being the negation and the circles through the reader — which is to
say, the two families the argument names. A later check ran the search the other way about,
letting the arc element be whatever a first figure generates — so that the first figure
solves the equation by construction and every arc element is in play at once — and hunting
for a second figure positive and held apart from it. Over forty restarts the hunt buys the
separation it was told to buy, and the positivity with it, and pays for both with a residual
of a hundredth where a figure that genuinely shares an arc element sits at 10⁻¹⁶: four orders
of magnitude short of being a solution at all. And the direction it strains in is the tell.
It presses both figures' nearest extents toward zero — toward the two figures meeting at the
reader — which is the search walking toward the one excluded case and not arriving.

The same algebra says more about the shape of the solution set than the count now needs, and
it is worth keeping, because it is what makes the fitting of step two well behaved. Suppose
*r* and *r* + ε*h* both satisfy the equation for the same arc element. To first order in ε,

    r h + r′ h′ = 0

At a turning point of the figure — a place where *r*′ vanishes — this reads *r h* = 0, and
since *r* is positive there, *h* must vanish too. **Every perturbation of a solution must
vanish at every turning point of the figure.** More than that: solving the linear equation
near a turning point at *a*₀ gives *h* behaving as |*a* − *a*₀| raised to the power −*r*₀/*r*₀″,
and at a *minimum* of the figure that power is negative. A perturbation that survives to the
minima is unbounded there. So no bounded perturbation exists at all, and **positive solutions
are isolated**: whatever else the solution set contains, it does not contain a continuum
through a positive solution.

Measured, as a check on the argument: at the true figure the derivative of the equation with
respect to the fitted figure has no null direction, its smallest singular value standing at a
few per cent of its largest on every figure tried. And at *r* = sin(*a*) — a member of the
continuum that the circle's arc element does admit, and not positive — the same test returns a
null direction immediately, its smallest singular value at 5 × 10⁻¹⁴, with that direction
being cos(*a*), which is exactly the family's own tangent. The test sees a continuum where one
exists and finds none where the argument says there is none.

The same algebra also says where a figure is allowed to meet its arc element. Since
*r*′² = *f*² − *r*², the figure never exceeds its own arc element; and
where *r*′ vanishes the two are equal, both being positive. So **the figure touches its arc
element exactly at its own turning points and lies strictly below it everywhere else.** At
those same points *f*′ vanishes as well, so the touchings can only occur where the arc element
is itself stationary. Measured on an egg, a clover and a peanut: at every turning point the
figure and its arc element agree to within 10⁻⁷ and the arc element's slope is zero to within
10⁻³, and the figure is under it everywhere else.

That gives the solutions a discrete skeleton. A solution is fixed by *which* of the arc
element's stationary points it touches and whether it rises or falls between them, because
between two consecutive touchings it runs monotonically from the one value to the other with
no freedom left. Before the argument above was found, this was the route by which the count
was to be settled: a finite list of patterns, to be walked one at a time.

Where a second figure would have had to come from can be said exactly. Expanding both sides about
a touching, where the figure and its arc element agree and both are stationary, and writing
*r*″ for the figure's curvature there, gives

    r″² + f r″ − f f″ = 0

a quadratic, so **exactly two curvatures leave each touching**, and their sum is −*f*. One of
them is the figure's own. The other is where a second solution would have had to branch, and a
counterexample would have had to be built by taking it at one or more touchings and closing
up. By the argument above, none of them closes up in front of the reader.

That the list never had to be walked is fortunate, and the reason is worth recording, because
it is a property of the problem rather than of any one attempt, and anyone who tries to solve
this equation by following the figure round will meet it on the first evening.

Walking the checklist means following a departure from one touching to the next, and the
touchings are exactly where the equation goes soft: there the figure meets its arc element
*tangentially*, both being stationary, so the quantity under the square root vanishes to
second order. Every method tried broke on that. Following the figure in the turn and taking a
turn at each stationary point of the arc element assumes what the search is meant to
discover — the figure need not turn at all of them — and forcing it produced a curve that
appeared to close and was not a solution at all, its residual four parts in a thousand against
a scale near one. Detecting the next touching instead of assuming it fails the other way: the
meeting is tangential, so a test loose enough to see it fires while the two are still
together, and one tight enough not to fires never. Changing the independent variable from the
turn to the figure itself repairs both — the singularity becomes the integrable kind and the
meeting becomes a crossing rather than a tangency — and the integrated arc then follows the
true figure closely over most of its length before drifting, because the same change of
variable puts the steepest part of the integration exactly where the arc ends.

The lesson is worth more than the failures. **The touchings are where the equation is
solved, and the argument that settles the count never solves it** — it looks at one turn, the
turn of greatest disagreement, and asks what has to be true there. Nothing is integrated and
the arc element is never differentiated. That is why the singularity that defeated every
attempt to walk the list does not appear in the proof at all.

What the recovery does need is stated plainly. The figure must be met unevenly — met evenly
there is nothing of it in the arrivals to recover. It must stand in front of the reader
through the whole turn, which is what makes step three available and what makes step two's
answer the only one. It must be smooth. And what returns is the figure up to its overall
size, since that is all the arrivals carry.

So the ledger is exact, and better than it looked. Stepped evenly, the arrivals give the turn
and nothing of the figure. **Walked at a pace the figure sets, they give the figure** — its
arc element exactly, and from that the figure itself, the only figure that was ever in front
of the reader to be read, in every size it could have been.

## 13. Telling two figures apart

A question the previous sections earn, and it should be answered plainly because the
answer is a theorem rather than a shortfall. Two readers each meet a figure and each hand
over a stream of arrivals. Both streams are Cauchy. What in them says the figures were
different?

**Nothing, if both turns were stepped evenly — and not because the question is hard.**
§11's seven figures make the point at the strongest setting available: radii swinging by
up to five and a half fold, quartiles agreeing to three decimals, the same unit circle
back from every one to one part in 10¹⁶. There is no statistic left to invent. The
information that would have separated them was cancelled by the division, and cancelled
information is not information corrupted in transit — it is information that was never
sent. Any procedure claiming to sort such streams by figure is reading its own
assumptions and reporting them as findings.

**And what does differ between two Cauchy streams is the account's, not the pair's.**
Location, scale, and the projective frame the readings are expressed in — those vary, and
§12 shows they are all recoverable and all removable: the frame comes back from each
stream on its own, and both reduce to the same standard form, median at zero and quartiles
at ±1, with the only residue a single constant re-facing, which is exactly the family the
arrivals were never able to distinguish. So among relations met evenly in the turn, there
is in the relevant sense **one** Cauchy. Two streams that look unlike are one relation
wearing two accounts.

**The discrimination is therefore not in the statistic. It is in the schedule.** §12's
recovery says what to do: a reader that meets the figure at the figure's own pace rather
than evenly in the turn receives arrivals that are no longer Cauchy, and the departure is
the figure showing through the sampling — measurably, and then exactly, since the arrival
density is the arc element. Two figures become distinguishable at the moment the reader
stops stepping the turn evenly. What separates them was never in the readings; it is in
the relation between the readings and the reader's own clock.

Which carries a caution that belongs with the method, because it is the first thing a
practitioner would get wrong. **A departure from the Cauchy does not name its own cause.**
Measured on the same scale-free statistic that reads 3.078 for any Cauchy: stepped evenly,
a circle reads 3.05, a clover 3.04, an egg 3.07, a five-pointed star 3.05 — all Cauchy,
all indistinguishable, as §12 reports. Walked at each figure's own pace, the circle holds
at 3.09 while the clover falls to 2.61 and the star to 2.47 — the figure showing through.
But take the *circle* — the figure with nothing to show — and read it on a clock that is
merely uneven in the turn, neither the figure's pace nor an even step, and it reads
**4.78**: a larger departure than any real figure produced. The clover walked correctly
departs by 0.43; the circle read on the wrong schedule departs by 1.73, four times as far,
with no figure behind it at all.

So a departure is evidence that the arrivals were not even in the turn, and nothing more
by itself. To read it as a figure, the reader must know which schedule it kept — and a
reader that does not know its own clock cannot tell a shape it met from a rhythm it kept.
That is not a defect of the recovery; it is the same accounting as everywhere else in this
paper. The turn belongs to the relation; the schedule belongs to the reader; and a
quantity that mixes them belongs to neither until the reader's half is declared. §12's
recovery is entitled to its results precisely because it *stipulates* the schedule — a
walker at the figure's own pace — rather than inferring it, and everything it recovers is
conditional on that stipulation holding.

The honest edge, carried from §12: what the recovery returns is the arc element exactly,
and the figure from it up to the reader's own standpoint, with positivity fixing the
standpoint; the step from *isolated* solutions to *one* rests on evidence rather than
proof. Two figures met unevenly are therefore distinguishable in practice, demonstrated
over six hundred and fifty-eight convergences, and provably not confusable by any
continuum of alternatives — but the final count is owed.

## 14. The circle is nobody's

One deepening remains, and it corrects a reading this paper could invite. Everything so
far has said the circle belongs to the page — drawn whole, closed by the outside
account, priced from outside. That is true of the *holding*. It is not true of the
*telling*, and the distinction is a theorem: **homogeneity is unoccupiable.** A uniform
circle has no privileged point — that is what uniform means — and to give any serial
account of it at all is to stand somewhere, which breaks the symmetry in one of exactly
two ways. The reading **warps**: its density peaks at its own aim and thins toward the
horizon — the fisheye, this paper's subject. The page's serial account **cuts**: a
parameterisation needs a zero, a starting point the circle does not offer, and the cut
returns as multivaluedness — the wrapped count, the seam that must be tracked by hand.

And each court's famous pathology is its fee for the circle it cannot occupy, and the
two are mirror images. The reading's account has **no mean** — a circle asked for a
*centre*, with no privileged point to give one. The page's account has **the cut** — a
circle asked for a *starting point*, with no privileged point to give one. **Heavy
tails and the cut are one scar on the two sides of the border.** Neither is a defect of
a presentation; both are the honest signature of a homogeneous object that no
standpoint can hold without breaking.

So the paper's claim sharpens rather than reverses. The page holds the circle whole —
§2's closure is really the page's to perform — but holding is not telling, and the
moment either court *serialises*, it stands somewhere and pays. The reading pays in
warp, the page pays in cut, and the circle itself is nobody's: **the Cauchy is what the
uniform circle looks like from inside — and the missing mean is the inside view of the
circle's homogeneity.**

## 14a. What the reading finally yields

The paper can now say plainly what the ratio buys, because it has been measured rather than
argued, and it is not what the opening chapters were reaching for.

**The magnitudes are what make a signal, and the magnitudes are what hide its closure.** A
list of brightnesses or amplitudes is dominated by how large things are; whether anything in
it goes round, and how fast, is buried under that. Dividing a value by its own increment
throws the size away, and what survives is part of what was hidden. The confound and the
instrument are one fact seen from two sides.

And it is worth saying what a magnitude list *is*, because calling it impoverished gets the
matter backwards. **A list of magnitudes is the figure, held whole.** It is the parallel
reading — every value of the shape available at once — and in that form the figures are
plainly distinguishable: four of them, correlated at essentially zero with each other, each
carrying its own signature. Nothing is missing from it. What it cannot do is state a rate
without a clock, because a collection held all at once has no order to be traversed.

The conversion is therefore not the repair of a poor reading. **It is a purchase**: the
parallel form is spent, in part, to buy something the parallel form could not state. And the
price is set by which conversion is available. With two components the figure goes entirely
and the rate comes back clean — that is §11, measured at a spread of 0.0000 percent across
figures. With one list only the gain goes; the rate comes back carrying the figure, at a
spread of 87.6 percent. **A reader with one list buys a rate that still remembers what it
was looking at. A reader with two buys a rate that has forgotten.**

What survives is the period, and it survives exactly. For a single tone the reader's fulcrum
and the length of a cycle stand in a fixed ratio: the upper quartile of the tangent is
tan(3π/8), so the samples in one cycle are the fulcrum times 2π/tan(3π/8), which is 2.6026.
Measured from 55 Hz to 3 kHz, the recovered period lands within 0.01, 0.07, 0.11, 0.06, 0.08
and 0.99 percent. **A list of magnitudes, one held value and one subtraction, and the period
comes back — with no unit, no calibration, and no scale anywhere in the derivation.** Multiply
the entire list by 0.25, or by 37, and the answer changes by nothing at all: not robustly, but
identically, because a common factor cancels in a ratio of a value to its own increment.

Two limits belong in the same breath. The quantity recovered is the *dominant* rate rather
than the fundamental — increments are governed by the fastest strong component, so a voice
whose pitch is near 200 Hz returns something near 1200 — exact for one tone, an effective rate
for anything richer. And the measurement has conventional cousins: a zero-crossing count
estimates much the same thing from the same data. What is different here is not the number but
its standing. It arrives as a *fulcrum* — a landmark with a frame already attached, home and
horizon on either side of it, and an exact projective coordinate over the arrivals.

That coordinate also answers a question the reader could not otherwise ask. Four turns are
needed to close a circle, and they can only be found if the sampling is fine enough to hold
them apart. The fulcrum says whether it is, before any other test is run: samples per quarter
turn come out at 50 for a tone at 220 Hz, 9 for a voice, 4 for a cymbal and for a fire alarm —
and **0.9 for noise, which is not a signal that fails the test but one whose turns are finer
than its own sampling.** The fire alarm is the instructive case: its return is the strongest of
any natural signal measured here, 0.98, and its landmarks are still unresolvable. Closure and
resolution are two requirements, and this reading prices the second for the cost of a register.

**So the conclusion is narrow and firm. From a bare list of magnitudes the Cauchy hands back
the closure that the magnitudes conceal: the periodicity, unit-free, with a landmark and a
local geometry built on it. What that geometry is good for is not established.** The frame is
exact over any arrivals whatever — noise included — so exactness is no evidence of a referent,
and the fulcrum's usefulness beyond the number it carries remains open. Three tests would
settle it and none has been run: whether the fulcrum tracks a physical change or only the
sampling; whether two readers at different rates agree once each expresses the world in rungs
from its own fulcrum; and whether the frame earns anything where a unit is available anyway.
The mechanism is closed. The question of what it is for is the honest remainder.

## 14b. The cancellation is not the price. It is the mechanism.

One thing remains to be said about the cancellation, and it reverses the sign of everything
this paper has said about it.

§11 gave up the figure and §13 measured the giving-up: a reader that divides one extent by the
other has thrown away the shape before any number is sent, and no amount of looking recovers
it. That has been reported here as a loss, and in [[the-reach]]'s terms it is one — a shape
comes back only to a reader that moves itself and drives to the vanishings. But the loss was
being read as a cost paid *for* something, as though a better reading might keep the figure and
the rate together. It cannot, and the reason is worth stating plainly, because it turns the
paper's central deficit into its central mechanism.

**If the shape survived into the reading, the rate would not be recoverable.** Shape and
schedule would be one degree of freedom — which is precisely §13's confound — and a single
stream would yield neither. The division is what separates them, by destroying one of them
outright. What is left is not a residue. It is the only thing that could have been clean.

Measured, with every figure swept at exactly the same rate and read two ways: the ratio reading
returns the same period for a circle, an egg, a bulge, a clover and a spiky burr — identical to
the digit, a spread across figures of **0.00 percentage points**. The extent reading, which
keeps the shape, returns a different period for each — a spread of **71.28 percentage points**
from an identical schedule. The reading that remembers the figure cannot tell you how fast it
was turned.

### Where the shape goes instead

And yet the shape is not irrelevant to what a serial reader can learn. It is *indirect*. A
figure that moves under its own physics has already written itself into the schedule, because
the schedule is not free: a body swinging under gravity has a period set by its moment of
inertia about the pivot, which is a fact about its form.

Five bodies of the same size, given nothing but a magnitude per look — the projected extent of
the swing — return their periods to between 0.29 and 2.01 percent: a rod, a disk, a sphere, a
hoop, a point mass. And the *ratios* of those periods, which need no unit and no clock at all,
land within 2.3 to 3.6 percent of what the mechanics says they must be. **The reading discards
the shape; the physics smuggles it back.**

What returns is not the form but the equivalent length, I/md, and figures that share it are one
object to this reader — a hoop pivoted at its rim and a point mass on a string of its diameter
come back identical, as they must. That is the same equivalence class this paper has been
handing back throughout: not an identity, but everything that could be told without one.

So the position to hold is narrower than *shape is invisible* and more useful. **Serial
observation is blind to form and sensitive to dynamics.** It cannot tell a cog from a circle
when a hand turns both. It can tell a rod from a disk when gravity does the turning — because
then the shape has stopped being a decoration on the reading and become a term in the equation
of motion, and equations of motion are made of exactly the thing a divided reading keeps.

## 14c. What the reader was watching all along

Three findings of this paper fit together into one, and the one is a better description of the
subject than the title suggests.

§10a found the two parameters to be physical: the arrivals' location is the direction to a
body's centre of gravity, their scale its apparent size. §14a found that a serialised reading
returns the rate of what it watches, exactly and without a unit. §14b found that the shape,
having cancelled, comes back only through the physics — a swing's period is set by its
moment of inertia, so form reaches the reader as motion or not at all.

Put together: **what the reader tracks is a centre of gravity in motion.** Not a figure, not
an outline, not a surface. A point that a body carries, and the way that point moves.

The test is direct. Give four bodies — a uniform disk, a hollow ring, a thin bar, a disk with
a lump on one side — centres of gravity that orbit identically, and hand the reader one number
per look. It returns periods of 194.4, 193.3, 196.8 and 195.8 against a truth of 200: agreeing
with one another to within two percent, each within three and a half of the truth. **A ring
and a bar and a lopsided disk are one object to this reader.** Change the motion instead and
the reading follows it — orbits of 80, 140, 200 and 320 recovered at 2.3, 2.4, 0.4 and 7.5
percent, the last degraded because a longer period leaves fewer turns inside the same record.

The shape is carried by the body and left at the door. The motion is what arrives.

### What this explains

It accounts, after the fact, for every refusal this program has recorded against real signals,
and accounts for them without blaming the instrument. Handheld video of a room has no centre
of gravity in periodic motion. A struck cymbal and a spoken vowel are not one body moving but
many modes sounding at once, each with its own rate. A percussive chirp has a centre whose rate
is changing while it is watched. None of these is a case of the machinery failing. **They are
cases of the machinery being pointed at things it does not measure.**

And it says what to point it at instead, which the earlier sections could only guess at. Not a
particular shape — the shape is the one thing that provably does not matter. **One object, in
steady periodic motion, against a still background.** A pendulum, a metronome, a wheel, a fan
blade. What is wanted is a single centre of gravity going round, and nothing about the form
wrapped around it needs to be known, chosen, or even seen.

## 14d. The figure, level by level

Everything above reads at one grain. The grain can be turned, and turning it gives this paper its
plainest picture of what the horizon is.

Set a circle beside a figure and call it the **address circle**, because it holds no figure: its
angles are addresses, and what they address here is a *level of detail*. The relation on it is the
register's step against the figure's swing — the logarithm of the figure's largest reach over its
smallest. At an angle ψ from home the reading is tan ψ, and that reading is the **rung span**, the
swing counted in steps. In the paper's two components, H = cos ψ is the reader's unit and V = sin ψ
is the figure. At home H is whole and V is empty: the step swallows the whole swing, every reach
rounds to rung zero, and the figure is the reader's own unit laid all the way round — §11's census
circle, what a reader says when there is nothing else it can say. At the horizon V is whole and H is
empty: the step has gone to zero, and what remains is the figure itself, with no unit of the
reader's in it. Every angle between is both — the figure, stated in the reader's steps. Pan the
needle once round and the figure goes from circle to whole in each of the four quarter turns: a
circle at home and at the home opposite, whole at the two horizons, the two kinds of landmark
alternating as they always do. (The fulcrum of this relation is its own — the step matching the
swing, a span of one — and not the fulcrum of the arrangement's two extents: the same construct, on a
different relation.)

**The first word comes at or before the fulcrum.** The register says nothing about the figure until
the figure's largest departure from where the unit was laid exceeds half a step. That happens at a
rung span of 1/(1 + |u|), u being where the unit sits in the swing — nought at its middle, one at
either extreme — which on 288 registers matched the derivation to 4 × 10⁻¹⁶: a check of the
arithmetic, not evidence. On the address circle it falls between 26.6° and 45° into each quarter
turn: at 45° only when the unit is laid mid-swing, and at 26.6° — exactly the circle's first rung
below the fulcrum — when it is laid at an extreme. Library figures with the unit laid at their
median reach turn at 30.9° (a cross), 32.9° (a square), 35.0° (a clover), 37.0° (a star) and 44.4° (a
cloud), each where the rule puts it. Before that angle the figure is the reader's own circle; after
it the record carries departures, and departures are the figure's. Just after it, briefly, the
record is worse than the circle it replaced — its first symbols report a small departure in a step
larger than the departure — and on figures whose logarithm of reach is near a cosine the share of
the figure carried dips to between −0.68 and −0.83 at a rung span of 0.63 before it recovers.

**Past the fulcrum, detail doubles at fixed angles.** A figure is held on close to tan ψ + 1 levels:
at most two anywhere below 45°, then two at 63.4°, four at 76.0°, eight at 82.9°, sixteen at
86.4°, thirty-two at 88.2°, each rung outward halving the angle left to the horizon. On the clover
that is 5 levels at 75°, 12 at 85° and 58 at 89°, with the worst departure from the figure 12.5, 3.9 and
0.8 percent of the reach. So the first half of every quarter turn can say at most one thing about
the figure, and nearly all of its detail is packed into the last few degrees before the horizon.

**The figure is whole at ±π/2 and nowhere else.** Those are the poles of the tangent: the only two
places on the dial where the rung span is infinite rather than a number. Every finite reading gives
a finite span, and every finite span a partial figure, so the figure is complete exactly where its
address is written as infinity. The asymmetry of the two ends is now a matter of degrees. The circle
holds over a band — on the clover 35° either side of each home, 140° of the turn — and the whole
figure holds over no arc at all. Loss arrives at a finite angle and stays; arrival never finishes
short of the pole.

**Sweep the dial evenly and this is the Cauchy.** A needle turned at a steady rate meets every angle
equally often, so the level it visits is the tangent of a uniform angle — this paper's subject, in
its absolute form. Its median is a span of one, at the fulcrum, so half of every sweep is spent
below it, where the figure is at most one level from a circle. The whole figure has probability
zero, because it sits on the two poles. And the mean level is infinite, for §7's reason: past the
fulcrum every rung carries equal weight, and there are infinitely many of them before the pole.
**The missing mean is the approach to the one point where the figure is whole.**

**Two absences, still two.** None of this explains why the reading loses the figure. §11 does, and
nothing here touches it: the ratio cancels the reach at every angle, the poles included. What the
dial shows is the second absence, the one about delivery. A register handed the reach itself — one
magnitude per look, in units it does not know — gets every level of the figure short of the pole, and
the whole of it only there. A reader of the ratio gets less: the same dial, every level and every
mark on it, and at every angle its own unit circle. **The Cauchy carries the levels of detail, never
the detail.**

**And the dial belongs to reading, not to the figure.** The picture above needs a page — something
that holds the figure and the reader's unit side by side and can draw every level — which is §1's
drawn version and §11's clipping. In that account there is no dial to sweep: the figure is drawn, all
of it present at once, nothing approached, and a level of detail really is a setting, a coarseness
the page may draw at will. For a reader it is not a setting. It is the step the reader reads with.
The same words name a setting on the page and a limit in the reading.

**A level is global.** The step is one step everywhere, so at any level every direction round the
figure is held to within half a step, and halving the step halves that bound for all of them at
once: each rung is one more bit of precision across the whole figure. A reader that stops early
holds a coarse version of the whole figure, never a fine version of part of it. Two things are not
even. A new rung reveals itself only where the reach crosses a new boundary, so a square's corners
break out before its sides; and a fine feature riding on a larger one is not absent below its own
fulcrum but comes through with the larger feature — carried at 0.96 of its size in the median at
half its own fulcrum, with a spread across readers of 0.43 there and 0.09 at the fulcrum — unbiased
on average, unreliable in any one reading, and the figure's own only past its fulcrum. And a reader
whose arrivals are a walk round the turn rather than draws across it still receives each lap start
to finish; the whole-figure property then holds lap by lap. Repeated passes add detail only when
something changes between them — a finer step, one rung a pass; looks falling in new places; or noise
met by a reader that averages. A fixed register reading the same lap again returns the same record:
pinned, in the corpus's own measurement, at about a quarter of a rung however often it looks.

**So approaching the horizon is shrinking a radius.** The radius is the reader's unit measured
against the figure — H. Each pass that halves the step halves it, and moves the needle to the next
doubling mark; after n passes it stands at 2⁻ⁿ, and the horizon would take infinitely many. A radius
of zero is not the last pass. It is the end of the reader: a step of zero is no register at all, and
the figure it would hold is the figure with nobody's unit on it — which is why a reader holds every
level short of the pole and never the pole, and why only a page can draw the figure there. A lens has
a collapsing radius too, and it is a different collapse. A fisheye's reader shrinks toward its focus
and lands on one element, everything else sent to its horizon: local. The register's unit shrinks
against the whole figure, every direction finer at once: global. Both approach their limit by
halving, and neither gets there.

**What a smaller radius buys, where the figure has cancelled.** For a reader of the ratio the
question has a sharp answer, because the figure is on the dial at no angle: every level holds the
same thing, the reader's own unit circle, and the circle is the empty record. A finer grain buys
nothing more about the circle, which has nothing more to say. What sharpens is the addresses on it,
and when things arrive at them, and the return comes in three forms. The first is finer addresses.
Each reading names a direction, and a finer grain names it more exactly and tells more directions
apart — worth nothing alone, an address with nothing behind it being only a name, and worth
everything where something holds the data the address points into. The second is a sharper frame.
The fulcrum, the cross ratio and the local geometry built on them are limited by the grain: the
invariant's error falls from 16.2 percent at eight rungs a decade to 0.6 percent at 256, and the
fulcrum is the binding cost, since computed on the fulcrum a reader actually finds, the error at
the corpus's own grain is three times what a perfect one gives. The third is sharper timing.
Landmarks, quarter turns and the period arrive as events, a finer grain places them more exactly,
and this is the one channel by which the figure can return at all — §14b's rule, blind to form and
sensitive to dynamics. A rod and a disk swinging under gravity return different periods because
their forms set their moments of inertia; a lumpy wheel rolled rather than cranked judders at a rate
its outline writes into the schedule (§11). The figure is in no reading, but where the physics sets
the schedule it is in the timing, and a finer grain reads the timing better. **A smaller radius is
precision about where and when, and never about what.** The frame and the events come from the
reading; what is at the addresses comes only from something held beside it, or from a writer whose
motion carries its form into the schedule. For a reader handed the reach itself, one magnitude per
look, the answer is different and simpler: only the scale is lost, and a finer grain buys the
figure, up to its size.

## 15. The reputation, restated

So the charge sheet closes as one finding. The Cauchy is the circle, serialised. Its
missing mean is a centre that was never in the reading's vocabulary, and reappears — mean
zero, every moment finite — in the reader's own coordinate. Its heavy tail is the horizon,
in the one coordinate that has one. Its change of regime happens at the fulcrum, a boundary
derived without it. Its law is invisible from inside and its regime is not. None of this
is repairable, because none of it is broken: **it is the price of delivery, and the only
thing that would waive the price is being handed the shape whole — which is the one thing
that cannot happen.** §14d found where it would happen: at the poles of the tangent, where the
reader's own unit has gone to zero, and there is no reader left to be handed anything.
