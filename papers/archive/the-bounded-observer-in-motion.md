Stated flatly, as in v2: **none of SR's core identities is hard; what is hard is that they all relate through one object** — the shared denominator

$$h = \sqrt{1 - \beta_x^2 - \beta_y^2 - \beta_z^2},$$

**shared** across directions, **self-referential** (a function of the numerators it divides), and **signed** (carrying the indefinite signature and the finite horizon). Its six faces, each a famous difficulty discovered separately in the standard formalism:

- it **couples the slopes** — the perpendicular-velocity transformation is the common-mode write of gbo §9.2: change the run and every read moves, though no numerator was touched;
- it **carries the signature** — the minus that makes the horizon a finite speed;
- it **dilates the clock** — $h = \cos\theta$ shrinking under the slide;
- it **tilts the now** — the same rotation that tilts the worldline tilts the now-slice: simultaneity;
- it **binds energy and momentum** — $E^2 - p^2 = m^2$ is three numerators bound by one scale $E = m/h$;
- it **fails to factor across directions** — and this face now has a theorem behind it: the class does not factor (gbo §9), so composing non-aligned boosts *cannot* separate cleanly, and the residue is the Thomas–Wigner rotation (§6). The foundational instrument draws this face directly: the torus-versus-sphere pane is the tensor carrier beside the interleaved one, and the scar of forcing one onto the other is the same scar. And that scar now carries a number: the shared closure is the round sphere $S^d$, first Betti number $0$ — no independent loops, the directions do not separate — while the tensor alternative is the torus $T^d$, $b_1 = d$, one loop per direction, a clean product. "Fails to factor" and "closes round" are one statement, and $b_1$ is its certificate: sharing the denominator is exactly what dissolves the torus's $d$ loops into a single simply-connected pointing (the Cauchy companion, §4.3–§4.5, verified $b_1(S^{d\ge2}) = 0$ against $b_1(T^d) = d$). gbo §9.2 already carries this invariant in the equivalent Euler-characteristic form — the sphere's one glued horizon against the torus's crossing pair, $\chi = 2$ versus $\chi = 0$ — so $b_1$ is that census restated on the loop count rather than the horizon count. The non-factoring *is* the roundness, read as a topological invariant. (Hardening clause, v9: the certificate is qualitative and should not be dressed as more. $b_1 = 0$ certifies *that* the directions cannot separate — the carrier admits no per-direction loop for a private closure to live on — but it does not compute the residue; the quantitative Thomas–Wigner angle still comes only from §6's holonomy, Gauss–Bonnet on the velocity space. Topology names the scar; geometry prices it. A claim that the Betti number "derives" the rotation would be the reading over-drawn, and this paper does not make it.)

Six confusions, one object. The standard formalism spreads the denominator across four-vectors, the metric, and the line element, so the six faces are never seen as one. Built from the atom, the difficulty is named once, in advance. (And the object's own status — seat or value, register or atom — is settled in §4.1.)

## 4.1 Is the denominator itself an atom?

*(New. The question is natural — the paper's one hard object ought to have a named place in the paper's own taxonomy — and the answer turns out to restate the signature fork.)*

**Within one act: no.** The denominator is a **register** — the run, the seat every rise is read against — and a register is not an atom for the same reason a unary read reads nothing (gbo §1.25): an atom is a comparison, and $h$ alone has nothing to compare against. §7 already closed the obvious escape: fill $h$'s slot with a pair *within* the act and it flattens through $\otimes$ into gauge — no within-act observable. Inside the act, the denominator is a role, not a reading.

**Across acts: yes — and it is the only atom it can be.** The change-atom pointed at the run itself, $k = (h_{\text{now}} : h_{\text{then}})$, is §7's aperture atom, Bondi's $k$-factor, priced at one accumulator. So the question has a sharp answer that is also the fork of §0 restated: *within the act the denominator is a seat; across acts it is an atom; and special relativity is what happens when the denominator is given its atom.* The E-chart reads $h$ as the kept seat ($\cos\theta$, the clock rate); the H-chart is $h$ reading itself ($k$, the Doppler); the Gudermannian glues them because they are one register's two lives, not two objects.

**The self-reference face, closed by the glossary.** §4's strangest face was self-referentiality — $h = \sqrt{1 - \sum \beta_i^2}$, a function of the numerators it divides. How does a seat wear a number? By the register/pointing distinction (gbo, glossary note): $h$-the-register *divides*; $h$-the-number is a component of the **resolved pointing** — the value the seat wears after the whole act resolves, the recursion the eight line draws as the child atom (*wherever a register wears a number, a prior atom resolved it*). The self-reference is not a loop inside the object; it is two roles of one register quoted in one formula — as seat it divides the rises, as resolved read it is constrained by them. This is also a diagnosis of why the six faces stayed confused for a century: the standard formalism **reifies** the denominator — treats the seat as a number among numbers, a component to be transformed — and every face of §4 is a symptom of asking value-questions about a seat. The framework's answer to "what is the hard object?" is: not a hard number — a seat mistaken for one. Two echoes confirm the reading. §10's boost never touches a denominator as a value — it *re-seats*, one register up and the other down. And §8.5's mass is the one place a genuine scale is bolted onto the seat from the layer above, which is why $E = m/h$ looks like the denominator acting: it is the posited scale being read *through* the seat, not the seat producing a value of its own.

## 5. The test: switch off the denominator

If the difficulty is the shared signed denominator and not the relativity principle, switching the denominator off should kill all the hard content at once and leave the relativity principle standing. The switch is the Galilean limit $c \to \infty$: $h \to 1$, clocks stop dilating, and the simultaneity mixing vanishes *in lockstep* — because time dilation and the relativity of simultaneity are two projections of one rotation, they appear and vanish together. What survives is the boost itself: observers still move relative to one another, each at its own home. Galilean physics is SR with the denominator switched off — and, in v3's added vocabulary, the tensor limit: the run demoted from register to constant, the interleaved pointing relaxed to the grid. (§6.1 finds the same limit inside the pair law itself: the $\lambda = 0$ parabolic middle of the composition family.) The hard content of SR *is* the sharing. (Fork 2's remainder — whether the multi-observer stage is itself primitive — is the second-aperture edge, gbo §13.1, held there.)

**Hardening clause (v9): two degenerations wear one name, and they are not the same object.** The paper reaches "Galilean" by two roads it has been letting stand as one. The *kinematic* switch-off is $c \to \infty$ — §6.1's $\lambda = 0$, the parabolic middle: the fold vanishes, the horizon retreats to no finite address, and the carrier flattens to the open line. The *structural* defection is the tensor limit — each direction given a private denominator: every fold survives, per axis, and the carrier is the torus (§3), not the flat line. The two agree on what they kill — the six faces of §4, all coupling through the shared signed $h$ — and disagree on what they leave standing, and the disagreement is a theorem, not a nuance: $\lambda = 0$ leaves a carrier with $b_1 = 0$ and no closure; the tensor limit leaves $b_1 = d$. So "Galilean spacetime is the tensor limit" (§3) is exact about absolute time — the shared denominator demoted to a constant is indeed the grid's discipline — but the equation runs through what is *lost*, not through the surviving geometry, and the switch-off test of this section tests the kinematic degeneration only. A reader who wants the structural one tested must ask a different question (do the directions' reads factor?), and §8's non-factoring record answers it separately. One name, two walls; the paper now holds them apart.

## 6.1 Fork 1 contested — and held, with two amendments

*(New. §0's resolution was "held as resolved unless contested," which is a standing invitation; this section is the contest, run adversarially — three attacks, each pressed until it broke or bit. **Attack one: the pseudo-counterexample.** "Across acts ⇒ hyperbolic" looks falsifiable: compare your *heading* now to your heading one act ago, and the increments compose on the circle, mod the full turn — an across-act comparison living in the E-group. That the glossary distinction blocks it unprompted — it was installed for an unrelated confusion — is evidence it was the right distinction.

**Attack two: derivation, or stipulation in a derivation's clothes?** The sharper attack: why should "same register, one act ago" *select* the sign, rather than merely accompany it? Pressed, the mechanism surfaces, and it is cleaner than the slot rule: **what selects the setting is whether the pair of seats carries an order.** Two registers within one act have no order — neither seat is prior, and the swap $(A,B) \leftrightarrow (B,A)$ changes nothing the observer could read, so the read must quotient it: the portion read — part over whole, the fold — and the folded dial is compact — the E-setting. The forced readings stand exactly as §0 assigned them; the mechanism beneath them is now named. **Attack three: is the sign bit really binary?** No — and this is the finding. ## 6.5 The paradoxes: across-act relations asked as within-act properties

*(Carried from the archived predecessor, restated in the fork's vocabulary.)* One act is one atom: one pointing, one read, no memory (gbo §1, §3). The classic paradoxes share one shape — each takes a quantity that lives *across* acts (a relation; the H-court, by §0's fork) and asks about it as if it lived *within* one act (a property; the E-court). Asked as a property it looks contradictory; put back where it lives, the contradiction dissolves and the boost supplies the number. Three cases and a boundary.

**The twins.** "Who is moving" is a within-act question and has no answer — each twin is at its own home, always (§2). The across-act fact is the *path*: elapsed time is $\int h\,dt = \int \cos\theta\,dt$ summed along the word of acts, and the traveler's word bends at the turnaround while the stayer's does not; a bent path keeps less run. At $v = 0.6c$ the traveler ages $0.8$ of the stayer. Note what the comparison costs: an accumulator carried *across* acts — the M-axis purchase (gbo §13), the same purchase that buys the aperture atom its referent (§7) and buys monotone refinement its direction (*Tangent Bridge* §1.2). No act holds the age gap; the carried registers do.

**The ladder and the barn.** "Did both doors shut at the same time" asks two places for one now — a relation between events, held by no single act (an atom holds one pointing). Each frame orders the shuttings its own way, and the disagreement is the tilt of the now-slice (§4, face four): two events a distance $L$ apart and simultaneous in the barn are offset in the ladder's frame by $\Delta t' = -\sinh\zeta \cdot L/c$.

**Bell's spaceships.** Two ships a distance $L$ apart fire identical engines; a string joins them; it snaps — the twins and the ladder at once. "Identical" was certified with the launch frame's simultaneity (the ladder's lesson), so measured in the ships' own frames the gap has grown to $\gamma L$ while the string's natural length is still $L$. The factor is $\cosh\zeta$, the same boost, no new number; at $0.6c$ the gap is $1.25\,L$.

**Thomas precession** is the same diagnosis at its sharpest, already home in §6: holonomy is a property of a loop of acts, never of one.

**Where the diagnosis stops.** It covers asymmetries of *composition* — a word of acts whose facing changes. It does not cover global-topological asymmetry: in a spatially closed universe one twin circumnavigates on a geodesic and returns younger with *no* turnaround — both words compose symmetrically, and the lens would hunt for a bend that does not exist. The asymmetry lives in the manifold (the closed space carries a preferred frame), not in either word. A lens that only ever succeeds is one to distrust; this one has a stated edge.

One correction the predecessor reached late and this paper keeps as settled: **composition never needs a wider act.** Wigner rotation, the twin's deficit, every cumulative quantity — all live in registers carried *between* acts, never inside one, so the flat single-axis atom plus memory carries the whole of single-particle SR, non-collinear boosts included. The wider simultaneous act — the full pointing of gbo §9.2, the hemisphere of the old vocabulary — is needed only when one act must read several coordinates at once. Different problem, different purchase.

## 6.6 Length contraction: the word under the tilted now

*(Closes the last v2 build-out item; the derivation is §6.5 run in reverse — not a puzzle to dissolve but a quantity to read off the same face.)*

Dilation is a *within-act* fact: one register, one act — the kept run $h = \cos\theta$ shrinking as speed is taken on. A length is not a within-act quantity. A rod is a **word**: two seats — the ends — that must be read *at one now*, and §6.5's ladder already established that "one now" is an across-act relation, held by no single act. In the rod's own frame this costs nothing: the rod rests, every now agrees, and the word's span is $L_0$. Read from a frame where the rod moves at $\beta$, the now-slice is tilted (§4, face four) — the two end-reads that are simultaneous for the observer are *not* simultaneous for the rod, and the tilt prices the span at exactly

$$L = L_0 \cos\theta = L_0\, h.$$

The same register, twice. The clock is a per-act quantity, and $h$ multiplies its **rate**; the rod is a per-word quantity, and $h$ multiplies its **span**. Dilation and contraction are not two effects but one rotation read on the observer's two kinds of held object — which is the honest content of v2's phrase "the taken-on partner of dilation": what you take on arrives foreshortened by exactly what your clock loses, because both are the one kept register doing its one job.

One more honesty clause, and it belongs to this framework specifically. **No act ever reads a contracted rod.** A photograph is one act — one pointing, light arriving at one event — and what it records is not the simultaneity-word but the light-travel-time composite: the Penrose–Terrell result that the rod *looks rotated, not squashed*, and a boosted sphere still presents a circular outline. The framework already owns the reason: the boost acts on the celestial sphere by Möbius maps (§9), and Möbius maps take circles to circles — the *optical* sky is conformally rearranged, never crushed. So contraction lives exactly where this section put it: in the across-act word, invisible to any single pointing — a relation wearing the costume of a property, which is why it spent a century being asked as a paradox (§6.5) instead of read as a price.

## 7.1 The cone is not crossed: the seam is an address, not a passage

*(New; the theorem is gbo §1.45, and this section is that theorem read in kinematics. It corrects one row of §7's table — the old row said "no smooth boost; a discontinuous act," as if the crossing existed and merely lacked smoothness.)*

The k-chart closes. $k = 0$ (light receding) and $k = \infty$ (light approaching) carry one address on the closed dial — the seam — exactly as home and horizon do on the number line; and the closure itself is no longer inherited from gbo but derived in this paper (§0.1: the weld is PG's quotient — the two escapes differ by a common sign flip no read can see — and the two-chart atlas that carries it is forced by P4 and PF). What gbo §1.45 establishes about that closure: **identification of addresses creates no passage.** The two charts of the k-line — approach ($k > 1$) and recede ($k < 1$) — meet at exactly two addresses, and only one of them is a gate. The **corner** $k = 1$, rest, is where continuous kinematics re-charts: on one axis, a word of boosts that turns approach into recession passes through mutual rest, necessarily — the relative velocity through zero, the long way around. The **seam** is the other meeting, and it admits no traversal: approached asymptotically from both charts (the feet of the aperture atom under any drag), occupied by light alone, and absorbing there — light composed with anything is light, the seam a fixed point of $\oplus_H$, not a waypoint. §8's records are this sentence executed: 4,000/4,000 cone absorptions, 0/20,000 subluminal escapes.

Three consequences, each previously an assumption or an act of dynamics, now chart topology:

**No boost crosses the cone — and the modality is corrected.** Not "no *smooth* boost," as the old row had it: no act, discontinuous or otherwise, because there is no path. Subluminal closure is not a guard the arithmetic enforces; it is what the arithmetic's carrier *is*. ## 7.5 The balance, and the fare it sets: metallic means

## 9.1 Fork 3, adjudicated: the partition is per-observer; the partitioned structure is not

## 9.2 The four Möbius classes are the atom ladder

*(New. §9 named the carrier — the celestial sphere, boosts acting by Möbius maps — and §9.1 read $k$ off the flow's fixed points. This reads the *classification* off the same flow. Not new mathematics — the four classes are Möbius's, the sphere Penrose–Rindler's, the little-group split Wigner's — but the identification of each class with a framework object is the synthesis §9 left standing, and two of the four are named here for the first time.)*

Every non-identity Möbius map of the sphere is conjugate to $z \mapsto \lambda z$ — a flow between two fixed points with **multiplier** $\lambda$ — except in the degenerate case where the two fixed points have merged, conjugate to $z \mapsto z + 1$. Write $\lambda = e^{\zeta + i\varphi}$: the logarithm of the multiplier is a single **complex rapidity**, its real part the boost ($k = e^{\zeta}$, §9.1's eigenvalue of the sky), its imaginary part a rotation about the same axis. Four regimes of that one number, four classes, four objects:

| class | multiplier $\lambda$ | complex rapidity | object |
|---|---|---|---|
| **elliptic** | $\lvert\lambda\rvert = 1$ | $\zeta = 0,\ \varphi \neq 0$ | rotation — orientation, roll |
| **hyperbolic** | $\lambda > 0$ real | $\zeta \neq 0,\ \varphi = 0$ | boost — $\lambda = k$ (§9.1) |
| **loxodromic** | $\lambda \in \mathbb{C},\ \lvert\lambda\rvert \neq 1$ | $\zeta \neq 0,\ \varphi \neq 0$ | the screw — boost + roll, one axis |
| **parabolic** | $\lambda = 1$, one fixed point | — (fixed points merged) | the null little group — the seam's symmetry |

The middle two the paper owned: the sky rotates; the sky boosts by $k$. The outer two are the reading added here.

**Loxodromic is the screw — the single-axis boost-and-roll, one complex read.** A boost along an axis and a rotation about *the same* axis commute — this is not the interleaved non-collinear court of §6, which is holonomy; on one axis there is no remainder. Their product is one loxodromic flow, spiralling pole to pole, with multiplier the full $k\,e^{i\varphi}$ — a *complex* $k$-factor — and coordinate the complex rapidity $\zeta + i\varphi$. So the one-axis Lorentz motion is not "a boost, and separately a roll"; it is one number whose modulus is the Doppler and whose argument is the orientation — the same one-read-two-parts as the float's exponent and mantissa, or the atom's rise and run. (Interactive witness: pinch-and-twist in the flight instrument — a dilation and a rotation sharing the point between the fingers are one loxodromic flow; the pinch carries $\zeta$, the twist $\varphi$, and they are one gesture because they are one complex multiplier.)

**Parabolic is the seam's own symmetry.** A sub-luminal observer's velocity is timelike, and its stabilizer — the transformations that leave that velocity fixed — is $SO(3)$, the rotations: elliptic maps, the full orientation freedom kept at any speed. *On* the seam the velocity is null, and the stabilizer collapses to $ISO(2)$, the Euclidean group of the transverse plane, whose non-rotational part is exactly the **parabolic** maps — the null rotations. So the horizon is not only unreachable in *value* (§7.1, the cone is an address); it is a change of *symmetry class* — the little group degenerating from $SO(3)$ to $ISO(2)$, elliptic to parabolic, at $\beta = 1$. You cannot become a null-stabilized observer by tuning a timelike one, for the same reason a hyperbolic multiplier never lands on a parabolic one: distinct fixed points do not merge by scaling. The seam's uncrossability and this degeneration of the stabilizer are one fact — and the parabolic class is what the framework had no name for until the sphere supplied it: the symmetry a light ray's own direction still carries.

**The corollary that prices a flat control surface — counted in fingers.** Because the gestures of a two-dimensional touch surface *are* Möbius maps of that surface, how many Lorentz motions a hand can natively make is set by how many points it grabs. **Two fingers** determine a *similarity*: pinch the dilation — the hyperbolic modulus, the boost, scale factor literally $k$ (§9.1's eigenvalue, now read off the hand instead of the horizon); twist the rotation — the roll (pinch and twist together the loxodromic flow about the point between the fingers); pan the translation — the aim. Four parameters, because two point-correspondences pick out the unique translate-rotate-scale between them. The two it misses are the transverse boosts, velocity off the line of sight. **Three fingers reach all six.** Möbius maps are *sharply three-transitive* — three distinct points and their three images fix one conformal map of the sphere uniquely — so grabbing three stars and dragging them *is* the unique Lorentz transformation that carries those three that way, transverse boosts included. That is not a gesture to invent; it is §11's chart-fit performed by hand — *three landmarks buy the chart between two skies* — the third fingertip supplying exactly the two special-conformal directions the second could not. One clause keeps it honest: the reach is the *conformal* reading of the three-point drag, not the affine one — read affinely, three fingers make shears, turning star-circles into ellipses, which is no boost; read as the Möbius correspondence, the physical one since the sky *is* conformal, they are the full group. So the wall was mis-set at "four of six": two fingers reach four, and the missing two are the third fingertip. The count of Lorentz directions a surface can fly is the count of landmarks its gestures pin — two for the similarity, three for the whole group.

These four are the classes as *structure* — fixed forms, each a way the sky can be transformed. Read them instead as *motion*, one-parameter flows run in time rather than maps held still, and the same ladder becomes the complete inventory of what a bounded read can *do*: the cycle and the pulse, their screw, and the seam where both degenerate. That reading — pulses and cycles as bounded dynamics — is §9.3.

*(Held as a reading, on the same terms as the rest: the classification is Möbius's, the little-group degeneration Wigner's, the conformal sky Penrose's. What is claimed is only the correspondence — four classes, four framework objects — and its corollary, that a plane's gesture group is the Lorentz subgroup the plane can reach.)*

## 9.3 Pulses and cycles: the ladder read as motion

*(New. §9.2 read the four Möbius classes as *objects*; this reads them as **motion**, answering a question the bounded-observer stance makes unavoidable — what can a bounded read *do* in time? The mathematics is standard (the one-parameter subgroups of the Lorentz group, the flows of §9.2's classes); the claim is the correspondence — pulses and cycles are the complete inventory of bounded motion — and its corollary, that the runaway third option physics reaches for by reflex is a coordinate, not a motion. The companion gbo develops the two archetypes in full: the balance beam, §1.08, and the pulse, §1.09.)*

A bounded observer's carrier is closed — the atom is a circle, home welded to horizon (gbo §1.5, §1.09) — and a closed carrier admits exactly two one-parameter motions: **around** it and **into** its weld. Around is the **cycle**, the elliptic flow of §9.2, orbiting the two fixed points. Into is the **pulse**, the hyperbolic flow, running from one fixed point toward the other and — the two being one address — arriving where it began. There is no third. The motion physics reaches for by reflex — a read growing without bound, escaping to infinity — is not a motion *on* the carrier at all; it is the surveyor's open coordinate, the rapidity of §6, the tangent-curve of gbo §1.5 that grows forever precisely because it was never the bounded read. So the stance's first word on pulses and cycles is a deflation and a unification at once: they exhaust bounded dynamics, and linear runaway is the artifact of quoting a bounded quantity in its unbounded surveyor's chart.

**The pulse is the boost, read as out-and-return.** A collinear boost adds rapidity, $\zeta \mapsto \zeta + \Delta\zeta$, without ceiling — the surveyor's ledger, monotone and open. The observer's read is $\beta = \tanh\zeta$, and it saturates: the same act that runs $\zeta$ off to infinity walks $\beta$ out to its balance and no further. What the bounded chart does with a monotone boost is fold it into a pulse. The near register climbs — how much of the sky is *ahead*, gathered, in view — reaching fullest at the balance; past there the far register takes over and *empties* — how much is *hidden*, the receding hemisphere squeezed toward the antipode — running back to zero as $\beta \to 1$. That emptying is not a metaphor; it is the aberration and beaming of §9, and it is the flight instrument's own far-field leg: at high $\zeta$ the whole celestial sphere crowds into a forward point and the rest goes dark, the observable world folding back toward a single address — and that address is where it started. Light-speed is reached not by arriving at an edge but by the far field emptying to the origin. On the celerity atom rest ($0$) and light ($\infty$) share one address, opposite extremities at a single weld (gbo §1.45); read in the Doppler register instead it is the two lights that weld and rest that sits whole ($k = 1$ the corner, the dual cut of gbo §1.5) — and either way the horizon is an address the flow approaches from the corner side and never crosses (§7.1). "No rest frame for light," in this reading, is the pulse bottoming out at its own beginning: the corridor's far end is its near end, and whoever runs the whole corridor is back at the throat.

**The cycle is rotation — and with it every clock, phase, and orbit.** The elliptic flow closes: run it and the read returns, exactly, on a period. This is the motion the framework has rested on since it made time out of memory — a clock *is* a cycle, its period the unit the M-axis weighs against, "how long" reading as "how many laps of some atom" (§10; gbo's change-atom, whose corner is stasis and whose two charts are grow and shrink, is this circle read in time). Orientation is a cycle — roll, the elliptic multiplier $e^{i\varphi}$ of §9.2 — and so is any orbit, any standing wave, any phase: one turn of the closed atom is a $U(1)$, and an observer holding a bounded read holds, for free, the circle phase lives on. The observational signature is Doppler run through the argument: a source in circular motion sends its $k$-factor *cycling* about the corner $k = 1$, blueshift to redshift and back — the periodic modulation that reads a companion's orbit off a spectrum. Two consequences the stance underlines. An **invariant** is a read parked at its corner — the change-atom level, no grow and no shrink; an **oscillation** is a read *cycling* about that corner. Conservation and periodicity are then one fact at two amplitudes: to hold still is to sit at the balance, to oscillate is to circle it, and both are what boundedness looks like in time. Periodicity is not an assumption the physics adds; it is the only thing a bounded read can do *other* than approach its horizon.

**The screw is their product, and this theory already named it Thomas.** Turn a pulse while you run it and you get the loxodromic flow of §9.2 — a helix, one complex rapidity $\zeta + i\varphi$, modulus the boost, argument the roll. Physics meets it as the leftover of a non-collinear boost word: compose two boosts off-axis and the result is a boost *carrying a rotation*, the Wigner–Thomas precession of §6 — a pulse that comes back rotated, a cycle *bought* by a pulse that did not return along its own line. The flight instrument's torus mode is this flow in the hand: pinch to run $\zeta$, twist to run $\varphi$, and the bead spirals a helix because a screw is what a boost-and-turn *is*, one motion and not two. The pure cycle and the pure pulse are its $\zeta = 0$ and $\varphi = 0$ edges; the generic single-axis Lorentz motion is the helix between them.

**And the seam is the one limit both share and neither reaches.** At $\beta = 1$ the pulse's two fixed points merge and the cycle's axis goes null: the flow degenerates to parabolic, the null little group, $SO(3) \to ISO(2)$ (§9.2). The cycle loses its centre and the pulse loses its far pole into the same point — the seam — which is why light neither orbits a rest frame nor is boosted to another: it holds the fixed point of composition, not of the swap, and no cycle or pulse of finite parameter lands there (§7.1). The seam is the shared horizon of both motions, an address the closed carrier's own flows approach forever.

**What physics is offered here.** Four expectations, each a claim about what to look for rather than a new equation. First, **boundedness forces periodicity**: a genuinely bounded observable does not run away — it cycles about its corner or approaches its horizon, and a linear escape in the data is the tell that the plotted quantity is a surveyor's unbounded coordinate (rapidity, an unwrapped phase, a proper distance) and not the observer's read. Second, **the ultra-relativistic fold is a return, not an arrival** — the sky's collapse to a forward point as $\beta \to 1$ is the far-field leg of the pulse coming home, and the chart carries the headlight before the energetics do (as §7.1's wall precedes the dynamical $\gamma \to \infty$). Third, **phase is periodic because the carrier is a circle** — the $U(1)$ every wave carries is the closed atom's one loop, held by any bounded observer as structure rather than posited as machinery. Fourth, **conservation and oscillation are one atom at two amplitudes** — invariants sit at the corner, normal modes circle it, and the invariant/variant split is read off the change-atom's distance from its balance, so a "constant of motion" and a "mode" are the still and the moving readings of one bounded quantity. The stance does not replace the dynamics that sets periods and precession rates; it fixes what *kind* of motion a bounded world may have — exactly two shapes and their screw: pulse, cycle, and the helix between them, about a corner they turn on and a horizon they never cross. (Hardening clause, v9, on the first expectation: as stated it courts unfalsifiability — *any* runaway can be rescued after the fact by declaring the plotted quantity a surveyor's coordinate and hunting for a bounded chart. The expectation has content only as a conjunction: the bounded read must be exhibited, it must be principled — the atom's own chart of the flow, not a sigmoid fitted to taste — and its saturation scale must be *measured independently*, not tuned to the data it explains. Where those three cannot be met, the expectation makes no claim, and a use of it that skips them is the reflex this section criticizes, pointed the other way.)

**The unification: a pulse is a bounded read of any flow.** Boost and rotation are the same story in two keys. Each flow carries a *surveyor's* coordinate — uniform, additive, unbounded — and an *observer's* read, the atom's bounded chart of it, which folds that open coordinate into an out-and-back:

| flow | class (§9.2) | surveyor coordinate (open) | observer read (bounded) | shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| boost | hyperbolic | rapidity $\zeta$ | $\beta = \tanh\zeta$ | one pulse (out-and-return) |
| rotation | elliptic | phase $\phi = \omega t$ | amplitude / action | a **stream** of pulses (repeating) |

## 12.5 Sequential exchange: the accumulator's noise theory, and the Kalman shape
