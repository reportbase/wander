# What a Reader Recovers

**Known physics read from a standpoint: a companion to "Observation from Somewhere"**

*Tom Brinkman, with Claude. 27 October 2026.*

*This paper gathers in one place what the corpus behind "Observation from Somewhere" (OFS) has found about physics. For
the geometry it draws on OFS and claims nothing OFS does not; where the two differ, OFS stands and this paper is to be
fixed. The physics comes from the working record: exact checks, runs on published data, and experiments in a simulated
world. Each result says which of the three it is. Appendix A names the run behind each one.*

## Abstract

OFS works out the geometry of a reader that stands somewhere and holds one length of its own, H, but not the size of
what it reads. Two of its results matter most for physics. First, everything a reader receives at one standpoint is a
ratio, so scaling the whole world by one factor changes no reading; an amount needs one amount carried in from outside
(OFS Proposition 8). Second, what arrives along a direction cancels from the direction's reading, so the reader's
addresses are given before anything arrives and the payload met there moves none of them (OFS Theorem 6). This paper
puts those results to work on known physics. Nothing here is new physics. What is new is a sorting: for each quantity,
whether one standpoint reads it, or whether it needs an amount carried in across a named bridge (a baseline walked, the
signals' speed, a rest frequency, a known kind, an agreed standard). Physics already mixes the two, often within one
formula, without marking where one ends and the other begins. The test that places the boundary is OFS's: scale the
world and see what moves.

Three kinds of evidence are gathered, with a fourth, simulations of our own checked against a full referee, beside
them:
- **Exact checks.** Time to contact, the corners of motion, special relativity in unit relations, the energy–momentum
  relation, parallax's error law, the inverse square as an address count, the qubit as the two sweeps, weak values,
  and the many places 2/π turns up.
- **Published data.** Star counts and light on Hipparcos and Gaia, the CatWISE quasar dipole, single photons through a
  double slit, a neutron interferometer, and grid cells in rats.
- **A simulated world.** Twenty-eight experiments in a world built for a flight game, in which a reader holding only arrivals,
  its own path and its own clock recovers Doppler, Rømer's lag, parallax, Kepler's third law, a star's mass, escape,
  the distance ladder, the expansion's age, the dark night sky, temperature and the second law. Where the world has a
  medium at rest the reader finds it; with the rest removed, its readings become special relativity's, and a falling
  reader sees only tides.

What the reader cannot recover passes a test for convention: changing it changes no reading of any arrival. There are
seven: the unit of length, a mass apart from a chosen standard, G's number, the frame, which of two distant events came
first, whose clock an age is on, and the zero of entropy. Each is something a writer carries in, a capability OFS
assigns to the blueprint, not to the reader.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 What this paper is

OFS is a geometry paper and keeps physics out on purpose (its one exception is special relativity's sky, OFS §11). It
asks what geometry a reader needs when it does not know the complete object. A *writer* states things in amounts, every
length at once to one scale, and needs the true sizes to do it. A *reader* stands somewhere, holds one length of its own,
and reads ratios. The corpus that produced OFS also ran a long series of tests on physics: first inside a simulated
world, from 3 October 2026, then in exact arithmetic and on published data, from 10 October. This paper collects them.

The claim is narrow. Physics is not changed by any of it. What the geometry adds is a way of telling, quantity by
quantity, which part of a known result a single standpoint delivers and which part was carried in. The textbooks do not
say this, and they need not: the results are right either way. The value of the sorting is in the places where the two
parts are confused. One is shown here (inverting noisy parallaxes as if they were distances, §8.2). Others are
candidates, and they are marked as such.

### 1.2 Four standings

Every result below carries one of four marks.

- **[exact]** Mathematics checked to many digits, usually 50, or in rational arithmetic. Most of these are classical
  identities; what is ours is which pair they belong to and which side of the boundary they fall on.
- **[data]** A prediction written down before published measurements were opened, then the run. Predictions that failed
  are reported as failed (§12).
- **[world]** An experiment in Wander, a simulated world built for a flight game (§4). These show what a reader *could*
  recover from what it holds. They are not evidence that our world is like Wander, and where a result was built into the
  world rather than earned by the reader, the text says so.
- **[simulation]** A simulation of our own, checked against a referee that computes the whole of a known physics. It is
  not evidence about the world; it is evidence about a scheme that holds less than the referee does.

### 1.3 Results in brief

1. **The scale test sorts physics** (§3). A direction, a ratio of two directions' light, a count of sources brighter than
   a flux, the Doppler factor's ratio between two of one's own intervals: none moves when the world is scaled. A distance
   in metres, a luminosity, 1/d² itself, a frequency against a rest frequency: each moves, and each needed an amount
   carried in.
2. **A reader in a built world recovers the classical results it was set** (§4). Doppler, Rømer, parallax with
   light-time taken out, Kepler, mass by touch, Cavendish, escape, the distance ladder, Hubble–Lemaître, Olbers,
   temperature, the second law and Gibbs, each from arrivals, the reader's own path and its own clock.
3. **What it cannot recover is convention** (§5). Seven things, each passing the test that changing it moves no reading.
   Where Wander has a medium at rest, the frame and the "now" fail the test and the reader measures them; with the rest
   removed they pass, and Einstein's elevator returns.
4. **Motion and special relativity read in unit relations** (§6) [exact]. Time to contact is a reading over its own rate
   at every distance. The corners of motion come in two kinds. Special relativity has three pairs with three corners, and
   mass is the corner of the light-cone pair, which is why no boost moves it.
5. **The inverse square is an address count** (§7) [exact]. Along each direction the brightness is the same at every
   distance; the law is in how many directions a source covers. Laid on a flat receiver, an emitter's even spread is
   the Cauchy, and 1/d² is its height.
6. **Parallax has its corner at the Thales circle** (§8) [exact]. The relative error per unit angle is cosh of the
   levels from it; past a band of levels set by the step, a point is only a ray.
7. **The qubit is OFS's two sweeps** (§9) [exact]. The state is a direction at θ, its Bloch point the central sweep's
   point at 2θ, Born's weight the foot. Interference's duality relation V² + P² ≤ 1 is the facing, and published
   neutron and photon data sit on it (§9) [data].
8. **2/π is not one thing** (§11) [exact]. Of 65 occurrences, 34 are a mean over the uniform turn, 10 a share of it, 8
   the turn's length, 7 the circle as a shape.

## 2. The geometry, in brief

What follows is OFS's, stated only as far as this paper needs.

**Unit relations.** Any relation between two parties is a pair h, v, each between 0 and 1. For the reader both are shares
of its one length H; a writer, who holds both hemispheres, takes each as a share of its own party's breadth.
The reading is the ratio v/h, a direction. Neither unit relation can pass 1, so every relation turns over at one point,
v = h, the *corner*, the only reading fixed by the reciprocal x ↦ 1/x (OFS Proposition 1). Lowercase h and v are
geometry and carry no unit; uppercase H, V and the distance d are amounts. A conclusion crosses from one to the other only
across a named bridge: an amount supplied from outside.

**The reader's circle.** The reader holds one length, H, and nothing else to measure with. Turned uniformly, that one
length traces a circle, and the readings of the turn follow the Cauchy distribution: on the quarter turn, median at the
corner, quartiles √2 − 1 and √2 + 1, no mean (OFS Proposition 5).

**Address and payload.** The directions from the standpoint are the *addresses*; whatever arrives along them is the
*payload*. If what arrives along each direction lies at some unknown reach ℓ, its components in the reader's unit are ℓh
and ℓv, and the reading is ℓv/ℓh = v/h. So the payload cancels from every single reading (OFS Theorem 6(a)); order
statistics survive every change of payload, and of the means only an affine change commutes with them all (6(b), (c)).
Fingers order; colours add.

**Scale cannot be read** (OFS Proposition 8). Multiply every amount in the scene by λ and every reading is unchanged,
from any number of standpoints. One amount supplied from outside fixes every amount the readings link to it.

**The test: scale the world** (OFS §9). Multiply every breadth and distance by k. A reading does not change. A writing,
an amount stated in a named unit, changes by k. What scaling leaves unchanged is geometry; what it moves had to be
carried in.

**The two sweeps** (OFS §4). From home the directions are laid by the *inscribed* sweep, at angle θ; the same quarter seen
from the centre of the circle they land on is the *central* sweep, at 2θ. The two meet at one point for every direction
(Euclid III.20). The central point is ½(h² − v², 2hv) from the chord's middle, at (h², hv) over the chord of length 1;
its height 2hv = sin 2θ (in the radius) and its foot h² on the chord are bounded quantities with means.

**What a blueprint adds** (OFS §9). Ratios suffice for everything a reader does from where it stands. A blueprint, the
writer's statement in amounts, adds seven things no reading supplies, the first two being the size and a unit agreed
between parties that never see each other. In physics those are the constants and the units.

## 3. Reading and writing in physics

### 3.1 What the meeting is

Reading and writing describe the observer, not the world. So what follows is known physics recovered from the observer's
geometry: the form of a law from what a reader can and cannot read, its values carried in across a named bridge (c, a
rest frequency, Born's rule, a kind, a baseline). Physics is already a mix of reading and writing observers, often
within one formula, with the boundary between them unmarked:

- Ellis and Baldwin's dipole adds aberration, a reading of directions, to a weighting of the payload read from a
  catalogue's own ratios, and is set against a speed found from a Doppler frequency, which is a writing.
- A parallax is an angle read and a distance written through the astronomical unit.
- A distance ladder treats each rung as one written factor, and Hubble's 1929 brightest-star rung turns out to be a
  ruler of another layout.
- The inverse square is written as 1/d², while counts of sources by flux read the same law with no distance in them.

The test of §2 places the boundary for each quantity. Recovered physics can be exact and still partial: no recovery
establishes its own completeness, and only a second route that disagrees shows a gap (OFS §11, "Recovered, not
written").

### 3.2 The bridges

An amount enters a reader's account in one of five ways. Each is physics' own:

| bridge | what it carries in | example |
|---|---|---|
| travel | a baseline walked, in the reader's own lengths | parallax; the reader's own flight in Rømer's measurement |
| a constant | an agreed value, by which a unit is defined | c, and with it the metre since 1983; the SI since 2019 |
| a kind | an amount known of a class of things | a standard candle; a rest frequency; one material for density |
| a shared fact | one thing two readers both see | a closed polygon gives exactly the ratio of their units (OFS Proposition 7) |
| a standard | one thing chosen as 1 | a kilogram; a standard mass by touch (§5) |

Since 2019 the SI fixes every base unit by an agreed value of a constant (the caesium frequency, c, h, e, k_B, N_A and
the luminous efficacy). These are the agreed outside amounts of OFS Proposition 8(b), and the unit agreed between
readers of OFS §9. Dimensional analysis, which holds that only dimensionless ratios enter a law (Buckingham 1914), is
the same division seen from physics' side.

## 4. A reader in a built world

### 4.1 The world

Wander is a world of bodies built for a flight game: suns, planets, moons, travellers and belts, pulled by Newton's
gravity (softened over one world length), touching with momentum kept. Every system ticks on its own time and sends a
signal at each tick, tagged with the tick; signals travel at 20 units a second through the world itself, as sound
through still air. A reader receives the latest signal that has had time to reach it, and places the body where it was
when that signal left. So Wander has a medium at rest and one universal clock. A switch replaces the medium with no
preferred rest: then every clock reads its own time along its own path, steady arrangements shorten along their motion,
and each reader places what arrives in its own frame. The switch is Lorentz's change of frame, so special relativity's
form is built into that setting; what the experiments ask is whether a reader, from what it holds, reads it out again.

### 4.2 The experiments

From 3 October 2026, twenty-eight experiments took one result each, established from no standpoint, and asked what a reader
holding only arrivals, its own path and its own clock recovers. The rules were the corpus's:
- the prediction and what would kill it were written before each run;
- a killed run stays recorded as killed, and a fix is a new run with its own prediction;
- every kill is placed: on the world, on the reading, or on the experiment's own set-up;
- every reading was then gone through line by line for anything taken from the world rather than from what the reader
  holds, and the places where something was taken are said.

Of the twenty-eight, twenty-seven were not killed in their latest run. The one left, the elevator in Wander's medium, was
killed narrowly and is open, its cause known in outline (§5.4). One (laying a range first) is not a physical result and is
treated in §4.4. The kills along the way are in §12.

### 4.4 Two cautions

**What is built in.** Under no preferred rest, special relativity's form is built into the world by the Lorentz switch,
with clocks synchronised by Einstein's convention in the frame the world is written in; that frames cannot be told apart
follows from the construction. What the runs earn is narrower: readers holding only their own clocks, placements and
tags read special relativity's measured results out of them, with nothing added. One thing was not put in on purpose and
came back: Bradley's aberration, the star tilting toward the reader's motion by sin α = β (5.739°, 11.537°, 23.578°),
because in the reader's own frame where a body was when its signal left lies along the way the signal came. The three
quantum experiments draw outcomes by the Born rule, built in; they show what readers' records can tell apart, not why the
draw is so.

**A range laid first.** One experiment was not physics: it compared a reader that lays its addresses before anything
arrives with one that has only the addresses its arrivals make. It read a shape's breadth ratio b as the gap between the
arrivals' middle and the first reader's corner. Revisited exactly, that gap is the median direction of what arrives, the
payload's, and it equals b only because the arrivals were sent evenly in the shape's own parameter, a kind carried in;
the same shapes sent evenly by turn give 1 for every b [exact]. What laying the range first gives is a fixed thing to
read against: OFS's separation, in that set-up.

## 5. What is not recovered is convention

### 5.1 The test

On 3 October 2026 the experiments were set a wider conjecture: *everything measurable is recoverable from what readers
hold, singly or in company: arrivals, their own paths and their own clocks, to the grain; what is not recoverable is
convention.* To keep "convention" from excusing a failure, it was held to a test agreed before the runs: **a thing is a
convention only if changing it leaves every reader's reading of every arrival unchanged.** The conjecture dies if
something cannot be recovered and fails that test.

The test is OFS's scale test (§2) generalised. Scaling the world is one change; the test asks the same question of any
change: does any reading move? If none does, the thing changed is not in the readings, and a statement of it is carried
in.

### 5.2 Seven conventions [world]

Seven things were found not recoverable, and each passes:

| not recovered | found in | why it is convention |
|---|---|---|
| the unit of length | a whole weighing chain run on three rungs, every length ×λ for λ = ½, 1, 2 and every mass ×λ³ | every reading in the reader's own lengths agrees across the rungs to 0.009%; only the signals' speed differs, 40, 20 and 10 of the reader's lengths a second, the one thing the world holds fixed. What number to call it is agreement, as with the metre since 1983. |
| a mass apart from a standard | touches | masses come only as ratios; one body is chosen as the standard |
| G's number | Cavendish's measurement | measured once a standard is chosen; it moves with the choice (×20 with the star as standard instead of the planet) |
| the frame the world is written in | two readers, one passing, under no preferred rest | carried at any speed, the readings move by 2 × 10⁻¹⁶ |
| which of two distant events came first | two readers dating two flashes, under no preferred rest | one reader dates Q first by 0.50, the other P first by 1.20; written in either frame every arrival and every dating is the same to 10⁻¹⁵ |
| whose clock an age is on | the expansion, under no preferred rest | each reader reads its own clock's age and every reader is a centre; choosing whose clock to call the world's age moves no arrival |
| the zero of entropy | heat from outside the box | set by the reader's grain; no arrival changes with the grain, and the rise is the same at every grain the count fills |

The first three are what OFS §9 says a blueprint adds and no reading supplies: the size, and a unit agreed between
parties. The next three belong to the setting with no preferred rest, and pass there by construction (§4.4); what the
runs show is that the test separates the two settings. The seventh is the reader's own grain, OFS's resolution: the
zero of a count is set by the cells it counts in, the rise by the world.

### 5.3 The test has teeth: a world with a rest [world]

Under Wander's medium the frame and the "now" fail the test, so there they are measured, not chosen.

- **Michelson–Morley.** Three beacons flying with the reader, ahead, behind and to the side, on one clock: arrival less
  tag reads 10/(20 + u), 10/(20 − u) and 10/√(20² − u²) exactly, and the round trips along over across read 1.0050,
  1.0206 and 1.0911 at β = 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4, the shift Michelson and Morley looked for in 1887. The beacons' readings
  move by 0.333 between β = 0 and 0.4. With no preferred rest every beacon reads 0.5 at every β, and they move by
  7 × 10⁻¹⁵.
- **Aberration.** Wander places each arrival where its body was, not along the way its signal came, so a still star
  shows no tilt at any speed. In our world it tilts by sin α = β, which Bradley used in 1727; the reader under no
  preferred rest recovers it (§4.4).
- **The now.** Under the medium two readers date two flashes alike, at 0 and 0.5, written in either frame: one now for
  everyone, and its frame found by arrivals.
- **Two readers in company.** One passing the other at 0.4, each reading the other by Einstein's convention, the echo at
  half the round trip. Under no preferred rest each reads the other receding at 0.4 with its clock slow by
  √(1 − 0.4²) = 0.916515, reciprocal, unmoved by carrying the pair. Under the medium the clocks' readings disagree, by
  at least 0.078: in company, the pair finds the medium. In both settings the readers' speeds come out by relativity's
  subtraction, (β_y − β_x)/(1 − β_xβ_y): that is the convention's doing, even in a world built on Galileo's.
- **The expansion hides the medium.** A reader moving through the medium at 0.1 or 0.3 reads redshifts that differ by
  direction (0.0168 to 0.0210 at the nearest) and distances that differ with them; distance·(1 + z)/(C·z) is the time
  since the bang in every direction exactly. What parts the settings is not a centre but whose clock the age is on:
  the world's under the medium, the reader's own without it, so two readers who left the bang at different speeds read
  different ages, as two twins do.

### 5.4 The elevator [world]

Einstein's elevator, from somewhere. A reader and six light neighbours, ahead, behind and to either side along the line
to a star, fall together from rest. The reader never looks at the star; it reads only its neighbours, placed as it
places them.

In Wander's medium the reader sees more than tides. Its neighbours, seen a signal time late through a medium it falls
through ever faster, share a common drift away from the star of g·d/C, where g is its own fall and d/C each neighbour's
signal time: 0.0100 and 0.0200 at 0.5 and 1 off, against 0.0100 and 0.0200 predicted. From that drift and the tides
across its fall, the reader finds the star's distance (30.02 against 29.99) and its G·M (360.6 against 360), without once
looking at it. The equivalence principle fails in Wander, through the medium.

With no preferred rest, the reader placing each arrival in its own frame, the drift is gone: 0.04% to 0.17% of g·d/C,
where the medium gives 100%. The reader cannot find its own fall, the star's distance or its G·M from inside; it sees
the tides. Einstein's elevator comes back once the preferred rest is gone. So the equivalence Wander breaks belongs to
the rest, not to reading from somewhere.

What is not settled: along the line of fall, both settings leave small terms beyond the tides, of order a·u/C (a the
fall's acceleration, u a neighbour's own tidal speed), predicted from the arrival rule expanded to C⁻³ to 0.1% of the
tide in the setting with no rest, and missed narrowly (0.166%) in the medium's. The first step here is Newton's pull
in a written frame, not general relativity: no bending of signals by the pull, no slowing of clocks.

## 6. Motion and special relativity [exact]

### 6.1 Motion read from inside

A thing of half-size S at distance d closes at speed u. The reader reads x = S/d, the pair's v/h, and holds one amount,
its clock.

- **Time to contact is the reading over its rate,** x/ẋ = d/u, before and past the corner, with none of S, d or u known.
  Read from the angle arctan x instead, it is d/u times arctan(x)(1 + x²)/x: right far off, too long by a factor π/2 at
  the corner, and growing past it.
- **Two looks a tick apart.** x₁/(x₂ − x₁) is the time to contact at the second look exactly; x₂/(x₂ − x₁) is it one
  tick ago.
- **The levels of an approach.** Each doubling of the reading takes half the time to contact read at its start, so
  infinitely many levels pass before contact, in finite time.
- **How close it will pass.** With ω the bearing's rate, the closest approach as a share of the present distance is
  ωτ/√(1 + (ωτ)²). The clock cancels from ωτ, so this needs nothing carried in; a bearing that holds still is a
  collision, as navigators know.
- **One stream is one relation** (OFS Proposition 9). A thing coming nearer and one growing at the matching rate give the
  same stream. A neighbour at the same depth, read at once, separates them (OFS Proposition 10).

All of this is known kinematics: Lee's τ, the navigators' constant bearing, stereo error, surveying's right angle of
intersection. What the sorting settles is which reading carries each exactly: the ratio at every distance, the angle
only far off.

### 6.2 The corners of motion come in two kinds

Take a quantity of a pair through its ratio k.
- **Unchanged when the pair swaps** (f(k) = f(1/k)): then f′(1) = 0, so the quantity is stationary at the corner. A
  launch's range at fixed speed, (2U²/g)·k/(1 + k²), longest at 45° with complementary launches equal; the energy a
  head-on elastic collision passes to a ball at rest, 4k/(1 + k)², all of it at equal masses; a baseline's conditioning,
  2k/(1 + k²), best when the half-baseline equals the distance. The first and third are one form, the sine of the
  facing 2θ.
- **Sign flipped when the pair swaps:** the quantity changes regime at the corner. Braking: with stopping distance
  s = u²/2a, τ̇ + 1 = d/(2s) exactly, so Lee's critical τ̇ = −½ is stopping distance equal to distance, the pair at its
  corner.
- **Neither:** a launch's time of flight and greatest height rise with k throughout, with no corner.

### 6.3 Special relativity in unit relations

With c the bridge, v = β and h = 1/γ are unit relations on the unit circle, and the reading v/h is the celerity γβ.

- **Three pairs, three corners, three kinds.**
  - The Doppler pair (sent, received), its ratio k: the corner is rest, the fixed point of swapping who counts.
  - (β, 1/γ), equivalently (pc, mc²): the corner is β = 1/√2, pc = mc², where the two regimes hand over.
  - The sky's (fore, aft), read as tan(θ/2): the corner is the transverse plane, the median of a sky uniform in its own
    frame, and only there; a boost moves it.
- **γ and γβ are the even and odd parts of k in levels:** γ = (k + 1/k)/2, γβ = (k − 1/k)/2, so k = γ + γβ, and
  1/γ = 2k/(1 + k²), the facing's sine, the form of a launch's range.
- **Rapidity is the reader's layout of celerity, smoothed:** proportional near home, logarithmic far off, never more than
  1 − ln 2 nats from it.
- **The regime switch is the corner of (pc, mc²).** The energy's slope in levels against momentum is β², a share: ½ at
  the corner, and s(x) + s(1/x) = 1. The kinetic energy's slope is not a share and is not symmetric.
- **Uniform acceleration** takes one level of the Doppler factor per ln 2/a of proper time, evenly.

### 6.4 E² = (pc)² + (mc²)²

Divided by E this is the unit circle of the pair (pc, mc²), both read in the one unit E. Its unit relations are
v = pc/E, which is β, and h = mc²/E, which is 1/γ, the moving clock's rate: a clock runs at the rest share of its energy.

- **Home** (v = 0) is E = mc²; the **horizon** (h = 0) is E = pc, light. The **corner** is pc = mc²: β = 1/√2,
  E = √2·mc², kinetic energy (√2 − 1)·mc², 0.2117 MeV for an electron, 388.6 MeV for a proton.
- **Newton and the massless limit are reciprocals of each other.** mc² + p²/2m and pc + (mc²)²/2pc swap into each other
  when pc and mc² swap, and their relative errors satisfy e_N(x) = e_U(1/x) exactly. Newton's form is the better exactly
  inside the corner; at the corner both are √(9/8) − 1 = 6.07% high. This makes the corner the precise statement of
  "where relativity starts to matter".
- **Mass is the corner of the light-cone pair.** (E + pc)(E − pc) = (mc²)², so mc² is the pair's midpoint in levels. A
  boost with Doppler factor k sends E + pc to (E + pc)/k and E − pc to k(E − pc): a shift in levels, equal and opposite.
  The midpoint does not move, which is why mass is the same for every observer; shifts compose by multiplying, which is
  why rapidities add. Mass is the least value of E over boosts; the corner is the rest frame.

All standard: Bondi's k-calculus, light-cone coordinates, the two limits. What the corner adds is organisation: one circle
in one unit, home and horizon, the two limits as one approximation under the reciprocal, the invariant mass as a corner.
What it does not give: c's value, or why a change of frame is a hyperbolic shift and not a rotation. That sign is the
world's.

### 6.5 The sky of a moving observer

OFS §11 states the one physics result OFS keeps. Aberration is a map of addresses, z ↦ z/k in the stereographic chart,
a Möbius map of the celestial sphere (Penrose 1959; Terrell 1959). A sky uniform in its own frame is seen as the spherical
Cauchy, density (1 − β²)/(4π(1 − β cos θ′)²) = D²/4π (Kato and McCullagh 2020), with parameter ρ = tanh(η/2). Doppler
shift and beaming are a weighting of the payload, moving no address. Of the D⁴ by which an isotropic sky brightens ahead,
D² is the addresses' crowding and D² the payload. On the levels ξ = −ln tan(θ/2), a uniform sky lies as ½·sech²ξ, and
seen from the moving frame its median is moved to η: the rapidity is read from directions alone, with no clock, no
frequency and no c.

Two more readings of the same sky:
- **A thermal sky.** Each direction a blackbody at T₀·D; the hottest over the coldest is exactly (1 + β)/(1 − β) = k²
  at every speed, a reading, while a linear dipole fit reads β = 0.6 as 0.67 (Peebles and Wilkinson 1968).
- **Radar.** Two echo times give the Doppler factor as their ratio, t₂/t₁ = k², and the interval as their product,
  t₁t₂ = τ²: a common rescale of both stamps leaves the ratio and moves the product (Bondi 1964). One pair of stamps, a
  reading and a writing.

### 6.6 The quasar dipole [data]

The boost is read from directions alone only for a census taken whatever the brightness, and every real census is cut by
flux. With no flux limit a moving reader's census is D²/4π, its dipole 2β + (2/5)β³ + …, angles only: Ellis and Baldwin's
"2" is the addresses'. Cut at a flux limit it is D^(2 + x(1 + α)), where x is the counts' slope and α a two-band ratio, both
read from the same catalogue: the payload's weighting, still a reading, needing no rest frequency or distance, but no
longer of directions alone. On Secrest et al.'s CatWISE quasars (2021) the payload's term is two thirds of the 0.0074
expected.

Then the data. Four disjoint brightness slices give one β, (3.31 ± 0.27) × 10⁻³, 2.7 times the cosmic microwave
background's, as one boost should. The three colour terciles do not agree (p = 2.6 × 10⁻⁴; the reddest third 60° off),
which no boost can do, so part of the dipole is the sources' own anisotropy or a selection that depends on colour. The
direction lies 21–28° from the background's. These are consistent with Secrest et al.'s finding that the quasar dipole
exceeds the kinematic expectation; what the sorting adds is which part of the expected amplitude is addresses and which is
payload.

## 7. Light and the inverse square

### 7.1 The law as addresses and payload [exact]

Physics derives the inverse square from conservation and three dimensions and tests its exponent to extraordinary
precision; it is not ours to test. What the geometry says is where it lives.

1. **The paint per address does not carry the law.** Along each direction a source covers, the brightness is the same at
   every distance (the radiance theorem). The payload per address is constant.
2. **The law is in how many addresses a source covers:** its solid angle, about π(Δ/d)² far off, a reading of the pair
   (size, distance). Irradiance is the paint times that count. Distance changes the count, never the paint.
3. **An emitter's even spread, laid on a flat receiver, is the Cauchy, and the law is its height.** A source sending alike
   in every direction is the uniform turn aimed outward, the lighthouse of OFS. Laid on a flat receiver at distance d its
   power per unit area is (P/2) times the (D − 1)-dimensional Cauchy of scale d. On the axis that is the inverse
   (D − 1)-th power: 1/d² in three dimensions, 1/d in two. The receiver catches exactly half. (The cos³ law of
   illumination, by a change of variable.)
4. **The corner lives in the address count.** An isotropic line of half-length b at distance d gives
   E = (λ/4πd)·2·arctan(b/d): paint λ/(4πd) on every address it covers, falling as 1/d, and the count (2/π)·arctan(b/d),
   reciprocal-symmetric with its median exactly at b = d. Its slope in levels of light at b = d is −1.637, which is
   −1 from the paint and −2/π from the addresses.
5. **Which pair sits at its corner depends on the weighting.** An isotropic source's light on a plane puts |x|/d, |y|/d
   and |y/x| each at its corner, as OFS Proposition 5(e) has it; the radial r/d is no such pair, its median √3. A
   Lambertian emitter, weighted by the cosine, has radial median 1.
6. **The dimension.** The cosine-weighted share sin^(D−1)θ is reciprocal-symmetric only in three dimensions. D = 3 is
   singled out by Lambert's cosine, not by the turn, whose one-axis marginal is the Cauchy in every dimension.
7. **1/d² is a writing; counts by flux are a reading.** Scale every length by k with the paint fixed and every solid angle,
   irradiance and flux is unchanged. So 1/d² compares standpoints with the source's size held fixed, and needs d as an
   amount: a baseline walked or a kind carried in (a standard candle). The count of sources brighter than a flux needs no
   distance: for any homogeneous population it rises 3/2 levels per level of flux (log N–log S), and a layer's thickness
   turns it toward 1.

### 7.2 The near field turns at the corner of (size, distance) [exact]

A Lambertian disc's light on its axis has slope −2s²/(1 + s²) in levels, s = d/a: halfway at s = 1 exactly, and
symmetric about it, σ(s) + σ(1/s) = −2. So does a sphere counted from its surface. A sphere counted from its centre has
no near field. Where an angle or an unprojected solid angle is counted instead, the switch is off s = 1 and not
symmetric. The corner holds where the light counted is a share; restated in §7.1's terms, where the address count is
reciprocal-symmetric.

### 7.3 The two halves of the law [world]

Three experiments in Wander met the law's two halves.
- **A ball's share of the view.** Far off a ball of radius a fills s²/π of a reader's addresses (s = a/d), the inverse
  square counted in addresses. A reader walking toward a ball it is told nothing about reads its radius (5.0000), its
  distance (100.000) and the contact ahead (95.000) from its own travel and its count of filled addresses. The ball's own
  corner is the limb at 45°, s = 1/√2, where it fills 0.202 of the addresses; contact, s = 1, is the end of the sweep,
  not its corner [exact].
- **Past one address the law becomes a chance.** In a world without brightness a body narrower than one address fills it
  only if the address's ray happens to meet it: with chance (d*/d)², d* where it is one address across. The sky's filled
  share keeps its form 1 − e^(−reach/λ), each address a sample of the sky; a single body is seen whole in one address or
  not at all, and its size is gone.
- **With brightness, it becomes dimming.** In the ladder (§4.3), the width rung gives out where a body is one address
  across (a body of size 5 at 100,000 reads 50,000 whatever its distance, one address filled; at 400,000, none), and the
  light rung goes on past it to 0.05%. The two rungs past parallax are the two halves of the inverse square: resolved,
  and unresolved.

### 7.4 Light and number on the stars [data]

- **Two levels of light per level of distance.** On the Hipparcos stars (ESA 1997), with distance from parallax and
  kind from the spectrum: G0–G5 dwarfs within 23 pc lose 2.02 levels of light per level of distance (83 stars), K0
  giants where the catalogue is complete 1.93 (533). A known kind is a bridge about a level wide: K0 III scatters 1.4
  levels, and about one in ten so labelled is something fainter.
- **Within 100 pc, three levels of number per level of distance.** On the Gaia Catalogue of Nearby Stars (Gaia
  Collaboration, Smart et al. 2021), main-sequence stars by colour: 2.93 to 3.00 for each kind once no cut that
  depends on distance is used, plane and poles alike; by light, inside each kind's complete volume, about 1.5. The
  even spread holds within 100 pc. (Read after a run whose four predictions were killed, §12.)
- **Hipparcos counts rise 1.2 levels per level of light, not 1.5,** alike in the plane (1.229) and toward the poles
  (1.227). A count by light is a reading, with no distance in it: CNT's 1.2 reads the world's shape, between a sphere's
  1.5 and a slab's 1.0. Most of the stars counted lie past 100 pc in a thin layer of luminous kinds seen through dust;
  which of the layer and the dust does how much is untested.

The distance-based results (MAG, and GCN by distance) are writings, through parallax; the counts by light are readings.

## 8. Distance

### 8.1 Parallax from the corner [exact]

Two standpoints a baseline B apart read the parallax angle γ of a point at distance d on the bisector. The pair is
(B/2, d), with ratio k = (B/2)/d; its corner is the Thales circle on the baseline, where γ = 90°.

- The relative distance error per unit angle is cosh(ln k), least at the corner, and the same at k and 1/k; sin γ =
  sech(ln k), the central sweep's height sin 2θ (OFS §4).
- With angles read to a step ε, the band in which the relative error stays under one (ε·cosh(ln k) ≤ 1) runs
  acosh(1/ε)/ln 2 levels each side of the corner: 10.966 at ε = 10⁻³. Its far edge is about B/ε. Past it a point is only a ray: parallax needs a situated
  observation, and returns to one.
- The depth's corner is on the bisector only. Across the line of sight the error is the direction's, falling with
  distance; the two together are least at d = √2·(B/2). On the circle the error is round only at its top; elsewhere it
  is stretched by d/(B/2).

**Working back from what parallax already knows.** d = a/tan p, with the astronomical unit a measured outside the
parallaxes and now fixed by agreement. σ_d/d = σ_p/p is the far limit of the exact ratio γ/sin γ, which is π/2 on the
Thales circle. Not inverting noisy parallaxes (Bailer-Jones 2015) is the reciprocal's missing mean with the median kept:
a parallax with a Gaussian error has an inverse with no mean, and at the band's edge 16% of readings are at or below zero.
Inverting them as if they were distances is Lutz and Kelker's bias (1973): a reading taken for a writing. Of the
confusions this paper sorts, this is the one shown to cause a known difficulty.

**Turning in place gives no parallax** [exact]. A turn about the point seen from moves every direction alike and places
nothing. A head's eye sits r off its axis, so a turn Δ carries it a chord 2r·sin(Δ/2): a baseline, reaching r·sin Δ/ε.
The head (10 cm, a quarter turn) reaches about 100 m at 1 mrad; the eye alone, turning in its socket, about 4.5 m at one
arcminute.

### 8.2 Building the figure from many standpoints [simulation]

Physics constructs the complete view from situated observations: parallax, triangulation, multi-epoch astrometry, the
distance ladder. OFS Proposition 11 says why it can: each further standpoint brings back the figure, and one agreed length
its size. A figure of 20 landmarks read by 16 to 4,096 standpoints with bearing noise 10⁻³, one outside length with 1%
error, and a standard-candle rung above it:

- the figure improves with readings: the shape error falls as N^(−0.49) with a consistent (maximum-likelihood)
  construction;
- the scale does not: the common error stays at the outside length's 1% from 16 readers to 4,096, and by 4,096 the
  landmarks' errors are 99% correlated, one shift in levels shared by all;
- a ladder carries its calibration up: the candle rung's error is √(0.01² + 0.0112²) = 0.0150, whether it has a hundred
  far members or ten thousand.

Statistical error, which more readings average down, against systematic calibration error, which they do not, is address
against payload. All known in metrology and astronomy; the sorting is OFS's.

### 8.3 The distance ladder as a tower [data]

A rung's error is one factor, a shift in levels, only if it is flat against an independent distance. On Cosmicflows-4
(Tully et al. 2023), Hubble's rungs (Hubble 1929) are not (slopes 0.79 and 0.16 against 1): his brightest-star rung is a
stretch, a ruler of another layout, not a factor. A drift that fails to close between two rungs belongs to the sample
they share, not to the rungs: the apparent drift of the fundamental plane and surface-brightness fluctuations against
type Ia supernovae is their 58 shared galaxies', and away from that overlap neither drifts more than 0.016 mag per mag.
A network of loops whose routes cross different objects locates a misread link; a single comparison cannot. The angles,
flux ratios of one kind and ratios between rungs are readings; the astronomical unit, the anchors, every megaparsec and
H₀ are writings.

## 9. Waves and the qubit

### 9.1 The qubit is the two sweeps [exact]

A qubit's state is a ray with amplitudes h and v: a direction at θ, the inscribed reading. Its Bloch point is at 2θ,
(2hv, 0, h² − v²) on the real slice: the central sweep's point. Born's rule is the foot h². Orthogonal states are
antipodal on the sphere; a turn of the state by 2π gives −ψ, so 720° returns it; the complex ratio ψ₁/ψ₀ is the Bloch
sphere's stereographic chart; a pure state is on the sphere and a mixture inside. All textbook; the reading is OFS's.
Entanglement, Bell's theorem (Bell 1964) and measurement are not touched.

### 9.2 Interference's duality relation is the facing [exact, data]

On the unit circle sin 2θ = 2hv and cos 2θ = h² − v². With h and v two paths' amplitudes, the fringe visibility is at most
2hv and the which-path predictability is |h² − v²|, so V² + P² ≤ 1, with equality for a pure state (Greenberger and Yasin
1988; Englert 1996). Full visibility comes only at the corner, and 2hv is sech of the levels between the two amplitudes,
already in print as V = sech θ, P = tanh θ (Bramon, Garbarino and Hiesmayr 2004). The bridge is the Born rule: an
amplitude is the square root of a share of the counts.

- **Single photons** [data]. On Aspden, Padgett and Spalding's frames (2016), a double slit lit one heralded photon at a
  time: the fringe period fλ/d to 0.3% and 1.7%; the photons independent draws from one pattern that does not drift; the
  two slits' shares give 2hv = 0.997 and 1.000, the pair at its corner; the contrast, 0.69 and 0.56, below that bound,
  short by the source's coherence.
- **Neutrons** [data]. Summhammer, Rauch and Tuppinger (1987), one path attenuated to transmission a, read from their
  figure. Foils (each neutron passes with probability a: a pure pair, amplitudes 1 and √a) keep √a of the fringe
  amplitude, six points from a = 0.05 to 0.47 within 1.3σ; a chopper open a share a of the time (a mixture) keeps a,
  three points within 0.6σ. At a ≈ 0.24 the foil keeps 0.447 and the chopper 0.243, 45σ apart at the same counts. On the
  duality circle the foil is on it and the chopper inside. The thinnest gadolinium foils fall below √a, which the paper
  ascribes to uneven thickness.

### 9.3 Weak values and missing frames [exact]

- **A weak value is an address** (Aharonov, Albert and Vaidman 1988). A state pre-selected at angle θ_i and
  post-selected at θ_f has the weak value of σz ⟨σz⟩ − V·tan(θ_f − θ_i), where ⟨σz⟩ = h² − v² = cos 2θ_i and V = 2hv =
  sin 2θ_i. Under a uniform turn of the post-selection it follows the Cauchy at ⟨σz⟩ + iV: the inscribed reading, laid
  by the turn. Its share past the corner ("superweak") is ½ for a pure state. Born-weighted, its mean is ⟨σz⟩ and its
  variance V², so V² + P² = ⟨σz²⟩ = 1: the central point's height and foot.
- **A missing frame is a twirl.** Without a shared phase reference (Bartlett, Rudolph and Spekkens 2007) the reader
  averages over what it cannot fix, which is Jaynes's group, the method OFS builds on. The twirl is an operation on the
  payload: it lowers the ensemble's visibility and changes no member's. Without an orientation, one qubit is I/2 and two
  copies keep only their overlap (1 + n₁·n₂)/2. Two Gaussians' overlap is 2k/(1 + k²) = sech(ln k), the reference width
  a zoom.

### 9.4 The quantum experiments in the built world [world]

The three in §4.3 sort what readers' records can tell apart. Alone, a reader's clicks are an even share at every setting,
whatever the other reader does. In company, joined only by the rhythm of the clicks, payloads held in advance and payloads
drawn on demand are told apart (|S| = 2.001 against 2.840), in company and only in company. A single reader holds clicks,
never the amplitude: relative phases are recovered from counts, the overall phase is convention. And identity differs from
the classical case (§10.1): identical balls can be followed, identical quanta cannot, and the readers find that by counting
coincidences, not by following.

## 10. Many bodies

### 10.1 Heat, and Gibbs [world]

Temperature, equilibrium and the second law are usually set out over every particle and every state at once. In the
built world, 300 balls touching in a sphere were read by a reader outside it, from the rate at which each ball's tags
arrive, which gives its speed along the line. The speeds started flat and became a bell; temperature read to 3–4%; the
spread of what the reader lays on its sky rose by 0.63–0.64, against ln 2, when balls released into half the sphere filled
the whole, though every touch can be run backward. The spread's zero is set by the reader's grain, and passes the test for
convention; its rise is the world's, the same at every grain the count can fill.

Gibbs, from the same arrivals: a reader that keeps the balls' tracks apart reads a rise of about ln 2 per ball as two
halves mix, and one that only counts reads none. Whether tracks can be kept apart was then earned, not assumed. Laid at
depth and read faster than the balls touch, every identical ball was kept on its track through some 1,500 touches,
whatever depth the reader guessed; on the sky only, 80%; read slowly, none. So whether two batches have mixed, in a sense
that costs or yields anything, depends on what the reader can follow: a parameter of the reader, earned from arrivals.
Identical quanta cannot be followed at any rate, and there the built world cannot follow physics.

### 10.2 Cooling counted in contacts [simulation]

Five hundred smooth inelastic spheres, contact found where h + v = 1. Per contact per sphere the temperature falls by
(1 − e²)/3 of itself (with the known correction) to 0.8% wherever the gas stays even (Haff 1983). Density, diameter and
pair correlation cancel from the count; the clock needs them all, and near the clustering instability goes wrong about
twice as far as the count does. A heap at rest: without friction a sphere touches 6.18 others (Maxwell's 2d = 6), with
friction 4.74.

### 10.3 Contact read from inside [exact]

Each ball reads another as v = r_b/d and is told its reading h = r_a/d: radii over the distance between centres, one unit,
so unit relations with no bridge. Contact is h + v = 1, the sum cut of two standpoints' readings; equal balls touch at the
corner, h = v = ½. A cushion is a ball meeting its own mirror image, always at the corner. A contact turns an aim error by
s/(2r·cos φ); for a full hit, by 2^L − 1, where the pair started L levels apart. Played from inside, each ball in its own
size, a game of pool equals the referee's to rounding, and doubling the world moves no reading and no contact.

### 10.4 Holding a world by level of detail [simulation]

A hard-disc gas held as a window of discs round the reader and, beyond it, totals in cells whose breadth grows with
their distance. Against a referee computing every disc (Enskog's contact rate to 0.1%, Rayleigh's speeds, Henderson's
equation of state, Henderson 1975) the view's density, warmth and contacts were the referee's within 0.3%, for the work
of the window, for a world 256 times larger. It needed what open systems already need: the outside's pressure, a
reservoir at fixed chemical potential, and books kept where pushes balance. And it lost a pulse travelling toward the
reader: folding the payload into cells by distance is an irreversible mean. A signal must be held at its own grain, or
its kind carried in, whatever its time to arrival; adaptive mesh refinement already does this by gradients.

### 10.5 Ranging is writing [simulation]

A lumpy moon crossing the view of a reader at home. With a range on each detected direction (the signal's round trip
times its speed, an amount carried in) the face is read to 4% and distance and speed to 0.25%. With directions alone the
outline is free: the visual hull (Laurentini 1994) contains the moon, is tight where an edge touched it, keeps none of the
face elsewhere, and does not move when the world is scaled.

## 11. 2/π [exact]

The constant 2/π turns up across physics. Sixty-five occurrences were gathered from about forty fields (Buffon's needle,
AC meters, one-bit correlators, Kendall's τ, Onsager's Ising energy, Fabry–Pérot linewidths, the square wave's
fundamental, Hertz's contact, a disc's capacitance, Wigner's semicircle, and more), and for 62 of them the constant
computed from the step where π enters matches its source. Sorted:

| what 2/π is there | how many | what it needs |
|---|---|---|
| a mean over the uniform turn's directions of a bounded amount (the projection cos θ, or the central height sin 2θ) | 34 | the turn's uniformity; any other fair weight moves it |
| the turn's share below a direction, (2/π)θ | 10 | at the corner, only the exchange; elsewhere the uniform turn |
| the turn's length, or a square's mean ½ | 8 | nothing: 2/π under every fair weight |
| the circle as a shape (a semicircle's area, the arcsine integral) | 7 | an extent given in advance |
| other (a pole; a dimension; a convention) | 6 | |

So 2/π is not one thing, and a single 2/π does not fingerprint the uniform turn. The address reader of OFS holds shares
and medians, not means, so most of these 2/π belong to the payload's side: a mean taken over the turn. The even turn shows
instead in every projection's mean being 2/π at every offset. OFS's π/2 · 2/π = 1, the two sweeps' readings of one line
whose product returns the reader's own H, is one more: a ratio of two sums over the turn.

## 12. What failed, and what it taught

Predictions are written before the runs, so some fail. Each kill below was placed: on the world, on the reading, or on the
set-up.

- **Wander's bodies are not real worlds.** Its Moon spans 0.25 levels of relief against the real Moon's 0.0165. Under the
  rules of §1.2 this was never evidence; it is kept as the example of what not to count.
- **Real relief does not fill a reader's levels evenly.** On seven published shape models, relief per octave of degree
  was predicted to follow one power law at half per octave on every body. Earth, Mars and Venus hold near power laws
  (slopes −0.34 to −0.44); the airless bodies bend, their relief standing highest at a tenth to a fortieth of the radius.
  The share in each octave is the world's.
- **2/π is not always a turn** (§11): eight of sixty-five come another way.
- **Star counts.** The disc was predicted to flatten counts toward the poles more than in the plane; both give 1.23. On
  Gaia within 100 pc all four predictions failed: a quality cut on the astrometry removes more near stars than far ones
  and steepened the counts, and a fifth of the G and K colours within 50 pc are white dwarfs. Colour alone is not a kind.
- **The near field, counted otherwise.** A line counted from every direction, and an isotropic emitter on a flat patch,
  switch off s = 1 and are not symmetric; §7.1 explains why (the paint's slope mixes in).
- **The quasar dipole.** The colour terciles disagree and the direction is 21–28° off (§6.6); one estimator of mine missed
  the published amplitude by 22%.
- **The cosmic distance ladder.** Hubble's rungs are not one factor each (§8.3).
- **The elevator in the medium.** Along the line of fall the reading was predicted term by term and missed narrowly
  (§5.4); a full expansion predicted before a run is still owed.
- **Rømer's first run.** A moon's eclipses under the world's stepped gravity kept time too poorly (the orbit precesses);
  a steady beacon replaced it, and the speed came out exact.
- **A star's wobble, from near by.** The tilted row was 1.33% off; the cause, worked out after two wrong fixes, is
  nearness, an error of order (a/D)² that vanishes for planets round other stars. Laying the loop at its depth removed it.
- **Weighing from close by.** A loop not quite round, laid flat on a sheet at the star's distance, reads its near side
  large from within about one and a half reaches; laying each arrival at its own depth from the tags cured it, and the
  reader must not learn the signals' speed while itself going round a star.
- **The distance ladder's first run.** Parallax did not give out where predicted: a reader's own travel steps each held
  direction back and forth across its grain, and the fit reads through it while the shift spans a few addresses. That,
  not the grain alone, is why a ladder needs a second rung.
- **Holding a pulse by distance.** Three ways of carrying a travelling signal in cells by distance all lost it (§10.4).
- **The trick-shot engine.** A constant grip between balls was killed by the published fit, which falls threefold from slow
  to fast.
- **Least squares on bearing lines.** It left a floor on the shape error that more readings did not remove (errors in the
  variables); maximum likelihood removed it. A construction can carry a systematic of its own.
- **First-order stereo.** The worst error is x/(1 − x) on the far side, unbounded at d = 2B/ε, the baseline's horizon.
- **An angle is not exact with a call.** A double holds an angle in even steps and the ratio it stands for in steps
  growing with the ratio; the unit relation capped at 1 holds it to 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁶ at every size.
- **Grid cells.** Three of the first run's predictions failed by too few animals (§13).

Other kills were mine: a wrong derivation, a tolerance under the sampling error, a test leaving out a term. Each stays
recorded beside the run that replaced it.

## 13. Open, and queued

- **A registered prediction: Ω_m = 1/π.** An older draft (23 September 2026) predicted the matter fraction
  Ω_m = 1/π = 0.3183, with Ω_Λ = 1 − 1/π and w = −1, and through Friedmann's equations z_eq = (π − 1)^⅓ − 1 = 0.2890 and
  z_acc = (2(π − 1))^⅓ − 1 = 0.6240. Its derivation is not accepted here: it multiplies 2/π and ½, the mean projection of a
  uniform direction in two dimensions and in three, as if they were filters in sequence. But the numbers were written down
  before the next surveys report, so they are a fair test whatever their origin. Planck 2018 gives Ω_m = 0.3153 ± 0.0073,
  0.4σ from 1/π; DESI's second data release's BAO alone gives 0.2975 ± 0.0086, 2.4σ below. Against DESI's five-year BAO
  with Euclid (2027) and Roman's and Rubin's supernovae and growth (2027–2028): if the best combination sits more than 3σ
  from 1/π, the number is killed.
- **The step between grid-cell modules.** Grid cells in the entorhinal cortex map place in discrete modules, each coarser
  than the last by a measured ratio no physical law sets. On Gardner et al.'s recordings (2022) the pooled adjacent ratio
  is 1.432 in two rats, near √2, with Stensola et al.'s published mean of about 1.42 known beforehand. √2 is a doubling of
  area counted on a proportional plane; OFS's layout doubles distance, 2 in every dimension, so counting by area is itself
  the choice. Five or more animals with several modules each are needed to tell √2, √e and the turn's layout apart.
- **Why the counts give 1.2** (§7.4): a bright all-sky Gaia sample with extinctions, its slope by height above the plane.
- **The contrast against the slits' shares.** A double slit with one slit partly covered, recorded photon by photon with
  the shares measured, should lose contrast as sech of the levels between the amplitudes, the same at k and 1/k. The
  duality relation is long tested; what would be ours is reading it in levels.
- **The ratio or the angle, in a measured reader.** x/ẋ gives time to contact at every distance; θ/θ̇ overstates it by π/2
  at the corner. Responses to looming that run on to large visual angles would tell which an animal uses there.
- **A calibration as one shift.** A published parallax zero-point should appear as one common shift in levels across a
  catalogue's distances, not shrinking with the number of stars (§8.2).
- **The elevator's expansion** (§5.4), predicted in full before a run.

## 14. Discussion

**Nothing here is new physics.** Every result above is either classical or a reader's recovery, in a built world, of
something classical. What the geometry contributes is a single question asked of each quantity: if the whole world were
scaled, or the frame changed, or the grain, would this reading move? Asked of special relativity, the question separates
aberration (addresses) from Doppler shift and beaming (payload) inside one formula. Asked of the inverse square, it puts
the law in the count of directions and leaves the brightness per direction constant. Asked of a parallax, it separates the
angle read from the distance written, and the confusion of the two is a bias astronomers already correct.

**The labs and OFS.** The experiments in Wander predate OFS. Re-read with it, they fall into place. What the reader
recovered in its own lengths, from its own path and clock, is reading. What it could not recover is what a writer carries
in: the unit, a standard, a frame chosen, a grain. OFS's Proposition 8 says that no number of standpoints fixes the scale,
and the weighing chain run on three rungs is that proposition run end to end through Kepler's law: every reading agreed to
0.009%, and the one thing that told the rungs apart was the signals' speed, the amount the world holds fixed. OFS's
"recovered, not written" says that a reader can recover a thing exactly and still not know it has all of it; the elevator
in Wander's medium is a case of exact recovery whose last terms remain open.

**What the test for convention adds.** OFS's scale test is one change applied to the whole world. The experiments
generalised it to any change, and found that the conventions are exactly the changes that move no reading. In a world with
a rest, the frame and the now move readings, and are measured; in a world without one they do not, and are conventions.
That is a statement about the readings, made from the readings, which is the only place OFS lets a reader stand.

**Limits.** Wander is a flight game's world: its gravity is Newton's, softened; its signals cross a medium or, with the
switch, obey Lorentz's change of frame by construction; it has no general relativity, no fields, and quantum outcomes only
as a built-in draw. The data runs are few, some on two animals or one catalogue. The exact results are, almost all,
classical identities given a placement. Where the placement goes past what is shown, the text says "reading" or "open".
Whether any of this is how physics should be taught or done is not claimed; only that the boundary between reading and
writing can be drawn, quantity by quantity, and that physics already sits on both sides of it.
