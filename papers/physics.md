# Reader geometry and physics

*Started 10 October 2026, at Tom's word: "lets create a new physics paper and iterate there." A working paper: a reading,
revised as it is worked over, not a ruling. It takes its terms from SPN (`papers/serial-parallel-nowhere.md`), and §1
gives what is needed to read it alone. Each result here names its plan in `plans/`, where its prediction was written
before its run. A result goes into SPN only once it has been tested and ruled.*

**What this paper is for** (Tom, 10 October: "wanderer priority was user experience, not physics. the physics then
became unexpextedly interesting. we should do the correct physics testing, UI is secondary or even not needed"). SPN is
geometry: unit relations, the sweep, the corner, the levels. Physics is amounts: distances, sizes, light, radii. This paper
collects where the two meet, and tests each meeting on real measurements or on exact arithmetic. The flying page and its
bodies are pictures. They are never evidence here.

## How physics holds the inverse square (10 October, at Tom's "lets make sure we solid with the math")

Moved to its own paper, `papers/inverse-square.md` (Tom, 10 October: "lets give the inverse square law its own paper to
iterate on"). In brief: physics derives the law from conservation and three dimensions and tests its exponent (within
about 10⁻¹⁶ of 2 for the electric force), so the law is not ours to test. What the corpus tests is its bookkeeping in
levels (MAG, GCN) and where its geometry meets the law's edges: the near field (NRF) and the lay's form (ISQ).

## 0. The rules

- **Evidence is real data or exact arithmetic.** Published measurements (catalogues, fact sheets, shape models), or
  arithmetic checked to the last digit. Wander's world, the flying page, its bodies and the labs that run inside it,
  illustrate. A lab in Wander's world shows that a reader *could* recover something; it does not show that the world
  *is* so.
- **Prediction first, from the theory alone.** The plan is committed before the data is opened for anything but its
  column names. A killed prediction stays killed, and a fix is a new run with its own prediction.
- **Keep the case, and name the bridge.** h and v are unit relations, scale-free: geometry. H, V, d, Δ, L and R are
  amounts: physics. A result crosses from one to the other only by a named bridge, an amount supplied from outside: a
  size, a radius, a kind (SPN Part II).
- **Data.** Each dataset's source, licence and sha256 go in its plan, and large data is not committed. In this
  environment the astronomy archives (VizieR, CDS, ESA, NASA's PDS) are blocked; GitHub and PyPI are reachable.

## 2. Results, by standing

### 2.1 On real data

- **Two levels of light per level of distance** (MAG, `plans/mag-plan.md`). On the Hipparcos stars, with distance from
  parallax and kind from the spectrum:
  - G0–G5 dwarfs within 23 pc lose 2.02 levels of light per level of distance (83 stars);
  - K0 giants where the catalogue is complete lose 1.93 (533 stars);
  - past the catalogue's limit the slope flattens to 1.61.
  This is the inverse square read in levels.
- **Star counts rise 1.2 levels per level of light, not 1.5** (CNT, `plans/cnt-plan.md`). For an even spread the
  inverse square and the cube of the volume give 1.5 levels of number per level of light. The Hipparcos stars brighter
  than V = 7.3 give about 1.2 at every brightness: 1.33 for V 1–4, 1.22 for V 5–7.3. And they give it alike in the
  galactic plane (1.229) and toward the poles (1.227). The even-spread bridge gives out, and the disc's thinness alone
  does not say why.
- **Within 100 pc, three levels of number per level of distance** (GCN, `plans/gcn-plan.md`, read after run 1). On the
  Gaia Catalogue of Nearby Stars, main-sequence stars picked by colour on the band (G, K, early M, late M; about 125,000):
  - by distance over 25–100 pc, 2.93 to 3.00 for each kind once no cut that depends on distance is used, against 3 for
    an even spread; plane and poles alike (2.937, 2.924);
  - by light, inside each kind's complete volume, 1.37 to 1.57, about 1.5 on average, against 1.5.

  The run's own predictions were killed (§3); these are readings of the same data after it, not ruled. They say the even
  spread holds within 100 pc, kind by kind, so CNT's 1.2 is not in the counting itself but in the spread past 100 pc.
- **The step between grid-cell modules is near √2, in two rats** (GRD run 1, `plans/grd-plan.md`). On Gardner et al.
  2022's recordings (figshare, CC0; rats Q, R, S), each cell's grid spacing from its rate map's autocorrelogram, by a
  method fixed before the data was opened. Modules: Q 68.0 and 95.0 cm; R 56.2, 83.6 and 118.0 cm; S one module.
  - Pooled adjacent ratio 1.432 (P1 not killed, in the √2 band; √e, 1.7, e and 2 far off; 3/2 0.07 away).
  - P2 and P3 killed by their own minimum counts (one rat with three modules, two with two).
  - Post hoc, rat R's second day: the first step repeats (1.487, 1.491), the step to the largest module does not
    (1.412, 1.141). The largest module, about 0.8 of the 1.5 m box, is not measured reliably by this method.

  Two rats, the reliable steps 1.40 and 1.49: consistent with a halving of a map's area, D.1's 2^(1/D) with D = 2, and
  with Stensola et al.'s published mean of about 1.42, which was known beforehand. Weak evidence; more animals needed.
- **Real worlds are their corner within a hundredth of a level** (REF, `plans/ref-plan.md`, P4 and P5). By published
  radii:
  - the rocky worlds' flattening: Earth 0.0048 levels, Mars 0.0085, the Moon 0.0017;
  - the spinning giants about a tenth: Jupiter 0.097, Saturn 0.149;
  - Earth's relief, Challenger Deep to Everest, 0.0045; with its flattening the whole Earth is within 0.0093 levels.
- **A reader drops one octave of a world's relief per level of distance; how much each octave holds is the world's**
  (REF run 2, `plans/ref-plan.md`). Published spherical-harmonic models of seven physical surfaces (Mercury, Venus,
  Earth, the Moon, Mars, Vesta, Ceres), relief by octave of degree from 8 to 1023:
  - Earth, Mars and Venus hold one power law over six or seven octaves (within 0.11, 0.11, 0.25 levels), each octave
    about 0.4 levels of relief below the one before (slopes −0.44, −0.42, −0.34; Earth's bedrock as its surface);
  - the airless bodies do not: the Moon's relief per octave rises from degree 16 to 127 and Mercury's rises again at 64
    to 127, then both fall ever faster, and Vesta and Ceres steepen throughout; on all four degrees 64 to 255
    (wavelengths a tenth to a fortieth of the radius) stand above the body's line, the same octaves in the body's own
    size from 260 to 2,440 km (post hoc);
  - at the level where a band is first held its relief is 0.0002 to 0.07 of one of the reader's addresses, so dropping
    bands by distance moves no world's outline by as much as an address; on five of the seven each nearer level shows
    more relief in the reader's addresses, not the same.

  The octave packing is a reader's, done ahead of time in the models (a pre-read signal, SPN R250: a real signal
  arrives unpacked); the share in each octave is read from the world, not fixed by the geometry.

### 2.2 Exact arithmetic

- **Where 2/π shows up** (TPI, `plans/tpi-plan.md`). Sixty-three occurrences across about forty fields, from Buffon's
  needle and Viète to AC meters, one-bit correlators, Kendall's τ, the Lebesgue constant, Onsager's Ising energy, an
  infinite resistor grid, Fabry–Pérot linewidths, the square wave's fundamental in human contrast thresholds and the
  Kosterlitz–Thouless jump. Every constant computed from the step of its derivation where π enters matches its source
  (63 of 63, mostly to 40 digits). Fifty-four come from a turn taken evenly (an orientation, a phase, the equal-spread
  normal pair, a Haar measure) and read as something never negative, the inner reading of OFS §3.4. The other eight
  are the circle held as a shape (a semicircle's area or the arcsine integral, in contact, cracks, a disc's capacitance,
  faults, random matrices, stellar line profiles) and one pole (alternating permutations). In space the shadow on an
  axis gives ½, but the relative angle π/4 and the half-angle chord 2/3.

- **The lay has the inverse square's form** (ISQ, `plans/isq-plan.md`). Past the corner the lay is proportion in h/v, so
  its room per unit s is 1/s², the flip's Jacobian, exactly. Each level gets 2^−(k+1) of the room.
- **Standing on the outline is standing on a world** (HZN, `plans/hzn-plan.md`). From a place on a circle the readings
  are chords 2 sin φ, and:
  - the reader's corner (the geometric mean) is the radius, 30° below the horizon;
  - on a ball it is 2/e of the radius, 21.6° below;
  - rising by e radii, the horizon is √(2e) radii off, half a level per doubling of height. With R given, that is
    √(2Rh).

- **The near field turns at the corner of the pair (size, distance), where the light is a share** (NRF,
  `plans/nrf-plan.md`). In levels the slope of a Lambertian disc's light on its axis is −2·s²/(1 + s²), s = d/a:
  - halfway at s = 1 exactly, and flip-symmetric about it, σ(s) + σ(1/s) = −2;
  - the same for a line on a flat patch, and for a sphere counted from its surface (halfway at a gap of one radius);
  - a sphere counted from its centre has no near field;
  - where an angle or an unprojected solid angle is counted instead (P3, P4), the switch is off s = 1 and not symmetric.

  The projected solid angle, the standard measure of irradiance, makes the light a share of a two-part split, the form
  `near-far-classification.md` calls fair to the facings.
- **Contact read from inside, and what a contact costs** (POL, `plans/pol-plan.md`, runs 1, 2 and 9). Each ball reads
  another as v = r_b/d and is told its reading h = r_a/d: radii over the distance between centres, one unit, so fill
  levels with no bridge. Then:
  - contact is h + v = 1, an identity; equal balls touch at the corner, h = v = ½;
  - a cushion is a ball meeting its own mirror image, v = h, so always at the corner; a pocket is entered at v = 1;
  - a contact turns an aim error by s/(2r·cos φ), and for a full hit by 2^L − 1, where h + v = 2^−L at the start: the
    levels a pair was apart become levels of aim lost, to rounding once the moment h + v = 1 is found exactly;
  - skipping a contact with a ball j levels smaller (radius halved per level) leaves out (1 + e)/(1 + 8^j) of the
    closing speed (run 7).

  These are classical mechanics of hard balls, read in unit relations. They are not new physics; what they give is the
  count of levels, which the situated reader can use directly (a preview about three contacts deep on an open table).
- **Motion read from inside** (MOT, `plans/mot-plan.md`). A thing of half-size S at distance d closes at speed u; the
  reader reads x = S/d (the pair's v/h) and holds one amount, its clock. Then, to rounding or exactly:
  - **time to contact is the reading over its rate**, x/ẋ = d/u, before and past the corner, with no S, d or u known;
  - **read from the turn** arctan x instead, it is d/u times arctan(x)(1 + x²)/x: 1 far off, π/2 at the corner,
    growing as (π/2)·x past it (for a flat thing face-on);
  - **two looks a tick apart**: x₁/(x₂ − x₁) is τ at the second look exactly, and x₂/(x₂ − x₁) is τ one tick ago. This
    is POL run 10's kick reading 8% low: at τ = 10 ticks, one tick late reads it 9% low, before the cloth's slowing;
  - **holding the corner**: a reader that re-sets its unit to keep the thing at its corner turns it at d ln H/dt = 1/τ.
    Each level of the approach takes half the τ read at its start, so infinitely many levels pass before contact, in the
    finite time d₀/u (the walk's levels, in time);
  - **how close it will pass**: with ω the bearing's rate, m/d = ωτ/√(1 + (ωτ)²). ωτ is the reading of the pair (speed
    across, speed in), the clock cancelling, so this needs no bridge. At its corner the pass is 1/√2 of the present
    distance; a bearing that holds still is a collision;
  - **a breadth as a baseline**: two looks from the ends of a breadth B read the convergence γ, and d = (B/2)/tan(γ/2).
    Per unit of γ, ln d moves by 1/sin γ:
    - least at γ = 90°, where d = B/2, the corner of the pair (half-baseline, distance);
    - alike at d and its flip (B/2)²/d;
    - with γ read to a step ε, a thing at a tenth of the corner's distance and one ten times past it both have five
      times the corner's worst relative error;
    - far past the corner the worst error is x/(1 − x) on the far side (x = εd/2B), unbounded at d = 2B/ε, the
      baseline's horizon (an amount, the step).

  These are known as kinematics: Lee's τ, the navigators' constant bearing, stereo error, and surveying's right angle of
  intersection. What the run settles is which reading carries each exactly. The ratio does at every distance, the turn
  only far off, and the older of two looks. The corner is where a baseline reads best. That is SPN's "H is a zoom"
  (Proposition 1.2): set H to the scale of the thing read, putting it at the corner. Here the baseline is H.
- **Motion's corners, by kind** (MOT run 2, `plans/mot-plan.md`). Take a quantity of a pair through its ratio k:
  - **Unchanged when the pair swaps** (f(k) = f(1/k)): then f′(1) = 0, so the quantity is stationary at the corner;
    in levels it is even about it. Examples:
    - the range of a launch at fixed speed, against V_y/V_x: (2U²/g)·k/(1 + k²), longest at 45°, complementary launches
      equal;
    - the energy a head-on elastic collision passes to a ball at rest, against the mass ratio: 4k/(1 + k)², all of it
      at equal masses;
    - a baseline's conditioning, sin γ = 2k/(1 + k²) with k = b/d, best when the half-baseline equals the distance.

    The first and third are one form, the sine of the facing 2θ written in the ratio: a launch's range is (U²/g) times
    the sine of its facing.
  - **Sign flipped when the pair swaps:** then the quantity changes regime at the corner. Braking is the example. With
    stopping distance s = u²/(2a), τ̇ + 1 = d/(2s) exactly, so Lee's critical τ̇ = −½ is s = d, the pair (stopping
    distance, distance) at its corner. Below it the thing stops short, above it it overshoots. The ratio reader computes
    τ̇ = 1 − x·ẍ/ẋ² with no call, exactly (in rationals, 10⁴ states, none wrong). Judged from the angle with no call
    (θ/θ̇), the same test is right far off (1 wrong in 2,000), fails as the corner nears (182 in 2,000), and is a coin
    toss past it (996).
  - **Neither:** a launch's time of flight and greatest height rise with k throughout, with no corner.

  Three readers of one pair (b, d), each to one step, all for the same quantity, distance:
  - the angle is least wrong at the corner and alike at d and b²/d;
  - the unit relation capped at 1 likewise, and twice as good at the corner;
  - the plain uncapped ratio has no best place: it reads near home finely, but only over an unbounded range of
    addresses.

  At one budget of addresses covering every distance, the in-place lay (even in the unit relation on each side) reads
  best between 0.52 b and 1.91 b, by π/2 at the corner. The even turn, the lay that gives the Cauchy, reads best
  outside that, by 4/π far off. A ratio reader needs no trig for time to contact, stereo, closest approach, the
  collision test or braking. An angle reader needs a call for each (the collision test aside), and even then holds a
  big ratio poorly. A double spaces an angle evenly near 90°, so the ratio comes back held to ulp·(1 + x²)/x: 2e-13 at
  x = 1,000. That is the same reader with its step one ulp. Folding sends both ends toward 0, where a double is
  finest. Angles stay simplest only for advancing a steady turn. There the capped pair, rotated by two fixed constants
  and divided by its larger part, does as well with no call a step, stays bounded, and held 1e-13 where the angle
  drifted 1e-10.

  All of these are known as physics: complementary launches, equal masses, Lee's −½, rectified stereo, and Wildberger's
  rational trigonometry (spreads in place of angles). What the vocabulary adds is the account: which pair, which
  corner, which kind, and that the unit relation capped at the corner keeps both the ratio's algebra and the angle's
  best-conditioned middle.
- **Special relativity in unit relations** (MOT run 3, `plans/mot-plan.md`; after the archived *Special Relativity,
  Through the Fisheye*). With c as the bridge, v = β and h = 1/γ are unit relations, v² + h² = 1, and the reading v/h is
  the celerity. The Doppler factor k is a ratio of two of the reader's own intervals and needs no bridge; its levels,
  ln k, are the rapidity. Exactly, or to rounding:
  - **Three pairs, three corners:**
    - rest, for the Doppler pair (k ↔ 1/k is approach against recession);
    - β = 1/√2, for (β, 1/γ), equivalently (pc, mc²): k = 1 + √2, rapidity asinh 1 = 0.881374;
    - the transverse plane, for the sky's (fore, aft) (tan(θ/2) ↔ its reciprocal).

    A boost moves only the sky's corner: the moving observer's transverse plane is the rest frame's direction with
    tan(θ/2) = k. At the (β, 1/γ) corner that is 135°, and the forward hemisphere is seen within 45°.
  - **The two kinds** (run 2), in the Doppler pair's levels: γ = (k + 1/k)/2 is the even part of k and γβ = (k − 1/k)/2
    the odd part, so k = γ + γβ. Time dilation is unchanged when sender and receiver swap, and stationary at rest;
    celerity changes sign there. 1/γ = 2k/(1 + k²) is the facing's sine, the form of a launch's range; with
    k = tan(F/2), β = −cos F and 1/γ = sin F. This was exact in rationals for 10⁴ k.
  - **Rapidity is the reader's lay of celerity, smoothed:** proportional at home, logarithmic far off, with the lay's
    slopes at both ends, and never more than 1 − ln 2 nats (0.44 doublings) from it. The gap is 0.119 at the corner and
    rises throughout.
  - **A direction's level is its pseudorapidity.** A boost along the axis pans every level by ln k; fore and aft are the
    flip; a massless particle's rapidity equals it.
  - **The regime switch is the corner of (pc, mc²).** The energy's level-slope against momentum is β², a share of the
    circle: ½ at the corner and s(x) + s(1/x) = 1. The kinetic energy's is not a share: 2 at home, 1 far off, 1 + 1/√2
    at the corner, its swap sum running from 3 to 2 + √2. As NRF found for light, the switch is symmetric where the
    quantity is a share.
  - **Levels under acceleration:** with uniform proper acceleration a from rest, level n (k = 2ⁿ) comes at proper time
    n·ln 2/a, evenly. In the launch frame each level takes twice the last, while an approach at constant speed (run 1,
    P4) takes half.

  Known: Bondi's k-calculus, rapidity's additivity, pseudorapidity, the two regimes either side of pc ~ mc², hyperbolic
  motion. What the vocabulary adds is the account: three named pairs, their corners and kinds, the energy circle as the
  reason the switch is symmetric, and rapidity as the reader's lay.
- **E² = (pc)² + (mc²)² through the corner** (EMC, `plans/emc-plan.md`). Dividing by E gives the unit circle of the
  pair (pc, mc²), both read in the one unit E. Its unit relations are v = pc/E, which is β (the speed from dE/dp, to
  4.4e-16), and h = mc²/E, which is 1/γ, the moving clock's rate; a clock runs at the rest share of its energy. Exactly,
  in rationals from Pythagorean triples, or to rounding:
  - **The landmarks:**
    - home (v = 0) is E = mc²;
    - the horizon (h = 0) is E = pc, light;
    - the corner is pc = mc², with β = 1/√2, E = √2·mc² and T = (√2 − 1)·mc²: 0.2117 MeV for an electron, 388.6 MeV
      for a proton.

    The speed limit is the cap at 1 on v, which massive things never reach.
  - **Newton and the massless limit are flips of each other.** E_N = mc² + p²/2m and E_U = pc + (mc²)²/2pc swap into
    each other when pc and mc² swap, and their relative errors satisfy e_N(x) = e_U(1/x) exactly, with x = pc/mc².
    Newton's form is the better exactly inside the corner. At the corner both are √(9/8) − 1 = 6.07% high. With MOT P17
    (the energy's level-slope β², halfway there), this makes the corner the precise statement of "where relativity
    starts to matter", and symmetric when posed in momentum.
  - **Mass is the corner of the light-cone pair.** (E + pc)(E − pc) = (mc²)², so mc² is the pair's level-midpoint. A
    boost with Doppler factor k sends E + pc to (E + pc)/k and E − pc to k(E − pc): a pan in levels, equal and
    opposite. The midpoint does not move, which is why mass is the same for every observer; pans compose by
    multiplying, which is why rapidities add. The circle's shares move with every boost (10⁴ of 10⁴); the midpoint
    never does. The boost by (E + pc)/mc² lands at rest, the light-cone pair at its corner, in all 1,000 trials.

  All standard (the energy–momentum relation, light-cone coordinates with p⁺p⁻ = m², the two limits). What the corner
  adds is the organisation: one circle in one unit, home and horizon, the two limits as one approximation under the
  flip, and the invariant mass as a corner. What it does not give: c's value, or why a change of frame is a hyperbolic
  pan and not a rotation (the sign is the world's).
- **Physical instances of what reaches a reader of a payload** (PAY, `plans/pay-plan.md`).
  - **The geometry moved to the main papers** (R211; Tom: "this is pure geometry not physics actually. lets move this
    into the main papers"): holding and resolving, the two centres (the median of the addresses, the centroid of an
    unresolved payload), one stream as one relation's course, and standpoints bringing back the figure.
  - **Where it went:** SPN §4.7, and `observation-from-somewhere.md` §7.6, Propositions 9–12 of §9 and §8's "From far
    off". The drawing tool's labs had shown the shape side first.

  Its physical instances, exactly or to rounding:
  - **Looming:** one stream cannot tell a thing coming nearer from one growing at the matching rate; the time to contact
    is the same. A neighbour at the same depth, read at once, separates them.
  - **Unresolved binaries:**
    - the light-weighted photocentre arrives exactly, resolved or not;
    - its orbit is a·|B − β|, the same stream for any mass and light fractions with the same difference; resolved
      against a shared background, the two separate;
    - two equal Gaussian images part at s = 2σ, the Sparrow limit (the step, an amount).
  - **Gravity from far off:** a body is its total mass at its centre of mass.
    - About that centre the monopole's error falls two levels per level of distance (−1.9995); about any other, one
      (−1.0000).
    - The orders needed to read a body to 1e-6 run from 5 at 10 R to 157 at 1.05 R.
    - The exterior series fails inside the corner sphere, and Kelvin's inversion swaps its two forms.

  All standard: looming's ambiguity, astrometric binaries, the Sparrow limit, multipoles, Kelvin's inversion.

### 2.4 In simulations of our own (checked against known physics)

A simulation is not evidence about the world. But where its referee computes the whole of a known physics, a scheme
that holds less of it can be tested against the referee exactly. That is evidence about the scheme.

- **The referee is a hard-disc gas** (POL runs 11–18, `pool-infinite.html`). At area fraction 0.15 it makes Enskog's
  4.864 contacts per ball per second to 0.1% (with Henderson's contact value; in two dimensions the cross-section is
  two diameters wide), its speeds are Rayleigh's to 4e-4, its count fluctuates as Henderson's equation of state gives
  (0.526 against 0.530), and a pulse travels at that equation's sound speed (c = 9.76; half-maximum at 3.88 s, within
  the range predicted).
- **A world held by distance sees the gas** (runs 11–15). A window of balls round the reader, and beyond it totals in
  dyadic cells, each split while its breadth exceeds its distance from the reader. The view's density, warmth and
  contacts are the referee's within 0.3%, and the work is the window's for a world 256 times larger. It needed what the
  physics of open systems already says: the outside's pressure (kinetic and virial), a reservoir that fixes the chemical
  potential, not the count (the band's places free 51.1% of the time, Widom's 48.3% for the bulk), and books kept where
  pushes balance.
- **The trick-shot engine against the published game** (CUE, `plans/cue-plan.md`). The measurements (Alciatore's
  technical proofs, the journals) are blocked here; the reference is pooltool 0.6.0, an open-source simulator whose
  models name their sources, re-implemented from its source text: a step below raw data. Cloth (sliding 0.2, rolling
  0.01) and ball restitution agree. The grip between balls does not: the fit to measured throw falls from 0.095 to 0.03
  between a slow and a fast half-ball stun, so the engine's constant 0.06 threw 1.4° too little slow and 1.7° too much
  fast. Made the fit (run 2), the engine follows it to 0.13°, and the challenges still solve. The cushion agrees at
  medium speed and parts at slow speed, where the engine's weaker bounce resembles a speed-dependent restitution that
  pooltool keeps but does not use; measured data must decide.
- **A world held by distance loses a signal on its way** (runs 16–18; §3). Level of detail by distance holds for what
  stays put and averages out; what travels toward the reader must be held at its own size until it arrives. Adaptive
  mesh refinement already does this, by gradients. In SPN's terms: what is coming is near, whatever its distance.

- **Haff's law counted in contacts** (MRB part 1, runs 1–2; 500 smooth inelastic spheres, event-driven, contact found
  at h + v = 1). Two decades of cooling at three densities and four restitutions: −d ln T per contact per marble is
  (1 − e²)/3 (with the known a₂ correction) to 0.8% wherever the gas stays even. Density, diameter and pair correlation
  cancel from the count; the clock needs them (Enskog's ratio between densities carried to 1.2%). Near the clustering
  instability both drift, the clock about twice as far (2.1 and 2.2 times): neighbours' velocities grow alike, so each
  contact loses less and contacts come less often, and the count carries only the first. Past the instability (φ 0.3,
  e 0.5) the box's flow holds the energy and one total for the box stops describing it. No inelastic collapse at 500.
- **A heap held at rest** (MRB part 2, runs 3–4; soft spheres with friction, each contact held past h + v = 1). Without
  friction a marble at rest touches 6.18 others (Maxwell's isostatic 2d = 6); with friction 0.5, 4.74 (between d + 1
  and 2d). Bulk packings 0.642 and 0.589 against the measured 0.637 (shaken dense) and 0.60 (poured loose), from memory.
  The floor carries the weight to 10⁻⁵ read over a time unit.

- **Why the construction from situated observations can succeed** (STC runs 1–2, `plans/stc-plan.md`; Tom, 12 October:
  "Physics already constructs the completed view from situated observations, and the framework supplies a geometric reason the construction can succeed." "Several situated observations, plus one external amount that fixes scale, are assembled into a
  representation that no individual observation held."). A single reading is situated and cannot hold the true breadth.
  Parallax, triangulation, multi-epoch astrometry and the distance ladder assemble several readings and one amount from
  outside into a figure. The reason it can work is classical (similarity invariance, triangulation); the framework names
  it: the addresses are exact while the scale is arbitrary, and the outside amount moves every linked amount by one
  common factor, a shift in levels that redraws nothing (V = H stays drawn). Stitching needs links: overlap, the
  standpoints' placement known up to scale, a chain of readings to the outside amount. Co-adding exposures from one
  standpoint is not construction but resolving: it adds no standpoint and no scale. Tested on a figure of 20 landmarks
  read by 16 to 4,096 standpoints with bearing noise 10⁻³, one outside length with 1% error, and a standard-candle rung:
  - **the figure improves with readings**: with a consistent (maximum-likelihood) construction the shape error falls as
    N^(−0.49), from 0.013 to 0.0009;
  - **the scale does not**: the common shift stays at the outside length's 1% from 16 readers to 4,096 (0.0105 to
    0.0101), and by 4,096 the landmarks' errors are 99% correlated, one shift shared by all;
  - **a ladder carries its calibration up**: the candle rung's error is √(0.01² + 0.0112²) = 0.0150, the same for a
    hundred far members or ten thousand (0.0158, 0.0151), correlated 0.68 with the rung below;
  - **the construction can have a systematic of its own**: least squares on noisy bearing lines left a shape floor of
    0.0013 that more readings did not remove (run 1, §3).
  Statistical against systematic error is address against payload: what more readings improve is the figure, and the
  scale is exactly as good as the one amount from outside. All of this is known in metrology and astronomy; the reading
  is the corpus's.

## 3. What was killed, and what it taught

- **REF P1–P3: wander's bodies** are far rougher than real worlds. The Moon spans 0.25 levels against the real 0.0165.
  "Broad first" is the order in which bands are seen, not where the relief lies. Under §0 this was never evidence. It is
  kept as the example of what not to count.
- **REF run 2, P6–P8: real relief does not fill a reader's levels evenly, self-similarly or alike.** (The run framed
  this as whether worlds come packed as the reader reads; a real signal comes unpacked, SPN R250.) Each level of
  distance was predicted to drop the same share of a world's relief (a power law in degree), at half the relief per
  octave (the reader seeing the same at every level), and with one slope on every body. The airless bodies bend (their
  relief standing highest, against a line, at about a tenth to a fortieth of their radius), the slopes run from −0.21 to
  −0.98, and only Vesta's, an average over a bend, is near −1. The share is the world's.
- **TPI P1–P3: 2/π is not always a turn.** Every occurrence was predicted to come from an even turn read as a shadow or a
  share, in one of four forms, with ½ in its place in space. Eight of sixty-three come another way (seven the circle as a
  shape, one a pole); the Kosterlitz–Thouless 2 is a dimension and the Gaussian beam's a convention; in space only the
  shadow on an axis gives ½. 2/π is a strong clue for the even turn, not a proof of one.
- **MAG P4: K0 III scatters 1.4 levels**, not under 1.0. A known kind is a bridge about a level wide.
- **CNT P3 and P4: the plane and the poles count alike.** The disc was predicted to flatten the counts toward the poles
  more than in the plane. It does not: both give 1.23. Dust in the plane and the clumping of young bright stars are the
  likely offsets, untested.
- **CNT runs 2 and 3: "K0 III" is not one kind.** Within 50 pc, 4 of 36 stars so labelled are too faint to be giants:
  about one in ten throughout, 19% by 140 pc. So the K0 giants have no distance within which Hipparcos holds them all,
  and counting them by distance or light measures the label and the catalogue, not space. MAG's 140 pc "complete" range
  rested on the same false premise. Only the near G dwarfs counted cleanly: 3.47 levels of number per level of distance,
  against 3.
- **GCN run 1, all four: a cut that depends on distance, and a colour that is not a kind.** The RUWE cut (< 1.4) removes
  28% of stars at 25–35 pc and 18% at 70–100 pc, since near binaries wobble more in angle. It steepened the late M count
  to 3.14 (P1) and the poles past the plane (P2, the wrong way round). By light, G and late M sat just past the 1.55
  ceiling (P3). Within 50 pc a fifth of the G and K colours are white dwarfs (P4): colour alone is not a kind; colour
  with the parallax's magnitude is.
- **NRF P3–P5: not every count of light is fair to the corner.** The line counted from every direction, and an isotropic
  emitter on a flat patch, switch off s = 1 (σ(1) = −1.64 and −1.21) and are not symmetric under the flip. And the width
  of the switch is set by the power in the share, log₂(81)/k levels, not one band for all.
- **HZN run 1's P5 and run 2: a page reads only to its grain.** Near home, draw's levels page cannot read nearer than one
  segment or one direction, whichever is coarser. Run 3, with both grains doubled together, was not killed.
- **POL P35, three times (runs 16–18): totals by distance cannot carry a pulse toward the reader.** A hot disc 20 units
  across, 60 units off, was averaged into a level-3 cell 60 units wide before it moved. Diffusion cannot carry sound;
  a fluid on the cells first never ran (a setting overwritten), then filled the window (its wall pushed nothing back),
  then, with the wall's push, kept the window steady but delivered 22% of the peak, inside the noise. What it taught: a
  signal must be held at its own size until it arrives.
- **CUE P1–P3: a constant grip between balls.** The published fit falls threefold from slow to fast; one number is right
  only near a medium hit. Replaced by the fit in run 2. **CUE P6: the slow cushion** bounces 0.62 in the engine against
  0.80 in Han's model with a constant restitution; which a real cushion does is for measured data. **CUE P10** was my
  set-up: the smoke test's throws are not pure stuns.
- **MOT P6: the first-order stereo error.** The worst error per step was predicted as ε·d/(2B) within 10% up to
  εd/B = 0.2. It is x/(1 − x) on the far side and x/(1 + x) on the near (x = εd/2B), past 10% from 0.183. The far side has
  no bound at x = 1: the baseline's horizon.
- **MOT P10, its angle clause: a call does not make an angle exact.** The angle readers of time to contact and of stereo,
  with tan, were predicted to agree to 1e-12; they reached 1.9e-10 and 1.2e-12. Past the corner a double holds an angle
  in even steps and the ratio it stands for in steps growing as the ratio; the capped unit relation holds it to 1.5e-16
  at every size.
- **MRB P2: the clock at e = 0.7.** Haff's law in the referee's time was 5.2% slow while per contact it was 2.5%: the
  gas near its shear instability (run 2: the bias follows the box's size against L_c, at fixed e). **MRB P9 and P11 were
  my protocol:** a pile read at rest at one instant can be breathing (0.1% off), and stripping marbles with fewer than
  d + 1 contacts empties a frictional pile from its surface in; run 4, repaired, was not killed. **MRB P13: no Janssen.**
  The walls of a silo poured and left still carried −3% to +26% of the weight, no saturating share: Janssen's law needs
  the walls' friction mobilised, as the experiments do by moving a wall or the floor first.
- **POL P29: a derivation, not the gas.** The plan took a disc's cross-section in two dimensions as one diameter; it is
  two, and the referee matched Enskog once that was right.
- **STC P1: my estimator, not the readings.** The shape error was predicted to fall as N^(−½); it flattened (slope
  −0.394). Least squares on the bearing lines puts the noise into the lines (errors in the variables): a bias of 0.0013
  per landmark, flat in N and four times smaller at half the noise (run 2, P5). Maximum likelihood removed it (slope
  −0.491). A construction can carry a systematic of its own, which more readings do not remove.

## 4. The queue

Tests that could be run next, each on real data or exact arithmetic. Each would need its plan written first.

- **The ratio between levels, in a measured reader, on more animals** (GRD, `plans/grd-plan.md`; run 1 on two rats,
  §2.1). Grid cells map place in discrete modules, each coarser than the last by a measured ratio no physics law sets.
  D.1 allows only a halving without an added measure: 2 along a line, or √2 if a level doubles a map's area. Run 1 found
  √2 in two rats; P2 and P3 need a dataset with five or more animals with two modules (Stensola et al. 2012's, if
  released, or the Giocomo lab's on DANDI), its data section fixed before it is opened, and a box large enough for the
  largest module or a method that does not need it.
- **Why the counts give 1.2.** GCN's exploration (not a result) points to a thin layer of luminous stars seen through
  dust: 71% of the stars Hipparcos counts are past 100 pc, and even in the disc's middle the luminous ones stop rising
  with volume past about 125 pc. A test needs a bright all-sky Gaia sample (G ≤ 10) with parallaxes and extinctions, and
  a plan written first: the count's slope by height above the plane, and whether correcting for dust moves 1.2 toward a
  slab's 1.0 or a sphere's 1.5.
- **Clusters as one kind at one distance.** The Hyades, Coma Berenices, the Pleiades and Praesepe each have their own
  distance, from their members' mean parallax. At a fixed colour their main sequences should step two levels of light
  per level of the clusters' distance. This needs membership lists. Hipparcos membership is published, but the archives
  are blocked here.
- **The airless bodies' preferred level, in their own size** (from REF run 2, post hoc). On Mercury, the Moon, Vesta and
  Ceres degrees 64 to 255, a tenth to a fortieth of the radius, stand above the body's line, whatever the radius. If
  that is a breadth against the body's (the largest craters against the world), other cratered bodies with high-degree
  shape models should show it in the same octaves. Prediction first; pyshtools lists further shape models (Eros, Psyche,
  Callisto and others), their degrees to be checked before predicting.
- **The ratio or the turn, in a measured reader** (from MOT). x/ẋ gives time to contact at every distance; θ/θ̇
  overstates it by π/2 at the corner and grows past it. Responses to looming that run on to large visual angles (half-angle
  near 45° and past) would tell which a measured reader uses there. A published data set with the angles reached is
  needed; none is chosen yet.
- **Bertozzi's electrons** (from EMC). Speed against calorimetric kinetic energy, 0.5 to 15 MeV (*Am. J. Phys.* 32, 551,
  1964), all past the electron's corner (x from about 1.7 to 30). Read in levels, each doubling of momentum should add
  about ln 2 of rapidity. That is a real-data look at the far field, though it tests special relativity, which is
  settled, rather than the corner. The paper is blocked here and teaching copies of its table will not be used; Tom to
  supply it if wanted.
- **A calibration as one shift, on real data** (from STC). A published parallax zero-point, the instrument's own scale
  error, should appear as one common shift in levels across a catalogue's distances, the same for every star linked to
  it, and should not shrink with the number of stars. The archives that hold it (ESA, VizieR) are blocked here. (Also
  `papers/parallax.md` §9.)
- **One dimension against two.** ISQ's reading separates an angular size (falling one level per level of distance) from
  its square, the light (two levels). Stars with measured angular diameters (interferometry) and parallaxes would test
  the one-level half directly.
