The writer and the two readers

The readers stand on one side of the box and read; the writer walks all the way round it. From one standpoint the two reads give the frame, the writer's 1, at no size. A second standpoint, one step away, places the box and sizes it in that step. One amount carried in, the step counted in strides, writes it in metres. The writer needs no reading at all: it walks the whole thing. Scale the world, and see what moves.

The plan, drawn in the reader's own step H (so it does not move with k); the metre bar does. Gold: rays read from the first standpoint A; teal: from the second, B. Faint boxes: what one standpoint cannot tell apart. Dashed: the back of the box, turned away from the readers.
