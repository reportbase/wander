H is a zoom

A reader chooses one breadth, H, its thumb, and lays everything against it: in proportion up to the corner, where a thing is as large as H, and one equal step per doubling past it. It has only so many addresses, its level of detail. So setting H slides a window of held sizes whose width never changes. Below the window things fall into the first address and become home; past it they leave the lay. Inside the corner detail is absolute; past it, relative, every doubling alike. Only at the corner is a thing read both at true scale and in full detail, so the best H puts the thing read there.

From the reader: its lay, address by address

The lay from home to its edge, cut into addresses of equal room. Each dot is an address with something in it; a number is how many things share it, and so are not told apart. The corner, where a thing is one H, is the dashed line.

Addresses per level

How many addresses each doubling of size gets. Inside the corner they halve with every level inward, until a level has less than one and its things are points; past it the reader's lay gives every level the same (n·ln 2), the in-place lay halves them outward, the flip of the inside. Zooming slides the bars, never reshapes them.

Detail across sizes

The finest difference told apart at each size, as a share of the size: smaller is finer. Inside the corner it is one address of H, εH, so small things are coarse; past it, ε on the reader's lay and worsening on the in-place lay. The finest relative detail that is still true scale is at the corner.

Keep the case: x and H are amounts; the reader reads only s = x/H, which equals its unit relations' v/h under the drawing V = H. The level of detail is a count of addresses, which the geometry leaves open; the forms, in proportion inside the corner and one equal step per doubling past it, are fixed.
