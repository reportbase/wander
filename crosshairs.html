How H makes the scene

Hold up both thumbs, crossed, at arm's length. The crossing is the corner. H is the thumb: whatever the crossing points at is one thumb away by definition. Drag to look (or the arrow keys), W A S D to walk (▲ ▼ on a phone), a tap or Space holds H. The wireframes, the ground and the look are plain perspective and use no H at all; everything below is what H adds.

Where H comes from

  1. Pointing sets it. The line of sight through the crosshairs is followed into the field, and the first thing whose box it passes through is the thing pointed at. Its distance is H. Nothing else is measured: point somewhere else and H is somewhere else.
  2. Pointing at nothing keeps it. Looking at the sky, the thumbs stay where they last were.
  3. Holding keeps it on one thing. With H held you look round and walk while H stays on the thing that was pointed at, following its distance as you move.
  4. It glides. A new H is reached on its logarithm in about a fifth of a second, so the split slides through the field rather than jumping.

What every thing gets from H

  1. One reading, d/H. Each thing is read by its distance in thumbs. That one ratio decides everything else it shows.
  2. Its field. Below one thumb it is in the near field; at one thumb (within 3%) it is at the corner; past one thumb it is in the far field. The field is the same; the split is where the thumbs point.
  3. Its colour. Near: teal, brighter the closer it comes to one thumb, in proportion. The corner: gold. Far: violet, a little dimmer for each halving of the reading (one in two, one in four, one in eight).
  4. Its words (menu: words on things). Near, a fraction of the thumb ("0.42 of the thumb"); far, a ratio ("one in 12"). A writer would count the far field in thumbs (two thumbs, four, eight); the reader reads the ratio.
  5. Its place on the reader's map (menu). Round the eye, at its bearing from the look, out to d/H inside the gold ring and 1 + ln(d/H) past it: in proportion to the corner, then each stretch over which the reading halves gets the same room. The dashed rings are one in 2, 4, 8, 16 and 64. The line (menu) is the same lay without the bearing.

What H shares out

  1. The K addresses. The reader has K addresses along distance, at H·tan(kπ/2K). Half are always inside one thumb, whatever H is: a small H makes the near ones fine and close, a large H spreads them far. On the map and the line they are the faint rings and ticks, the same marks at every H, so the things slide under them as you point.
  2. The counts. How many things are near and how many far (menu: the readout, or the crosshairs' name and counts) is only how H falls among them.
  3. The rings on the ground (menu). The gold ring is the corner laid on the ground round your feet, radius H; the violet ones are one in 2, 4 and 8. They are the map's rings seen from where you stand.
  4. The walking pace. A step is a fraction of the thumb (half a thumb a second, at least 4 units), so the near field takes a moment to cross whatever H is. A convenience of this page, not part of the reading.

What H does not change

  1. Scaling the world. Scale every length by 10, the eye's place included, and H in units grows by 10, but the thing pointed at still reads one thumb, every reading, colour, word and place on the map is as it was. H in units is a writer's number; the readings are the reader's.
  2. The field itself. No thing moves when H moves. Only the near and far fields move, because they are where the thumbs are pointed.
