Crosshairs in a field

Reader geometry from inside. Hold up both thumbs, crossed, at arm's length: the crossing is the corner, and whatever it points at is one thumb away by definition, so its distance is H. Everything nearer is the near field, read in proportion, a fraction of the one thumb. Everything farther is the far field, read as a ratio, one in two, one in four, one in eight. Nothing in the field moves when you point somewhere else; the near and far fields do, because they are where the thumbs are pointed.

Drag to look, W A S D to walk, a tap (or Space) holds H on what is under the crosshairs.
The field as the reader lays it: bearing from the look, and d/H out to the corner ring, 1 + ln(d/H) past it. Ticks: the K addresses, half inside the ring at every H.
The same lay as a line: home at the left, the corner where the crosshairs are, the far field in ratios past it (one in two, one in four, one in eight; a writer counts them as two thumbs, four, eight). The grey ticks are the K addresses: the same marks at every H, so the things slide under them as you point.

The writer's numbers (H in units, the distances) move with the world's scale; nothing the reader reads does. See Reads and Writes §6, essay 4 (near and far) and the crosshair is the corner. All demos.
