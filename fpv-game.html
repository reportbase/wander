Friend or foe

A small first-person game with only K depth bands. Each pair is a red foe and a blue friend on nearly one line of sight, the same size on the screen, slowly swapping which is nearer. Only which one covers the other tells you which is in front, and the depth test sees only K bands: two discs in one band cannot be ordered and shimmer in stripes. Shoot a foe in front (+1), never a friend (−1). Laid by the turn about the focus, the pair under the crosshair gets the finest band there is; laid by log depth or the standard buffer, it gets what it gets.

Drag (or the arrow keys) to turn; tap the view (or space) to shoot at the crosshair. A pair in one band z-fights: stripes, which colour shows at the crosshair flickering.

The K bands along depth (home at the bottom), each pair in view a column, red and blue at their addresses; a pair in one band boxed. Gold: the focus H.

Geometry, not realism: drawn by angle, so a disc's size says nothing of its distance; no fog, no shading. The bot is a fixed policy, not a person: it shows how often the pair it is looking at can be read, not whether a player would notice.
