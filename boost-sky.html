Sampling a boosted sky

A sky is sampled for its light while the view changes. The change here is a boost (moving fast along the axis), which squeezes the sky forward and brightens it there: a Möbius map of the directions. The Cauchy carries that change exactly, with no table. A table of the sky's own brightness, built once, carries the rest. Only the table rebuilt for every view has to start again, and it falls behind as the view changes more.

The sky as seen, at this η. Across: the azimuth. Down: the level ln tan(θ/2), from straight ahead (top) through sideways (dashed) to behind (bottom). On this axis the boost is a pure slide upward by η, nothing redrawn. Brightness is the light arriving, f = D^k·L (log scale). Dots: 2,000 directions from the chosen sampler.

The same directions in the sky's own frame, where the boost is undone, on the same axes. Shading is the payload L alone. The Cauchy's dots are uniform over the sphere here (a band about sideways on this axis) at every η, and the mixed sampler's follow L alone: neither changes when η moves. The rebuilt table's do.

The lens bends the seen angle, θ′ = θ + (λ/2)·sin 2θ. Only slides of the level are Möbius maps on this axis, so with λ ≠ 0 the view change is no longer one: closure breaks. Pushing samples through the lens still works if each carries the lens's derivative (a normalizing flow), but no single z describes them any more. Or the Cauchy is refit to the lens: a few slid spherical Cauchys (z's) fitted to the lensed addresses, sampled directly. That is unbiased, since the mixture is the density drawn from, and costs only variance. A lens that spreads the level (λ < 0) is fitted well by three z's. One that squeezes it (λ > 0) is not: along the axis every spherical Cauchy has one width on the level, and a mixture of them can be wider than that, never narrower, so three z's collapse onto one. Off the axis they reach it: a spherical Cauchy whose point is tipped off the axis, its azimuth drawn at random, is a ring about the axis, as narrow as its ρ makes it, its density still in closed form and drawn with no table. Three rings take the squeezing lens at λ = 0.5, η = 0.5 from V 0.187 to 0.0011.

V is the relative variance of one sample's estimate of the total light, (∫ f²/q)/F² − 1: 0 is a perfect sampler. It is computed by quadrature, not from the dots. A view change costs the rebuilt table 8,192 evaluations of f plus its running sums; it costs the Cauchy and the mixed sampler one 2×2 matrix product.

V against η from 0 to 4 (log scale): uniform, the Cauchy, the table rebuilt per view, mixed. The marker is this η.

From IMS run 1 (plans/ims-plan.md): with the sun and arrivals counted, the Cauchy's V is 124.75 and the mixed sampler's 0.068 at every η, while the rebuilt table's climbs from 0.17 to 4,098 at η = 4. What a view change leaves over is the payload's own weighting: with beaming, the mixed sampler rises to 0.2–0.4. This holds only for view changes that are Möbius maps (turns, zooms of the stereographic chart, boosts); a lens distortion breaks it. All demos.
