Walking to the mountain

Hold your thumb out at arm's length and walk toward something whose size and distance you do not know. The thumb never changes; only the reading does. Each look is one place, now; that you are nearer is the writer's account, the looks laid in order. Far off the thing is a fraction of the thumb. At one place it exactly covers the thumb: the corner, found by looking. Laid in order, the readings past it climb toward the horizon, a limit no look meets, and at the foot you are looking down at your feet. Count your strides from the corner to the foot and you have written its size.

What you see. Your thumb (gold) held at arm's length, the crosshairs on the thing. The thumb is the same size the whole walk; the thing grows behind it.

The reading on the quarter turn: home (the thing on your horizon, far off) to the horizon (at its foot). The corner at 45°, where it covers the thumb. Ticks at one in 8, 4, 2 and 2, 4, 8 thumbs; the faint ray is the reading's mirror about the corner.

Time to contact, τ = x/ẋ from two looks joined, in your own ticks (a count, a write), against ticks walked. Its slope τ̇ is scale-free and a write too: −1 at a steady pace, past Lee's −½ (you arrive at speed); held at −½ you stop at the foot.

The turn the thing spans, taken in at two paces (Reads and Writes §8, "The mountain's own corner"). Its own corner is where the distance is half its breadth: it spans a right angle there, Z = i, sixty times nearer than where it covers the thumb. Left, per halving of the distance: sech x, x = ln(V*/2d), peaked at its corner, the same on both sides. Right, per unit of distance walked, counted in its half-breadths: the Cauchy, highest at the foot, half there at its corner. One turn, two measures.

The writer's ground, which the reader never sees: you, the thing, the distance, the corner's place. Strides counted from the corner are marked.

Keep the case: h and v are unit relations, the reading v/h has no scale; V*, d, the corner's distance and every stride are amounts. Behind it: Reads and Writes §8 ("Motion, from reads", "Walking to the mountain") and §6 ("The crosshairs"); the corner as the thing equal to the thumb, §2. Other demos: the mountain and the thumb, the walk, the crosshair is the corner, all demos.

In physics. Two looks a tick apart give time to contact exactly, in ticks (Lee 1976); under steady braking Lee's τ̇ = −½ is stopping distance equal to distance, the pair at its corner. Seconds need a clock carried in. (Reads and Writes in Physics, §4.1.)
