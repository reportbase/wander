A cube of heat

The cube is the blueprint's (classical geometry, shown for comparison): dots at places. Stand at one dot, look one way, and every dot in front takes the read's heat: hottest at the dot looked at, cooling every way without end. Behind the eye, nothing: one read is half the cube. The heat has no edge and no shape.

Across the look. Each dot at its direction about the look, laid so equal room holds equal shares (radius √(1 − cos α)): rings at 37.5° (half heat), 45° (one thumb off) and 60° (half the directions); the rim, 90°, never reached.
Along the look. Across: distance laid as (2/π)·atan(d/H), the eye at 0, one thumb at ½, the horizon at 1; down: the share off the look. The cube's evenly spaced dots crowd toward the far side: the near field in proportion, the far in ratios (2, 4 and 8 thumbs marked), the horizon never reached however large the cube.
