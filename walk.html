The walk

I am the horizontal, you are the vertical, and the sweep is my walk to you. Far off, you sit low on my horizon and every level nearer costs twice the steps of the one after it; near, past the corner, the walk is in proportion. The sweep's angle is the angle at which I look up at you: 0° on the horizon, 45° at the corner, where the distance equals your height, 90° at your foot.

The ground. Me (gold, horizontal, one step wide) and you (teal, vertical, V = 10 steps tall). The sight line to your top makes the sweep's angle θ; the footprints are equal steps already walked.

The sweep in fill levels. Far off, v fills against a full h (up the right edge); past the corner, h empties against a full v (along the top). The ray is θ; the ticks are the levels, one doubling of distance apart.

Steps left, against the sweep (θ from 0° to 90°). Each dot is five steps of walking: spread out near you, bunching faster and faster toward the horizon.

Keep the case: h and v are fill levels, the geometry, and they have no scale. "Close" and "far" are amounts, my steps against your height; the pair (V, d) is the bridge, and its corner, d = V, is set by those amounts. The far end is never reached: as d grows without end, θ only approaches 0. Behind it: Serial, Parallel and Nowhere §1 (the sweep, the facing) and The inverse square, "The law in SPN's words".
