# Essay 1: Reading and writing — an outline

28 Oct 2026. The first of the long essays (`plans/essays-plan.md`, SPN R562; Tom: "merge it and start essay 1
outline"). This is an outline for Tom to reorder and cut before any prose is written.

Its geometry is OFS's: §3.4, §3.8, §9 ("Ratios suffice" through "Recovered, not written") and Propositions 7, 8 and
13. Its history is `plans/rwf-literature.md`; quotations marked [checked] were read in the source.

## The one thing the essay says

Every reading falls in one of five situations, and they divide into reading and writing. From where you stand,
everything you read is a ratio, and it comes free. To state an amount (how many metres) you must carry one amount in
from outside, and you can count how many: one for yourself, and one more for each other reader who must agree.

At the centre is the circle without the V. The writer's circle is an equation in two breadths. The reader's is one
length turned, and read from where it stands it is the Cauchy. The Cauchy is the circle's geometry with the second
breadth gone.

## What it does not claim

- That invariance under rescaling is new. Measurement theory has it (Stevens 1946; Krantz, Luce, Suppes and Tversky;
  Narens), and Poincaré's doubling is a century old (Tom: "rather mundane"). They get a line of credit, no more.
- What it adds:
  - the five situations as a sorting of every reading;
  - the standpoint, which no invariance account ties to it;
  - the count of amounts carried in, per reader;
  - the Cauchy read as the circle with no V.
- No new physics and no new theorem. The mathematics is classical: tan of a uniform angle is the Cauchy. What is new is
  what it is read as.

## Shape

About 9,000–11,000 words in nine sections, each opening on a scene and ending on what it showed. The case of h, v, H
and V is kept throughout. The five situations carry the essay.

### 1. What a reader can do with no unit at all
- **Scene:** a reader with no ruler maps a room. One stride between two standpoints, and every corner is placed and every
  thing sized against every other, all in strides (`room-ratios.html`).
- **Point:** none of this needed a unit; the map is complete up to one number. Name the two acts:
  - reading gets all of this free;
  - writing (saying how many metres) costs that one number, carried in.
- **Demo:** `room-ratios.html`, steps 1–3.

### 2. Three questions, five situations
- **The rule (OFS §3.1).** Three questions place any reading in exactly one situation:
  - Is anything related to the reader? If not, situation 0.
  - Is the other breadth V known? If so, the whole is held: 1 if V equals H in every direction, 2 otherwise.
  - If not, the reading is from somewhere. It has the addresses (4) and, where something arrives, their paint (3).
- **Each situation with a scene:**
  - **0, nothing:** the reader has its length and nothing to relate it to.
  - **1, the circle:** h² + v² = 1, held whole.
  - **2, every other shape:** a blueprint, a breadth known in every direction.
  - **3, the paint:** a stained-glass window, each direction lit by what arrives along it.
  - **4, the addresses:** the fingers of a hand held up, directions alone, no amount.
- **The cases that look like a sixth** reduce to these: V known within bounds, several readers, a changing system,
  nothing arriving, a thumb that changes between readings.
- **Demo:** `writer-box.html`, the three vantages.

### 3. Reading and writing, sorted
- **The map of the situations:**
  - 2 is the writer's alone: amounts, a box, the number line on each axis, front and back, from no standpoint.
  - 3 and 4 are the reader's alone: from a standpoint, no second breadth.
  - 1 is shared. The writer holds it as an equation, the reader as its one length turned.
- **Why the framing is hard to see:** the line between reading and writing runs straight through the most familiar
  object in mathematics, and classical geometry works in 1 and 2 without saying so.
- Martens [checked]: "A unit is thus merely a relation—to a standard reference body … in disguise."
- **Demo:** `blueprint.html`, `blueprint-3d.html`, `writer-readers.html`.

### 4. The circle without the V
- **Scene:** a lighthouse's lamp turning steadily, or a head turning along a row of trees.
- **The writer's circle** needs two breadths and their equality: V known, V = H, h² + v² = 1, situation 1.
- **The reader's circle** needs one. Its own length H, turned uniformly, traces the circle because the length is fixed.
  What it reads at each moment is the direction, v against h, two shares of the same H. No second breadth is ever
  had or needed (OFS §5, R533).
- **Read that way, the circle is the Cauchy.** The turn's readings v/h have the Cauchy law (half of it on a quarter turn).
  The law is the circle's geometry with V gone:
  - the median at the corner, 45°, where v = h;
  - half the readings near and half far;
  - the reciprocal carrying one half onto the other.
  Every one of these the writer's circle has with V; the reader's has them without.
- **What the missing V does:**
  - The writer's circle closes at V, a place.
  - The reader's ends at the horizon, the direction [0 : 1], reached along every line and never a place.
  - On the chart v/h the readings have no mean, because nothing caps the far end.
  - The missing size is what gives the corner its role and makes the horizon (R370).
- **What it means:**
  - The Cauchy is not a law of what is out there. It is the measure of directions a reader has before anything arrives
    and without knowing the size of anything.
  - Scale the world and nothing in it moves, because no V ever entered: it is reading through and through.
  - "V = H" is the writer's name for the reader's circle, not something the reader sets; the reader has no V to set.
  - This is why practice is right to take the median of such quantities and wrong to take the mean (OFS "What the paper
    is for").
- **Hand-off:** the Cauchy's own story (addresses and payloads, Jaynes's invariant measure, the second test) is
  essay 3.
- **Demo:** `one-read.html` (one turn, the ray and the radius meeting), `sweep-zero.html`, `central-reader.html` step 1.

### 5. The test: scale the world
- **Point:** multiply every length by k, the observer's included.
  - A quantity that does not change is a reading.
  - A quantity that moves by k is a writing.
- Worked through at k = 1/10, 1 and 100 on the mountain range (OFS "Two tests"). Situations 3 and 4 never move; 2
  moves by k; 1 moves for the writer and not for the reader.
- **History (one line each):**
  - Poincaré 1914 [checked];
  - Bridgman 1922, "the absolute significance of relative magnitude" [checked];
  - Stevens's ratio scale;
  - Narens's meaningfulness [checked from his papers].
- The test is theirs. What is new is asking who can apply it, and from which situation.

### 6. What writing costs: a count
- **Point:** moving from 3 and 4 to 2 costs amounts carried in, and they can be counted.
  - One standpoint writes nothing new.
  - One known step writes everything in the reader's own H.
  - One exchanged number lets a second reader agree.
  - One agreed notch writes in a public unit.
- RWT's nullity on the page: 4, 1, 0, 0, 1, 0, 1, 0 (`write.html`).
- **History:**
  - Buckingham's n − r;
  - bundle adjustment's gauge, seven numbers with one scale (Triggs et al. 2000);
  - Duff, Okun and Veneziano.
- Each counts amounts for a theory or a reconstruction; the essay counts them per reader.

### 7. Many readings, one amount: the survey and the body
- Roy's base on Hounslow Heath, 1784: thousands of angles read, one base walked, every side written.
- One's own eye height as the base carried in (Sedgwick), and Warren's R/L = 0.88 for short and tall alike [0.88
  checked].
- **Point:** in perception the carried-in amount is the body.
- **Demo:** `ground.html`, `room-walk.html`.

### 8. The tower, and closing a loop
- **The tower:** six readings fix seven lengths up to one amount, a₁/b₁ = 27/16. One amount at the top writes all of
  them, and a misread link moves its room by one factor (9/10). Paper sizes (ISO 216) are the same: √2 and one written
  amount.
- **The loop:** a tree of readings cannot find its own error; a second route can. Completeness is never a reading.
  Recovered is not written: a reader that reaches the writer's statement got there by cleverness.
- **Demo:** `slide-rule.html`.

### 9. Why the distinction is worth having
- **Where taking a reading for a writing goes wrong:** the drawing taken for a measurement ("mountain equals thumb" read
  as metres); Lutz–Kelker as the one shown case.
- **What it gives:** a question to ask of any quantity (which situation is it in?), a count of what must be brought in,
  and a reason a lone reader can do so much with no unit.
- **Close:** back in the room. The map in strides was finished long before anyone asked for metres; the metre is the
  one thing it had to be handed.
