## The connection itself

- **(a) A point source's flux through a plane as the Cauchy, joined to directions from a standpoint: partly found.**
  Xiaoqi Lu, "Cauchy Distribution: A Physics Perspective" (a blog post, 16 March 2020): a point bulb's illuminance on the
  ground is proportional to the Cauchy density, for a line, a plane and d dimensions, from flux proportional to solid
  angle. Light only: no gravity, no electrostatics, no lighthouse. Dupuy, Belcour and Heitz, arXiv:1909.07605 (abstract
  opened): a uniform point of the hemisphere projected on a plane is the "projective-Cauchy", its integral over a region
  that region's solid angle. Not found: gravity or the electric field joined to a standpoint's directions as one fact.
- **(b) "Gauss's law gives the Cauchy", or the inverse square's flux through a plane equated with the lighthouse law:
  not found.** The pieces sit apart in print.
- **(c) Cauchy closure in gravitational lensing: not found.** The one Cauchy in lensing found is another thing: Petters,
  Rider and Teguia, *J. Math. Phys.* 50, 122501 (2009), random shear tending to shifted Cauchy laws.
- **(d) A field at a point as the difference of what arrives from opposite half-spaces, set against a field of view:
  not found.**

## What this leaves

"Even from a point lands as the Cauchy" is in print for light (Lu 2020; Dupuy et al. 2019), so it is not ours. Not
found: its statement for gravity beside a standpoint's directions; the add-and-subtract difference between a look and a
pull; the Cauchy kept along a line of lens masses. One search does not make these new (R925).
