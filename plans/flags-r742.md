# Flags after R742: the reader doing trigonometry

7 November 2026. R742 (Tom: "trig is a writers tool or reader activity. writer uses N and both hemispheres to describe
the reader that uses neither.") makes trigonometry as a whole the writer's tool. This note lists the places in *Reads and
Writes* and the essays that still give the reader an angle, an arctangent or a trigonometric step. It was a list for Tom to rule
on, as `plans/flags-r584-r592.md` was. **Applied as proposed** (R744, 7 Nov; Tom: "proceed"), items 1–5 below.

The search covered `papers/readers-and-writers.md` and `papers/essays/*.md`, looking for "angle", "arctan", "tan", "sin"
and "cos" within a sentence of "reader", "read" or "reads", leaving out sentences that already name the writer.

## Not flagged (they already fit R742)

- *Reads and Writes* §4 "Tangent and arctangent are the writer's tools" (line 490 on) and §5's π (line 523): already the
  writer's.
- Essay 7 §2, "a reader standing at the lamp … holds tan θ itself": the reader holds the ratio v/h; "tan θ" is the
  writer's name for it (R658). Fine as it stands.
- Essay 12 §5 (θ/sin θ, sin θ/θ): already marked "a writer measures it" (R717).
- Essay 14 §6–§7: the writer laying arrays by arctangent; the reader runs tangent addition "on readings, never on angles".
- Essay 10 lines 42, 189, 200: the inscribed angle and the slope read as two charts of one number, as the Tangent Bridge
  has them (R742's source).
- Essay 17 §1, line 75: "a direction θ off home reads s = v/h = tan θ" states the coordinate, a writer's sentence about it.
- Essay 6 line 140, *Reads and Writes* line 967: "twice the read's error angle", the writer's analysis of an error.

## Flagged

1. **The step ε as an angle.** *Reads and Writes* §8, "Knowing V, by degrees" (line 1055): "ε the smallest angle the
   reader can tell apart". Essay 6 §5 (line 161): "Read the angle to a step ε"; its table (line 175) heads a column "step
   in angle". The step is the reader's grain in direction; naming it as an angle (in mrad, arcmin, mas) is the writer's.
   Proposed: "ε the smallest step in direction the reader can tell apart (an angle, in the writer's units)"; essay 6
   "Read the direction to a step ε"; the column "step (as an angle)".
2. **A survey's angles read.** *Reads and Writes* §11 (lines 1074–1075): "thousands of angles read … The angles are
   readings"; essay 1 (line 443): Snellius "read angles from church towers"; essay 6 (line 361) the same survey. What the
   surveyor reads is the directions between towers; the theodolite's degrees are a scale written on the instrument (a
   share of the whole turn, the writer's). Proposed: "thousands of directions read, each written down in degrees by the
   instrument; one base walked and counted". The point (many readings, one writing) is unchanged.
3. **The thing itself as H.** *Reads and Writes* §8 (line 1069): "an angle α across it … puts the reader 1/(2·tan(α/2)) of
   its breadth away". The reader holds the two ends' directions; α and the tangent formula are the writer's statement of
   where that puts it. Proposed: "its two ends read from one look; the writer's sum for where that puts the reader is
   1/(2·tan(α/2)) of its breadth away, α the turn between the ends".
4. **The turn index as the reader's.** Essay 10 §2 (lines 112–118): the place ⌊K·(2/π)·arctan(v/h)⌋, "one index serves
   both sweeps", and "the turn index needs only the zoom", set against the writer's count index ⌊N·x/X⌋. By R572 laying
   an array on the quarter turn is the writer's (K counted, the arctangent, the floor), and by R742 the arctangent is the
   writer's. The contrast still holds (one index needs the extent X, the other only H), but both indices are the writer's
   bookkeeping: the count index of a world written to its end, the turn index of a reader's turn. Proposed: one clause,
   "both are the writer's indices; the turn index is the writer's bookkeeping of what the reader's turn holds, and it
   needs only the zoom".
5. **The hyperbolic angle as the reader's lay.** Essay 4 (line 272): "The hyperbolic angle is the reader's logarithmic
   lay made smooth"; essay 8 (line 149) the same. By R596 the lay's ln is the writer's formula for the reader's ratio, and
   the hyperbolic angle ζ = asinh x is the writer's measure (the area of a hyperbolic sector). Proposed: "the hyperbolic
   angle is the writer's smooth formula for the reader's lay of ratios".

Items 1 and 2 are wording in degrees and units; items 3–5 say whose the formula is. None changes a number.
