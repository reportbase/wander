# Building out from the bar, one step at a time

Started 19 Nov 2026 (SPN R1122). Tom: "we need to build out from the bar one step at a time." Each step is Tom's, in his
words; nothing is added until he gives it. Anything of Claude's is marked so.

1. The bar is a place holder for any extent. ("we just established the bar is a place holder for any extent.")
2. Two reads create an extent. ("two reads create an extent.")
3. ~~In parallax there are no bars, only extents.~~ ("in paralax, there are no bars, only extents."; corrected by 4.)
4. Parallax uses the thumb for the bar, so there is a bar. The extent two reads create is another extent: there are now
   two extents. ("parallax uses the thumb for the bar, so there is a bar actually.   the extent that is created from two
   reads is another extent.    So we now have two extents.")
5. You have to sweep the bar to create the geometry; no geometry without the sweep. ("you have to sweep the bar to
   create the geometry.   no geometry without the sweep.")
6. Just being an extent is not enough for the geometry. ("just being an extent is not enough for the geometry.")
7. The bar is a write, the sweep is the read. ("the bar is a write, the sweep is the read.")
8. Geometry requires a previous write. ("that is helpful reframe.   so geometry requires a previous write.")
9. Geometry requires physics, and then physics requires geometry, and so on. ("i know.   that is a major reframe.
   according to this idea, geometry requires physics, and than physics requires geometry, etc.")
10. Rotating a bar is similar to parallax: the motion creates the writes. ("rotating a bar is similar to parralax, its
    the motion that creates the writes.")

Claude's reading of 1–3 (that parallax needs no bar) withdrawn by step 4. Claude's candidate step "one read creates
no extent" offered, not taken.

Claude's question on step 5, put to Tom: R1119 said the bar has the geometry and does not create it; step 5 says the
sweep creates it. Read together: the geometry is the extent's, free, but there is none until the bar is swept?
Answered by step 6: an extent alone does not have the geometry; R1119's "intrinsic to any extent" is narrowed to an
extent swept.

Claude's reading of 5–7, not ruled: the geometry takes a write and a read together, the bar (discrete, with a size,
R1039) and its sweep (continuous, no size); neither alone has it.

Claude's flags on step 8, for Tom, not ruled: it reverses the order the papers have used, where a read needs nothing
(R297 "reading is free") and the geometry stands without physics (R852). If the bar is a write (a size, moved by
scaling, R1039), the geometry now waits on one. H as "the value chosen to start the system" (R226) would then be that
first write.

Step 9 answers the flags on step 8: a major reframe, accepted. R852 ("the geometry is complete without physics") and
R297 ("reading is free") no longer stand as an order; the two come in turn. Claude's, not ruled: this is the cycle
already in the papers (R644 "a write is over and must be read"; R900's read, write, read, write; R1065 "everything reads
and writes"), now with geometry on the read's side and physics on the write's. Open: whether the cycle has a first
term.
