The worked case of steps 5, 6 and 11 (20 Nov; Tom: "so much derived with so little"): the Cauchy from the bar alone.
Given: one bar of length 1 (the two points: pivot at home, trace one bar out), the sweep counted by its own walk, and
the reading s = v/h. Then:
- The trace for each pointing: h² + v² = 1 is the bar keeping its length (no circle drawn), so h = 1/√(1 + s²),
  v = s/√(1 + s²); the one square root a read needs (R814).
- The trace's walk per pointing: dh = −s·ds/(1 + s²)^(3/2), dv = ds/(1 + s²)^(3/2), so √(dh² + dv²) = ds/(1 + s²) = h²·ds.
- The even sweep is the trace walking equal steps, so each slope's heat is 1/(1 + s²): the Cauchy's shape, before any
  constant.
- The corner the median: s ↦ 1/s keeps ds/(1 + s²) (up to its sense), so the walk below 1 equals the walk above.
- No mean: s·ds/(1 + s²) does not close toward the horizon.
- The total: home to horizon the trace walks Ω, the only place a constant enters; (1/Ω)/(1 + s²) sums to 1; the pivot's
  end the rate, the trace's the total (R1110, R1112, R1113).
No angle, trigonometric function, circle, V or unit. The extent alone gives a length and nothing else; a sweep with no
extent has no trace; each line above uses both. The arithmetic is classical (d(arctan s)/ds = 1/(1 + s²)); the reading
(the walk per pointing, the corner and the missing mean before the constant, one length the only given) is the series'.
Tom, of its standing: "appreciation is a qualitive judgement so its a comment for a blog post not a math paper": the
papers show what it derives, not how much it is valued.
