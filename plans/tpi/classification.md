# Where π enters, and how the formula is used: 65 entries

Input: `neutral-list.md` only. Derivations and uses are from standard textbooks and papers (named in the rows) and a few web searches.

Q1: (a) an angle or phase; (b) a position along a segment or across a region whose ends or boundary are given lengths; (c) something else, named.
Q2: (a) compute from known sizes; (b) infer an unknown from a measurement; (c) both, commonly; n/a for a pure identity.

Conventions used in the table:
- A Fourier coefficient taken over one full period (in time, phase, electrical angle or one grating period) counts as (a): the π is the period's phase.
- A transform over a physical length that is not a period of the kernel (a pixel aperture, a dipole's length) counts as (b), and the row says that the π comes through the wave's phase across it.
- A π that enters through the Gaussian normalisation √(2π) counts as (c). Its usual evaluation squares the integral and uses a polar angle; the row says so.
- Where a field has two standard derivations that give different letters, the more common one is the letter, and the other is given as "alt".
