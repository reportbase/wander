# REA: re-analysing the Cauchy's classical conclusions

*18 October 2026 (Tom's local date). After TIM, Tom:*
- *"I can't help but think that classical results about cauchy were under the assumption that they were investigating
  nature of the payloads."*
- *"the obvious question now must be asked, is classical results that use cauchy now invalidated."*

*Claude answered no: the theorems and measurements stand, and only some practice fails. Claude kept a class of Cauchys as
"really a property of the system" (a resonance's pole). Tom: "I'm not persuadedd, ff your conclusion is based on the idea
that cauchy is a property of the payload, than yes, your conclusions make no sense." Claude agreed. Even the resonance's
Lorentzian is the uniform turn of the phase as the frequency is swept, (1/π)·dδ/dω with δ = arctan(γ/(ω − m)) (OFS §7.2,
R358). Tom then ruled: "Every previous cauchy based conclusion is now suspect, at a miniumum. Those results will have to
be re-analyzed to see how they are affected. Like you say, there is probably a wide variety of impacts." (SPN R362.)*

*This plan is that re-analysis's first pass: a fixed catalogue, one set of questions for every entry, a blind classifier,
and predictions about the spread of impacts, all written before any entry is re-analysed.*

## Known beforehand

- **The rule** (OFS §7.2, R358). A Cauchy variable is a direction, never an amount: find the turn, find the amount.
  - Once a Cauchy, always a Cauchy: operations on directions move only z = m + iγ (R359).
  - Only an amount entering breaks it.
- **The three layers** (this conversation):
  1. theorems, true whatever the variable is taken to be;
  2. measurements, numbers as published;
  3. conclusions, what a source says the world is like because its data or model came out Cauchy.

  R362 makes layer 3 suspect. Layers 1 and 2 are kept, but every entry is still checked against them.
- **HZT** (`plans/hzt-plan.md`, `plans/hzt/neutral-list.md`, `plans/hzt/classification.md`). A fresh agent gathered 58
  occurrences of the Cauchy and its kin (H1–H58), from its own knowledge, without having seen the corpus. A second blind
  agent classified them by whether a sweep reaches a horizon:
  - 12 have one;
  - the commonest origin is a pole a half-width off the line (29);
  - then stable sums (7).

  HZT's question was narrower than the rule's. A turn of the phase as a frequency is swept counts for the rule (R358) and
  did not count there (its convention 1).
- **TIM, LOM and SUM.**
  - In TIM's real data, a width read in frequency and a lifetime read in time are one height in two units.
  - In LOM, a ratio's median read on the right chart works where the amount tools fail; classical statistics knew this.
  - In SUM, a sum of Cauchys is a relay of standpoints, not an addition of amounts.

## The catalogue

HZT's 58 entries, as listed, unchanged. They were gathered blind, before this question was asked, so they were not chosen
to fit it. Entries added later go in a second list and are counted apart.

## The questions, for every entry

1. **The claim.** What does the source conclude, in its own terms? Give the conclusion, not the formula.
2. **The layer.** Is the source's use of the Cauchy:
   - a theorem (1);
   - a measurement (2);
   - a conclusion about the payload, meaning what arrives or what emits it (3p);
   - a conclusion about a method or an instrument (3m)?
3. **The turn.** What turns uniformly, about what point, and against what is it counted: its own turning, a clock, a
   frequency, an energy, a population? Name it, or say "none found" and why.
4. **The amount.** What amount enters: a height γ, a foot m, a strength, a speed, a count?
5. **The impact.** One of:
   - **U, unaffected:** a theorem or a measurement only; nothing about the payload was concluded.
   - **R, restated:** the conclusion survives as a statement about the turn and the amount, with the same predictions.
     For example, "the state's energy is spread over Γ" becomes "the phase turns at rate Γ/2, read over the frequency
     sweep".
   - **D, a different prediction:** the address reading predicts something the source's reading does not. State the
     prediction and the data that would decide it.
   - **F, falls:** the conclusion inferred the payload's nature from the Cauchy form, and nothing else in the source
     supports it.
6. **The test, for D and F.** What data, where, and what result would decide it.

## Procedure

- **The classifier.** A fresh agent answers questions 1–6 for all 58 entries. It is given the catalogue, the rule in one
  paragraph, and the definitions of the layers and the classes. It is not shown this plan's predictions or the corpus's
  rulings. It may use the web to read sources, and records which entries it read beyond memory.
- **The check.** Claude then reads the classification against the sources, entry by entry, records every disagreement
  with reasons, and gives a second class where they differ. Neither overwrites the other.
- **The count.** Each class is counted for the classifier, for Claude, and for the entries where both agree.

### Predictions (before any entry is classified)

- **E1, a turn for every entry (the rule).** For each of the 58, at least one of the two readers names a turn.
  - *Killed* if an entry is "none found" for both readers, after the source is read.
  - This is the rule's own test at the scale of the catalogue. HZT found a sweep reaching a horizon in only 12, but a
    turn is a weaker demand than that.
- **E2, no theorem changes.** Every entry whose use is layer 1 only is U for both readers.
  - *Killed* if any layer-1-only entry is given R, D or F with a reason that holds when checked: that is, the theorem
    itself would be wrong.
- **E3, a wide variety of impacts** (Tom's expectation). U, R and D each hold at least 3 entries on the agreed count, and
  F holds at least 1.
  - *Killed* if any class falls short.
- **E4, the physics of widths mostly restates.** Take the line shapes and spectra, H17–H48: 32 entries. A width read
  from them is the phase's turn rate, an amount that survives (TIM T5).
  - On the agreed count, at most a third of them are F.
  - At least half are R or U.
  - *Killed* if either fails.
- **E5, conclusions about nature from tails are the weak point.** H51–H54 infer the world's nature from heavy tails:
  interference in a network, prices, foraging, seed dispersal.
  - At least two of them are D or F on the agreed count.
  - *Killed* otherwise.
- **E6, at least one test can be run.** At least one D entry has a decisive test on public data that this environment
  can reach.
  - *Killed* if none does. Then the re-analysis stays a reading until data can be had, and I say so.

E1 tests the rule. E2 tests that the reading does not overreach into mathematics. E3–E5 test where Tom and I expect the
damage to be. E6 tests whether any of it can be decided.

## What would and would not be shown

**Shown, if not killed:**
- where in a fixed, blindly gathered catalogue the Cauchy's classical conclusions stand, restate, change their
  predictions or fall;
- which of them can be tested.

**Not shown:**
- that any conclusion is wrong. A D needs its test run, and an F needs the source read in full and the absence of other
  support confirmed;
- anything about Cauchys outside the catalogue.
