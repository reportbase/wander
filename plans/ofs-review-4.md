**Adversarial read of the current draft**

The paper is carefully written, marks its premises, and does not hide that several load-bearing steps are assumptions or conjectures. That is to its credit. The adversarial case is that the central interpretive claims are stronger than the derivations that support them, that the novelty is largely a re-description of classical facts under an epistemic framing, and that the framework remains a principled construction rather than a forced geometry of observation.

**1. The main result overclaims what the mathematics shows.**

Proposition 5 / Theorem 6 is elementary once an even turn is granted: the density of the ratio \(v/h\) under uniform measure on angle is the Cauchy, and any reach \(\ell\) along the ray cancels from the ratio. That is classical (gnomonic projection, Knight, McCullagh, etc.). The paper's stronger claim is that "the distribution belongs entirely to the reader's addresses and not to anything that arrives," and that this "explains" the missing mean.

The cancellation is real, but the interpretive leap is not forced. The Cauchy is the push-forward of a measure on the reader's parameter (angle). One can equally say the measure is on the directions of the world, or on the projective line, and that the reader is simply sampling it. Calling it "addresses rather than payload" is a reading of where one chooses to locate the measure, not a theorem that the measure must be located in the reader's layout. The median/mean split follows immediately from any order-statistic versus moment distinction on a heavy-tailed distribution; it does not require the addresses/payload ontology. The ontology is optional packaging.

**2. \(V = H\) is definitional, not derived.**

Proposition 3(a) says the reader "must set \(V = H\)" because it has only one breadth to assign. The paper itself notes that this is "close to a definition." A reviewer can reject the move: a situated reader might simply refuse to assign a breadth, or assign a different convention, or work entirely in projective terms without ever choosing a unit. The claim that the assignment is forced by the existence of a relation is circular if "relation" has already been defined to require two assigned breadths. Once the assignment is made, the rest (corner at radius 1, circle under Euclidean length) follows, but the force of the premise is weaker than the language "must" suggests.

**3. The Euclidean choice and the even turn remain assumptions.**

The paper is explicit that the circle follows only if the sweep is a turn of a rigid bar, and that the facing motivates but does not prove this. The square and the line are equally valid cuts of the same wedge of ratios. Nothing in the reader's knowledge state (one breadth, no preferred standpoint among attitudes) selects the Euclidean norm over the \(\ell^\infty\) or \(\ell^1\) norms. The candidate principle in §11.1 does not close the gap: "no attitude preferred" already treats the attitudes as turns. The argument is therefore circular on the point that decides the circle versus the other drawings.

The same circularity appears for the Cauchy. Uniform measure on angle is the Haar measure of the turns. If the turns themselves are not forced, neither is the uniform measure. The claim that "one uniformity lies behind both" the circle and the Cauchy simply relocates the burden.

**4. Premise (S) is scale invariance under another name.**

Proposition 4 derives the logarithm from the functional equation that (S) imposes. The paper asks whether (S) follows from "any \(H\) will do." It does not. "Any \(H\) will do" is invariance under global rescaling of the reader's unit, which is already used to put the corner at radius 1. (S) is the further claim that the *room* a far reading receives depends only on the ratio, not on absolute position. That is an independent invariance assumption. The second route through additive measures on chained relations is equivalent, not weaker. The smooth join \(c = 1\) is an extra matching condition. The two address spaces are therefore constructed under stated premises, not forced by the single fact of having one breadth.

**5. The candidate principle selects the transformations it needs.**

§11.1 offers "equal addresses to whatever its readings cannot tell apart" as a conjecture that recovers the corner, the circle, the even turn, and the logarithm. The three transformations (swap, turns, rescaling) are exactly the ones whose invariant measures give the desired structure, and they generate the group that preserves the Cauchy family. This is Jaynes's method applied after the fact. It does not show that a minimal definition of a situated reader must be ignorant of precisely these maps and no others. A reader could be ignorant of additional transformations, or of fewer. The principle is elegant packaging; it is not an independent derivation.

**6. The three-vantage reading and the factors \(\pi/2\), \(2/\pi\) are ruled, not forced.**

The arithmetic on the arc is correct: a chord of 1 has arc \(\pi/2\); the average shadow is \(2/\pi\). The assignment of which number belongs to which situated vantage, and the claim that a situated reader "must construct" the true breadth because it cannot observe it, are readings (R193–R199). Nothing prevents a situated reader from treating its own reading as the completed one, or from using a different comparison of the unit line and the circle. The blueprint example and the telescope-mosaic analogy are apt illustrations of a construction; they do not prove that the construction is geometrically required rather than instrumentally useful.

**7. Propositions 9–12 are mostly classical restatements.**

- Proposition 9 is the elementary observation that ratios are invariant under simultaneous rescaling of both parties.
- Proposition 10 is division.
- Proposition 11 is triangulation up to similarity.
- Proposition 12 is the standard fact that a kernel of mean zero reports the centroid of unresolved mass, with residual variance.

These are correctly proved and usefully organised under the addresses/payload language. They do not constitute new geometric content. The slogan "fingers order; rings add" is a restatement of the order-statistic versus moment distinction already present in the Cauchy discussion.

**8. Novelty is in the framing, not the pieces.**

The paper is accurate when it says the pieces are classical. The contribution is the claim that they form one structure set by what a reader knows of two breadths, and that the Cauchy is the addressing scheme of a situated reader. That is a coherent and interesting re-description. It is not a derivation that observation alone forces this geometry, nor a result that could not be stated in ordinary projective or measure-theoretic language. The risk is that the epistemic language ("addresses," "payload," "thumb and mountain," "must construct") makes the re-description appear more compulsory than the mathematics warrants.

**9. What the paper does well under adversarial pressure.**

It lists the open questions clearly (§11.2). It marks \(V = H\) as close to definitional, the Euclidean choice as an assumption, and the candidate principle as a conjecture. It does not claim new physical laws. The mountain-range example is concrete and correctly shows what is exact (directions, corners, skyline shape up to scale) and what is shifted by one factor until a baseline is supplied. These are strengths.

**Bottom line under an adversarial reading.**

The paper offers a clean, self-contained construction of an addressing scheme for a reader that holds one breadth and not the other, together with a consistent interpretive frame in which the Cauchy is the layout of that scheme. The construction works under its stated premises. Those premises (especially the Euclidean turns and premise (S)) are not derived from a thinner definition of situatedness, and the central interpretive claims (addresses rather than payload, necessity of construction, the candidate principle as a single source) are stronger than the derivations. The result is a well-organised principled construction and a suggestive re-reading of classical facts, not a proof that this is the geometry observation forces.

### Response (11 October, Claude; edits proposed, not yet made)

**The verdict matches what the paper already concedes in §11.2.** Its open questions 1, 2, 5 and 6 are this review's
points 4, 3, 3 and 2, and its last line ("whether a situated reader's ignorance is exactly these three transformations")
is point 5. What the review exposes is a mismatch of tone: the abstract and introduction use "must", "explains", "the
only consistent layout" and "forced", while §5, §6 and §11.2 say "close to a definition", "premise" and "conjecture".
The fix is to bring the front of the paper into line with its back, not to change any result.

1. **Partly right.**
   - *Theorems, which survive:* Theorem 6 is more than "the reach cancels from a ratio".
     - (a) The law of the readings is unchanged by every change of payload, any ℓ(θ).
     - (b) Order survives every increasing change of payload.
     - (c) Only an affine map commutes with the mean on every set.
   - *A choice, as the review says:* step 1 of §7.2, which places the even measure in the reader's sweep. §7.2 already
     declines to claim that every observer samples in equal turns. If the evenness is the world's instead (sources
     spread isotropically), the same Cauchy is a property of the world's directions, and "addresses" is the wrong name.
   - *"Explains" overstates.* The Cauchy has no mean because of its tails, whatever one calls it. What Theorem 6(b)–(c)
     adds is a reason for a reader to prefer order: it is what survives a payload the reader does not know.
   - **Proposed (A):** in the abstract and §7.2, state the condition ("for a reader whose even sweep is its own laying")
     where "belongs entirely" is claimed, and say "accounts for" or "gives a reason for" rather than "explains".
2. **Right, and already half-conceded** (§5: "close to a definition"; §11.2 question 6). The abstract's "To read at
   all, the reader must use its own breadth as the unit" is stronger than the corpus itself holds: ratios need no unit
   ("v needs no V", SPN R189), so a reader can read relations projectively, as the review says.
   - **Proposed (B):** "To read the other party in amounts, and to draw the relation at one scale, the reader uses its
     own breadth as the unit." This touches R190's "forced", so it is Tom's call.
3. **Right; already premises** (§5, "The Euclidean choice"; §11.2 questions 2 and 5). The new point is fair: §11.1's "no
   attitude preferred" already treats the attitudes as turns, so the principle cannot choose the circle over the square.
   - **Proposed (C):** one sentence in §11.1 saying that the principle presupposes the turn group, and so cannot settle
     question 2.
4. **Right in substance.**
   - *What §6 shows:* a lay whose increments depend on position builds in a scale the readings cannot fix (Proposition
     3(b)). Requiring the lay not to build one in is scale invariance of its increments: a premise motivated by 3(b),
     not derived from "any H will do", which only places the corner.
   - *Equivalent, not weaker:* the second route (additive measure on chained relations) is equivalent, as the review
     says.
   - *An extra condition:* the smooth join is a further condition, which §6 already says.
   - **Proposed (D):** answer §11.2 question 1 in place: (S) is scale invariance of the lay's increments, motivated by
     Proposition 3(b), not derived from it.
5. **Right; already labelled a conjecture.** It is covered by (C) and by §11.2's last line. No further change is needed
   beyond saying plainly, in §11.1, that the three transformations are chosen, not derived.
6. **Partly right.**
   - *Exact:* the arithmetic (θ/sin θ and sin θ/θ, reciprocal at every turn, π/2 and 2/π over the quarter turn).
   - *Readings:* which vantage gets which measure, and "must construct", rest on SPN R193–R199.
   - **Proposed (E):** one sentence in §3.4 marking the assignment as a reading of the arithmetic, not a theorem, and
     changing "must construct" to "can only construct, if it wants the true breadth".
7. **Right.** Propositions 9–12 are classical: ratio invariance, division, triangulation, and the moments of a
   convolution. Their proofs are one line because they are elementary; the placement is ours.
   - **Proposed (F):** say so at the head of §9's new block.
8. **Right, and consistent with §1** ("What is new, and what is not": the contribution is a construction of a bounded
   observer's representation).
   - *Not ours:* the claim that observation forces this geometry is not made in §1, and should not be implied by the
     verbs elsewhere. (A), (B) and (E) do that work.
9. **Noted.**

**Whether the paper should claim more.** Two places resist the review and could be argued harder, if Tom wants:
- Theorem 6(c) is a real negative result, that no non-affine summary of payload commutes with every rescaling. It is
  the precise sense in which "fingers order, rings add" is more than the order/moment distinction.
- The cancellation of the reach is pointwise, in every single reading, not on average. That is why the address reading
  holds for one reading as well as for a distribution.

Neither makes the geometry forced; both make the address reading more than packaging.
