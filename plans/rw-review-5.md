## Taken

- **The Euler product (its §4).** Right. §9 "Read, write, read, write" and essay 15 called each factor 1 + iθ/n "one read,
  a slope and nothing else". As a complex number it has modulus √(1 + θ²/n²) > 1, the stretch the paragraph before already
  names. Now: the read is the pair [1 : θ/n]; the length is the representation's; divided by it, uⁿ is the turn alone,
  angle n·arctan(θ/n), length 1 at every n, tending to e^(iθ). Tangent addition composes the pairs with no division. Note:
  the normalized product is not e^(iθ) at finite n (it falls short by θ³/(3n²)); the review's "uₙⁿ → e^(iθ)" is a limit.
