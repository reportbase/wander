# *Reads and Writes* checked against R895

12 November 2026. A list, not an edit: no paper, essay or demo is changed by it. It asks what
`papers/readers-and-writers.md` would have to say differently if R895 stands, passage by passage, and marks
which changes rest on what Tom ruled and which rest on Claude's readings from the same exchange.

## What R895 rules (Tom's words)

1. **The write is physics**: a mark made somewhere in the physical world ("if we say write is physics, it still
   work"). Restores R653; reverses R851 for the write.
2. **Parallax itself needs no write**: "parallax just needs two reads, the human eyes do parallax. so no write
   needed, the eyes just need to coordinate their reads."
3. **A write carries a read forward**: "to do complicated parallax, you would need to write the previous read
   somewhere in the physical world."
4. **A write uses none of the blueprint's tools**: "we conflated the write to include tools it would never use";
   it cannot use trig.
5. **Heat and paint are the words**; "address" is computing's.

Not ruled, Claude's (marked C below): the π/2 vantage is a read; the central read holds the shares; neither
read has its number; the unit is one kind of mark; coordinated reads need a name; discrete means counted, not
rational. Tom's other candidate, the write as the discretization of the continuum, fits rule 1 (a mark is
finite) and is treated as consistent, not as a second definition.

## What does not change

- No mathematics. Every identity, table of numbers and test stays as it is.
- The second test, the no-race statement and the heat-and-paint wording.
- The blueprint as classical geometry, a point of comparison (R891).
- "Steps, never positions": a position still needs something kept, and that something is now plainly a write.
- The rule that the paper claims nothing about what the world is: "a write is physics" says where a mark is
  made, not that the world is discrete (R645).

## Size of the change

Thirteen passages must change and five take a word; all are in the abstract, §1's definitions, §2, §6, §7 and
two places in §9. The essays that carry the same sentences are 1 ("Two writes, and where physics enters"),
2 §9, 16 §13 and 19 §8, and the physics paper's opening. Items 19 to 24 would be a larger change (the tier
tables and "Four things") and should wait for Tom's ruling on each.
