9. **The walk and the landing on Euler's circle.** **Whose:** Tom, 11 Nov ("It would be rigorous and persuasive if
   this idea could be explained via the euhler's identiy."; R868). **Standing:** checked exactly (WLK run 2, E1-E6,
   nothing killed); classical mathematics (the Cayley transform, the three kinds of Mobius map), the reading ours.
   - With x = h/v, w = (x + i)/(x - i) puts the whole approach on the upper half of the unit circle: far off w = +1,
     the corner w = i, the foot w = -1. Euler's identity is the arrival; i.i = -1 is the corner taken twice; a place
     and its mirror (d and d_c^2/d) multiply to -1, the paper's w(s).w(1/s) = -1.
   - The three motions are the three kinds of map of that circle: a turn of the look fixes no point of it (elliptic),
     the lander's fixed share fixes two, +1 and -1 (hyperbolic), the walker's stride fixes one, +1 (parabolic).
   - So the lander flows from one fixed point toward the other and never reaches it: -1 is its limit. The walker has
     no fixed point at the foot, so it passes through -1 in a finite count (and, walking on past the mountain, comes
     round the lower half toward +1: the whole circle is the whole line).
   - On Euler's plane (the logarithm) the lander is a constant slide and the walker is not.
   - Caution: the identity names the arrival; what explains the two paces is the circle and its three kinds of map.
     The value pi is still only the writer's (R807, R808); every check here was rational.
   **Next:** if Tom wants it in the paper, a short passage in section 8 beside "Walking to the mountain"; and a view
   on `approach.html` or `euler.html` drawing w for both paces.

12. **Parallax as the link the law collapses; an infinite parallax collapsing into Euler's.** **Whose:** Tom, 11 Nov
   ("Seems like parallax is the best candidate for the collapse. Maybe an infinite parallax collapses into ehulers"; R872). **Standing:** discussion; partly in *Reads and Writes* section 6 already ("the reads telescope").
   Claude's reply:
   - Parallax is the smallest unit with both halves: two reads and one counted step. A walk is that unit repeated.
   - The finite collapse, exact and free: the ratios Z_(j+1)/Z_j along the walk multiply to Z_N/Z_0, the two end reads
     alone, for any number of strides. No limit.
   - The infinite collapse, the writer's: as the stride shrinks the parallax between neighbouring looks becomes a rate
     (time to contact along the line of sight, motion parallax across it), and the turn gathered from far off to the
     foot is the half circle +1 to -1, the writer's pi, e^(i.pi) = -1.
   - Cautions: two different collapses (ends only, free; the total, a limit); and the strides are not Euler's
     (1 + i.theta/n)^n, which is n equal small turns: a stride is a slide on the writer's line, uneven arcs on the
     circle, largest at the foot. Both end at -1 by different routes.
   **Checked** (WLK run 3, C1-C6, nothing killed; R873; Tom: "Yes, run the test"). It corrects what I said above:
   - Nothing needs the stride to shrink. Two looks one stride apart give the strides left exactly at any stride size,
     and the arcs compose by tangent addition exactly: from x0 to the foot the half-arc's slope is exactly x0.
   - From the corner to the foot that slope is exactly 1: a quarter of Euler's circle, in any number of strides.
   - The half circle (the identity whole, -1 from +1) needs the walk to have started without end: short by 2/x0 from
     x0 corners out (0.067 from thirty, 2 millionths from a million). The infinite is the far start, the horizon
     behind the walker.
   - The stride must shrink only if slopes are added as though they were angles: the excess falls as the stride
     squared. Composing is the geometry's and exact; adding is the writer's and needs the limit.

14. **The scaffold, revisited** (11 Nov; Tom: "Let's revisit the idea. It's related to centering. We also used it for
   basic geometry."). Where it is: the archive's *Degrees of Freedom* section 3.1 "The centring" and its 19 Sep note
   ("the term underneath may be better called the scaffold"); *The Cauchy, Inside and Out* and *The Order of Limits*
   ("the indexes are the scaffolding, the values the stones"; section 6a.4 "Two frames"); `papers/v-and-h.md` items
   66-74 (the basic geometry) and 45c (the arch demoted, 24 Sep). *The Centring* itself (17-18 Sep) is cited there and
   is not in this repository.
   - What it said: a frame is put up so that two readings can bear on each other; the result must not depend on which
     frame (holds 1, 7, 1000, 0.004 all give 0.75); only its magnitude comes down, its presence stays (set it to zero
     and the reading diverges: "the horizon is the scaffold gone").
   - The basic geometry built on it: every figure is a centring; the residues r(t2)/r(t1) across directions are the
     shape, they compose, and give the figure "one number short" (the size); the circle is the figure whose residue is
     1 everywhere; a lap multiplies to one (a check on the reader, not the figure); v^2 + h^2 = 1 "a receipt" that two
     readings were taken against one denominator.
   - Why it was demoted: "readers don't build arches"; building is not a reader's act; the arch is a thing in amounts,
     not an address; which span is the arch was never ruled.
   - Claude's reading, in today's terms (not ruled): the demotion's objection is answered by R594 and R850. Building
     is writing, and a scaffold is a **write of the relation**: something kept, with no unit that matters, so that
     reads can be joined. Two things carried the name and should be kept apart: (a) the thumb, present in every read,
     any size, never zero; (b) the kept step between reads (parallax), whose size drops out of d1/d2 and stays in d/H.
     R881's "the least is not zero" is the old rider "the scaffold is never struck". The basic-geometry part survives
     in *Reads and Writes* section 2 ("by its ratios", a0 the one number short) without the picture of building.
   - Not agreed: bringing the arch back (the two objections above still hold), and "dividing strikes the frame" for
     the thumb (R654: nothing is removed from a read; the thumb's size was never in it).
   **Next:** Tom's call. Candidate: "scaffold" as the plain word for a write of the relation (put up, any size, never
   absent, not in the result), and the residues' lap as an exact check if it is to carry weight.
   **Tom, 12 Nov (R883):** "Denominators are very important to a read, they are the in terms of. But they simplify away
   as shown by the tower idea. So they act like scaffolding." Taken as the idea's statement: the scaffold is the
