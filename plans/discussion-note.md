## From discussion

7. **The walk to the mountain is the breath: read, step, read, step.** **Whose:** Tom, 11 Nov ("the walk to the mountain
   I think reveals the breath/exhale cycle. which is just read/write/read/write … as you approach the mountain. Euler's
   captures this idea I think."). **Standing:** a reading, building on `plans/breath-note.md`, R644, R785 and R804.
   Claude's additions, unchecked:
   - Under the two writes (R850) every exhale on the walk is a write of the relation (one more stride counted), so the
     whole walk, corner to foot, is prior to physics; the write of the scale comes once, at the end, if a stride is given
     in metres.
   - In the reciprocal reading the walk is a count: with steady strides h/v falls by the same amount each stride
     (h/v = d/V*), from 1 at the corner to 0 at the foot. So the reads form an even ladder in h/v and the count of
     rungs is V* in strides. To check exactly against `approach.html`'s `stridesFromCorner`.
   - Euler's formula holds it as law and steps (R864): e^(iθ) is the law given whole; (1 + iθ/n)ⁿ is the same thing
     taken apart into n steps (ELM), and each factor is one breath, a small turn with a small stretch.
   - Cautions. The walk is not a loop: a breath that is a turn and a scaling closes (BRL), a walk goes one way, so
     "cycle" here means the alternation, not a closing. A stride is a translation, not a slide, on Euler's plane; only a
     pace that covers a fixed share of what remains is a steady slide across, and that pace never arrives. And the
     sequence exists only for whoever keeps it (R651, R652): a read has no "nearer".
   **Checked** (WLK, `plans/wlk-plan.md`, run 1, exact, nothing killed; R866): the ladder is exactly even, the rung
   S·(H/L)/V* in h/v (the note's "h/v = d/V*" was a slip: it is d/d_c), 514 2/7, 2,571 3/7 and 128,571 3/7 strides
   from the corner to the foot for the house, tower and mountain, each returning V* exactly. What the test adds:
   - The even ladder is the writer's number line in units of d_c (R338). It has no corner: the steps before, across
     and after h/v = 1 are equal. The corner belongs to the looks, the ladder to the strides.
   - The foot need not be walked to. Any two looks a counted stride apart give the strides left, x₂/(x₁ − x₂), and
     the breadth in strides, (H/L)/(x₁ − x₂). One breath (look, stride, look) already holds the whole walk.
   - The even stride and the even breath are equal steps on two rulers (h/v and its logarithm); a pace even on both
     is no pace. So Euler's plane holds the breath as a steady slide only for the pace that never arrives; the walk
     that arrives is even on the writer's line.
   - All scaled, no rung or count moves (a write of the relation); the stride kept, the count moves by k.
   **Next:** decide whether the breath belongs in the paper at all (R779 kept it out: "no breathing in the paper").
   Open, Claude's: whether "one breath holds the whole walk" is the better statement of Tom's idea than the cycle.

8. **Walking to the mountain and the butterfly landing on a leaf are one idea; the geometry is the free baseline.**
   **Whose:** Tom, 11 Nov ("seems like walking towards the mountain and the butterfly landing precisely on a leaf is
   the same idea. no doubt different creatures solve this problem differently, but geometry is the baseline they all
   build on, the geometry part is free and available to all creatures to build on."; R867). **Standing:** a reading;
   Claude agrees, with a sharpening from WLK W7 and two cautions.
   - The same: an approach to a thing whose size is never read; the reading grows, the corner is met by looking, two
     looks give the time to contact in the creature's own ticks.
   - Two paces, not one (WLK W7): the walker's equal strides (even on the writer's line, arrives in a finite count,
     the count a write of the relation that gives V* in strides) and the lander's fixed share of what remains (even on
     the logarithm, the steady slide on Euler's plane, never arrives in the ideal, so touches at almost no speed). The
     lander holds one reading steady and counts nothing: a strategy of reads alone.
   - Known, from memory, to check: honeybees landing hold the image's rate of expansion constant (Srinivasan et al.
     c. 2000; Baird et al. c. 2013); Lee's tau-dot >= -1/2 (in the paper, section 8).
   - Cautions: the paper says should, not does (R261, R262, R455), so what creatures do stays out of it; and the last
     moment of a landing is not the geometry's (the ideal fixed-share pace never touches; the ending is the creature's).
   **Next:** check the two landing references; if Tom wants it in, one paragraph beside "Walking to the mountain"
   naming the two paces, no claim about any creature.
