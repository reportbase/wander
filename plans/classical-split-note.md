## The claim to keep narrow

"Missed" is too strong. `plans/ofs-literature.md` found every piece in print and did not find the reading that joins
them. The claim is only this: the pieces are classical, and placing the split at the reader's standpoint is new as far as
the search went.

## Five reasons the split was easy not to draw

1. **The completed view comes first.** Classical work fixes coordinates and units outside any reader and hands them
   over before anything is read. An address is then a coordinate already given, not something laid, and there is
   nothing to separate from what arrives. The split appears only for a reader that stands somewhere and does not know
   V.
2. **With V known the two sweeps coincide.** The unsituated reader holds the chord and its sum, h + v = 1, and a
   direction and an amount are one number written two ways. The sweeps differ by the factor 2, a ratio against its
   square. That matters only to a reader that must lay addresses before the amounts arrive.
3. **The Cauchy was filed as a payload law.** It is taught as a law of outcomes: the one with no mean, the lighthouse's
   landings. It is seldom taught as an even turn's directions, laid by a party on a line before anything arrives. Filed
   as payload, its missing mean reads as a defect of the data, not as a property of addresses.
4. **The pieces sit in separate fields.**
   - Geometry: the inscribed angle and stereographic projection, taught as facts about angles.
   - Probability and robust statistics: the Cauchy, the median and the mean.
   - Optics and quantum mechanics: squares at twice the angle (Malus, Stokes; amplitude and probability).

   Each holds its piece for its own reasons; none had a reason to read them as one circle seen from two pivots.
5. **The decisive step looks trivial.** "Only the centre and the circle turn an even sweep into an even arc" follows
   from a theorem every student meets as a lemma about angles. Read that way it says nothing about a reader, so no one
   needed it as a principle.

## Where the classical world came closest

- Quantum mechanics separates an amplitude from its square, the probability: close to the two charts (OFS §4, "What each
  sweep reads").
- Signal processing separates the sampling grid from the samples.
- Robust statistics separates the median from the mean.

Each holds a fragment of the split; none places it at the standpoint.
