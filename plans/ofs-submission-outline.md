## Decisions for Tom

1. **Venue and audience.** It sets the length, the citation style and how much probability or geometry to assume. My
   suggestion is a mathematics or probability journal with a foundations bent, which fits Theorem 6 and Jaynes. A
   philosophy-of-science journal is the other fit, but it would want §3's account of the reader kept, not cut.
2. **The spine.** The address/payload separation (above), or "ratios suffice, a blueprint adds" (R530's spine for OFS).
   I suggest the separation as the spine, with "ratios suffice" as one paragraph of §7, since the separation is what
   the theorems prove.
3. **§3's account of the reader.** R203 says never to fold it into a short table in OFS. For the submission I propose two
   paragraphs and a pointer to the long version; OFS keeps it whole.
4. **The author line.** OFS reads "Tom Brinkman, with Claude". Most journals do not accept an AI as an author and ask
   for AI assistance to be disclosed in the acknowledgements or methods instead. I suggest "Tom Brinkman" with a
   disclosure line; your call.
5. **Where the long version lives.** Citing OFS as "the long version" works best if it is public with a stable link
   (arXiv, Zenodo, or the GitHub file at a tagged commit).
