## Known beforehand

**Classical.**
- **The upper half-plane.**
  - Its hyperbolic distance: cosh d = 1 + |z₁ − z₂|²/(2γ₁γ₂).
  - Its area element: dx·dy/y².
  - The affine maps z ↦ az + b (a > 0, b real) are isometries.
- **Böröczky's binary tiling (1974).** Each tile is a cell [x, x + w] × [w/2, w]. A tile meets two below it, one above it
  and one on each side. The tiles are congruent, and the tiling's adjacency graph is quasi-isometric to the hyperbolic plane.
  The dyadic tree alone, without the sideways neighbours, is not.
- **Spitzer, and the harmonic measure.** A Brownian motion from z lands on the line with law C(m, γ), the Poisson kernel.
- **Cell averages and the Poisson kernel.**
  - Averaging over a cell of width w multiplies e^{2πinx} by sinc(πnw), up to a phase.
  - The Poisson kernel at height γ multiplies it by e^{−2πnγ}.
- **Image pyramids, quadtrees, Haar wavelets, mipmaps.** These are the coarse-to-fine store of a signal: totals per cell,
  details per level.

**In the corpus and in draw.**
- **ADR run 1:** the distance along the height is the level (ln 2 a doubling), and mode k is kept from height γ as
  e^{−kγ} (A4).
- **`wedge-browse.html`:** a binomial cascade of 2⁵³ − 1 items, made from their addresses and never stored. Its cells are
  opened by size on the screen, and the drawn always add up to the whole.
- **`pool-infinite.html`:** totals in dyadic cells, held by level.
- **`.tvf3d` (R250):** coarse first, read as a prefix.
- **OFS §7.3, §8 (R373):** the floor discards the fraction, whose binary digits are the finer levels.
- **draw's TFE lab** ("two fisheyes on one array", `labs.html`, draw `ea07709`): "the pyramid in rungs", and everything
  beyond a window as one bar per octave.

  No lab in draw places the cells in the half-plane, measures them there, or sets the floor's fade against the Cauchy's.

## The reading, before the run

Give the cell of level j and index k (width w = 2^{−j} on [0, 1)) the point z = m + iγ, with m = (k + ½)w its middle and
γ = w/2 its half-width. Then:
- the floor keeps j = 0;
- each binary digit of the fraction takes one step down;
- the fraction is the other coordinate of a point in a plane that has its own metric.

"Another dimension", in this sense, is the height of the half-plane. The address is two numbers, m and γ.

The claims are four:
1. the cell is the Cauchy's own interquartile range from its point;
2. every level is the same as the next, by an isometry, with equal area per tile;
3. the cells with their sideways neighbours measure the plane, while the tree alone does not;
4. the floor's fade and the Cauchy's are not the same function.

### Predictions

- **D1 (the cell is the address's middle half).** From the cell's point z, the Cauchy C(m, γ):
  - puts exactly ½ in its own cell, ¼ in each half;
  - puts (1/π)(arctan 1 − arctan ½) in each outer quarter, and the same share pattern at every j and k.

  Checked at 50 digits for j ∈ {0, 1, 5, 20, 52}, three k each.
  - *Killed* if any share differs by more than 10⁻⁴⁰.
- **D2 (every level is the next).**
  - Parent to child: d = arcosh(3/2).
  - Siblings: arcosh 3.
  - Same-level cells n apart: arcosh(1 + 2n²).
  - Each is independent of j and k, to 10⁻⁴⁰, for j ∈ {1, 5, 20, 52}.
  - The map z ↦ 2z − k carries level j + 1 onto level j.
  - *Killed* if any differs.
- **D3 (the tiles are congruent).** The tile [kw, (k + 1)w] × [w/2, w] has hyperbolic area exactly 1, at
  j ∈ {0, 3, 20, 52}, both by its closed form and by quadrature to 10⁻³⁰.
  - So every address has the same room in the plane's own measure.
  - *Killed* otherwise.
- **D4 (the tree alone misreads; the tiling measures).** The graph distance g counts steps to a parent, a child or a
  sideways neighbour.
  - (a) Across the middle of level j (k = 2^{j−1} − 1 and 2^{j−1}), the tree distance is 2j, while d stays arcosh 3,
    for j = 2 … 30. *Killed* if d changes, or the tree distance is not 2j.
  - (b) For every pair at level 10 (all 523,776), |d − g·ln 2| ≤ 3. *Killed* if any pair exceeds 3.
  - (c) The ratio d/g for the pair k = 0 and k = 2^t at level t + 1, t = 10 … 40, tends to ln 2: within 0.05 of it at t = 40. *Killed* if not.
- **D5 (the floor's fade is not the Cauchy's).** For w = 1, compare e^{−2πnγ} with |sinc(πn)| over n ∈ [0, 20] (step
  10⁻³).
  - The least, over γ ∈ [10⁻³, 2] (step 10⁻³), of the largest difference is at least 0.1.
  - *Killed* if some γ brings it under 0.1: then a Cauchy read at some height would be the floor's cell average, to within
    0.1.
  - I report the best γ and the half-power points, the Poisson's at nγ = ln 2/(4π) and the sinc's at nw ≈ 0.443.
- **D6 (the verdict).** A judgment. The floor's discarded fraction is the half-plane's height:
  - each level is one isometric step of ln 2;
  - every cell has the same area;
  - the cell is the middle half of its own Cauchy;
  - with sideways neighbours, the cells measure the plane.

  What does not follow is that keeping digits is reading a Cauchy from that height. The two fades differ (D5).
  - *Killed* if D1–D4 fail, or if D5 is killed.

D1–D4 are classical, so a kill there would be my set-up. D5 is the question R373 left open.

### Run 2: how much of each level's detail a reader keeps (predictions, 18 Oct, before the run; Tom: "yes")

**The set-up.**
- **The detail.** A level's detail is its wavelet ψ_{j′,k}(x) = 2^{j′/2}·ψ(2^{j′}x − k), normalised to ‖ψ‖ = 1.
- **The reader.** It stands at height γ and keeps P_γ ∗ ψ, the Poisson (Cauchy) smoothing.
- **The share kept.** R = ‖P_γ ∗ ψ‖² = ⟨ψ, P_{2γ} ∗ ψ⟩, by the semigroup P_γ ∗ P_γ = P_{2γ}.
- **The three details:**
  - Haar's step ψ_H (+1 on [0, ½), −1 on [½, 1));
  - a smooth step, the Gaussian's derivative ψ_G ∝ x·e^{−x²/2};
  - the Mexican hat ψ_M ∝ (1 − x²)·e^{−x²/2}.
- **The variable.** u = γ·2^{j′}, the reader's height in the detail's own widths. A reader at its own cell's point at
  level J (γ = 2^{−(J+1)}) reading level j′ has u = 2^{j′ − J − 1}.
- **The floor, for comparison.** Cell averages at level J keep every Haar detail coarser than J whole (j′ < J, u ≤ ¼)
  and none finer (u ≥ ½): a step.

**Known beforehand, or derived here before running:**
- |ψ̂_H(η)|² = sin⁴(πη/2)/(πη/2)², with η in cycles.
- The Poisson kernel's transform is e^{−2πγ|η|}.
- So R(u) = ∫|ψ̂(η)|²·e^{−4πu|η|} dη.
- In space, the double integral of P_s over a box comes from G(t) = (1/π)(t·arctan(t/s) − (s/2)·ln(t² + s²)), with
  G″ = P_s. So R_H has a closed form.
- **Small u.**
  - A step's jumps make 1 − R go as u·ln(1/u). Its jumps, in the unit of ψ_H, are 1, 2 and 1, so Σ jump² = 6; at
    s = 2γ the leading term is (6·2/π)·u·ln(1/u) = (12/π)·u·ln(1/u).
  - For a smooth detail the loss is linear: (1 − R)/u → 4π·E|η|. For ψ_G that is 4/√π.
- **Large u.** R is set by the vanishing moments:
  - ψ_H and ψ_G have one, so |ψ̂|² ∝ η² near 0 and R ∝ u⁻³. The constants are R_H·u³ → 1/(64π) and R_G·u³ → 1/√π.
  - ψ_M has two, so R ∝ u⁻⁵.

**Predictions.** All at 50 digits with mpmath, ψ_G and ψ_M at σ = 1.

- **Q1 (one variable).** R_H computed directly in space at level j′ ∈ {0, 3, 10, 30} and index k ∈ {0, 5}, with
  γ = u·2^{−j′}, is the same at each u ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}, to 10⁻³⁰. So the share kept depends only on the reader's
  height in the detail's widths: a cut in levels, the same at every depth.
  - *Killed* otherwise.
- **Q2 (space and frequency agree).** The closed form and the frequency integral agree to 10⁻²⁵ at those four u.
  - *Killed* otherwise. A kill here would be my set-up.
- **Q3 (a soft cut, coarser than the floor).**
  - R_H falls monotonically from 1 to 0 on a grid of u from 10⁻⁸ to 10⁸.
  - Its half point u* lies in [2⁻⁶, 2⁻²]. So a reader at its own cell's point keeps half of the step detail only 2 to 6
    levels coarser than its own cell, and less than half of its own level's (R_H(½) < ½).
  - From R_H = 0.9 to R_H = 0.1 takes between 2 and 10 levels (log₂ of the ratio of the u's).
  - *Killed* if any clause fails.
- **Q4 (the step's tails).**
  - (1 − R_H)/(u·ln(1/u)) is within 5% of 12/π at u = 10⁻³⁰.
  - R_H·u³ is within 1% of 1/(64π) at u = 10³.
  - *Killed* otherwise.
- **Q5 (smooth details).**
  - (1 − R_G)/u is within 10⁻⁶ (relative) of 4/√π at u = 10⁻³⁰.
  - R_G·u³ is within 1% of 1/√π at u = 10³.
  - The log slope of R_M at u = 10³ is within 0.05 of −5.
  - *Killed* otherwise.
- **Q6 (the verdict).** The rule's variable is universal: the reader's height over the detail's width, so a soft cut
  that is the same at every depth (Q1). The curve's shape is not universal:
  - near the reader, a step loses u·ln(1/u) and a smooth detail u (Q4, Q5);
  - far off, the detail's vanishing moments set the tail (u⁻³ or u⁻⁵).

  So how much of a level's detail stays readable is fixed by the address (its level) only together with the kind of
  detail, which is the payload's.
  - *Killed* if Q1 fails, or if R_H and R_G coincide under some rescaling of u. Their near tails differ in form, so that
    would mean my tails are wrong.

### Run 3: geometry, payload or kernel (predictions, 18 Oct, before the run; Tom: "yes")

The three losses, each tested for where its law comes from. Exact at 50 digits with mpmath, except where a count of maxima
is bisected (C3, C4), which uses double precision and says so.

**A. The address window is the line's, never a payload's.** The window is the reader's own law from its point; no payload
enters it. Only the line can change it.
- On the open line the middle half is 2γ at every height.
- On a closed line of length 1 (the horizon closed, the wrapped Cauchy with ρ = e^{−2πγ}), the middle half is
  W(γ) = (2/π)·arctan(tanh(πγ)). This is derived before the run from the wrapped law's quantiles: the wrapped Cauchy is a
  Möbius image of the uniform turn.
- **A1.** W(γ) equals the middle half found by quadrature of the wrapped density (Σ of shifted Cauchys, summed to
  convergence), at γ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 0.25, 1}, to 10⁻²⁵. *Killed* otherwise.
- **A2.** Near the open line's value, (1 − W/(2γ))/γ² is within 10⁻⁶ (relative) of 2π²/3 at γ = 10⁻⁶. As the height grows,
  W → ½, half the circle (within 10⁻¹⁰ at γ = 10). *Killed* otherwise.

**B. Variation depends on the payload's structure.** Run 2 had the step and the smooth detail. Here are a family between
them and a mixture of levels.
- **B1 (the crossover).** Take the step softened at its edges, ψ_s = ψ_H ∗ g_σ with g_σ the Gaussian of width
  σ = s·2^{−j′}, normalised.
  - At s = 10⁻⁶, |R_s − R_H| < 10⁻³ at u ∈ {10⁻³, 10⁻², 10⁻¹}: above the softening, a step.
  - At u = 10⁻¹², (1 − R_s)/u is within 1% of 4π·E_s|η|, the mean of |η| under |ψ̂_s|², by quadrature: below it,
    smooth.
  - So the near law is set by the payload's own finest scale against u.
  - *Killed* if either fails.
- **B2 (levels do not read apart).** The cross term c(γ) = ⟨ψ_{0,0}, P_{2γ} ∗ ψ_{3,0}⟩ between a level and a finer one
  inside it is exactly 0 at γ = 0 (orthogonality). It is under 10⁻⁴ in size at γ = 10⁻⁶ and over 10⁻³ at γ = 0.1, by
  the closed box formula.
  - So a mixture's share kept is not the sum of its levels' shares: what a reader keeps of one level depends on the levels
    around it.
  - *Killed* if |c(0.1)| ≤ 10⁻³, or |c(10⁻⁶)| ≥ 10⁻⁴.

**C. Telling payloads apart depends on the kernel.** A threshold is the least separation at which the sum of two profiles
has two maxima.
