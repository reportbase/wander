Ranked: the substantive claims first (tables, the seven-step chain, the worked example's summary, a proposition's

1. **L677–682, §3.1 (table after "The writer's views are written")**: last column "measures a line of true breadth 1 as", reader rows "π/2" and "2/π", with no "as a writer measures it". R591/R595 (§3.4's table was retitled; this one was not). Suggested: retitle the column "a line of true breadth 1, as a writer measures it through this vantage", as in §3.4.

2. **L829–833, §3.4, the seven-step chain, step 2**: "From a standpoint the line is had through a facing: the central sweep measures it as π/2, the inscribed sweep as 2/π". R591 (the measuring is the writer's). Suggested: "a writer measuring the line through a facing finds π/2 (central) and 2/π (inscribed)"; step 3 then reads cleanly.

3. **L3382–3383, §10 closing paragraph**: "What is the same for every reader, and exact: … and the two sweeps' readings of the thumb, whose product is 1." R591; contradicts step 3 (L3345–3346), which now calls them the numbers an observer holding the whole turn writes. Suggested: drop "the two sweeps' readings of the thumb" from the reader's list, or say "and the writer's measure of the two sweeps, whose product is 1".

4. **L3130–3132, §9 "Two ways to represent a thing"**: "Of the unitless values, the geometry fixes some: the corner at 1, π/2 and 2/π, the near field 1/(n + 1)." R596(b), R591: π/2 and 2/π are made only while writing; contradicts §5 "Circular mathematics without V" (L1427–1431). Suggested: keep the corner and 1/(n + 1) as the reader's; move π/2 and 2/π to "fixed by the geometry, measured by a writer".

5. **L3949–3956 and L3959–3966, Appendix A "Archimedes' proof, the writer's and the reader's"**: "What it has is the turn from home. Its measure sin θ/θ is a measure of that turn"; "π/2 at the limb from the turn alone"; "a situated reader … has the same numbers from the turn and the reciprocal"; then "The turn gives the halvings without a polygon" leading to Viète. R591, R592, R596(c), R597: these numbers (and Viète's square roots) are the writer's. The heading itself ("the writer's and the reader's") is now wrong. Suggested: say the reader holds neither polygon nor these numbers; the numbers are a writer's measure of the turn, got without a polygon but with the turn completed; Viète's halvings are the writer's (as §3.4 L826–827 already says).

6. **L1163–1165, §4 "The address points at the payload", bullet "In the two readings"**: "Read along the ray, line over arc, the inscribed sweep gives sin θ/θ, 2/π over the whole turn; read along the arc, arc over line, the central sweep gives θ/sin θ, π/2". R591 (called "readings", unattributed). Suggested: "measured by a writer, the inscribed sweep gives … the central …".

7. **L1213–1215, §4 "What each sweep reads"**: "density 2/(π(1 + s²)) = (2/π)·h²: at every address, the inscribed reading 2/π times the foot". R591, R596(h): the Cauchy's 2/π is the writer's normalizing constant, not "the inscribed reading"; contradicts Theorem 6(d) (L2135–2137). Suggested: "the writer's constant 2/π times the foot"; or state the shape 1/(1 + s²) = h² as the reader's and the 2/π as the writer's.

8. **L691–695, §3.1 "Two other parties", "Both read"**: "Over a whole hemisphere the central measure is its area over its projection on the base, 2 …; the inscribed measure is its mean height …, ½; and 2 · ½ = 1, as π/2 · 2/π = 1". R596(g), R591: unattributed here, while §3.4 L818–824 calls them a writer's. Suggested: add "as a writer measures them (the hemisphere's totals)".

9. **L747–749, §3.3 after the bullets**: "the reader's live on the unit line, and its two sweeps are the two ways of comparing the layouts … The unit line is the address space". R584, R591, R597: the unit line is the angle θ laid straight, length π/2, a share of the completed turn; the reader has readings and their order, not θ. Contradicts the bullets above it (L740–743, "as a writer measures it"). Suggested: "a writer lays the reader's addresses on the unit line"; keep "the number line is what is addressed".

10. **L273–275, Results bullet**: "What is known of the breadths sets the vantage, one of two: a writer, who holds the circle or another shape whole, or a reader". R594 (roles, not kinds), R598 (knowing V by degrees). Suggested: "What is known of the breadths sets what an observer is doing: writing, holding a shape whole, or reading from somewhere; knowing V comes by degrees, through parallax (§9)."

11. **L289–292, Results bullet**: "With three, the one length H turned in two angles traces one sphere, and the addresses fill one octant of it". R591, R595: the sphere is the writer's picture of the reader; the reader reads with the Cauchy (§3.7 L962–963 says so). Suggested: "traces what a writer draws as one sphere (the one-length sphere); the reader's addresses are one octant, a wedge".

12. **L46–47, Abstract; also L294–295, Results**: "past it … the only consistent layout is logarithmic". R596(d): the reader holds the count of addresses a doubling; 1 + ln x is the writer's formula for it (now stated after Proposition 4, L1548–1551). Suggested: "past it … the only consistent layout gives every doubling the same count of addresses: logarithmic, in the writer's formula".

13. **L49–50, Abstract**: "The geometry needs that value and a shape, set by how many unit relations the system includes: two give the circle, three the sphere." R591, R595: circle and sphere held whole are the writer's. Low weight but in the abstract. Suggested: "two give the one-length circle, three the one-length sphere, each held whole only by a writer; from one place the reader has a quarter or a wedge".

14. **L597–600 and L628–632, §3.1 (vantage table; "The rule", question 2)**: table "the reader | … | V never known"; rule "Is V known? If it is, the whole is held … If it is not, the reading is from somewhere"; and L632 "Every reading is the writer's or the reader's, and none is both". R598 (knowing V by degrees, in what unit, how well, of which things), R594. Contradicts §11 "Knowing V, by degrees" (L3485–3492). Suggested: keep the two vantages as the limits, with a sentence pointing to §11: between them an observer knows V for some things, to some accuracy, in some unit.

15. **L1008–1014, §3.8 closing paragraphs**: "it never knows V … A reader that comes to know V has written … A writer, knowing V, has a closed circle". R598: binary. Not wrong for one standpoint, but now at odds with §11's "by degrees". Suggested: add "from one standpoint"; and "knowing V comes by degrees as standpoints are joined (§11), the exact writer its limit".

16. **L3148–3153 and L3159–3160, §9 "Reading and writing"**: "The circle is the reader's one length turned, there before anything arrives … one circle held two ways, made by the one from H alone and known by the other"; "Writers and readers are told apart by their vantage"; "To know a breadth is to hold the whole … which no standpoint supplies". R595 (the closed one-length circle is held only while writing; the reader has the open quarter), R594, R598. Contradicts §5's opening (L1298–1302) and §3.1 "Roles, not kinds". Suggested: "the one-length circle, whose open quarter the reader has from H alone and which an observer writing holds closed"; "reading and writing are told apart by vantage, and are roles one observer passes between"; "no one standpoint supplies".

17. **L886–891, §3.6, the five things a situated reader holds**: "*the sweep's progress*, g = (2/π)·arctan(v/h)". R584: arctan and π are the writer's; contradicts §2 L450–452 ("The formula is the writer's account of it; the reader evaluates nothing"). Suggested: "the sweep's progress g, the share of the quarter turn passed (written by a writer as (2/π)·arctan(v/h))".

18. **L1750–1751, Proposition 5(c)**: "Placing each reading at its angle of turn, rather than at its value, gives the *fisheye*". R584, R597: the angle is a share of the completed turn, the writer's. Also L670 "their running total is the fisheye" in the reader's bullet. Suggested: say the fisheye is the writer's drawing of the reader's addresses by turn, or "at its share of the turn passed".

19. **L2234–2235, §7.2 "Directions are read by their order"**: "to average, average the angle, which is bounded". R584/R597: the angle is the writer's chart; the paragraph otherwise tells a reader to take the median. Suggested: "a writer who wants a mean takes it on the bounded chart, the angle"; leave the median and tangent addition as the reader's.

20. **L3596–3603, §11 "Special relativity's sky"**: "A reader holding directions alone reads its own rapidity, a reading, with no clock, no frequency and no c carried in". R596/R597: rapidity η on ξ = −ln tan(θ/2) is a number in nats, a writer's formula; the reader has the moved median direction. Suggested: "reads the shift of the median direction, which a writer states as its rapidity η; scaling the world moves neither".

21. **L3993–3995, Appendix A "Poincaré's enlarged universe"**: "His representative space … against his geometric space … is the reader's sphere against the writer's cube (§3.7)". R591, R595 ("the reader's sphere" renamed; the sphere is the writer's read of the reader). Suggested: "the reader's addresses (which a writer draws as the one-length sphere) against the writer's cube".

22. **L1579–1583, §6 "A second route", Saint-Vincent**: "Two ways of holding a relation … Hold the whole … the swept area is the turn … Hold only the corner … the swept area is ln(v/h) … every doubling the same area ln 2". R597 (e completes the scale; the reader counts doublings, nats are the writer's). The "holding" wording gives ln to the reader. Suggested: say both areas are a writer's measures, the one of the turn and the other of the count of doublings.
