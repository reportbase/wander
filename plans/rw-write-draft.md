# A read and a write, defined: draft passage

12 November 2026. A draft for Tom to read in the paper's voice, from the exchange recorded in
`plans/discussion-note.md` item 16. **Not settled and not in any paper.** If settled it would replace D4 and D5's
"what moves is a write" in `papers/readers-and-writers.md`, fold D3 into D2, and reverse R880.

---

**A read and a write.** Two properties sort everything this paper discusses. One is whether a thing is
continuous or discrete. The other is whether scaling the world, the thumb included, moves it.

*A read is continuous and unmoved by scaling.* From one standpoint, with the thumb held out as H, every direction
has a slope s = v/h, and the slopes are spread by one density, ds/(1 + s²) up to its total: the Cauchy density.
Nothing in it is bounded, nothing in it is counted, and it is the same over a world of any size. Two looks held
together are still a read: the distance they give, in thumbs, is a continuous ratio of their slopes,
1/s₁ + 1/s₂, with no count in it.

*A write is discrete and moved by scaling.* It is a bounded thing with a size: a stone, a stride, a mark on a
wall, a metre bar. A write is over once it is made, and it is used only by being read. Reading is continuous, and
what is read is discrete.

These two are the diagonal of a table.

| | unmoved by scaling | moved by scaling |
|---|---|---|
| **continuous** | a read | the number line in a unit |
| **discrete** | a count | a write |

The other two boxes mix the properties, and both are tools of description. A count is discrete and unmoved by
scaling: the strides to the mountain are writes, and "514" is an account laid over them. The number line in a
unit is continuous and moved by scaling: a length as a real number, the circle held whole, π. That box is the
blueprint, classical geometry, which this paper sets beside a read's form for comparison. The same holds of this
paper's own terms. The slope, the density's formula and this table are descriptions too, and a read is described
by them. When something will not sit on the diagonal, the first thing to ask is whether it is a description.

This is a reading, offered as a way to sort, and it claims nothing about what the world is made of. Its use is
practical: the first test (scale the world) and the second (change what arrives) keep their work, the first now
sorting descriptions of writes into ratios and amounts in a unit.
