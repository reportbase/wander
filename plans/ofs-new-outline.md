## 1. The new frame (R453, R454)

What changed, in Tom's words:
- "the reader is not a radar."
- "the fingers are laid by cauchy, the fingers are the inscribed sweep. the light rays of the world are the payloads,
  they draw on the readers fingers"
- "the fingers are pure cauchy geometry, the readers does nothing, it is free. where ever he looks, this geometry is
  created."
- "the laying of the address is inside, the drawing on thise addresses outside. there is no sweeping going on, the
  sweep is just a term that describes what the geometry does for free."
- "we have used sweep to describe an action by the reader, as if it is a radar or something, that is wrong"
- "this paper has been treating the address reader and the paylad reader as two different types of readers. as if they
  are something reader can be or do. the sweeps are geometric, and describe the inside sweep of addresses, and the
  outside sweep of painting of those addreses."

What that gives:
1. **One situated reader.** It is not two types (the address reader and the payload reader). Its acts are only where it
   stands, where it looks, and its H. Holding is a separate question (R309, R321).
2. **Two sweeps, both geometric**, neither an act:
   - *the inside sweep*: the addresses, laid from the standpoint alone before anything arrives. These are the fingers,
     the uniform turn's measure on the directions, the Cauchy. It is the inscribed angle, a quarter turn, 2/π.
   - *the outside sweep*: the world's light painting those addresses, arriving from outside along each finger. It is the
     central angle, a half turn, π/2: where each finger lands on the circle on the chord (Euclid III.20).
   - Their product, 1, gives back the line, as before. Both numbers now belong to the geometry, not to a reader.
3. **Nothing moves.** "Sweep" names what the geometry gives for free. The head turn, the radar, the beam and the lamp
   are pictures of it, not its mechanism. A lighthouse really turns and sends: it stays as the picture of the same turn
   aimed outward.
4. **The writer is unchanged:** the box, a number line on each axis, amounts, both signs, no corner (R338, R445).
   Ranging is writing (R388).
5. **Two vantages, not three:** the writer and the situated reader. "π/2 to one reader and 2/π to the other" becomes
   "the outside sweep's π/2 and the inside sweep's 2/π".

### Still evolving: open, to be ruled before or while writing

- **A. Situations 3 and 4.** The record defines them by what a reader receives: amounts along given directions (3), or
  directions alone (4). Proposed: they become the two sweeps of every situated reading, the painting (3) and the
  addresses (4). A reading of the addresses with the paint set aside (a census of directions) stays possible, so 4 can
  occur alone; 3 never occurs without 4. Alternatively, the five situations shrink to four: 0, 1, 2, and the situated
  reading.
- **B. What the paint carries.** The record's "the central reading is an amount" (R307) and the payload reader's radius
  along each given direction (R451, R452) came from the radar picture. Painting carries no distance (R426). It carries
  what the light brings: brightness, colour, what is nearest on the line. Proposed: the polar shape r(θ) and its modes
  move to "what a writer or a second standpoint adds". The paint's own amounts (strengths) stay payload. Their
  arithmetic (sums, means) is the payload's, not the geometry's (Theorem 6(c)).
- **C. Where π/2 lives.** Proposed: as the outside sweep's measure, the arc over the chord, a fact of the circle on the
  chord. No reader reads it.
- **D. Words.** Proposed: "inside" and "outside" are introduced once, as where each sweep comes from (the standpoint
  for the addresses, the world for the paint), and "inscribed" and "central" are used after that as the formal names.
  "Inside" and "outside" were dropped on 16 October as names for the hemispheres (R304). This is a different use.
- **E. "One read, two breakdowns" (R332).** Kept as: one finger, its address and its paint, meeting at one point
  (III.20).

- **F. Scope: the geometry, not reader adaptations (R455).** Tom: "This paper is about the geometry, not reader
  adaptations." Proposed as adaptations, and so moved out of the body, kept in SPN and the plans or reduced to a sentence
  that names the condition a result needs:
  - holding, at once or in order (§3.5, §7.6 "Order needs holding", the holding clauses in §3.1);
  - the instruments in "Two other parties" (camera, lidar, scanner, total station) and §3.5's "with no motion at all";
  - the head and the eye (§9 "Turning in place gives no parallax"), the robotics paragraph, "More points, fewer told
    apart" (§8);
  - the reader's acts as acts ("chooses", "takes standpoints"), where the geometry can say "given a standpoint and H".

  Kept as geometry: the writer's propositions on amounts (7–14), scale the world, the tower, the room and the survey as
  worked cases. These are open to Tom's ruling item by item.
- **G. Recursion, noted and not claimed (R455).** Tom: "The recursive, fractal nature of observation is noted, but not
  well understood, inside and outside implies a recursive situation, but that can only be speculated about."
  Proposed: "Writers within writers" and "Within one box, exterior to the rest" (record §9) leave the body. One paragraph
  in §11's open questions notes the nesting that inside and outside suggest, as speculation. This is a different sense
  from R188's ruling that the levels do not recurse (plain doublings), which stands. Open point D is affected: if
  "inside" and "outside" name the sweeps, the paper says once that the words describe where each sweep comes from, not a
  nesting.
