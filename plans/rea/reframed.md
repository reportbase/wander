The terms used in every row:
- **The address** is the variable a uniform turn lands at, a direction [x : 1].

- **The payload** is the amounts that arrive or are carried: strengths, counts, sizes.
- **The reframed result** is the entry's conclusion stated in those terms.

## What the reframing shows

- **Every Cauchy-carrying entry has a standpoint z.** In the physics (H17–H48) the height is a rate: a decay, a collision,
  a diffusion. The amounts are the strengths and the counts. So in the line shapes the address is the frequency offset
  over the width, and the payload is the strength. The width is not a spread of the payload.
- **Where a reader's scale appears,** it is H: the reference scale of Jeffreys's prior (H55), A in Gelman's half-Cauchy
  (H56), c in the Cauchy loss (H58). Two corners also appear: the half-Cauchy's corner at σ = A, and the tunnelling
  barrier's full transmission at Γ_L = Γ_R (H40).
- **Every "breaks"** is an amount entering: a cutoff, a threshold, an added amplitude, a Gaussian part. These are OFS §7.2's
  breakers, found in the catalogue.
- **The two D entries** are where the payload's nature had been read from the tail: a forager's optimum, a dispersal
  mechanism. Reframed, their tails are the standpoint's height (the distance to edges, the release height), and that can
  be tested.
