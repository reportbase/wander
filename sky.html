The dark sky

If bodies filled an endless world that had always been, every line of sight would end on one and the night sky would blaze. Farther shells are fainter, but there are more of them, exactly enough to make up for it. The sky is dark because a reader holds only what has arrived: the bodies began sending a while ago, and past the reach of their light there is nothing yet to see. The dark between them reads how long they have been sending.

A patch of your sky, 160 by 160 cells. A cell is lit once light from the first body on its line of sight has had time to arrive. Lit cells are equally bright, near or far.

The lit share against the reach. Line: 1 − e−L/λ. Dot: this patch.

Why every shell gives the same light

Shells of equal thickness, out from you. Gold: how many bodies, growing as v². Teal: the light you catch from each, falling as 1/v². White: their product, the shell's light, the same for every shell (before nearer bodies hide farther ones). The inverse square loses nothing; it spreads the same light over more room.
