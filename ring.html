A ring of slopes. Each item of the array is read by one slope, s = (distance from the focus, in items) ÷ H. The bar lays it at x = s/(1 + |s|): fractions only, no angle, no π, no square root.

The H items either side of the focus take half the bar, in proportion: the near field. Past them the far field bunches toward the ends, which are the horizon: two thumbs out at ⅔ of the way, ten at 0.91, never the end. Every item has a place; near the ends they are a strip of their paint. So about the same number are told apart whatever N is: K, not N.

Repaint the array and nothing moves: the addresses are the read's, the contents the payload's. Drag to move the focus (the item under your finger stays there), wheel or pinch to change H, tap an item to bring it to the middle.

Counted round is N known. Without wrap the array runs out to both horizons and there is no index to jump by, only the slope from the focus. With it the ends meet, and they can meet only because N is known: a count mod N, not a turn. A known N is a snapshot of the array as it stood; add items and where the ring closes moves, while the slopes without the wrap stay where they were.

Addresses only draws the empty slots, each with its slope, before any payload. The gold outlines are those slots drawn over the paint: repaint, choose another kind of array or multiply every number by a million, and the paint changes under outlines that never move. Double N or keep adding grows the array with the focus held: the count told apart stays where it is, only the strips at the horizon thicken (the plot keeps both). The writer's layout gives every item an equal share of the width, 2W/N each: a large N leaves each under a pixel, an array without end has no width.

The games page's ring lays equal turns by tan and keeps the middle: the same idea in the dual vantage's terms, and a ring that closes. This one has two horizons and no turn; "wrap" only counts round the array.
