The crosshair is the corner

A first-person view, laid by its addresses instead of drawn as a picture. Across: the direction from the crosshair. Up: each thing's depth address, from home at the bottom to the horizon at the top. Every one of the K depth cells has the same room on the screen; what differs is how much distance each holds. Lay depth by the turn about the focus and the thing under the crosshair sits at the corner, address ½, in the finest cell there is, π/K of its own distance, whatever the scene's range. Things nearer and farther coarsen alike, in mirror image. Tap a thing to look at it.

The view laid by its addresses. Across, the direction from the crosshair (equal angles, ±60°); up, the depth address, one band a cell. A thing is coloured by its cell's width relative to its place, over π/K: 1, 5, 30 and more. Twins (1.25 apart in distance) in one cell are drawn as one, ringed in red. The gold line is the corner (address ½); with the turn, the focus sits on it.

Along the levels, ln d from the near plane n = 1 to the scene's far end X. Top: each scheme's K cell edges (turn, log, standard); the turn's are mirror images about the focus, d ↦ f²/d. Below: each scheme's relative cell over π/K, in doublings, the band where the turn is finer than log depth shaded, ±acosh(ln(X/n)/π) nats about the focus. Ticks along the bottom are the things.

The numbers are runs/fpv/fpv.py's (FPV, exact): at the focus the turn's cell is π/K whatever the range; log depth's is ln(X/n)/K, 5.13 times coarser over 10⁷; at 32 bits the turn is 97 times finer than reversed-Z there, for about 7 doublings either way. Geometry, not a renderer: no claim that a game should, only what the corner gives where it is put. See also the focus is the corner, H is a zoom, the corner in flight, all the demos.
