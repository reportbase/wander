The mountain and the thumb

A reader looks at a mountain range, face on. Its thumb is its one measure, H = 1 mile. From nowhere we also know the mountain, V = 20 miles wide, and its distance, 10 miles. The reader knows only its thumb. Read from where it stands, the range's ends sit exactly at its corners; to say anything in miles it must set the mountain equal to its thumb, and it is then off by twenty; one walk of a thumb's length gives it the truth.

From nowhere

The blueprint, from above: the thumb (gold), the range (teal) and the sight lines to its ends. Every amount is held, and the 45° guides are simply where the ends happen to be.

From where the reader stands

The reader's fingers, spread evenly over its half turn, with the corners at ±45°. Fingers that reach the range carry a ring: the ridge's elevation. Scale the world and nothing here moves.

Mountain equals thumb

The reader's lay of distances, in thumbs: proportional inside the corner (one thumb), one equal step per doubling past it. Gold: where the reader puts the range when it sets the mountain equal to its thumb. Teal: where it truly is. The gap is V/H, the number of thumbs the mountain really is.

Keep the case: h and v are unit relations, and the reader's view holds only their ratios, which have no scale. H, V and d are amounts, in miles; the reader has only H. Each pair has its own corner: the range's ends sit at the corner of the pair (half-width, distance), not of the reader's thumb against anything.
