The focus is the corner

Nothing in the geometry says where the reader's corner goes; H is its own choice. Look at a thing and the choice is made: turning carries the corner, the direction where v = h, onto it, and designating it draws it at H, which puts it at the corner of distance too. Both are free. And the corner is where a focus should be: the finest relative grain, the reciprocal's fixed point, and the place from which the next look is nearest on average. How far the thing really is stays outside every reading.

(the things: )

From above, home at the bottom. The frame's quarter turn from h to v, its K equal-angle addresses (gold) and its corner (the dashed bisector, v = h); the K distance addresses at H·tan(kπ/2K) (teal rings), drawn on the reader's lay: in proportion inside the corner circle (radius H), then 1 + ln(d/H). Things outside the quarter turn belong to another facing and are dim.

The grain. A cell's width relative to its place, (π/2K)(s + 1/s), against the levels of s, over its least, π/K. One curve serves both corners: s = v/h in direction (gold dots) and s = d/H in distance (teal dots). The ring is the thing tapped.

The travel to the next look. Its place X follows the uniform turn weighted by 1 + a·cos 2θ (a > 0 nearer, a < 0 farther). For a focus at c, the expected travel E|g(X) − g(c)| on three rulers: the angle, the levels ln s and the in-place lay, each drawn over its own least. All three are least at the same c, the law's median: the corner when a = 0.
