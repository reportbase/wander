The payload doesn't matter, in 3D

The reader sweeps evenly over a whole octant: up from h toward v, round from h toward v₂. Where each ray points is its address, [h : v : v₂], fixed by the sweep alone. Put anything behind it, at any size, or nothing at all: the addresses do not move. Only what each ray finds, the payload, changes. The same in 2D.

Home at the corner where the three axes meet: h, v (up) and v₂. Drag to turn, scroll or pinch to zoom. Each ray of the sweep, and a dot where it meets something, in its colour; a ray that meets nothing runs to the horizon. Distances on the reader's lay: in proportion out to H (the gold arcs), then each tenfold the same width (arcs at 10, 10³, 10⁶, 10⁹, 10¹²).

From home: the wedge is the triangle h + v + v₂ = 1, every address at its shares. The dashed medians are the three pairs' corners (v = h, v₂ = h, v₂ = v); the middle triangle, the near field in both readings, holds a third. The dots never move; their colours are the payload.

Lambert's equal-area map of the wedge: azimuth across (h on the left, v₂ on the right), height v up. The sweep's grid is even here, one cell per address, the corners dashed. Painted, it is a picture of whatever the rays met; the cells themselves are the same in every world.

For contrast, an equal-metres reader: a flat screen one H away along h, its pixels spaced evenly out to the world's size, shown from home. It needs that size. Every S moves its addresses, and with an unknown or empty world it has none.

The address is the sweep's; whatever arrives can be swapped at every address and no address moves (Observation from Somewhere, Theorem 6(a), Proposition 5(e)). So nothing the addresses say is about the payload, and the world's size never enters them: equal angles are free, equal metres cost N. All demos.
