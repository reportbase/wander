A bridge behind the dial

The calendar's dial browses a list with no end: the gold needle is a rate, the blue needle a place, and the record under it is one thumb times sinh of how far it has been pushed. Behind it a Tangent Bridge holds a window round the record, K leaves, and is rebuilt at every move. Push the needle out to a trillion thumbs and watch what stays put. A window in ratios holds content that is the same in ratio at the same error everywhere, at a cost of K and with no angle; content with a size in records needs a window in records.

Press and drag sideways to push the gold; let go and the needle rests where it reached. The wheel nudges it. The heat band outside the arc is cos²θ, all at home, half at the corner.

The window round the record: the content (grey), the bridge rebuilt from its K leaves (teal, the leaves as dots), and the error, magnified to be seen (red).

From the dial test (DBR, plans/dbr-plan.md): at 24 leaves the ratio window's error on ratio content is 1.14–1.23·10⁻³ from 10 to 10¹² thumbs; at 48 it is at the double's rounding. A ratio window cannot hold content with a size even at ten thumbs; a window in records holds it until the machine stops telling the records apart, near 10⁹ thumbs. Each read is K terms, with no length of the list in it, and no angle.

Without N, only a sweep. Given the list's length the dial can browse by index, a window recentred after each move; given only a thumb there is nothing to divide by, so it pushes out from home at a rate, and that is enough: any record in time growing with the logarithm of how far, the same edges and heat held at every place. A known N is a snapshot: once the list grows, everything laid against the length moves, and the sweep's places do not.
