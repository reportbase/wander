The floor's depth

An array index is ⌊x⌋: the floor keeps the cell and throws the fraction away. But the fraction has a home. Each binary digit is one step down, and the cells, laid by level, are points m + iγ of the half-plane the Cauchy lives in: the cell's middle and its half-width. In that plane every cell has the same room, every level is the next, and the cells with their neighbours measure the distances. What the floor drops is the second coordinate: how deep.

Tap to place x (a second tap below the middle places the second place). Rows are levels, the reader's line at the bottom; gold the cells holding x, violet the second place's; below L the cells are merged into what is kept. Shade: the totals, a cascade made from each cell's address, never stored. Teal on the line: the Cauchy read from the kept cell's point.

"In levels" draws each level the same height, as the half-plane's own metric does (a level is ln 2 tall); "as the plane" draws γ itself, so the deep levels crowd onto the line.

Two fades

Against the frequency n (in cycles a cell): gold what the floor keeps of a wave, the cell's average |sinc(πn)|, with zeros and side lobes; teal what a Cauchy read from height γ keeps, e^(−2πnγ), smooth and never zero. No height makes them one: the closest leaves 0.22.

How much of each level a reader keeps

A reader at its own cell's point (level L above, so γ is half that cell's width) reading the detail of every level, coarser to the left, finer to the right. Teal: the share of that level's detail it keeps. Gold outline: what the floor at level L keeps, everything coarser whole and nothing else. The reader's cut is soft and the same at every depth, since only its height over the detail's width counts; its shape is the detail's. A step and a smooth bump of the same width fade differently.

Telling two apart

Two sources read through one kernel: each faint, their sum bright, its peaks marked red. Whether they read as two depends on the kernel and on the sources: equal Cauchy profiles part at 2/√3 of their width, Gaussians at 2, a cusped kernel at any separation. A fainter second source needs more room, and the Cauchy's long tails bury it sooner than the Gaussian's.

The window, open and closed

The address window, the middle half of the Cauchy read from height γ, against γ (both on log scales). Gold: on an open line, 2γ at every height. Teal: on a closed line of length 1, (2/π)·arctan(tanh πγ), the same while the reader is low, and never more than half the line however high it goes. The dot is the reader at level L above. No payload enters either curve: the window is the line's.
