One vantage

A read is a single hemispherical vantage: one standpoint, everything in front of it, nothing behind. The blueprint is the dual hemispherical vantage, both hemispheres at once, drawn from no standpoint. Five panels, one idea each, taken from the sky city and the walk on whole numbers.

1. What is held, and what is shown

Seen from above. Every thing in front of the eye is weighed by the keep rule (its breadth against the grain times its distance, the grain growing off the look and away from the thumb); the flat screen shows only the gold wedge. Held and on the screen: teal. Held and off it: violet. Behind, or too small: grey.

2. Made as needed

How far the world goes. A blueprint stored out to that reach holds every cell; the read looks at the same cells near the eye whatever the reach, and each thing is made from its cell when the read reaches it. Only what has been changed has to be kept.

3. A law in time, or state to keep

Car A (gold) goes round its ring by a law in the tick, so its place at any tick comes from the tick alone. Car B (red) drives at its own pace but waits whenever A is close ahead: it has no law, so its state has to be stepped from the start and held.

4. No trigonometry, no π

A turn is a half-slope, 1/8: its rotation is the pair (63/65, 16/65), whole numbers over a whole number. Turned n steps and n back in exact fractions, the point comes home exactly. The classical way, cos and sin of the same angle in floating point, drifts. The cost of the exact way is digits. The same holds for a loop that comes back turned: built from rational half-slopes, its leftover's half-slope is a fraction (two turns, half-slopes 2 and ¾: cos(Θ/2) = 17/25).

5. A bounce writes its size

Two balls, the right one four times the left in size, bounce and distance, each seen from a standpoint four times as far: the same picture, the same directions at the top and the foot. Under one pull the larger takes twice the ticks, so the ticks, counted, give the size.

The bounce is not alone. A pendulum's period in units of √(ℓ/g) depends on its swing only, and a Kepler orbit's in units of √(a³/GM) is 2π: from one standpoint the ticks read no size, and with g or GM carried in the ticks counted write the length or the orbit's size.
