An infinite star field

The writer sees the stars where they are: give the world more and its far field fills. The reader sees each star only as a direction, one of its own addresses at equal angles, and holds the share of what it reads at each. However many stars there are, N or 2N or infinitely many, that view is the same: the unit circle, in proportion on both sides of the corner. N is invisible to the reader.

The writer's view

Where the stars are, every distance an amount: home at bottom left, h to the right, v up, laid in proportion out to H (the gold arc) and every doubling the same width past it (arcs at 10, 100, …), so the infinite field fits. The corner, v = h, dashed. Double N and the far field holds twice the stars. Drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom; point at a star for its index, click to pin.

The reader's view

Each star read as a direction only, counted at the reader's own addresses (equal angles, h to v). Each address is a ray as long as its share of what is read, times the number of addresses: an even share reaches the unit circle (gold), whatever N is. Inside the corner the reading is v/h, past it h/v, both in proportion: for the reader there is no far field to fill. Above at N, below at 2N. With fewer stars than addresses the reader sees single stars, spikes and gaps; once N is well past its addresses, N and 2N are the same circle.

The writer needs N: to place a star it needs its distance, and the far field's count grows with N. The reader needs none of it: its addresses are fixed by its turn, and its view is the share of what it reads at each, the same at N, 2N and ∞ (the payload doesn't matter). The same in 3D, with H to change. All demos.
