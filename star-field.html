An infinite star field

The writer sees the stars where they are: give the world more and its far field fills. The reader sees each star only as a direction, one of its own addresses at equal angles, and holds the share of what it reads at each. However many stars there are, N or 2N or infinitely many, that view is the same: the unit circle, in proportion on both sides of the corner. N is invisible to the reader. Its count of addresses, K, is fixed and comes first: choosing H, its zoom, only splits K in two, half inside H and half past it.

The reader's addresses in distance, and H

The reader's addresses laid along distance by its turn, d = H·tan θ at equal angles θ, on a scale of doublings of u (the world's own unit). Half of them always lie inside H (teal, v/h ≤ 1, in proportion) and half past it (violet, h/v ≤ 1, wider and wider to the horizon). The count K is fixed and comes first; H is the zoom that splits it. A small H packs the near half into a small stretch of the world, each address proportionally smaller; a large H spreads it out and reaches farther. Whatever N is, the reader holds it at these addresses, never more of them.

Both readers, one K: the reader's K × K

The address reader reads which of its K directions a star lies in (the rays, at equal angles; half either side of the corner, v = h, gold dashed). The payload reader reads which of its K amounts along that direction (the rings, at d = H·tan(kπ/2K), drawn on the in-place lay: d/H inside H, 2 − H/d past it, so the whole field is 2H across, the far rings mirroring the near ones about H and the horizon the outer edge). Every star falls in one cell of the K × K, whatever N is. Move H: no ray moves, so no star changes its direction address; the rings slide, so stars change their distance address. The reader interacts with K; the writer, who needs every star's distance, interacts with N. (Lines drawn are thinned to at most 64 a side; the cells counted are all K.)

The writer's view

Where the stars are, every distance an amount: home at bottom left, h to the right, v up, laid in proportion out to H (the gold arc) and every doubling the same width past it (arcs at 10, 100, …), so the infinite field fits. The corner, v = h, dashed. Double N and the far field holds twice the stars. Drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom; point at a star for its index, click to pin.

The reader's view

Each star read as a direction only, counted at the reader's own addresses (equal angles, h to v). Each address is a ray as long as its share of what is read, times K, the number of addresses: an even share reaches the unit circle (gold), whatever N is. Inside the corner the reading is v/h, past it h/v, both in proportion: for the reader there is no far field to fill. Above at N, below at 2N. With fewer stars than addresses the reader sees single stars, spikes and gaps; once N is well past its addresses, N and 2N are the same circle.

The writer needs N: to place a star it needs its distance, and the far field's count grows with N. The reader needs none of it: its addresses are fixed by its turn, and its view is the share of what it reads at each, the same at N, 2N and ∞ (the payload doesn't matter). The same in 3D. All demos.
