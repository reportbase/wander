Planes of directions

A reading [h : v] is a line through home: one direction, no amount. The Grassmannian Gr(k, n) is the next thing up: every k-dimensional plane through home in n dimensions, a reader holding k directions at once. Two planes meet not at one angle but at k of them, the principal angles (Jordan, 1875), each a direction with its own corner at 45°. Gr(1, 2) is OFS's own space of readings, the quarter turn.

One plane, k angles

The plane drawn uniformly (every turn alike), against the home plane (the first k axes). Its k principal angles laid as fingers on the quarter turn from home: teal inside the corner (θ ≤ 45°, tan θ ≤ 1), violet past it. The dashed line is the corner.

How many angles are inside the corner

Bars: the exact law of the number inside (de Bruijn's Pfaffian, n = 2k). Gold ticks: the planes drawn here. Each angle alone is inside half the time; together they repel, so the count bunches in the middle and both ends grow thin.

The Cauchy is the directions'; the corner is the exchange's

One entry of X, a single direction's reading, against the standard Cauchy (teal curve): the same at every k and n. Violet: det X, the plane read as one number; at k = 2 its law is ½(1 + |r|)⁻² (dashed), not a Cauchy.

The tangents' product, ln ∏ tan θᵢ. Gold line: its median; white: zero, the corner of the whole plane. With n = 2k swapping the plane for its complement (X ↦ X⁻¹) changes nothing, and the median sits on the corner. With n = 2k + 1 there is no such swap and it drifts off.

All k at once

ln P(all k angles inside the corner) against k. Dots: exact (k ≤ 8 in closed form, 20 … 60 from 300-digit Pfaffians). Curve: −(ln 2/2)k² − ln(1 + 1/√2)·k + 0.2674, both leading terms derived (the angles' repulsion squeezed into half the room; the room's entropy and the arcsine weight losing an end).

What it says about OFS

  • The addresses are the first Grassmannian. A reading [h : v] is a point of Gr(1, 2), [h : v : v₂] of Gr(1, 3). The uniform turn is the only measure on it every turn leaves alone; in the chart v/h it is the Cauchy (Proposition 5(d)), in two charts the wedge's (5(e)).
  • The Cauchy belongs to directions. Go up to planes and the uniform measure is still a Cauchy, the matrix-variate one (Dickey, 1967), and every single entry, one direction's reading, is the standard Cauchy at every k and n. Read the plane as one number (det X) and it is not: an amount entering breaks the family.
  • The corner comes from the exchange, not from the law. The product of the tangents has its median at 1 exactly when swapping the plane for its complement is a symmetry (n = 2k): Theorem 6(e)'s reciprocal symmetry, one level up. Without it (Gr(2, 5)) the corner is the lower quartile.
  • Directions held together repel. Each angle is inside the corner half the time; all k at once only about 1.31·2−k²/2·(1 + 1/√2)−k: ½, 0.107, 0.011, 6·10⁻⁴ … The near field is a property of one direction at a time.

Classical throughout (Jordan's angles, Dickey's Cauchy, de Bruijn's Pfaffian, the log-gas); the reading as a reader's addresses is ours. In OFS only as Appendix A's "Planes of directions".
