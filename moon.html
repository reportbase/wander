One moon

A single moon crossing the sky, nothing else. The reader's addresses, the inscribed sweep, cover the whole wedge, given before anything arrives; each tick the moon's light paints a few of them: a small patch, moving over addresses that stay put. From the patch alone come the moon's track, its turn per tick, its time to contact and its path's angle, all ratios and ticks. Its shape about its own centre is a radius along each direction from that centre, an amount in a unit nothing reads, had only by ranging (a writing) or from further standpoints; shown here over the face it turns toward you. One amount carried in writes its distance and speed.

The inscribed sweep: the whole wedge, Lambert's equal-area map (azimuth across from h to v₂, height up to v), every cell an equal share, given. Gold: the corners. White: the addresses the moon paints this tick; teal: its track.

The same addresses near the moon, one pixel a cell. Nothing here is a size: the patch is its share of the turn.

The shape about the moon's centre (radii, a writing, over their geometric mean) along the directions seen from home (bright), and all seen so far (dim), viewed from the start.

The test is MON (run 2: detections unmoved by scaling; ω exact; τ and the path angle exact for the smooth moon, off by up to 9% for the one with relief, whose turning profile its share takes in). Other demos: the inscribed sweep among many bodies, all demos.
