Circles and standpoints

Epicycles draw a shape with circles on circles, each turning a whole number of times a lap: the reader at the centre, turning evenly. The Cauchy can draw with the same circles in two ways. From a height, it shrinks circle k by e−γ|k| and moves none: the fine circles fade first, which is level of detail. From standpoints stepped toward the detail, each term is the circle seen from a point inside it, and a dent that takes eighty circles takes about a dozen such terms. Where nothing is local, the even circles win.

Error against terms, on a log scale: gold the best n epicycles, teal the aimed terms. The dashed lines are 10⁻², 10⁻³ and 10⁻⁴; the white tick is the slider's n.

Classical throughout: Abel–Poisson summation, Takenaka (1925) and Malmquist (1926), Qian's adaptive Fourier decomposition (Qian and Wang 2011). The reading is ours: even circles are one standpoint at the centre; the aimed terms are standpoints moved toward the detail, the focus at the corner applied to drawing a shape. The outline's size, a₀, is in none of the terms. The test is EPI (plans/epi-plan.md, runs 1 and 2).
