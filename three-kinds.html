Three kinds

Two readings compose by one rule, s ⊕ a = (s + a)/(1 − κs·a). With κ = 1 it is a turn (the circle), with κ = −1 a boost (the hyperbola: speeds added as relativity adds them), with κ = 0 a stride (the line: speeds added as Galileo did). Each walk below starts at home and takes n equal steps of half-slope t, in exact fractions.

One identity holds for all three, 1 + κ(s ⊕ a)² = (1 + κs²)(1 + κa²)/(1 − κsa)²: Pythagoras's sum for the turn, its difference for the boost (γ² − (γβ)² = 1), nothing to keep for the stride. Each kind leaves one weight alone: the turn's heat (its angle), the boost's rapidity, the stride's plain ds. Only the turn's has a total, π, so only the turn has the Cauchy and its corner. A rational turn comes home only at the quarter turns (t = 1 in four steps, t = 0 at once); no rational boost comes back; a stride never returns. The boost never reaches light's speed, s = ±1, the one place every boost leaves where it is.
