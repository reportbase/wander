The pinhole

The reader never divides. Light from the top of a thing comes through the pinhole along one ray, and the ray is the reading: a direction, which is already a ratio. Make the thing bigger and farther together and nothing the reader holds moves. Send its breadth to infinity and it arrives as one more direction, the horizon. The division V*/d belongs to the description, the writer's account of the reading.

The scene, drawn to fit: the pinhole O, the thing standing at its distance, the ray from its top. Scaling the world gives the same figure; only the bar of one H changes.

A flat screen one unit behind the pinhole: the image at V*/d, unbounded. An infinite breadth leaves it.

The reading as a direction: (h, v) on the quarter turn, bounded; an infinite breadth is the point (0, 1), the horizon.

The ruler reader: it measures two amounts in its own H and divides them. Its amounts move when the world is scaled; only their quotient does not. It touches breadths: it writes first and reads afterwards.

The pinhole forms the image point x/z by itself; no one computes it from x and z. The reach cancels between the two legs of each reading before it arrives. Behind it: Observation from Somewhere §9, "The inscribed reading is the ratio itself"; homogeneous coordinates (Möbius 1827). Other demos: two readers, writing costs one amount a step, all demos.
