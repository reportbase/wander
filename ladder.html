The distance ladder

How far is a body? A reader holding only its own grain has three ways, each one length read against another. Its own travel against the distance (parallax); the body's own width against the distance; and its light, thinning as the inverse square. Each rung gives out at its own limit: parallax and width at the resolution limit, where what they read falls under one address; light goes on past both, because the inverse square keeps going as dimming.

How far off each rung reads, by distance

Lines: the most each rung can be off (log scales). Parallax and width read a whole number of addresses, so they can be off by half an address over the addresses read, which grows as that number falls; they stop where it reaches zero, the dashed lines, their resolution limits. Dots: this distance's readings. Light reads exactly here; with photons counted, its error grows as 1/(2√N) and it too gives out, where fewer than a few photons arrive: the photon limit.

From the labs' LAD (the distance ladder): runs 1–2 (parallax, then width by Leavitt's beat), run 3 (light, the standard-candle rung, past the resolution limit); and Serial, Parallel and Nowhere, the central result (where the resolved becomes the unresolved: the resolution limit).
