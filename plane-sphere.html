Why the plane is special

In the plane, the reader's Cauchy plays four parts at once: the uniform turn carried by a Möbius map, where a random walker from the standpoint lands (harmonic measure), the law whose averages are the powers of the standpoint, and the two sweeps' rates combined. In space those parts come apart into separate laws, and turns stop commuting. Move the standpoint and watch one curve in the plane become several on the sphere.

The plane: one law

A reader at z inside the unit circle (drag it). Faint rays: its uniform turn, landing unevenly (violet ticks, their centroid the violet dot, z/2). Teal band: harmonic measure from z, where a walker released at z first meets the circle; its mean point is the gold dot, exactly z.

Density round the circle against the angle from z's side, times 2π (the uniform law is 1). Teal: harmonic measure. Gold dashes: the uniform law pushed through the Möbius map that takes the centre to z, computed from the map. Violet dots: 2L − 1, twice the landing less the uniform. Grey: the landing L itself. The first three are one curve.

The sphere: three laws

The same reader inside the unit ball, on the axis at distance r. Density on the sphere against the angle χ from the axis, times 4π. Teal: harmonic measure from the reader. Gold: the spherical Cauchy at ρ = r, the uniform sky under a boost of rapidity η = 2·artanh ρ, pushed through the boost here. Thin dashes: the nearest harmonic measure to the gold, and (violet) the best a·L + b. Grey: the landing L.

Turns: the plane's take turns, space's do not

The plane: an arrow turned by a and then by a/2 (gold), and by a/2 and then by a (teal). One place.

Space: a book turned by a about x and then about y (gold), and about y and then about x (teal), from where it started (grey). Two places, unless a is 0 or the turns share an axis.

What survives in space: a point is still an amount times a direction, and scaling the world moves only the amount; every pair of parties keeps its corner; from home, at the centre of the directions, all three laws are the uniform one, so the wedge's results stand.
